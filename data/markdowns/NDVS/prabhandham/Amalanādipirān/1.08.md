## english_scripts

pariyan āgi vanda avuṇan uḍal kīṇḍa
amararkkariya ādi ppirān araṅgattamalan mugattu
kariya āgi ppuḍai parandu miḻirndu śevvari ōḍi
nīṇḍa apperiya vāya kaṇgaḻ ennai ppēdaimai śeydanavē !

## meaning

He who ripped apart the mighty body of Hiraṇya when he came against Him; who is unattainable even to the Devās; who is the origin of everything; who helps all beings; who resides in Śrīraṅgam; and who is supremely pure—His eyes, overflowing with grace, dark and wide, streaked with red lines, have made him a fool.

## purport

When Hiraṇyakaśipu received a boon from Brahmā that he would not be slain by any of Brahmā's creations, he became arrogant and unleashed chaos throughout the three worlds. However, he failed to realise that there exists One who transcends even Brahmā, the Supreme Being who grants liberation to all and within whose divine being the entire universe resides. Blinded by pride and ignorance, the foolish Daitya persecuted the young and devoted Prahlāda, who remained steadfast in his devotion to Bhagavān. Unable to tolerate the suffering inflicted upon His beloved devotee, the Lord manifested without delay as Narasiṃha. Tearing apart the tyrant, He brought an end to his atrocities and delivered the worlds from fear and oppression.

That Narasiṃha is beyond all the Devatas. What none among Brahmā, Rudra, Indra, or any other celestial being could accomplish was effortlessly achieved by Bhagavān alone. He is the Supreme Reality, unattainable even to the greatest of the gods through their own efforts. This primordial Lord, the source of all existence, now resides in Śrīraṅgam, embodying absolute purity and perfection. The Āḻvār celebrates Him through the words *pariyan āgi vanda avuṇan uḍal kīṇḍa amararkkariya ādi ppirān araṅgattamalan mugattu*.

The Āḻvār's gaze then rests upon the Lord's divine eyes, which overflow with grace, compassion, and affection. Who could behold such eyes without melting in devotion? Brahmā, the creator himself, meditates upon those eyes. Ikṣvāku lost his heart to their beauty. Yāmunācārya became a servant of the Lord through their enchanting influence, and Dhanurdāsa abandoned all worldly attachments after realising that nothing in existence was worthy of contemplation when compared to those divine eyes. After beholding the eyes of the All-seeing Lord, what else remains worthy of being seen?

Those eyes are dark, expansive, and adorned with delicate reddish streaks. The Āḻvār delights in their beauty and symbolism. Just as a fisherman attracts fish with bait, the Lord draws the minds and hearts of devotees towards Himself through the irresistible charm of His eyes. Their glance captivates, comforts, and conquers all who behold them. At this stage, the Āḻvār can no longer maintain composure. Having ascended to the heights of *Parama-bhakti*, he becomes completely overwhelmed by the Lord's beauty. The divine eyes of Raṅganātha have entirely seized his heart, leaving him intoxicated with devotion and unable to think of anything other than the Lord.
