## tamil_scripts

கொண்டல் வண்ணனைக் கோவலனாய் வெண்ணெய்
உண்ட வாயன் என் உள்ளம் கவர்ந்தானை
அண்டர்கோன் அணி அரங்கன் என் அமுதினைக்
கண்ட கண்கள் மற்று ஒன்றினைக் காணாவே

## english_scripts

koṇḍal vaṇṇanai kōvalanāy veṇṇey uṇḍa vāyan
en uḻḻam kavarndānai aṇḍar kōn aṇi araṅgan
en amudinai kkaṇḍa kaṇgaḻ maṭronṟinai kkāṇāvē

## meaning

My eyes, which have seen Him who has the form of a moisture-laden cloud, who has the mouth with which He ate butter when born as a Yādava, who stole his heart, who is the Lord of the Nityasūris, who rests in Śrīraṅgam, an ornament to this world, and who is nectar to him, will not see anything else.

## purport

Tiruppāṇ Āḻvār has now beheld the complete divine form of the Lord in the previous pāsuram. Having experienced the full beauty of Raṅganātha, he no longer desires to see anything else. His eyes have attained the most precious vision that can ever be beheld. That divine form is *Aprākṛtika*, entirely beyond the realm of material existence. What could possibly be compared to such a form? The Āḻvār declares that this vision has completely stolen his heart, just as the Lord once stole butter from the homes of the Gopīs when He appeared as Yādava Kṛṣṇa. The Lord who is the master of even the Nitya Sūris, who are untouched by karma and eternally established in His service, has now captured the heart of the Āḻvār.

Overwhelmed by this experience, the Āḻvār wonders, "What remains to be seen now?" Having beheld the Supreme Being reclining upon Ādiśeṣa, the embodiment of sweetness, simplicity, and unfailing protection, nothing else appears worthy of attention. What could ever equal that divine vision? What could compare with the form that enchanted even the great ascetics and Ṛṣis, who longed to embrace Him when He appeared before them as Śrī Rāmacandra? This alone is the true nectar, infinitely superior to the celestial nectar that merely grants temporary freedom from death and suffering. The beauty and grace of Bhagavān bestow an eternal fulfilment that transcends all worldly attainments.

Having attained the culmination of divine experience, the Āḻvār now prays for Mokṣa. Responding to this heartfelt appeal, Raṅganātha bestows liberation upon him. Through the grace of the Lord's sacred lotus feet, the Āḻvār attains Paramapadam, the eternal abode that is the true goal of all spiritual endeavour. Thus ends the sublime journey of Tiruppāṇ Āḻvār, who not only experienced the unsurpassed beauty of the Lord but also revealed to the world the path that leads to Vaikuṇṭhanātha Himself. Therefore, surrender is offered unto that blessed Āḻvār, who showed the way to the eternal abode through his divine experience and sacred hymns.
