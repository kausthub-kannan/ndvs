## english_scripts

ālamā marattin ilaimēl oru pālakanāy
ñālam ēzum uṇḍān araṅgattaravin aṇaiyān
kōlamā maṇi āramum muttuttāmamum muḍivilladōr ezil
nīla mēni aiyō! niṟai koṇḍaden neñjinaiyē!

## meaning

He who was (lying as) a small child on a leaf of the giant banyan tree and swallowed all seven worlds, and who is lying on the sweet bed of Ādiśeṣa, His blue colored (dark) divine body without par in its beauty and with the beautiful and special gemmed garlands and pearl necklaces has alas, stolen the completeness of my heart.

## purport

After the dissolution of the entire universe, nothing remains except the Supreme Lord. The *Mahānārāyaṇopaniṣad* declares that from this primordial Being, who alone exists upon the cosmic waters, Brahmā, known as Hiraṇyagarbha, comes into existence. The *Viṣṇu Purāṇa* further describes how the Lord floated upon the cosmic ocean as a divine child resting upon a banyan leaf. The Āḻvār recalls this wondrous manifestation and marvels that the very same child now reclines upon Ādiśeṣa in Śrīraṅgam. This experience is expressed through the words *ālamā marattin ilaimēl oru pālakanāy ñālam ēẓum uṇḍān araṅgattaravin aṇaiyān*.

The Āḻvār's heart is now completely captivated by the *Svarūpa* of Bhagavān. Before him appears a divine blue-hued child, adorned with radiant ornaments, pearl garlands, lotus decorations, and countless celestial embellishments. His beauty is immeasurable and beyond all description. The Lord's dark blue complexion resembles a rain-laden cloud, ever ready to shower compassion and grace upon His devotees. The Āḻvār delights in this enchanting vision through the words *kōlamā maṇi āramum muttuttāmamum muḍivilladōr ezil nīla mēni aiyō! niṟai koṇḍaden neñjinaiyē!*.

Overwhelmed by this experience, the Āḻvār exclaims in wonder and amazement. The Lord's divine form has completely filled and possessed his heart, leaving no space for any other thought or attachment. In beholding such unsurpassed beauty, the Āḻvār realises with certainty that Bhagavān alone is the supreme goal of existence, the ultimate *Puruṣārtha* sought by all beings. Having experienced His beauty, compassion, and majesty, there remains nothing else worthy of pursuit. The Lord Himself stands revealed as both the means and the end, the highest treasure to be attained and eternally enjoyed.
