## english_scripts

tuṇḍa veṇpiṟaiyan tuyar tīrttavan
añjiṟaiya vaṇḍuvāz pozil śūz araṅga nagar mēya appan
aṇḍar aṇḍa pagiraṇḍattu
orumānilam ezumāl varai
muṭrum uṇḍa kaṇḍam kaṇḍīr aḍiyēnai uyyakkoṇḍadē

## meaning

That Śrīraṅganātha, who removed the sin of Rudra, who wears the white crescent moon; who resides in Śrīraṅgam, surrounded by gardens where bees with beautiful wings dwell—His throat, which swallowed this world, the other worlds, the seven mountains, and the entire universe, has saved him, who is His servant.

## purport

As a consequence of severing one of Brahmā's heads, Rudra incurred the sin of Brahmahatyā and sought a means to free himself from its effects. Rudra, revered as one of the greatest Vaiṣṇavas, performed penance to Raṅganātha on the banks of the Candra Puṣkariṇī. Through the grace of Mahā Viṣṇu, the bearer of the white crescent moon upon his matted locks was finally relieved of his suffering and freed from the burden of that sin. This is indicated by the expression *tuṇḍa veṇpiṟaiyaṉ tuyar tīrttavaṉ*. The same compassionate Lord resides in Śrīraṅgam, the sacred city surrounded by flourishing groves filled with flowers and resonant with the humming of bees, as celebrated in the words *añjiṟaiya vaṇḍuvāḻ poḻil śūz araṅga nagar mēya appaṉ*.

Finally, the Āḻvār revels in the immeasurable glory of the Lord through the words:

The Āḻvār reflects upon the time of *Pralaya*, when the entire universe reaches dissolution. At that time, the Supreme Lord, in His form as Saṅkarṣaṇa, withdraws all creation into Himself, leaving nothing apart from His own divine existence. The worlds, the mountains, the celestial systems, and all beings, from Brahmā down to the smallest ant, are absorbed into Him, only to be brought forth again at the beginning of the next cycle of creation. Gazing upon this wondrous act, the Āḻvār becomes captivated by the Lord's divine throat, which has contained the entirety of existence within itself.

Filled with astonishment, the Āḻvār reflects, "That very Lord, who creates, sustains, and withdraws the universe, has now taken possession of me and granted me salvation." By revealing His true *svarūpa*, severing the bonds of saṁsāra, and bestowing the privilege of rendering *kaiṅkaryam* with complete joy and fulfilment, the Lord has rescued the Āḻvār from the endless cycle of birth and death. Thus, the Āḻvār delights in the Supreme Protector, who unfailingly comes to the aid of those who seek refuge in Him. His deep devotion shines through his wonder at the Lord's cosmic deeds and his profound gratitude for the personal grace that transformed his life and secured his ultimate welfare.
