## english_scripts

amalanādhi pirān aḍiyārkkennai āṭpaḍuttha vimalan\
viṇṇavar kōn viraiyār pozil vēṅgaḍavan\
nimalan ninmalan nīdi vānavan neezh madiḻ araṅgatthammān\
thirukkamala pādham vandhu en kaṇṇin uḻḻana okkinṟadhē

## meaning

He who is devoid of all impure qualities and is responsible for creation, thereby being called the Primordial One, made him serve His servants. The Lord of the Nityasūris, whose bliss is without a second, shines as Veṅkaṭeśvara on the divine hill. He, the ruler of Paramapadam, never expects anything in return and is compassionate towards His devotees. The same Lord of all the devas is reclining in Tiruvaraṅgam, which is surrounded by lofty walls. Oh! The lotus feet of Raṅganātha have come to him and are residing in his eyes.

## purport

Śrīman Nārāyaṇa is celebrated as *Paratattva*, the Supreme Reality, who is the source, sustainer, and controller of all existence. Tiruppāṇ Āḻvār proclaims this truth through the very opening word, *amalanādhipirān*, declaring Him to be the Lord who stands above all devas and celestial powers. This title itself reveals His unsurpassed supremacy and His flawless, blemishless nature.

The significance of this opening word extends even deeper into Vedāntic thought. The sacred syllable *Praṇava* (*A-U-M*) encapsulates the essence of all Vedic wisdom. The first letter of *amalanādhipirān* corresponds to the first sound of the *Praṇava*, which denotes Nārāyaṇa Himself. This profound connection is beautifully reflected in the iconography of Śrīraṅgam, where Namperumāḷ reclines upon Ādiśeṣa beneath the *Praṇavākāra Vimāna*, signifying that He is the very meaning and embodiment of the *praṇava-rahasya*. The Āḻvār further reinforces this supremacy by addressing Him as *viṇṇavar kōn*, the sovereign Lord of the celestial realm. Even Indra, the king of the devas, derives his authority from Him and remains dependent upon His grace.

Yet, while His greatness is immeasurable, it is His compassion that captivates the hearts of devotees. The Lord does not remain distant in His majesty. Instead, He lovingly reaches out to uplift souls trapped in *saṃsāra*. When a devotee surrenders completely to Him, the accumulated burden of karma and the deep-rooted impressions of countless births are gradually removed through His grace. It is for this reason that Tiruppāṇ Āḻvār praises Him as *nimalan*, the Pure One who purifies others.

The Āḻvār's meditation then rests upon the divine feet of the Lord. These sacred feet, softer than newly blossomed lotus petals, are adorned with auspicious marks and possess incomparable beauty. They are the refuge sought by devotees, sages, and even the gods themselves. These are the very feet that measured all the worlds during the *Trivikrama-avatāra*, revealing His sovereignty over the entire cosmos. Their beauty is inseparable from their mercy, for they descend to reach those who are unable to rise by their own effort. Even Śrī Mahālakṣmī, the eternal Divine Mother, joyfully serves those feet of Her own accord, recognizing them as the source of all auspiciousness.

The teachings of the *Brahma-sūtras*, as understood in the Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition, are beautifully reflected in this pāsuram. The *jīva* is an eternal conscious entity, subtle like an *aṇu*, whose intrinsic nature is to serve the Supreme Lord. This truth is echoed in the phrase *aḍiyārkku eṉṉai āṭpaḍutta*, where the Āḻvār rejoices in being placed in the service of the Lord's devotees. *Brahman*, on the other hand, is the Supreme Reality who pervades, governs, and transcends everything. This is the Lord praised as *amalanādhipirān*.

The relationship between the *jīva* and *Brahman* is one of *Śeṣa-Śeṣi-bhāva*, the relationship between the servant and the Master. Though inseparably connected, the two remain eternally distinct. The greatest obstacle preventing the *jīva* from realizing this relationship is ignorance born of ego and false identification with the material world. Bound by this misconception, the soul wanders through the cycle of birth and death. Yet the Lord, praised as *nimalan*, removes these impurities through His grace and draws the soul towards Himself.

Ultimately, the path to liberation lies not in personal strength or intellectual achievement, but in surrender. Tiruppāṇ Āḻvār experiences this directly when he declares that the *tirukkamala-pādam*, the divine lotus feet of the Lord, have entered and become firmly established within his vision. To seek refuge at those sacred feet is to attain the highest goal. They alone grant freedom from *saṃsāra* and lead the soul to its eternal destiny of loving service in the presence of the Supreme Lord.
