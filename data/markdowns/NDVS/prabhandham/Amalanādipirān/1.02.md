## english_scripts

uvanda uḻḻattanāy ulagam aḻandaṇḍam uṟa
nivanda nīḻ muḍiyan anṟu nērnda niśāśararai
kavarnda veṅgaṇai kkāguttan kaḍiyār pozil araṅgattammān
araiccivanda āḍaiyin mēl śenṟadām en śindanaiyē

## meaning

My Lord, who with a joyous mind measured all the worlds by growing as tall as the heavens, with a crown that reached the skies; who once, as Rāma, took the lives of the opposing Asuras with His fierce arrows; and who now rests in Araṅgam amidst fragrant forests—upon Him, his mind has settled.

## purport

The Lord draws all beings towards Himself through the irresistible charm of His auspicious qualities and His wondrous divine form, whose beauty transcends ordinary description. Every attribute of His exists solely for the welfare and delight of His devotees. There is not even a single quality in Him that could give rise to aversion or displeasure. For this reason, the scriptures describe Him as *Nirguṇa*, not in the sense of being devoid of qualities, but as being entirely free from all inauspicious attributes. He is the embodiment of limitless auspiciousness and boundless compassion. Reflecting upon this truth, the Āḻvār addresses Him as *uvaṇḍa uḷḷattaṉāy*, the One whose heart overflows with delight and affection for His devotees.

By referring to the Lord as Vāmana, the Āḻvār simultaneously recalls His manifestation as Trivikrama through the expression *ulagam aḷandaṇḍam uṟa*. The seemingly diminutive Vāmana assumed a cosmic form and measured all the worlds with His divine strides. This act was not merely a display of supreme sovereignty but also an expression of universal grace. As His sacred lotus feet traversed the worlds, every region, every being, and every realm received the sanctifying touch of those auspicious feet. Thus, the *Trivikrama-avatāra* revealed both His immeasurable greatness and His desire to bless all creation.

The Āḻvār next contemplates the beauty of Śrī Rāmacandra and describes Him through the expression *nivaṇḍa nīḷ muḍiyaṉ*. This evokes the Lord's luxuriant curly locks and His majestic bearing. The beauty of Śrī Rāma is celebrated throughout the tradition as captivating not merely human beings but all forms of life. Indeed, the very name Rāma is traditionally explained as:

He who delights and enchants all through His beauty, virtues, and conduct.

The Āḻvār therefore delights in the exquisite form of the Lord, whose outward beauty perfectly reflects His inner excellence.

The remembrance of Śrī Rāma naturally leads to His role as the upholder of righteousness. Rāvaṇa incurred destruction because of his lustful disposition and his improper attitude towards Sītā Devī, the universal mother and the eternal consort of Śrīman Nārāyaṇa. Yet the same Lord who destroyed Rāvaṇa readily accepted the *śaraṇāgati* of Vibhīṣaṇa, Rāvaṇa's own brother. In this context, the term *niśācarar* refers to the Asuras. Although both Rāvaṇa and Vibhīṣaṇa belonged to the same race, the Lord's dealings with them differed according to their disposition. Rāvaṇa was subdued through the Lord's strength and power, whereas Vibhīṣaṇa was won over through His love, mercy, and affectionate acceptance. Thus, the Lord demonstrated both His justice towards the arrogant and His boundless compassion towards those who sought refuge in Him through *prapatti*.

The Āḻvār then recalls the noble lineage into which Śrī Rāma descended. The illustrious Ikṣvāku dynasty produced many celebrated rulers and warriors, among whom Kakutstha occupied a place of great distinction. Since Rāma appeared in that revered lineage, He is praised as *kākuttan*. The people of Ayodhyā cherished Him with profound love and reverence, affectionately addressing Him as Perumāḷ. This title signifies not merely greatness but also the intimate bond of love between the Lord and His devotees. Since Raṅganātha was worshipped by Śrī Rāma Himself, the Lord of Śrīraṅgam came to be known as Periya Perumāḷ, the Supreme Lord who is adored even by the Lord who appeared as Rāma.

The pāsuram concludes with the Āḻvār's contemplation of the Lord's divine attire. His gaze, having journeyed through the Lord's qualities, incarnations, and form, now comes to rest upon the sacred garment described as *araiccivanda āḍaiyiṉ*. This refers to the Lord's *Pītāmbara*, the reddish-yellow garment that adorns His divine form. The Āḻvār becomes wholly absorbed in its beauty, for the brilliance of the garment forms a striking contrast with the Lord's dark *tirumēṉi*, which resembles a rain-laden cloud. In this harmonious union of colours, the devotee beholds yet another expression of the Lord's incomparable beauty, and his mind remains completely captivated by that enchanting vision.
