## tamil_scripts

## english_scripts

śadura mā madiḻ śūz ilaṅgaikkiṟaivan talaipattudira ōṭṭi
ōr veṅgaṇai uyttavan ōda vaṇṇan
madura mā vaṇḍu pāḍa mā mayilāḍaraṅgattammān
tiru vayiṭrudara bandam en uḻḻattuḻ ninṟulāginṟadē

## meaning

He who resides in Śrīraṅgam, where bees sing sweetly and majestic peacocks dance; who, with a single fierce arrow, caused the ten heads of the lord of Laṅkā, surrounded on all four sides by lofty walls, to fall; and whose complexion is that of the cool ocean—His ornament, which He wears around His waist, has now entered his inner heart and settled there.

## purport

To the south of Bhārata lies Laṅkā, which was once ruled by Kubera. Rāvaṇa seized this magnificent city from the Yakṣa king and established his dominion over it. Laṅkā was comparable to the celestial realm of Indra in its splendour and prosperity. Surrounded by formidable fortifications and adorned with resplendent golden structures, it stood as one of the most glorious cities in existence. Thiruppān Āḻvār observes that only an exceptionally powerful ruler could govern such a kingdom and therefore refers to Rāvaṇa as *Iraivan*, the mighty sovereign of Laṅkā.

Rāvaṇa's tyranny and the disturbances he inflicted upon the worlds of the Devatas ultimately led to the descent of the Lord as Śrī Rāmacandra. The Lord effortlessly destroyed this formidable Asura, causing his ten heads to fall one after another as easily as a puppeteer manipulates and casts aside his puppets. Through the expressions *udhira oṭṭi* and *vem kaṇai uyttavan ōda vaṇṇan*, the Āḻvār delights in the Lord's *Vīrattva*, His unsurpassed valour and heroic prowess, manifested in the destruction of Rāvaṇa.

Thiruppān Āḻvār is immersed in *Viṣṇu-samleśa-bhāva*, a state of profound and intimate union with the Lord through devotion. Having reached the pinnacle of his experience of Raṅganātha, he perceives the entire environment of Śrīraṅgam as participating in divine worship. The bees hum melodiously as though chanting "Raṅga, Raṅga," while the peacocks dance in joyous response to that sacred refrain. In the Āḻvār's vision, both the bees and the peacocks celebrate the glory of Raṅga. Through the phrase *madhura māvandu pāda māmayil āḍu araṅgattu amman*, he experiences the omnipresent Lord whose presence fills and sanctifies all creation.

The Āḻvār then contemplates the celebrated Dāmodara-līlā of Namperumāḷ through the words *thiruvayiṟṟu udhara bandham en uḷḷattil niṉṟu ulagiṉṟadhe*. Namperumāḷ is the Supreme Reality, beyond the reach of even Brahmā and Rudra. Yet this very Lord allowed Himself to be bound by a simple rope in the hands of Yaśodā. This līlā reveals the incomparable love and affection that He bears towards His devotees and *Prapannas*. The rope with which He was bound left a mark upon His divine stomach, a mark that now remains vividly present within the Āḻvār's heart. As he meditates upon this sacred sign, he becomes immersed in the Lord's *Saulabhya*, His extraordinary accessibility and loving condescension towards those who seek refuge in Him.
