## english_scripts

pāram āya pazavinai paṭraṟuttu
ennai ttan vāram ākki vaittān vaittadanṟi ennuḻ pugundān
kōra mādavam śeydanan kolaṟiyēn araṅgattammān
tiru āra mārvadanṟō aḍiyēnai āṭkoṇḍadē

## meaning

He who removed his association with unbearable and ancient sins; who not only made him a person filled with love for Him and bound to Him, but also entered his very mind—for which he knows not when he performed such great and arduous penances—such Raṅganātha's chest, the abode of Lakṣmī, and the ornament adorning it, have made him serve Him.

## purport

Thiruppān Āḻvār speaks of how Raṅganātha Himself transformed the course of his life. From beginningless time, the Jīvātmā accumulates the results of its actions. This accumulated store of karma is known as *Sañcita Karma*. It serves as a major obstacle that prevents the Jīvātmā from attaining Mokṣa. When the Āḻvār declares *pāram āya paḻavinai paṭṟaṟuttu*, he indicates that the grace of Raṅganātha severed all connections with these accumulated karmic bonds. This idea is beautifully echoed in Rāmānujācārya's *Gadyatrayam*. In the *Śrī Raṅga Gadyam*, he states that when Raṅganātha casts His merciful glance upon His devotees, sins as vast as mountains are instantly reduced to ashes. Not only does He destroy their sins, but He also enters their hearts, enabling them to perform *Kaiṅkaryam* to Himself and to His devotees. This experience is expressed through the words *eṉṉai ttaṉ vāram ākki vaittāṉ vaittadaṉṟi eṉṉuḷ pugundhāṉ*.

The Āḻvār then marvels at the Lord's boundless compassion and wonders what extraordinary merit he could possibly have accumulated to deserve such grace. "What great austerities could have been performed by me," he asks, "for Paravāsudeva, reclining upon Ādiśeṣa as Raṅga, to destroy my sins and draw me close to Himself?" A similar sentiment is found in the works of Parāśara Bhaṭṭar, the eminent scholar and Ācārya of the Śrī Vaiṣṇava tradition. In one of his hymns, he pleads before Raṅganātha, saying, "O Lord, I am the lowest among the lowly. What will You do when one such as I enters Your temple precincts, which are themselves Vaikuṇṭha?" Both Bhaṭṭar and the Āḻvār celebrate the Lord's *Dayā-guṇa*, His infinite compassion. Even those who possess no worthiness of their own are uplifted by His grace, drawn into His presence, and made to realise the true purpose of life.

The pāsuram concludes with the Āḻvār's contemplation of Bhagavān together with His inseparable consort, Śrī Mahālakṣmī. Although Mahālakṣmī is eternally united with Bhagavān, the two together constitute the complete *Īśvara-tattva*. Therefore, she eternally resides upon His divine chest, reposing there in bliss. Her presence is associated with the auspicious mark known as *Śrīvatsam*, which adorns the Lord's chest. When the Vedas seek to identify the Supreme Reality, they glorify Brahman through countless distinguishing attributes, known as *Viśeṣaṇas*. Among all these exalted characteristics, particular emphasis is placed upon His being *Śrīyapati* and *Śrīman*—the Lord who is eternally united with Śrī, Bhū, and Nīlā Devīs. The scriptures proclaim that the Supreme Puruṣa, the creator and sustainer of all beings, is He who is never separated from Lakṣmī. Reflecting upon this truth, the Āḻvār acknowledges that it is through the *anugraha* of Mahālakṣmī that he has been blessed with the privilege of rendering service to the Lord.
