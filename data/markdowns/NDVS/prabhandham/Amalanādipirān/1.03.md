## english_scripts

mandi pāy vaḍa vēṅgaḍa mā malai\
vānavargaḻ śandi śeyya ninṟān araṅgattaravin aṇaiyān\
andi pōl niṟattāḍaiyum adan mēl ayanai ppaḍaittadōr ezil\
undi mēladanṟō aḍiyēn uḻḻattin uyirē

## meaning

He who stands in the mountains of the northern Tirumalā, where male monkeys leap from branch to branch, and where the Nityasūris worship Him with flowers; He who rests in Tiruvāraṅgam, reclining upon the sweet bed of Ananta (Ādiśeṣa)—upon His red garment and His most beautiful navel, from which even the creator Brahmā was born, has his innermost heart settled.

## purport

Here is the refined version, preserving the original meaning, theological intent, and devotional tone while improving grammar, flow, and readability:

Thiruppān Āḻvār is experiencing Thiruveṅgaḍamuḍaiyān of Tirumalā. Here, the Āḻvār uses the expression *Vaḍa Veṅgaḍam*, referring to the Northern Veṅgaḍam. Āḻvār Tirunagari is traditionally known as the Southern Tirumalā, and therefore the Āḻvār explicitly identifies Tirumalā through the phrase *mandhi pāy vaḍa veṅgaḍa māmalai*.

Here, *vāṉavar* refers to the residents of the upper worlds, namely the Devatas. These celestial beings descend to worship Śrīnivāsa Perumāḷ at Tirumalā, while human beings ascend the seven sacred hills to offer their worship. Thus, Śrīnivāsa stands atop the hill as a meeting place for both the Devatas and human beings, granting equal access to all who seek to render service unto Him. The very same Lord who reclines upon the serpent couch of Ādiśeṣa as Namperumāḷ in Śrīraṅgam stands upon the summit of Tirumalā as Thiruveṅgaḍamuḍaiyān. Thiruppān Āḻvār simultaneously enjoys the forms of Śrīnivāsa and Raṅganātha while describing Him through the words *vāṉavargaḷ sandhi seyya niṉṟāṉ araṅgatthu araviṉ aṇaiyāṉ*.

The Āḻvār once again turns his attention to the reddish *Pītāmbara* adorning Raṅganātha and alludes to it through the phrase *andhi pōl niṟattadaiyum adhan mēl ayaṉaip paḍaittathōr ezhil*. Above this divine garment shines the Lord's lotus navel. This is the very navel from which *Ayaṉ*, Brahmā, the creator of the universe, was brought forth. Here, the Āḻvār enjoys the Anantapadmanābha-svarūpa of the Lord. Overwhelmed by this vision, he marvels, "Is this not the very navel that gave birth to Brahmā? Now it has completely captivated my heart!" This experience is expressed through the words *undi mēladanṟō aḍiyēn uḷḷattin uyirē*.
