## english_scripts

kaiyin ār śuri śaṅganal āziyar nīḻvarai pōl
meyyanār tuḻaba viraiyār kamaz nīṇ muḍi em
aiyanār aṇi araṅganār aravin aṇaimiśai mēya māyanār
śeyya vāy aiyō ! ennai ccindai kavarndaduvē !

## meaning

He who bears the conch and the flaming Cakra (Tiruvāḻi) in His divine hands; who possesses a divine body resembling a great mountain; who wears a divine crown fragrant with sweet Tiruttuḻāy (Tulasi); who is his Lord; who reclines in Śrīraṅgam, an ornament among sacred places; who rests upon the sweet bed of Ādiśeṣa (Tiruvanantāḻvān); and who performs wondrous deeds—His red lips have stolen his mind.

## purport

Bhagavān is traditionally depicted with two prominent divine weapons: Pāñcajanya, the conch, and Sudarśana, the discus. Pāñcajanya symbolizes divine knowledge, while Sudarśana represents His *Saṅkalpa-śakti*, the irresistible power of His divine will. Although the Lord has no need of weapons for His protection or victory, these eternal attendants remain ever ready to serve Him. To Asuras and Daityas such as the Hiraṇyas, they appear fearsome and destructive. The Sudarśana, whose glories are celebrated in the *Ahibudhnya Saṃhitā*, is said to destroy the Lord's enemies even before receiving an explicit command. Similarly, Pāñcajanya subdues adversaries merely through the awe and terror inspired by its resounding call. Yet to *Prapannas*, these very weapons appear not as instruments of destruction but as beautiful ornaments adorning the Lord. Through his divine vision, the Āḻvār beholds these sacred weapons and delights in the beauty of the Lord who bears them, as expressed in the words *kaiyiṉ ār śuri śaṅgaṉal āḻiyar*.

The Āḻvār then contemplates the Lord's majestic form. His physique is powerful and perfectly proportioned, while His broad chest resembles a great mountain in its grandeur and magnificence. This is the very chest that grants refuge and peace to devotees and serves as the eternal abode of Mahālakṣmī, the Mother of the universe. Adorned with fragrant garlands of *tulasī*, the Lord appears both transcendent and infinitely compassionate. For devotees, the vision of this divine form alone constitutes the highest attainment and fulfilment. The Āḻvār celebrates this beauty through the expression *meyyaṉār tuḻāba viraiyār kamaḻ nīṇ muḍi em*. This very Lord reclines upon Ādiśeṣa as Raṅganātha in the sacred city of Śrīraṅgam.

As the Āḻvār's gaze ascends further, it comes to rest upon the Lord's radiant red lips. At this point, he is overwhelmed by the intensity of the Lord's beauty. The experience becomes almost unbearable in its sweetness and charm. Captivated beyond measure, the Āḻvār instinctively exclaims, "Aiyo!" in astonishment and wonder. Those crimson lips completely conquer his mind and heart, leaving no room for any other thought. Through this experience, the Āḻvār reveals the many facets of Śrīraṅganātha: the bearer of divine weapons, the possessor of an incomparable and transcendent form, the Lord who reclines in Śrīraṅgam, and the enchanter who captivates the hearts of His devotees through His boundless beauty. The depth of the Āḻvār's devotion shines forth in his detailed contemplation of the Lord's attributes and in the ecstatic delight that arises from beholding His divine form.
