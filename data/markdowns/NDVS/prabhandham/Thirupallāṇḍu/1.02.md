## meaning

May You, along with us, Your inseparable servants, abide for everlasting ages. May Śrī Devi, who adorns You as a living ornament upon Your right chest, be eternally preserved. May the luminous, blazing cakra in Your right hand, fierce and sharp, ever capable of annihilating foes, endure through endless time. May Your divine conch, the mighty Pāñcajanyam, whose thunderous sound pierces through the tumult of battle, resound for everlasting years.

## purport

Continuing from the previous pāsuram, Periyāḻvār persists in seeking protection for Bhagavān Himself. In this pāsuram, the Āḻvār expresses *Bhāgavata-śeṣatvam*. He not only prays for Bhagavān to live long, but also extends his blessings to the *prapanna-kūṭam*—the assembly of great devotees. Yet he adds a significant condition: they should not merely live long, but live long in eternal service to Him, never becoming separated from Him. Periyāḻvār, being thoroughly grounded in Vedānta, begins the pāsuram with the word *aḍiyōm*. By this, he establishes that all *jīvātmās* are the servants of the Lord. This is the very *svarūpa* of the *jīvātmā*—to remain *śeṣa* to Bhagavān, the *Sarva-śeṣi*. Therefore, he prays that all devotees may live long solely for the purpose of rendering service to Him in this world and beyond.

Continuing his benedictions, the Āḻvār next turns to Śrī Mahālakṣmī and prays for her well-being. He exclaims, “May Śrī Devī, who eternally resides upon Your chest, seated on the Śrīvatsa mark, also live long!” This declaration carries profound Vedāntic significance. Śrī, celebrated in the scriptures as the *puruṣakāra-bhūtā*, is the compassionate mediatrix who guides the *jīvātmās* towards *Puruṣottama*. She is never separated from Bhagavān, and therefore both together constitute the complete *Īśvara-tattva*. Indeed, it is through her grace that the world comes to recognize Nārāyaṇa as the Supreme Brahman. Periyāḻvār joyfully proclaims, “May that Mahālakṣmī live long!” He further delights in the thought that she not only resides upon His chest, but dwells there in perfect bliss. Thus he blesses both Divine Consorts together, praying that they may ever remain united and never be separated from one another.

Having offered protection to Bhagavān and Śrī Mahālakṣmī, the Āḻvār next beholds the Lord’s two divine weapons. His gaze first falls upon Sudarśana, whose radiance surpasses the brilliance of thousands upon thousands of suns. Sudarśana embodies the *saṅkalpa-śakti* of Bhagavān. The universe itself comes into existence through the mere will of Nārāyaṇa, and that divine will is symbolically represented by Sudarśana. Though this blazing discus appears terrifying to the *asuras*, it appears as the most beautiful ornament to the devotees. Mesmerized by its splendour and majesty, Periyāḻvār exclaims, “May that divine Cakra live long!”

Thereafter, he turns to Pañcajanya, the Lord’s sacred conch, and blesses it as well. Pañcajanya is renowned for instilling fear in the hearts of the wicked through its resounding call. In the *Bhagavad Gītā*, while narrating the events of Kurukṣetra to Dhṛtarāṣṭra, Sañjaya declares: sa ghoṣho dhārtarāṣhṭrāṇāṁ hṛidayāni vyadārayat The mighty sound of the Pāṇḍavas’ conches, led by Kṛṣṇa’s Pañcajanya, shattered the hearts of Dhṛtarāṣṭra’s sons with fear. Yet to the devotees, that very conch is an object of immense beauty. Periyāḻvār marvels at how its pure white radiance perfectly complements the dark-blue hue of the Lord’s *tirumēni*. Held in the hand of the *nīla-megha-śyāma* Lord, the dazzling whiteness of Pañcajanya enhances the beauty of Bhagavān even further. Unable to contain his joy upon witnessing such divine splendour, the Āḻvār bursts forth in loving benediction: “May that Pañcajanya also live long!”
