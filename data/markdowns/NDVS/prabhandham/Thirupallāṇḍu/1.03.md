## meaning

If you are devoted to kainkaryam, the joyful service unto the Lord and the highest goal of life, come and join us. Let us carry the sacred mud and sandal paste to be distributed as prasādam during the festival. Those who seek rewards other than service to the Lord have no place among us. Free from blemishes and worldly desires, our lineage remains untainted across generations. We sing Pallāṇḍu in praise of Śrī Rāma, who destroyed the rākṣasās of Laṅkā with His divine army.

## purport

After praising the Lord, Periyāḻvār calls upon His devotees to join him in singing for the Lord’s protection. In this pāsuram, he first invites the foremost among devotees those for whom service to Bhagavān is the ultimate goal. For such devotees, serving the Lord in His *arcā-rūpa* in this world and, upon liberation, serving Him eternally in *Śrī Vaikuṇṭha*, is everything. They are known as *Bhagavad-lābhārthīs*, those who seek only serving Bhagavān.

Among devotees, the scriptures broadly classify four types. Bhagavān Himself describes them in the *Bhagavad Gītā*: chatur-vidhā bhajante māṁ janāḥ sukṛitino ’rjuna |\
ārto jijñāsur arthārthī jñānī cha bharatarṣhabha ||\
teṣāṁ jñānī nitya-yukta eka-bhaktir viśiṣyate |\
priyo hi jñānino ’tyartham ahaṁ sa cha mama priyaḥ || 7.16–7.17 ||

Among the devotees are those who seek worldly possessions (*arthārthīs*), those who seek relief from distress (*ārtas*), those who seek to know the nature of Brahman (*jijñāsus*), and those who are firmly established in that knowledge and remain immersed in the experience of Bhagavān (*jñānīs*). It is this last category that Periyāḻvār calls upon. These are the devotees who seek nothing other than service to Bhagavān. For them, serving Him is everything.

The Āḻvār invites them to come and sing the divine names of Bhagavān. He describes them as those who serve Him with both mind and body. They do not merely contemplate Bhagavān inwardly; they also engage in physical service to Him. These are the *prapannas* whom the Āḻvār welcomes into his company.

At the same time, he clearly excludes those who do not serve the Lord, those who are immersed in material pleasures and spend their lives praising and serving mortal human beings. Such people have no place in this assembly. Periyāḻvār declares that those who glorify humans instead of the Supreme Lord should not join them. Those who seek to glorify humans would not agree to seek protection for Bhagavān whole heatedly. They would indeed not let others too sing the glories our Lord. The devotees whom he calls are those who are ready to serve Bhagavān through birth after birth. For them, service is more desirable than liberation itself. Liberation is merely a natural consequence of devotion; service alone is their cherished goal.

Therefore, Periyāḻvār calls out to such devotees: “Come! Let us together praise the Bhagavān who destroyed the rākṣasas of Laṅkā as Śrī Rāma. Let us sing His glories and seek to protect our Rāma through our auspicious benedictions.”
