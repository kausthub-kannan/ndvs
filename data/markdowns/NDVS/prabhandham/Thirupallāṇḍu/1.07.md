## meaning

Upon our upper arms we bear the sacred imprint of the divine cakra, signifying our lineage of unwavering service to the Lord. It is He, Kṛṣṇa, wielder of the discus, who hurled His blazing weapon against Bhāṇāsura and severed his thousand arms. Let us raise our voices in praise of that glorious Lord and sing, “May You live long for many years!”

## purport

The *Kaivalyārthis* whom Periyāḻvār had previously called have now joined the assembly as well, but with a remarkable transformation. Upon their shoulders shine the sacred marks of the blazing Sudarśana Cakra and the divine Pañcajanya. These marks indicate that they have undergone the sacred rite of *Pañca-saṁskāra* under the guidance of an Ācārya. No longer content with mere *ātmānubhava*, they have embraced the path of complete surrender and service to Bhagavān.

Piḷḷai Lokācārya extols the immeasurable grace of the Ācārya. When a *jīvātmā* comes under the shelter of an Ācārya, it receives the divine *Aṣṭākṣara Mahāmantra*. Through meditation upon this sacred mantra, Bhagavān Himself becomes attainable. Once Bhagavān has become one's own, what remains unattained? Even *Paramapada*, the supreme abode, becomes readily accessible. Through the grace of Periyāḻvār and the lineage of Ācāryas, these former *Kaivalyārthis* have realized that the bliss of Bhagavān far surpasses the limited joy of self-experience.

The emblems of the Cakra and Śaṅkha impressed upon their shoulders signify an even deeper truth. They now acknowledge that they belong entirely to Bhagavān. Whether associated with the body or separated from it, the *jīvātmā* remains His possession alone. Having realized their true nature as His servants, they join the assembly in singing His glories.

The devotees now praise Bhagavān as the conqueror of Bāṇāsura. This celebrated episode is narrated in the *Harivaṁśa* and elaborated upon in the *Śrīmad Bhāgavatam*. Bāṇa, the mighty son of Bali and a descendant of Prahlāda, imprisoned Aniruddha, the grandson of Śrī Kṛṣṇa, after Aniruddha and Uṣā had become attached to one another. In response, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma marched against Bāṇa's city to rescue Aniruddha.

Bāṇa was a great devotee of Rudra and had obtained the boon that Rudra himself would come to his aid in battle. Thus, when Kṛṣṇa arrived, a tremendous conflict arose. Yet before the Supreme Lord, all opposing forces proved powerless. Kṛṣṇa subdued the armies of Bāṇa and overcame the forces led by Rudra. The Lord bewildered Rudra and neutralized his resistance. Thereafter, He turned His attention to Bāṇa himself and severed his many arms.

However, Kṛṣṇa did not kill Bāṇa. Out of regard for Rudra's request and in keeping with the boon He had earlier granted to Prahlāda that He would not destroy his descendants, the Lord spared Bāṇa's life. He removed all but four of Bāṇa's arms and humbled his pride. As stated in the *Śrīmad Bhāgavatam*.darpopaśamanāyāsya pravṛkṇā bāhavo mayā sūditaṁ ca balaṁ bhūri yac ca bhārāyitaṁ bhuvaḥ

The Lord declares that He severed Bāṇa's arms in order to destroy his arrogance and reduce the burden he had imposed upon the earth. Reflecting upon this divine victory, the transformed devotees proclaim: “We sing the glories of that Bhagavān, the subduer of Bāṇa. We sing for the protection of Śrī Kṛṣṇa, who severed Bāṇa's arms with the blazing Sudarśana Cakra. The same Cakra whose emblem we bear upon our shoulders stands as the mark of our eternal servitude. It proclaims that we belong to Him alone. Therefore, let us sing *pallāṇḍu* to that victorious Lord and celebrate His eternal glory.”
