## meaning

We, Your servants, wear the garments once worn by You. We partake of the sacred remnants offered to You and adorn ourselves with garlands and Tulasi that once graced Your divine form. We long to serve You eternally, both here and in the supreme abode, seeking everlasting service at Your feet.

## purport

Now, together with all the gathered devotees, Periyāḻvār sings auspicious benedictions for Bhagavān. In this pāsuram, the Āḻvār expresses that he desires only those things that have already been used and enjoyed by Bhagavān, never anything fresh or independent of Him. He declares that he wishes to wear only the *Pītāmbara* worn by the Lord. Here, the Āḻvār refers to the Lord's divine garment not merely as cloth, but as a sacred servant of Bhagavān. Just as Sudarśana is the foremost among the Lord's weapons and Garuḍa among His vehicles, *Pītāmbara* is revered as a *Nitya-sūri* who serves the Lord in the form of His divine garment.

Periyavāccān Piḷḷai explains that devotees cherish garments that bear the remnants of Bhagavān's sandal paste and turmeric, for such garments have already adorned His divine form. The devotee desires no ornament, fragrance, or adornment apart from that which has first been associated with Bhagavān.

Likewise, the Āḻvār proclaims that the devotees partake only of food that has first been offered to the Lord. Indeed, Śrī Kṛṣṇa teaches that all food should be offered to Him before being consumed, for everything that sustains life is ultimately granted through His grace. Offering food to Bhagavān is therefore an expression of gratitude and acknowledgment of His supreme ownership. The devotees do not consider anything their own; they receive everything back as His *prasāda*.

The Āḻvār further declares that they would gladly wear the garlands adorning Bhagavān. This sentiment evokes the divine episode of Āṇḍāḷ, who lovingly wore the garlands before offering them to the Lord. Those very garlands, sanctified by both the devotion of Āṇḍāḷ and the acceptance of Bhagavān, become objects of immense reverence. The devotees desire to wear only such garlands, for they have already been enjoyed by those whom they cherish the most.

Periyāḻvār then describes the spirit in which devotees serve Bhagavān. Their service is not mechanical or merely dutiful. It is rendered with utmost care, affection, and attentiveness. Just as Hanumān, when sent as a messenger to Sītā Devī, accomplished far more than the task assigned to him by assessing the strength of Rāvaṇa, understanding the condition of Laṅkā, and demonstrating the power of Śrī Rāma to the enemy, true devotees always seek to perform whatever pleases Bhagavān the most. Their sole concern is His delight and welfare.

Thus, the gathered devotees join Periyāḻvār in singing the Lord's glories. They praise the Lord of *Śravaṇa* Nakṣatra. They behold His divine form resting upon the pure white coils of Ādiśeṣa. Against that radiant white background, His dark cloud-hued form shines with incomparable beauty, resembling a rain-laden cloud resting upon a snow-white mountain peak.

Captivated by this vision, they sing in loving concern and *vātsalya-bhāva*: “O Lord who reclines upon the spotless white Ādiśeṣa! O dark cloud-hued One of immeasurable beauty! May You live long for countless ages and eons. May Your glory ever flourish. May You remain eternally auspicious!”
