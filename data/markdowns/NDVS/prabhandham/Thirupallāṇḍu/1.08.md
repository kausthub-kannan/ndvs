## english_scripts

meyyiḍa nalladōr śāndamum tandu

## meaning

The Lord has bestowed abundant grace upon me. He provides nourishing food, grants opportunities for daily service, allows service in His royal assembly, and blesses me with sacred remnants and honours. He adorns me with ornaments and fragrant sandal paste, purifying me and transforming me into a sanctified soul. I sing in praise of Him, the Lord whose banner bears mighty Garuḍa: “May You live long for many years!”

## purport

Following the same progression, the *Aiśvaryārthis* too have now joined the assembly. Through the grace of Periyāḻvār, they have come to realize the true greatness of Bhagavān. He is the Lord of all beings, the ruler of countless universes, and the sole giver of all blessings. More importantly, they have understood that Bhagavān does not merely grant what is asked of Him. Out of His boundless compassion, He grants far more than what His devotees seek.

Periyāḻvār beautifully expresses this idea through the analogy of "ghee in rice" rather than "rice in ghee". Ghee represents the most cherished and enjoyable object of desire, the *bhogya-vastu*. Ordinary devotees approach Bhagavān seeking limited and temporary benefits. Their prayers are like asking for a small quantity of ghee to be mixed into rice. Yet when a devotee truly surrenders, Bhagavān reverses the measure. Instead of merely granting the desired object, He bestows Himself. It becomes as though the rice itself is immersed in ghee. When Bhagavān Himself becomes the reward, what remains to be sought?

Periyavāccān Piḷḷai beautifully illustrates this principle through an incident from the life of Śrī Kṛṣṇa. When Kṛṣṇa arrived in Hastināpura, many eagerly invited Him to their homes, saying, “O Lord, please come to our house and bless us.” Yet Kṛṣṇa chose to stay at the house of Vidura. Vidura did not approach the Lord with a sense of ownership or pride. Instead, he welcomed Kṛṣṇa saying, “O Lord, please make Yourself comfortable in this house, for it is not mine but Yours.” Having realized that Bhagavān is the *Sarva-śeṣi*, the true owner of all beings and all things, Vidura approached Him with complete humility. Pleased by such pure devotion, free from ego and possessiveness, Kṛṣṇa joyfully accepted his hospitality.

The transformed *Aiśvaryārthis* now understand this truth. They realize that Bhagavān knows what is truly beneficial for His devotees far better than they themselves do. Therefore, rather than seeking temporary pleasures, they seek Him alone. Bhagavān adorns His devotees with ornaments such as *kuṇḍalam* and other divine decorations that delight Him when He beholds them. He allows them to wear the sandal paste offered to Him and partake of the *prasāda* sanctified by Him. Through these acts of intimate association, Bhagavān gradually increases the *sattva-guṇa* within His devotees and enables them to perceive His divine nature more clearly. What began as devotion motivated by worldly desires is transformed into pure devotion directed solely towards Him.

Having undergone this transformation, they too join the chorus of devotees singing the Lord's glories. They proclaim: “We sing for the protection of that Lord whose banner bears Garuḍa, the mighty enemy of serpents. We sing for that compassionate Bhagavān who grants not merely worldly rewards, but the highest of all attainments. He bestows Himself upon His devotees and makes them His own. May that Lord, the bearer of Garuḍa-dhvaja, live long for countless ages and eons. May His glory ever increase! May He be eternally auspicious!”
