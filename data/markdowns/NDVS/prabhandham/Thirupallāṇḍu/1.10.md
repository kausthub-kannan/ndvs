## meaning

Service rendered purely out of love, without expectation of reward, is the highest joy. On the day we surrendered ourselves as Your eternal servants, Your grace uplifted even our humble lineage. Let us therefore sing Pallāṇḍu in praise of You, O Lord who was born in Mathurā, shattered the mighty bow of Kaṁsa, and subdued the serpent Kāliya through Your divine play.

## purport

The *Bhāgavatas* continue to sing the glories of the Lord alongside Periyāḻvār. Among them, the former *Kaivalyārthis* now proclaim with great joy: “We have realized that serving You alone grants the highest bliss, a joy that can never be attained either through mere *ātmānubhava* or through the pursuit of fleeting worldly pleasures.” Having experienced the grace of Bhagavān, they now understand that the delight of *bhagavad-kainkarya* surpasses every other attainment.

They declare that the day they surrendered at the lotus feet of Bhagavān through the grace of an Ācārya was the day they were truly reborn. It was a birth that would never lead to another birth. On that auspicious day, they beheld the beauty of the Lord, were uplifted from the sufferings of *saṁsāra*, and obtained the assurance of His protection. They further proclaim that the grace they received extended not merely to themselves, but to their entire lineage.

Just as Śrī Rāma granted refuge not only to Vibhīṣaṇa but also to all those who accompanied him, though Vibhīṣaṇa alone formally sought surrender, Bhagavān extends His protection to all who are associated with His devotees. Such is the greatness of His compassion. Therefore, the devotees declare that the day one receives the grace of an Ācārya and is blessed with the sacred *Aṣṭākṣara Mahāmantra* is the day the *jīvātmā* begins its ascent beyond the cycle of birth and death. “All glories,” they proclaim, “to that Lord who grants the highest and most enduring joy!”

The devotees then sing the protection of the Lord who appeared among humanity as Śrī Kṛṣṇa. They marvel at His boundless compassion, wondering how eyes fashioned from material elements were blessed with the fortune of beholding His divine form, His incomparable beauty, and His wondrous *līlās*. Overwhelmed with gratitude, they exclaim: “O Lord, we have surrendered unto You, who appeared under the auspicious conjunction of Aṣṭamī and Rohiṇī Nakṣatra. O Lord of Jayanthi, we sing Your protection and pray that You may live long for countless ages!”

Their minds then turn to the glorious deeds of Kṛṣṇa. “Did You not dance upon the hoods of the serpent Kāliya and subdue his arrogance? Did You not shatter the pride of Kaṁsa and break the mighty bow in Mathurā? Did You not repeatedly protect the righteous and destroy the forces of evil?” Recalling these divine acts, the devotees are filled with *vātsalya-bhāva* and loving concern for the Lord.

Therefore they conclude their song with affectionate benedictions: “O Kṛṣṇa, protector of all worlds, may You live long! May no harm or misfortune ever approach You! May Your glory ever increase! This alone is our desire. We seek neither worldly pleasures nor liberation itself. Our only wish is to sing Your praises and offer our loving protection for all eternity.”
