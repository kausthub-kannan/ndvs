## tamil_scripts

## english_scripts

pallāṇḍu pallāṇḍu pallāyirattāṇḍu
pala kōḍi nūṟāyiram
mallāṇḍa tiṇdōḻ maṇivaṇṇā !
un śēvaḍi śevvi tirukkāppu

## meaning

O Lord, whose powerful bhuja-balam subdued the formidable wrestlers in Kaṁsa’s court, You shine with the brilliance of a radiant blue gem. May the divine splendour and exquisite beauty of Your lotus-red sacred feet be preserved and protected for countless crores of years. May Your supreme divinity remain ever undiminished through all ages to come.

## purport

Periyāḻvār, among all the Āḻvārs, expresses *vātsalya-bhāva* in its highest form. This composition of *Tiruppallāṇḍu* begins with the Āḻvār exclaiming, “Long live the Lord! May You live long! May You live for countless eons and ages!” This expression is the very embodiment of *vātsalya-bhāva*. Though Nārāyaṇa is the creator of Brahmā himself, and though the Vedas proclaim Him to be beyond complete description even by themselves, the Āḻvār prays that this Supreme Brahman, the *sarva-śeṣi* of all beings, should live long.

At first glance, this may seem paradoxical. To wish long life for the Supreme Being—the primordial cause of all causes—appears ironic. Yet this is the nature of *vātsalya-bhakti*. For Periyāḻvār, Bhagavān is beheld with the tender care and affection of a mother toward her child. For a mother, no matter how strong or capable her son may be, she constantly wishes for his well-being, health, and peaceful life. Similarly, though Bhagavān is the Supreme Being Himself, the Āḻvār is ever concerned for His welfare and therefore seeks to protect Him through his praises.

Hence, the Āḻvār continually glorifies Bhagavān with the benediction of *pallāṇḍu*. He blesses those shoulders of Bhagavān that uphold the entire universe, those very shoulders that vanquished Rāvaṇa and Hiraṇyakaśipu. “May those shoulders live long!” he proclaims. The Āḻvār also beholds the Lord’s lotus-red feet, the very feet that grant liberation to all beings. Gazing upon those sacred feet, he again exclaims, “May they live long!” Thus, out of boundless *vātsalya* and loving concern, Periyāḻvār offers auspicious blessings even to the One who is the eternal protector of all.
