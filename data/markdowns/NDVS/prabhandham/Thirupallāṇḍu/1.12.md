## english_scripts

pallāṇḍenṟu pavittiranai\
paramēṭṭiyai\

villiputtūr viṭṭuśittan virumbiya śol\
nallāṇḍenṟu navinṟuraippār\

pallāṇḍum paramātmanai\
śūzndirundēttuvar pallāṇḍē

## meaning

This hymn of Pallāṇḍu was composed by Viṣṇucitta of Śrīvilliputtūr in praise of the Supreme Lord who resides in the eternal realm. With heartfelt devotion he sang, “May You remain ever prosperous.” Whoever recites this hymn with devotion and regards it as a blessing bestowed by grace will join in chanting “Namaḥ Nārāyaṇāya.” By His mercy, they shall attain liberation and dwell near Him forever, singing Pallāṇḍu in endless praise.

## purport

Periyāḻvār concludes the *prabandham* by calling upon all devotees to sing auspicious benedictions for the Lord at all times, in all places, and under all circumstances. The *Tiruppallāṇḍu* is unique among hymns, for it is a composition that offers protection to Bhagavān Himself. Filled with boundless *vātsalya-bhāva*, the Āḻvār sees the Supreme Lord and instinctively bursts forth in concern for His welfare, blessing Him with *pallāṇḍu*. Bhagavān is celebrated in the *Viṣṇu Sahasranāma* as *Pavitrāṇāṁ Pavitramyo*. He is the purifier of all that is pure and the auspiciousness behind all that is auspicious. In ordinary experience, when something impure comes into contact with something pure, the pure object itself becomes tainted. Yet Bhagavān is unlike anything in this world. Even when the most inauspicious of beings comes into contact with Him, it is not He who is affected. Rather, the impure itself becomes purified by His association. He is the source of purity, the measure of auspiciousness, and the one who bestows sanctity upon all things. Therefore, He is praised as *Pavitrāṇāṁ Pavitram* and *Maṅgalānāṁ Maṅgalam*.

Periyāḻvār further reflects upon the relationship between Bhagavān and the *jīvātmā*. Ordinarily, the *jīvātmā* governs and directs the body in which it resides. Bhagavān, however, stands in an even higher relationship to all beings. He is not merely the indwelling ruler of the body; He is the *Ātmā* of the *jīvātmā* itself. As the *Antaryāmin*, He controls, sustains, and guides both the *jīvātmā* and the body. Thus, He alone is the true inner ruler of all existence.

This sacred hymn was composed by Viṣṇucitta, whose mind was constantly immersed in the contemplation of the all-pervading Viṣṇu. Those who recite this *Tiruppallāṇḍu* with devotion, understanding their true nature as belonging entirely to Bhagavān, and who meditate upon the essence of the *Aṣṭākṣara Mahāmantra* embodied in the phrase “Namo Nārāyaṇa,” attain the grace of the Lord. Through such remembrance, the *jīvātmā* becomes freed from all inauspiciousness, transcends the cycle of birth and death, and attains the supreme abode of Bhagavān.

Therefore, Periyāḻvār's final message is an invitation to all devotees: “Sing *pallāṇḍu* to our beloved Lord everywhere, at all times, and in every direction. Glorify Him ceaselessly and proclaim His auspiciousness. For when the Lord is praised and protected through loving devotion, even the most inauspicious of times becomes supremely auspicious. Let every place become sanctified by His names, and let every moment be filled with *pallāṇḍu*.”
