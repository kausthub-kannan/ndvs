## english_scripts

aṇḍakkulattukkadibadi āgi

## meaning

Having left behind those who pursue only material wealth, you have now entered the company of true devotees who seek nothing but loving service to the Lord. Come and bow at the sacred feet of Hṛṣīkeśa, the Master of all senses, the ruler of countless worlds, the protector of the virtuous, and the destroyer of evil. Let us chant His thousand names and sing, “May You live long for many years, for thousands upon thousands of years!”

## purport

Periyāḻvār now turns to another class of devotees known as *Aiśvaryārthis*. These are devotees who worship Bhagavān, not for the purpose of pleasing Him, nor for attaining *ātmānubhava*, but for obtaining temporary material benefits. Their devotion is directed towards Bhagavān, yet their aspirations remain centered upon worldly prosperity and enjoyment.

The Āḻvār addresses such devotees and reminds them of the true greatness of Bhagavān. He is not merely the ruler of a single world, but the Lord of countless universes extending beyond all measure. He is the supreme *Īśvara* of all realms and all beings. It is He who sustains and governs the cosmic order, protecting society and righteousness by destroying the forces of evil such as the *asuras* and *rākṣasas* who disturb the harmony of creation. He is also celebrated as *Hṛṣīkeśa*, the Lord of the senses. The name *Hṛṣīkeśa* signifies the One who governs and directs all the senses. When the senses are engaged in His service, He subdues and purifies them, guiding them towards their proper purpose.

Among these devotees are those who seek wealth and prosperity, as well as those who seek the recovery of wealth and comforts that have been lost. Indeed, Bhagavān alone, being the supreme *Īśvara*, has the power to grant such blessings. Yet Periyāḻvār gently instructs them that such material attainments should not be the ultimate object of their prayers. Bhagavān is the protector of all His devotees. No one desires our welfare more than He does. Out of His boundless compassion, He provides what is truly beneficial, granting what is necessary and withholding what may be harmful. Therefore, the devotee need not approach Him merely for temporary gains.

Instead, the Āḻvār teaches that service to Bhagavān alone should become the highest aspiration. In order to transform the mind and redirect it from fleeting worldly desires towards eternal service, one must constantly chant and meditate upon the countless divine names of Bhagavān. Each of His names reveals one of His infinite auspicious qualities. Through remembrance of these divine qualities, devotion gradually becomes purified of worldly motives, and the heart begins to desire nothing other than service to Him.

Therefore, Periyāḻvār urges these *Aiśvaryārthis* to chant the thousands upon thousands of names of Bhagavān. Such remembrance cultivates a divine disposition and frees the mind from attachment to temporary pleasures. By constantly glorifying the Lord, the devotee learns to seek the supreme fruit of *bhagavad-kainkarya* rather than the fleeting rewards of this world.

Thus the Āḻvār proclaims: “Let us worship that Supreme Lord, the destroyer of evil forces and the protector of all beings. Let us sing His countless names and offer auspicious benedictions for His protection.”
