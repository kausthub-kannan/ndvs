## english_scripts

ēḍu nilattil iḍuvadan munnam vandu
eṅgaḻ kuzām pugundu
kūḍu manam uḍaiyīrgaḻ varambozi
vandollai kkūḍuminō
nāḍu nagaramum nangaṟiya
namō nārāyaṇāyavenṟu

vandu pallāṇḍu kūṟuminē

## meaning

O seekers of this sacred path, come without delay. Join those who live solely for the Lord and seek none but Him. Let worldly attachments fall away and let the longing for liberation awaken. Come and be among those who sing together with surrendered hearts, declaring that they seek nothing for themselves but only service to the Lord. Let this sacred message spread everywhere as we proclaim, “May You live long for countless years!”

## purport

Periyāḻvār now turns his attention to a specific group known as *Kaivalyārthis*, those who are absorbed in the contemplation of their own self and seek only *ātmānubhava*. Their aspiration is not Bhagavān Himself, but the experience of the individual self. Such seekers attain a state known as *Kaivalya*, an intermediate condition that is distinct from both worldly existence and the supreme liberation of *Śrī Vaikuṇṭha*. In *Kaivalya*, the *jīvātmā* experiences its own nature and remains free from the sufferings of material existence. Yet it does not attain the unsurpassed bliss of serving Bhagavān, nor does it experience the fullness of divine communion found in *mokṣa*. In many ways, the Ācāryas explain that attaining *Kaivalya* can become an even greater obstacle to ultimate liberation than remaining within *prakṛti*, for the soul becomes satisfied with its own limited experience and no longer aspires for Bhagavān.

Recognizing this subtle deficiency, Periyāḻvār compassionately calls out to such souls: “Come and join us! Abandon this lesser aspiration of merely experiencing the self. Realize that your true nature is not independent self-enjoyment, but eternal service to Bhagavān. Come, sing His divine names along with us and attain the highest goal!” The Āḻvār further emphasizes that service to Bhagavān is not something imposed upon the *jīvātmā*. Rather, it is the very nature of the soul itself. Service to Him is what brings true fulfilment and joy, for Bhagavān is an infinite ocean of love, compassion, and auspicious qualities. To serve Him is not a burden but the natural expression of the *jīvātmā's* true identity.

The Āḻvār acknowledges that the body is impermanent and that worldly desires are the root of suffering. However, merely renouncing material pursuits is not sufficient for liberation. One must also understand one's true identity. The *jīvātmā* is by nature the *śeṣa* of Bhagavān, existing solely for His pleasure and service. Without realizing this fundamental truth, even the attainment of *ātmānubhava* remains incomplete and falls short of the *jīvātmā's* ultimate destiny. The *jīvātmā* is *asvatantra*, while Bhagavān alone is *sarva-svatantra*. Dependence is the intrinsic nature of the jīvātmā, whereas Bhagavān remains completely independent and self-sufficient. Even the experience of *ātmānubhava* is possible only because of His presence within the soul as the *Antaryāmin*. Therefore, the Āḻvār calls out to these seekers and urges them to realize that even the bliss derived from self-experience is but a faint spark of the infinite bliss that arises from the experience of Bhagavān Himself.

Therefore, Periyāḻvār directs these *Kaivalyārthis* towards the path of true realization. He urges them to meditate upon the sacred *Aṣṭākṣara Mahāmantra*, adorned with the divine *Praṇava*. He invites them to contemplate the mantra:

Through the word *namaḥ*, the *jīvātmā* realizes, “I do not belong to myself.” It recognizes that its existence is wholly dependent upon and dedicated to Bhagavān. Through the name *Nārāyaṇa*, it understands Him to be the primordial Supreme Being, the indwelling Lord of all beings, and the cause of all causes. By meditating upon this divine mantra, great yogins transcend limited self-experience and attain the supreme abode of *Śrī Vaikuṇṭha*, from which there is no return.

Thus, Periyāḻvār lovingly calls out to the *Kaivalyārthis*: “Come! Chant ‘Namo Nārāyaṇāya’ with us. Abandon lesser attainments and join the assembly of devotees. Let us together sing auspicious benedictions and seek the protection of Bhagavān through our loving praises.”
