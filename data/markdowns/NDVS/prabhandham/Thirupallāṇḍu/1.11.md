## english_scripts

alvazakkonṟum illā
aṇi kōṭṭiyar kōn
abimāna tuṅgan śelvanaippōla
tirumālē ! nānum unakku ppazavaḍiyēn
nalvagaiyāl namō nārāyaṇāvenṟu
nāmam pala paravi
palvagaiyālum pavittiranē !
unnai ppallāṇḍu kūṟuvanē

## meaning

O Tirumāl, Consort of Śrī Lakṣmī, I am now and forever Your devoted servant, just as the noble Selva Nampi was. Pure and free from wickedness, he was renowned for his devotion to You and Your devotees. I too am Your ancient and eternal servant. Let me sing Pallāṇḍu in Your praise and proclaim with all my heart, “Namaḥ Nārāyaṇāya.”

## purport

Finally, the transformed *Aiśvaryārthis* join Periyāḻvār in singing auspicious benedictions for the Lord. They now proclaim that they have become devotees like Selva Nambi. Even among those devoted to Bhagavān, there remains the subtle danger of pride. A devotee may think, “Through my actions, devotion, and service, I have attained Bhagavān or become qualified for liberation.” Such pride, though seemingly spiritual, still obscures the true nature of the *jīvātmā*. The true *prapannas*, however, recognize that everything is attained solely through the grace of Bhagavān. They declare, “It is only by the mercy of Bhagavān that I, a servant of His servants, have been able to attain Him and become eligible for the supreme abode of *Paramapada*.” Selva Nambi was one such devotee who was completely free from even this subtle form of pride.

Selva Nambi, who belonged to the sacred town of Tirukkoṣṭiyūr, a place that shone like a precious jewel among holy kṣetras, was a great *Bhāgavata*. A Pāṇḍya king, troubled by doubts concerning Vedānta and desirous of knowing who the Supreme Brahman truly is, sought the assistance of Selva Nambi. Although Nambi himself knew the answers to the king's questions, he desired that not only the king but the entire royal assembly should benefit from hearing these truths from a great devotee of Bhagavān. Therefore, he advised the king to convene a grand assembly where scholars and devotees could gather and address these profound questions.

It was in this assembly that Periyāḻvār established the supremacy of Nārāyaṇa and expounded the true nature of the *jīvātmā*. Through his words, the court came to understand that Nārāyaṇa alone is the Supreme Brahman and that the *jīvātmā* exists solely for His service. Delighted by Periyāḻvār's wisdom and devotion, the king honored him by seating him upon a royal elephant and leading him in procession. During this glorious occasion, Bhagavān manifested before the Āḻvār, inspiring the outpouring of *Tiruppallāṇḍu*. Thus, Selva Nambi became an instrument through whom this divine event was brought about.

Selva Nambi was renowned for being free from all inauspicious qualities. He was called “Selva,” meaning one who possesses true wealth. Yet his wealth was not material prosperity. His true wealth was *bhagavad-kainkarya*, loving service to Bhagavān. Therefore, the transformed *Aiśvaryārthis* now proclaim, “We too seek that same wealth possessed by Selva Nambi. We no longer desire worldly riches or temporary attainments. Our treasure is service to Bhagavān, and we have come here solely to serve Him.”

They further declare: “We chant the Lord's names in countless ways and glorify Him through innumerable expressions of devotion. We are the servants of Tirumāl. We are the servants of the Divine Couple together, Śrīman Nārāyaṇa united eternally with Śrī Mahālakṣmī. We remain forever under Their service and protection. O Lord, we are Yours alone. We desire to serve You through every possible form of *kainkarya*. Therefore, we sing auspicious benedictions for Your protection. May You live long forever. May Your glory flourish through endless ages and eons!”
