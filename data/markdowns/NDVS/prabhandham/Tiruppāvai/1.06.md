## meaning

The birds have risen from their nests and they make noisesas they poise for flight in search of food. From the temple of Viṣṇu, who is the Lord of their master Garuḍa, emerges the loud booming sound of the white conch summoning the devotees to worship. Haven't you, O girl, heard that sound? You might wonder how we have got up and who has awakened us. The munis and yogis have securely installed in their hearts the Lord who, as child Kṛṣṇa, has sucked poison from the breasts of Pūtanā (and her life with it), and who, crying for a feed, has stretched his leg, and with one kick smashed to smithereens the cart, whose shape was assumed by a demon with intent to kill him. They have their minds occupied with the Lord, the Creator of the universe, who in deep concern for its protection has lain in yogic sleep, stretched on the cool, soft and delicate snake-bed in the ocean of milk. As these munis and yogis slowly awaken themselves, they constantly repeat the name of Hari; and the loud sound caused by this repetition has penetrated into and refreshed our hearts and roused us from sleep. Bestir yourself and join us.

## purport

From this pāsuram to the next nine, Āṇḍāḷ tries to wake up ten gopikās to gather together and perform the nombu. Metaphorically, these ten gopikās indicate the indriyas - five being karmendriya and the next five being jñānendriya. As the pāsurams of Āḷvārs echo about śeṣattvam of the jīvātmā to Nārāyaṇa, i.e., we are all devoted servants of Bhagavān and it is our svarūpam. Āṇḍāḷ wakes up the indriyas so that they can be used to perform service to Bhagavān. Now, she starts by waking up a gopī who is novice to Bhāgavata śāstram and the devotional path. While doing so, the gopikā questions Āṇḍāḷ and the gathered bhāgavatas, "What is the sign that dawn has occurred?" A beautiful and sweet conversation unfolds in this pāsuram.

When Āṇḍāḷ wakes her up, the gopī questions, "How do you know dawn has occurred?" The reason she asks is because the previous night she did not sleep, being immersed in devotion towards Kṛṣṇa. Now she doesn't want to share her experience with other bhāgavatas, and she doesn't want to move out of that experience. Such behavior is novice in nature. Envy should be completely absent in the devotional path. There should be a process of sharing knowledge with each other. She fears she might lose that experience by sharing it. This stage is called childhood spiritual experience. Bhagavān Himself says to Arjuna in Gītā 10.9 that the great prapannas who have surrendered to the lotus feet of the divine couple discuss their experience of devotional service with other bhāgavatas (mac-cittā mad-gata-prāṇā bodhayantaḥ parasparam).

Hence, Āṇḍāḷ tries to educate her to transcend such a novice mindset and enjoy serving Bhagavān with other devotees. She states that birds are chirping and going in search of food. These birds represent the great preceptors who are ready to share knowledge and have assembled near temples. Such ācāryas have vairāgya, bhakti, jñāna, and karma as their wings. They not only possess great knowledge but also perform actions according to that knowledge. Those with just knowledge are like birds with no wings, and those who perform actions without understanding the essence behind them are like birds which are blind. Clinging to such people is of no use. But the ācāryas who have knowledge and perform actions accordingly are like great birds who can help us cross the ocean of saṃsāra if we cling to them.

Now the gopī disagrees, stating that in Gokula (as Āṇḍāḷ perceives she is in Gokula), even birds don't sleep, thinking about Kṛṣṇa, immersed in Kṛṣṇa-prema. Hence, they too have gone in search of food without sleeping, thinking it's dawn. Āṇḍāḷ further justifies that it's time for sunrise by saying that these bhāgavatas have assembled near the temple of the Lord of Garuḍa. The term often used for temple in Tamil is kōyil. Interestingly, 'kō' refers to the King of birds, Garuḍa, and kōyil refers to the residence of the Lord of Garuḍa. Hence, devotees have assembled near the sanctum of Garuḍa to move towards the inner main sanctorum.

She further continues by saying that the conch appears white (as dawn has appeared and there is better lighting). We can hear the loud booming sound of the conch in the temple. The gopī argues, saying that such sounds can be heard every hour; this doesn't indicate dawn. The spiritual essence of calling Nārāyaṇa as the Lord of Garuḍa refers to the fact that He is praised by the Vedas, as Garuḍa represents Veda. Hence, by following the Vedas, we reach Bhagavān. The sound from the conch is praṇava, which at its core essence reminds us we are śeṣa, i.e., servants of the Supreme, Kṛṣṇa.

Āṇḍāḷ calls the gopī piḷḷai, i.e., a novice and ignorant child. She calls upon the gopī to come and enjoy the Bhagavat-kaiṅkaryam, i.e., experience serving the Lord in this group of devotees. That is indeed the right way to show devotion towards the Lord. The gopī questions further, asking who woke up the group which has arrived to wake her up. At this time, Āṇḍāḷ provides a beautiful explanation.

Kṛṣṇa sucked the breasts of the demon Pūtanā who foolishly disguised herself as Yaśodā. Another time, He destroyed the false cart Śakaṭāsura, when Yaśodā laid Him under the cart for protection. These two incidents indicate how a jīvātmā can be liberated from saṃsāra. Pūtanā represents prakṛti with her two breasts representing ego and possessiveness (ahaṅkāra and mamakāra). Bhagavān Himself sucked the breasts and caused her death, i.e., He Himself will remove the essence of ego and possessiveness from the devotees who have surrendered to Him. Śakaṭa represents the body. Unless we are liberated from the body, we cannot completely enjoy Kṛṣṇa. By breaking Śakaṭa using His foot, it indicates that by surrendering to the lotus feet of the Lord, we indeed can be liberated from this body. Āṇḍāḷ says that yogis have assembled in the temple chanting the name of Kṛṣṇa as Hari. The divine name Hari refers to one who destroys or tears down all sins. These chants entered our ears, made us remember Kṛṣṇa and His pastimes, increasing the heat of agony and awakened us. Hence she calls upon the gopi to wake up! The pasuram emphasise on the aspect that once we have Bhagavatha Sambhandam .i.e company with great devotees, our mind is set on Bhagavan and try to protect him (out of love) but until then we are immersed fear to protect ourselves.
