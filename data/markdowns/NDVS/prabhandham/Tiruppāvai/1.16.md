## english_scripts

nāyaganāy ninṟa nandagōbanuḍaiya kōyil kāppānē !
koḍi ttōnṟum tōraṇa vāyil kāppānē !
maṇikkadavam tāḻ tiṟavāy
āyar śiṟumiyarōmukku
aṟai paṟai māyan maṇivaṇṇan nennalē vāy nērndān
tūyōmāy vandōm tuyil eza ppāḍuvān
vāyāl munnam munnam māṭrādē ammā
nī nēya nilai kkadavam nīkkēlōr em pāvāy

## meaning

O Guard protecting the mansion of Nandagōpa, the Commander of all! Let us in. O Keeper of the gate, which shines bright with its wreathed decorative arches and flag aloft! Lift the latch of the doors studded with gems. Lord Kṛṣṇa of sapphire-hue and mysterious power gave us his word yesterday that he would give us the musical instrument called 'pārai.' We, the gopikās, have not come here seeking any other benefit. We have come here with pure minds. We have come to awaken Kṛṣṇa with our song. Good Sirs! Do not say 'nay' to us. We pray that you yourselves will open the doors which, drawn by mutual love, stand close and tight. Open them and let us in.

## purport

Āṇḍāḷ with all awakened gopikās gather in front of the mansion of Nandagōpa where Kṛṣṇa lies sleeping on a couch with Nappinnai (Nīladēvī). She requests the guard of the mansion to open the doors. Now she is requesting the guard and Nandagōpa to open the doors. Āṇḍāḷ specifically calls Nandagōpa as commander of all, including Kṛṣṇa. How so? Isn't Kṛṣṇa Īśvara, which literally translates to the supreme controller? Yes, indeed he is Īśvara, yet he is Bhakta parādhīna. Though he is independent of all, yet he voluntarily decides to serve his devotees. Rāma calls himself as Dāśarathi, similarly Kṛṣṇa calls himself as Nandagōpa Kumāran. Āṇḍāḷ knows He loves it when we call him with devotees' names such as Devakinandana, hence she calls the mansion as belonging to Nandagōpa. In Nityavibhūti Śrī Vaikuṇṭha or līlā vibhūti, he always shows himself to be dependent on devotees. Ananta, Garuḍa, Viśvaksena do not leave him and keep him in their control in the supreme abode. Here, he listens to Prahlāda, Balarāma, Nanda, Yaśodā, Arjuna and all those who have surrendered to him. His love for his devotees has no bounds so much so that he is ready to be a messenger, a charioteer, sevaka, dvārapālaka to his devotees. The guard is the ācārya, kōyil referring to the mantra. Nandagōpa translates to 'the one who enjoys ānanda and protects' i.e., ācārya. They are jñānis who enjoy Nārāyaṇa and protect the mantra. The mantra, which is the residence of Lord, helps us to reach him. Without permit of Nanda, we can't see Kṛṣṇa; without the permit of ācārya we cannot receive the mantra and without which we cannot reach the supreme abode to enjoy Bhagavān.

Āṇḍāḷ requests the guard to open doors of the mansion which are embedded with gems. She calls the guard as kōyil kāppāne i.e., one who protects the residence of temple where Bhagavān resides. Though calling him as guard might sound rude due to the discriminative mindset of ours, in reality devotees love when they are associated with Bhagavān and ācāryas. People call themselves as dāsan of ācāryas and Bhagavān indicating they always love it when they are called as servants of Lord. The gate studded with jewels has made all the onlookers stand there stunned. This is very similar to the Vaikuṇṭha. Rāmānujācārya in Vaikuṇṭha Gadyam states that the supreme place desired by mumukṣus is shining with all types of jewels, calling it as maṇi maṇṭapam. When the liberated jīvas see the door, they get stunned and stuck there, unable to move on. The door represents prakṛti, which attracts us. Beyond the door lies the loved by all, Vāsudeva. By his wish alone the door opens, thereby unstunning the liberated ones to immerse them again in much greater ānanda i.e., himself. Similarly here, the dark cloud, emerald and sapphire-hued Kṛṣṇa rests beyond the doors; to see him we need to go beyond the three guṇas. That mansion has a red flag waving indicating indeed this is the place of Kṛṣṇa. The flag represents Praṇava. Kṛṣṇa is called as Karuṇā sāgara hence he is the ocean. The Praṇava mantra acts as the pointer to this ocean or lake. That flag has Garuḍa on it who represents the Vedas, the Vedas which lead us to Bhagavān.

The pāsuram stands as a beautiful conversation between the gopikās and gate keeper. The gate keeper is on duty to protect Kṛṣṇa at all cost. As said, devotees know that he is Īśvara yet they always have vātsalya bhāva towards Kṛṣṇa which makes them always keep an eye on him and protect him at all cost, even if it costs their lives. The guard is not ready to let them in as he fears they could be people with not the right mindset. Āṇḍāḷ urges saying that who can threaten the lord of Ananta? How can there be fear there? Guard argues that asuras keep threatening him. Pūtanā, Śakaṭāsura, Ṛṣabhāsura and so many have tried to kill him, hence he can't let them in so easily. Gopikās argue saying they are women, how can they do that, for which the keeper protests saying wasn't Tāṭakā, Pūtanā demons too? Now Āṇḍāḷ states that they have come here with pure mind to serve him alone. Kṛṣṇa himself had called them and promised to give the parai instrument. The guard laughs asking isn't it contradicting to say you came here to serve him alone but you ask for the instrument too? Gopikās correct it by saying they have asked for the instrument to sing the glories of Kṛṣṇa. The guard is not ready to listen; he states that by doing so you can awaken him. They say in chorus, they will just sing and watch him from the doorsteps, so please open the door. The guard knows they are indeed here to serve but yet wants to listen to how they glorify Kṛṣṇa. Now the gopikās, tired, plea to the keeper, "If you do not help us who can help us? You are the protector, the keeper of the treasure like Kṛṣṇa. You have to listen to our plea and pain. Good Sir, please open the door!" The words of Āṇḍāḷ clearly show the guard is none other than ācārya. He is the one who mediates and protects the jīvātmās who have surrendered to him. On behalf of us, he requests Lord to accept us and grants us liberation.
