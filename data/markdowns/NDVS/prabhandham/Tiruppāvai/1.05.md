## english_scripts

māyanai mannu vaḍamadurai maindanai
tūya perunīr yamunai ttuṟaivanai
āyar kulattinil tōnṟum aṇi viḻakkai
tāyai kkuḍal viḻakkam śeyda tāmōdaranai
tūyōmāy vandu nām tūmalar tūvi ttozudu
vāyināl pāḍi manattināl śindikka
pōya pizaiyum pugudaruvān ninṟanavum
tīyinil tūśāgum śeppēlōr em pāvāy

## meaning

Bhagavān Śrī Kṛṣṇa's miraculous deeds evoke reverent wonder everywhere. He is the outstanding Leader of Northern Mathurā, which is ever closely associated with godliness. The ferry of the pure and deep waters of the Yamunā, being His favorite haunt, has become His distinguishing mark of identification. He is the auspicious radiating light of the gopa family; and eager to bring effulgence to Yaśodā's womb, He has allowed Himself to be bound by her and thus has become Dāmodara. Let us purify ourselves as we approach Him; let us worship Him with fresh flowers; let us join our hands in reverence; let us sing His praises; and let us, with all our hearts, meditate on Him. Should we do so, all our accumulated sins of the past and the entire pile of sins which is to accrue to us in the future will be burnt to ashes like cotton in fire. Let us therefore join in glorifying His name.

## purport

Āṇḍāḷ now starts beautifully describing the divine līlās and attributes of the beloved Lord. She calls Him as the one who has the power of māyā and hence, using it, was born in Mathurā as its Lord. Kṛṣṇa, who appears as a cowherd of Gokula, is indeed the Supreme, unimaginable Brahman. The Taittirīya describes Him as unimaginable by the mind, and the mouth cannot sing all His glories! He is beyond the capacity to be perceived. Even though He is inaccessible, He is very accessible to His devotees. To Devakī and Vasudeva, He was born as Kṛṣṇa. Unlike us who are born out of karma and vāsanās, Bhagavān incarnates with His own will and through His yogamāyā He takes incarnation. Kṛṣṇa Himself states this to Arjuna in Bhagavad Gītā 4.6 as "ajo 'pi sannavyayātmā bhūtānām īśvaro 'pi san | prakṛtiṁ svām adhiṣṭhāya sambhavāmyātma-māyayā" - i.e., though He is imperishable, unborn, He appears through His divine power of yogamāyā. When He appeared to Devakī and Vasudeva, Śrīmad Bhāgavatam 10.3.9 glorifies His divine form as: "tam adbhutaṁ bālakam ambujekṣaṇaṁ catur-bhujaṁ śaṅkha-gadādy-udāyudham śrīvatsa-lakṣmaṁ gala-śobhi-kaustubhaṁ pītāmbaraṁ sāndra-payoda-saubhagam" - i.e., He was born with divine attributes of the Supreme: disc, conch, mace, Śrīvatsa mark and the auspicious jewel Kaustubha. This speaks about how accessible He is to His dear devotees, which is termed as saulabhyam.

Āṇḍāḷ calls Him as "vaḍamadurai maindanai" - i.e., the Lord of the Northern Mathurā (specifically calls northern as the famous city called Madurai in Tamil Nadu is the Tamil term for the Sanskrit word Mathurā). This divine city always has association with Bhagavān. The appearance of Vāmana or the rule of Śatrughna with the divine worship of Lord Varāha is always associated with Mathurā. She calls Kṛṣṇa as the Lord of Mathurā due to His courageous activities which left everyone stunned and mesmerized. She continues to sing Kṛṣṇa's glory, calling Him "tūya perunīr yamunai ttuṟaivanai". Kṛṣṇa spent most of His childhood at the banks of Yamunā. The river Yamunā is glorified to be equivalent to the river Virajā, and Mathurā being the goal to attain like Vaikuṇṭha. The river always had association with Kṛṣṇa. He used to play with gopīs in the river, perform līlās too. When Vasudeva carried Kṛṣṇa, Yamunā reduced the level of water so that Bhagavān could reach without any obstacles. Similarly, if we provide a seat to Kṛṣṇa in our hearts, we too can cross the ocean called saṃsāra.

Next, she describes the Dāmodara līlā in the same pāsuram. She describes the one who is called as māyā, the Īśvara, the ruler of universe, comes down as son of Yaśodā. He who liberates jīvātmā from knots of saṃsāra is tied by Yaśodā as Dāmodara. This shows the saulabhyam guṇa of Bhagavān that to His devotees, He Himself will serve them and listen to them. By remembering Śrīman Nārāyaṇa as Dāmodara, one is liberated from all sins. Gopīs move forward remembering Kṛṣṇa, that there is no ultimate motive other than attaining Kṛṣṇa and serving Him. By doing so, they are sure Kṛṣṇa would be unable to bear their strain and at least then He would fly to reach them, thereby burning their sins. The purpose to meditate upon Kṛṣṇa is not liberation, which is secondary, but to attain Him and serve Him alone. As one meditates upon Kṛṣṇa, involuntarily the sañcita karma of that jīvātmā burns down like cotton on fire. Hence, Āṇḍāḷ calls upon gopīs to assemble and shed their sins to start this vrata by remembering Kṛṣṇa.
