## english_scripts

aṅgaṇ mā ñālattaraśar
abimāna paṅgamāy vandu nin paḻḻikkaṭṭiṟ kīzē
śaṅgam iruppār pōl vandu talai ppeydōm
kiṅgiṇi vāy cceyda tāmarai ppū ppōlē
śeṅgaṇ śiṟu cciṟidē emmēl viziyāvō
tiṅgaḻum ādittiyanum ezundāṟ pōl
aṅgaṇ iraṇḍum koṇḍeṅgaḻ mēl nōkkudiyēl
eṅgaḻ mēl śābam izindēlōr em pāvāy

## meaning

Just as mighty kings, once rulers of vast and resplendent realms, abandon their pride—believing no force could surpass their own—and humbly gather before the throne of a conquering emperor, so too have we assembled in great numbers beneath your divine seat of glory, our ego utterly broken, our arrogance cast aside. We come not as subjects, but as surrendered souls, yearning for your mercy.

O Lord, gently part your eyes—those lotus-like eyes, half-open like the tender lips of a blossoming bell, blushed with the warmth of your compassion. Let them fall upon us with kindness. If those divine eyes, shining like the sun and the moon rising together in the firmament, were to glance our way, even the heaviest burdens of karma, once fastened to our fate like an unshakable curse, would dissolve and disappear—unable to withstand the brilliance of your gaze.

## purport

Just as kings who once ruled vast and splendid realms abandon their pride and kneel before the triumphant emperor, so too do we, stripped of ego and humbled, gather beneath your divine seat. Though rulers of their lands, those kings forget that the Supreme alone is the true possessor, while they are but dependent souls feigning dominion. So also are we—individual selves mistakenly believing in our own independence—now returning to your feet, realizing we are yours alone, like queens surrendering before the one true King.

From Brahma to the lowliest ant, all beings suffer from the same delusion: that what they see, feel, or command belongs to them. Yet their positions—earned by karma and gifted by grace—are but roles in a play designed by the Lord. The more one clings to ego, the deeper the sorrow becomes. Only when this conceit collapses can the soul draw near to the Supreme. Even Brahma, blessed by divine births, attains liberation only after long penance and divine mercy. But the humble, with no possessions to surrender, reach God with ease, for they have always believed all belongs to Him.

The gopikas mirror these humbled kings. They leave behind the pride of femininity and gather not before a throne, but beneath Krishna’s couch—seeking not restoration but refuge. They fear returning to worldly roles, lest others misread their silence as independence. Instead, they choose to remain together at His feet, proclaiming their belonging by their closeness. Like Bharata, who faints at being called a king, they refuse titles and roles that separate them from Him. Togetherness beneath the Lord’s gaze is their only desire.

They wonder how, after countless births and estrangement, they now find themselves near Him. Like the Emperor’s son raised by hunters who discovers his true heritage, they awaken to their natural kinship with Krishna. Karma had hidden it; now grace reveals it. Their long journey back to Him—slow, subtle, and blessed—unfolds like a miracle. Like Hanuman, a monkey entrusted with divine service, they marvel at the union of the lowly with the Lord.

Now, exhausted by separation, they beg for a tender, gradual glance. They compare His half-open eyes to the shy bloom of a lotus or the gentle lips of a bell, asking for His mercy to arrive like gentle rain after a drought. Sudden grace may overwhelm their fragile hearts—let it come slowly, nourishing them like starved souls fed with care. For in those eyes—half-open with mercy, glowing red with love—they find the healing of all sorrow, the end of all curses, and the dawn of bliss in union with the Supreme.
