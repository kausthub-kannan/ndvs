## meaning

Hear, O Lord, the true purpose behind our arriving before dawn, our singing your praises, and our devoted service at your radiant, golden lotus-like feet. Born into the clan of cowherds, who attend to their cattle before their own hunger, it is your dharma to honour such sincere devotion. Reject not our heartfelt offerings of love and service. We have not come merely to receive the parai and leave — our longing is deeper. What we seek is an unbroken bond with you, a kinship that transcends this life and continues through all births to come. Let us serve you alone, forever, as your consecrated servants, with no desire other than to be yours. And we pray — may no other longing ever find place in our hearts.

## purport

The gopikās arrive at Kṛṣṇa’s abode before dawn, unmindful of the darkness or the chill. This early hour, sittiram siṟukālē, symbolizes the spiritual twilight — the moment when the darkness of ignorance begins to yield to the light of God-realization. Their intense longing for union with Kṛṣṇa reflects the urgency and anguish of a true aspirant seeking God, not for material benefits but for His presence alone. The very act of coming to Him is seen as a breach of their own śaraṇāgati (complete surrender), as the ideal is to await His coming — yet their yearning overpowers protocol. They approach not as mere seekers of the paṟai (symbolic gift) but as eternal servants desiring ananya-kainkarya — exclusive, unbroken service unto Him.

They acknowledge Kṛṣṇa’s human birth in the humble community of gopas, who prioritize serving cattle before self-care, and request He not reject their heartfelt service. They highlight that their singing, serving, and longing are not to gain rewards but are acts of pure devotion, born out of intimate love. They urge Him to listen, not for their sake, but to uphold His own dharma — for He was born to receive such love and respond to such surrender. Their plea is not for gifts, nor for any heavenly boons, but to be permanently accepted as His personal attendants — to carry His flute, press His feet, hold His garments, and perform all acts of intimate service.

The gopikās declare their wish to be inseparably united with Him through all births, whether He is in His supreme Vaikuṇṭha form or among them as a cowherd. They seek every possible relationship with Him — as friend, mother, consort, or servant — fearing that limiting themselves to one form of kinship might deprive them of others. This total offering of self is akin to that of Lakṣmaṇa with Rāma, or Yaśodā’s care for her mischievous child. Even though they admit their faults in approaching and serving Him, they plead that such actions are expressions of the pain of separation He Himself has caused.

Importantly, they reject all other desires — even the desire for spiritual enjoyment — praying for pure, selfless service (śuddha-kainkarya), untainted by ego or the pursuit of pleasure. They caution against possessiveness (mamatā) and the idea of enjoying the Lord’s service for one’s own delight (bhoktṛtva-buddhi), declaring that bliss lies solely in pleasing Him. This total surrender encapsulates the essence of the Aṣṭākṣarī mantra, as the gopikās express the meanings of the syllables by affirming their identity as His alone, bound in an eternal relationship, with no other refuge or aim.

Through this pāsuram, they convey the culmination of their nōṟrāda-pōdhu (vow)— not as a ritualistic fulfillment, but as a living declaration of their soul’s purpose: to be in God’s presence, to serve Him endlessly, and to want nothing but Him, through every birth, for all time.
