## english_scripts

vaiyattu vāzvīrgāḻ ! nāmum nam pāvaikku
śeyyum kiriśaigaḻ kēḻīrō
pāṟkaḍaluḻ paiya ttuyinṟa paraman aḍi pāḍi
ney uṇṇōm pāl uṇṇōm nāṭkālē nīr āḍi
maiyiṭṭezudōm malariṭṭu nām muḍiyōm
śeyyādana śeyyōm tīkkuṟaḻai śenṟōdōm
aiyamum piccaiyum āndanaiyum kai kāṭṭi
uyyumāṟeṇṇi ugandēlōr em pāvāy

## meaning

All, who though born in this world of sorrow, yet enjoy bliss as contemporaries of Kṛṣṇa, listen to the manner of our performing this ritual. We shall gently sing benediction to the feet of the Supreme Person, who has been resting quietly on the Kṣīra Sāgara (sea of milk). During the period of this ritual, we shall take our bath before dawn. We shall not partake of milk or ghee. We shall not adorn our eyes with collyrium nor shall we deck our hair with flowers. We shall avoid actions not sanctioned by our elders; nor shall we ever utter anywhere falsehoods or words that might hurt others. We shall honor the wise with liberal gifts of grain and money. We shall bestow charity on the brahmacārīs (celibates) and the sannyāsīs (ascetics). We shall always reflect upon the way of higher life. May you witness this and feel happy!

## purport

After calling all the gopīs to perform the nombu, Āṇḍāḷ speaks to them saying that we are fortunate to live in this divine place, much glorified compared to Śrī Vaikuṇṭha itself! "Vaiyattu vāzvīrgā" here refers to this essence. Why is Āṇḍāḷ saying this? Compared to the Supreme Abode, we can enjoy the infinite divine and auspicious qualities of Bhagavān here, in the Līlā Vibhūti. The Nitya Vibhūti, i.e., Śrī Vaikuṇṭha, is ever-shining as there is no scent of karma or vāsanā there. Though Bhagavān has so many auspicious qualities, they cannot be expressed fully in the Supreme Abode compared to the Līlā Vibhūti, i.e., the universe. The comparison can be given as: the importance of light and its beauty can be experienced to its fullest when it is brought to a place which is dark. Bhagavān, who is called parama jyoti by Nārāyaṇa Sūktam of Taittirīya, His divine qualities can be experienced beautifully and their importance known well here in this Līlā Vibhūti where we are stuck in the cycle of saṃsāra by ignorance, which is darkness personified.

She again refers to "mamum nam pavaiku," which refers to the same essence that we, who have left all desires and only want Kṛṣṇa, have come here to participate in this nombu. Āṇḍāḷ refers to Kṛṣṇa as Kṣīrābdhi-nātha. The reference here is of the Kṣīra Sāgara, which is as large and plentiful as His divine qualities which are supreme and incomparable, hence she calls them param. Also, the sleeping posture of Nārāyaṇa is always seen to be much more beautiful compared to others. Viśvāmitra, seeing Rāma in yoga-nidrā, is so mesmerized and sings to awaken Him. Sītā too is mesmerized by Rāma who is laying on her lap. Āṇḍāḷ, remembering Raṅganātha, who is none other than Kṛṣṇa, is mesmerized. Though He remains in this yogic posture, He is always alert to protect the surrendered ones. Then she, with the participants of the nombu, decides to follow certain disciplined rituals for the nombu.

She states to avoid eating milk and ghee. By remaining immersed in devotion and love towards beloved Kṛṣṇa, she is not ready to partake of food like milk and butter which are considered royal, rich, and 'elixir on earth'. After enjoying and in the process of enjoying the divine elixir called Kṛṣṇa-prema, how can these worldly things sound fitting to the mind? Leaving these, she also calls upon them to take bath early in the morning. Referring to this, the pain of separation always triggers heat. To cool down the body, often devotees take early bath. Bharata, separated from Rāma, used to take morning baths. This is an expression of pain of separation towards Kṛṣṇa by Āṇḍāḷ and all who have joined her group.

Next, she repeats to give up adorning collyrium for her eyes and she states to not wear flowers. In the pain of separation, the wife doesn't desire to decorate herself. All she waits for is her husband to return. Āṇḍāḷ here says the same, that Kṛṣṇa Himself has to provide the collyrium and flowers to them. The inner meaning of what Āṇḍāḷ tries to infer here is: collyrium which decorates the eye and makes it look bright refers to jñāna, and the flowers which are pleasurable to look at, smell, and wear, which shows bhoga, represents bhakti. Āṇḍāḷ repeats the same - she doesn't want to use jñāna yoga and bhakti yoga to attain Bhagavān. He Himself is the means and He Himself will make them attain Him. If He desires to bestow knowledge and devotion to the prapanna, i.e., those who have helplessly and totally surrendered to Him without any expectation, then she is ready to accept it.

Āṇḍāḷ further continues by referring to "śeyyādana śeyyōm" where she tries to emphasize following the regulations laid down by Ācārya Puruṣas. Vedas sometimes provide certain flexibility in ways to lead life, such as kṣatriyas having the eligibility to hunt. Though such flexibility is given, they are said to be optional, and Ācāryas recommend highly to avoid them. In our example, though kings were allowed to hunt, often these ended up in bad outcomes and mostly became the causes of death in the long term. Daśaratha, Pāṇḍu, Parīkṣit are some examples of this reasoning. Hence, Āṇḍāḷ states even if Vedas provide that optional flexibility, we still follow the Ācāryas and won't perform such activity. This is the same stated by Bhagavān in Gītā 4.34 - "tad viddhi praṇipātena paripraśnena sevayā | upadekṣyanti te jñānaṃ jñāninas tattva-darśinaḥ", i.e., Bhagavān tells Arjuna to gain this knowledge from Ācāryas who have the knowledge about Brahman. The Taittirīya Upaniṣad states the same in Śikṣā Vallī 1.11.4 - "atha yadi te karmavicikitsā vā vṛttavicikitsā vā syāt . ye tatra brāhmaṇāḥ saṃmarśinaḥ . yuktā āyuktāḥ . alūkṣā dharmakāmāḥ syuḥ . yathā te tatra varteran tathā tatra vartethāḥ", i.e., it states "If there should arise any doubt regarding your acts, or any uncertainty in respect of your conduct in life, you should act in those matters exactly as those Brāhmaṇas who are present there, who are thoughtful, religious (experienced), not set on by others, not cruel (i.e., gentle) and are devoted to Dharma." Hence following the directions of Ācārya, she is performing the nombu. She says she will donate to the needy and the Brāhmaṇas until they are satisfied (and feel sorry to give no more). It also infers that she is donating the knowledge about the kalyāṇa guṇas of Bhagavān to the people who are ready to ask. Āṇḍāḻ expressed her pain of sepearation and intends to perform nombu for the quickness to attain.
