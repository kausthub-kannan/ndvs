## english_scripts

nōṭru ccuvarkkam puguginṟa ammanāy
māṭramum tārārō vāśal tiṟavādār
nāṭra ttuzāy muḍi nārāyaṇan
nammāl pōṭra ppaṟai tarum puṇṇiyanāl
paṇḍoru nāḻ kūṭrattin vāy vīznda kumbagaraṇanum
tōṭrum unakkē perun tuyil tān tandānō
āṭra anandal uḍaiyāy ! aruṅgalamē
tēṭramāy vandu tiṟavēlōr em pāvāy

## meaning

O fortunate girl, you who have completed the rite before our arrival and experienced happiness! Won't you speak to us, even if you do not open the door? Nārāyaṇa, whose crown is adorned with garlands of fragrant tulasī, is so generous a benefactor that He bestows upon us, who have nothing of our own, the desired objective of 'paṟai,' merely for singing His benediction. He once cast Kumbhakarṇa into death's jaws. Could it be that demon has been defeated by you in a contest of sleep and has surrendered his rich possession of sound sleep to you? O dear one, whom dense drowsiness never seems to leave, you, who are the śirobhūṣaṇa (crown-jewel) of us all, arise from sleep, shake off your drowsiness and come to open the door. Open your mouth and speak. Remove the veil and reveal yourself to us.

## purport

Waking up the Gopikā, Āṇḍāl asks her, "Have you already performed the Nombu? Is that why you have already attained Kṛṣṇa? Is it right for you to remain in your nature and not allow us to attain the same state? Hence, please wake up!" The Gopikās address the sleeping lady, who appears to have attained the state of one who has completed all spiritual rites (kṛta-kṛtya). They observe that while they intended to perform rites to attain Kṛṣṇa, she has achieved the fruit of these rites through her profound realization that Kṛṣṇa Himself is both the means (upāya) and the end (upeya).  This profound understanding aligns with the declarations of Vedic scholars and spiritual masters, who proclaim Kṛṣṇa as the eternal dharma. While karma (works), jñāna (knowledge), and bhakti (devotion) are means to attain God, they are not always accessible. However, God Himself remains ever available as the ultimate means. The Gopikā, understanding this tattva (truth), has surrendered completely, making no independent effort, similar to what is described in the Bhagavad-gītā's Puruṣottama-prāpti Yoga. Her state reflects one who has truly understood the nature of Prakṛti (material nature), Jīva (individual self), and Īśvara (Supreme Lord). 

The Gopikās address her as "Ammā" (Mother) and as one who has reached Svarga (heaven). Here, Svarga represents the eternal bliss (ānanda) of being with Kṛṣṇa, as exemplified in Sītā’s words to Śrī Rāma: that being with Him is heaven, and separation is hell. They recognize her as their potential savior, capable of securing divine union for the entire group, as she perpetually dwells in God-consciousness without intermission. The Gopikās ask her, "If you are not waking up, won’t you at least speak to us? Reply to us!" This shows how desperate they are to hear from her. The Gopikā, as we know, represents the Āchārya. Āchāryas have experienced Bhagavān and are therefore capable of sharing their experiences with us. They do so for those disciples who have surrendered to them by narrating their divine experiences. Here too, the Gopikās eagerly wait for the narration of the Gopikā, who is immersed in Kṛṣṇa-bhāva. She is called upon to open the door—to break the doors of ignorance standing between them and Bhagavān. The Āchārya is the one who destroys our ego and imparts knowledge to help us attain Bhagavān.

Seeing the Gopikā not waking up, the gathered Gopikās ask her, "Has that Kumbhakarṇa, defeated by Rāvaṇa, left his sleepiness to you? Why aren’t you waking up? We are waiting for you!" This statement seems out of the ordinary and may appear as a strange comparison. The Gopikā, who is said to be immersed in Kṛṣṇa-prema, is being compared to a Rākṣasa like Kumbhakarṇa. How is this possible? The comparison is sāttvic. The Gopikās only speak about sleep, not other qualities. Additionally, the term "Kumbhakarṇa" refers to two personalities: Rāvaṇa’s brother and one born from a Kubha (jar), i.e., Agastya. 

Here, the Gopikās question why she is sleeping like Rāvaṇa’s brother. Kumbhakarṇa, due to his tāmasic nature, slept for half the year, only to wake up and eat during the other half. But the Gopikā, on the other hand, does not possess such a tāmasic quality. Her sleep is sāttvic, immersed in Kṛṣṇa-bhakti. Hence, how appropriate is it for her to wake from that slumber to perform service to Kṛṣṇa along with them? How can she deny them such pleasure? Like Kumbhakarṇa stood between Sītā and Rāma, if she does not join them, Kṛṣṇa will not reveal Himself to them. He has promised to grant them Parai (the opportunity to serve). Thus, the context of Kumbhakarṇa must be understood in this Pāsuram. Additionally, she is compared to Agastya. Agastya, the great Ṛṣi belonging to the spectacular seven sages, is known for three important deeds: destroying Vindhya’s ego, drinking the entire ocean, and vanquishing Vātāpi, the cunning demon. Similarly, the Gopikā possesses three important qualities: conquering her ego, gaining spiritual knowledge as vast as the ocean, and maintaining unbroken meditation on Bhagavān.

The sleeping Gopikā is then compared to a crown jewel. This signifies that even in her state of inaction, she is as precious as a jewel. Unlike other objects of decoration, jewels remain intact yet add beauty to those who wear them. Such is the presence of the Gopikā. Her mere presence makes the place beautiful and filled with bliss. How so? Kṛṣṇa has spent the night with her! Āṇḍāl is able to smell the fragrance of Tulasi, which confirms that Kṛṣṇa has indeed visited her! The devotion of the Gopikā is like the fragrance of Tulasi. Even if hidden, the fragrance is perceived by those nearby. Similarly, the divine qualities of the Gopikā can be identified even if she remains asleep. The gathered group, along with Āṇḍāl, urges her to carefully wake up and open the door.
