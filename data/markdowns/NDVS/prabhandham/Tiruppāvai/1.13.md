## meaning

All our companions have reached the place of ritual, singing all the way, the glories of Kṛṣṇa (who has saved himself and us by splitting, in twain, the mouth of Bakāsura who has taken the form of a crane); and the glories of Rāma (who has in sport pinched, as one nips the tender buds, the ten heads of Rāvaṇa and thrown them down into dust). O girl, with eyes resembling lotus-flowers enfolding bees within, and with looks resembling the deer's! Look! Venus is rising; Jupiter is setting; and the birds are twittering. Why do you continue to lie in bed, instead of having a cool refreshing bath which stills the torment of separation from Kṛṣṇa? O girl of tender susceptibility! On this auspicious day, give up your shyness, join us and share your bliss with us.

## purport

While waking up the gopī, Āṇḍāḷ remembers the līlā's of Kṛṣṇa. Glorifying the killing of Bakāsura, Āṇḍāḷ remebers the divine Līlā of Bhagavān. Among may of the līlā's, the destruction of Bakāsura was a intresting one. Kṛṣṇa let himself to get close to the asura and waited for the evil crane to opne it's mouth, just in time to tear it apart. Before this he lets himself to be swalloed by the carne which make it go beserk and possed letting Bhagavān out. Like the crane he indeed would destory the lust and all sins of the surrednered souls. Here the crane repersents prenticous people who act as if they have rencouced everything to attain Bhagavān. The crane stands at the corner of the shore with eye shut partaially similar to yogic meditation. But as soon as it sees the fish, within a second it catches and let it be that day's meal. Similar are the ones who have dhamba. Though they look like yogic like the crane yet at the level of self, they remain pretencious and have not rencouced anything. Such people also act as obstacles to true devotees who want to attain Bhagavān. The true devotees are the Prapannas who are loved by bhagavān. They have rencouced everything, in the sense that they have even left the intent of keeping their efforts to reach Bhagavān. They do not think their devotion will help them to reach him but his compassion will. Their devotion is just to please Him, his happiness is what they desire. All the kaiṅkaryas they perform are for His pleasure alone and not as a means to attain him. He himself are the means for them. Indeed he is everything for them. Such great ones are called as Bhāgavatas or Ekānta Bhaktas as per Pāñcarātra. To protect such devotees from pretencious ones, Bhagavān himself take the responsibilty. Splitting the beak of Bakāsura, he ensures that he always protect the surrendered souls.

Contuing to be immersed in līlā's of Nārāyaṇa, Āṇḍāḷ remebers Rāma avatāra and slaying of Rāvaṇa. She describes him as the courages Lord who pincked the heads of the wicked rākṣasa. If I wish indeed I can do it but I do not." Indeed he is the all poweful Supreme.
