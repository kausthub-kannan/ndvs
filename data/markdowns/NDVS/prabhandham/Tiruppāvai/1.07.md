## meaning

Knowing they must remain separated throughout the day, the bhāradvāja birds gather at dawn from all directions, conferring in groups. Have you not heard their melodious discourse? O maddened one! As the gopikās engage in churning curds, their hair-bands loosen, and their flowing tresses, adorned with flowers, suffuse the air with fragrance. The movement of their hands causes their bangles to tinkle, and this sound, merging with the resonance of their neck ornaments, ascends to the heavens. Have you not heard this surging, rhythmic symphony? O Leader! How can You remain still, even after hearing the voices praising the Lord who, though pervading all creation through divine love, chose to incarnate as Kṛṣṇa specifically for our vision, and vanquished our enemies like Keśi? We behold Your radiance. Open the door and allow us to experience Your splendor without any impediment.

## purport

Āṇḍāḷ tries to wake the gopī again who was immersed in Kṛṣṇa-prema. She speaks about chirping birds, specifically calling them bharadvāja birds who are discoursing with each other before moving apart to gather food. The reference to 'bharadvāja' indicates these birds to be brāhmaṇas. Those who possess the knowledge about Brahman are called brāhmaṇas. These people are well-versed in Vedas and are mumukṣus. These brāhmiṇs, also called śrotriyas, recite Vedic verses in the morning. Having understood the meaning behind the verses, they don't merely recite them but also put them into practice. As seen in the previous pāsuram, birds represent the preceptors, and Āṇḍāḷ continues that analogy here. She emphasizes that both knowledge and its practical application are crucial. These great brāhmaṇas or ṛṣis, when they sleep, immerse themselves in spiritual experience, then wake up to impart knowledge to us. These great devotees engage in all their daily actions as service to Bhagavān, hence Āṇḍāḷ questions the gopī, "Haven't you heard the conversations of these great sages?" But the gopī argues that these birds (sages) have been conversing throughout the night about Kṛṣṇa, hence they continue their discourse even now, not realizing dawn has broken.

Āṇḍāḷ provides another justification for daybreak. She describes married women churning curd, their golden bangles colliding and creating sounds. The fragrance of fresh flowers in their hair spreads everywhere, indicating morning's arrival. These gopikās who churn curd are immersed in remembering Kṛṣṇa. While churning, they chant His name in hymns. When selling dairy products, they don't shout "Butter, Curd, Milk" but joyfully sing "Govinda, Dāmodara, Mādhava." They are in such an exalted state that they hardly comprehend what they're selling. Such women are married in all sense to Kṛṣṇa. The surrender of a jīvātmā to the paramātmā is akin to a divine marriage. Jīvātmās represent femininity, with Bhagavān being the puruṣārtha (ultimate goal). When jīvātmā surrenders to the Lord, it takes three vows, like the three knots of the sacred maṅgala sūtra: ananya śeṣatva, ananya bhogyatva, and ananya śaraṇatva. Ananya means 'exclusive'. These three vows represent: eternally serving the Lord, desiring to be enjoyed by Him alone, and surrendering to Him exclusively.
Āṇḍāḷ addresses the gopī as a leader, encouraging her to guide the assembled devotees. She calls them to join in remembering the divine names Nārāyaṇa and Keśava. She elaborates on Kṛṣṇa's grace and magnificence. He is called Nārāyaṇa as He pervades everything, including the jīva-tattvas. His love is boundless - during pralaya, He absorbs all jīvātmās, providing protection. He safeguards devotees by vanquishing demons and ill-wishers, as evidenced by His destruction of the asura Keśī when gopālas and gopīs feared for Kṛṣṇa's safety. Though unbounded by form, He manifests for His devotees. Though capable of accomplishing everything through mere will, He incarnates and plays among us. In Vaikuṇṭha, He shines like infinite suns, eternally praised by the nitya-sūris, yet desires all jīvātmās trapped in saṃsāra to reach Him, hence engaging in creation. Āṇḍāḷ questions the gopī why she hesitates to join them in chanting such beautiful names of Kṛṣṇa, who loves us more than anyone.
