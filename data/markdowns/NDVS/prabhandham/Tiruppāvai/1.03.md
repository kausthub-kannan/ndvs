## meaning

As we chant the sacred name of the Supreme Person, Trivikrama (who, upon receiving the gift from Emperor Bali, grew in stature up to the sky and measured the three worlds with His feet) and take our bath under the auspices of this ritual, may the world be refreshed with three good showers a month and be free from all physical ills and feel happy! While the paddy plants shoot up to the sky and fish leap about in the waters of the corn-fields, and the charming bees slumber in lotus flowers, may the land smile with rich harvests! May the cattle sheds abound with cows whose udders, on a mere touch of the steady milkman (gopāla), release potfuls of milk! May the whole land be filled with prosperity that never wanes nor comes to an end! May the whole land ever flow with milk and honey!

Andal says we perform this nombu for the happiness of the whole world. She feels for all those who are unabelt o perform the nombu and hence says she perform for the bliss of the word not for herself. The ritual of the gopikās is described as bringing prosperity to the land in multiple ways. She explains how their worship ensures three showers of rain every month, making the fields abundant with tall paddy crops where fish leap about. The imagery extends to bees sleeping in lotus flowers and cows yielding endless pots of milk, symbolizing the material abundance that flows from proper devotion. This prosperity is described as being permanent and extending beyond their immediate region to benefit the entire world, similar to how during Śrī Rāma's reign, all people experienced happiness (hṛṣṭam), contentment (tuṣṭam), and freedom from disease (ārogam) and fear (bhayam).

The deeper spiritual significance of these images is then elaborated. The three showers of rain represent three kinds of spiritual knowledge: ananyaśeṣatvam (belonging solely to the Supreme), ananyaśaraṇatvam (taking refuge in none but the Supreme), and ananyabhogyatvam (finding relish only in being enjoyed by the Lord). The paddy fields filled with water represent consciousness filled with thoughts of God, while the fish jumping in the water symbolize sages reveling in divine consciousness. The lotus flowers represent the hearts of sages where Śrīmannārāyaṇa resides with Lakṣmī.

Andal explains the significance of milk abundance as representing spiritual knowledge flowing from preceptors (Ācāryas) to disciples. The cows represent the Ācāryas who, like the cows nourished by Kṛṣṇa's touch and flute music, are sustained by divine grace and God-consciousness. The four teats of the cows correspond to four reasons why preceptors bestow knowledge: divine ordinance, answering disciples' prayers, compassion for suffering souls, and their natural inability to contain wisdom. This Ācārya sampada (abundance of spiritual teachers) is described as being inexhaustible and constantly available for the benefit of all seekers.
