## meaning

On a bed, distinguished for its comforts of height and width, coolness, whiteness and softness, spread on a cot with ivory leg-supports, and surrounded by clusters of wicks burning bright, lies Nīladēvī with bunches of blooming flowers decorating plaits of her tresses; and close to her sleeps, Kṛṣṇa with his broad bosom pressing her breasts.
O Kṛṣṇa! Won't you open your mouth and speak! O Nīladēvī! how impressive are your large eyes decorated with collyrium! How long will you prevent your lover from rising? Not to endure separation from him, even for a while, is inconsistent with your nature and your characteristic tendency!

## purport

After waking up the gopī, Āṇḍāḷ with all the gopikās try to wake up Kṛṣṇa with his divine consort Nīladēvī. In the supreme abode Vaikuṇṭha, Śrī, the divine consort who is inseparable from Lord expands herself as aṃśas in Bhū and Nīladēvī. The Vedas glorify her as Nīlā in Nīlā Sūktam, a part of Taittirīya Saṃhitā. Āṇḍāḷ prays to Nīladēvī to wake up Kṛṣṇa, who is mesmerized by the beautiful eyes of Nīlā and lays on the bed with his broad chest pressing against her blossoms. It is a common principle to always approach the provider who is of higher status through the referral of a mediator. Similarly, a mumukṣu approaches Nārāyaṇa for liberation and the fulfillment of serving him eternally through Lakṣmī, she being the mediator. The Pāñcarātra Āgamas beautifully glorify this as Lakṣmī being the will of mercy and Nārāyaṇa who acts upon. He is called as the protector because he acts on the will of mercy which is Mahālakṣmī. She indeed is called as Dayā Dēvī. To this Śrītattva, Āṇḍāḷ (who herself is incarnation of Lakṣmī) prays to wake up Kṛṣṇa and fulfill our desire to serve the eternal couple.

On the ivory-legged bed lay the divine couple immersed in love. Behind them there is a lamp burning with five wicks. The lamp represents Nīladēvī. A lamp has the unique nature to shower brightness thereby making itself visible and making the surroundings visible too. Bhagavān is difficult to be perceived by our own efforts. Our Karmas restrict us to see him. Immersed in Ānanda he stays. To us who are suffering in saṃsāra, his beloved showers compassion. Pity on us she develops the will in him to look at us and take us. Just like the lamp she decides to show herself and from her compassion leads us to Nārāyaṇa thereby becoming the mediator. Through providing us knowledge via the 5 lights from 5 wicks which are: Smṛti, Śruti, Purāṇa, Itihāsa and Āgama, she helps us reach him. This lamp can be moved anywhere. Unlike us, who are bonded to this Saṃsāra and hence the constant association with Kṛṣṇa is difficult, Nīlā on the other hand is independent and can always be with him. She doesn't have any restrictions of dawn and night. Through her own will she serves him with love and devotion. This Nīladēvī has mesmerized Kṛṣṇa with her lotus-like eyes which is not letting him be away from her. Āṇḍāḷ prays to provide us too the bliss of the divine couple. Both are immersed in ānanda, due to their very nature, similar to Rāma and Sītā in vanavāsa who enjoyed each other. Vālmīki quotes that Lakṣmaṇa too was in Ānanda, but why was he in bliss? For Lakṣmaṇa, serving the divine couple itself is great bliss and hence the three all together were in ānanda. Āṇḍāḷ prays to offer us too the same opportunity to serve them.

The ivory stand of the bed represents Kṛṣṇa being the great warrior, the one who defeated the elephant of Kaṃsa called Kuvalayāpīḍa by removing its huge tusks. Nīladēvī is the warrior consort of this Kṛṣṇa. The aspect here is similar to Nīladēvī, all the prapannas i.e. the surrendered ones fear nothing. They have completely surrendered to Lord and hence entire responsibility of theirs has been put upon him. Sītā didn't fear to walk in the forests, she had complete faith in Rāma, so too the prapannas. They, the Īśvara tattvas, lay on this bed immersed with love towards each other. When the jīvātma attains liberation, it climbs the couch of Ādiśeṣa to attain the lap of Bhagavān where questioning who it is, it says "Ahaṃ Brahmāsmi" i.e. I have realized the antaryāmī within me who I am subservient to. To attain the bed of Nārāyaṇa i.e. Mokṣa, it is one of the Brahma Vidyās called Paryaṅka Vidyā. He, the Para Vāsudeva, rests upon such a bed with jñāna, bhakti, vairāgya, aiśvarya as the supports, which here are compared to ivory ones. Thereby Āṇḍāḷ requests her wake up Kṛṣṇa. "How is it possible for a mother to rest well when her children are suffering? How are you able to rest with Kṛṣṇa O Divine mother, when we are screaming loud for your help to serve you and him! Please do let us also rest upon the bed where you two lay" Āṇḍāḷ states. The divine bed has five natures; Coolness and warmth according to surroundings, Fragrance, Whiteness, Softness, Spaciousness. These five represent the Arthapañcaka of Śāstras i.e. "The nature of Brahman to be attained, The nature of the individual self that is worthy to attain, The means to attain God, The result to be gained by the attainment of God, The impediments to the attainment of God." Thus who realizes this fivefold knowledge attains Vaikuṇṭha.

Nīladēvī adorns divine flowers which are blessed to touch Kṛṣṇa when he presses his divine chest. The gathered group with Āṇḍāḷ have vowed not to wear flowers until they meet Kṛṣṇa. Āṇḍāḷ asks the divine mother how right is it to wear flowers when your children cannot wear them as the flowers would fade with the heat of separation of these. Hence, she asks her to bestow the same pleasure of association with Kṛṣṇa to them. Now they peek to see that Nīladēvī wakes up to open the door on the other hand Kṛṣṇa pulls her and throws her on bed, letting her rest upon his broad shoulders. Now Āṇḍāḷ calls upon Kṛṣṇa "Kṛṣṇa, you are immersed towards her and her tresses touch you, that touch blossoms the flowers. But can't you spare one word for us? We desire your happiness alone, O Lord please open the door!". The Supreme is avikāra i.e. he doesn't change, neither does he expand or contract yet due to kinship with Śrī he blossoms like a flower. That blossom is his compassion. Āṇḍāḷ doesn't want to separate them which Rāvaṇa did, hence she just wants to hear the words of protection from Kṛṣṇa.

Resting below Lord, Nīladēvī wears the black collyrium. Her eyes have made Bhagavān go insane for her. Like a fish which protects its eggs just with its eyes, so does Bhagavān protect his children. But if his eyes are keeping us alive, it's her eyes which keep him alive. Looking at her encapsulating eyes Kṛṣṇa says "I cannot live without looking at this beauty even for a minute". It looks as if she is not letting him get up or speak. Āṇḍāḷ beautifully quotes "You are not ready to let your love wake up. You have entangled yourself with him, his will being yours and your will being his. You can never be away from him, always resting on his chest. Yet physically you still do not want to remain away from him, hence are you not letting him wake up O mother? Is it fair to your children like us who want him? You have monopolized him, he is yours. Please let us too near him. We are not here to separate you two like Rāvaṇa but here to serve you two as the divine supreme couple!" She further continues saying "This is not of your true nature. You are the divine mother filled with compassion alone. Ready to bestow anything asked for! You are known for your nature of easy accessibility, compassion and love. Please favor us and let us serve you two." Lakṣmī is personification of compassion. She will bestow anything to her children from material wealth to Bhagavān himself. After all he is her treasury! When asked by Hanumān who was ready to crush rākṣasīs of Laṅkā, she bestowed protection on them as they internally had surrendered to her. Even without any explicit call she assured protection. Now Āṇḍāḷ asks to bestow the similar oppurtunity serve Kṛṣṇa to them. She being the Queen and divinity of Lord as stated by Śrī Sūktam, bestows all kind of pleasures to her children. The reference to Āṇḍāḷ asking for opportunity to serve the divine couple to Nīladēvī is beautifully captured by Rāmānujācārya in his Śaraṇāgati Gadyam where he calls her as a reflection of Lord with all attributes same as his, qualities same as his to bestow the opportunity to serve them and their devotees, to bestow the referral for Mokṣa.
