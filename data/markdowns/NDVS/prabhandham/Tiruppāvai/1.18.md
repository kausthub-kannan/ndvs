## english_scripts

undu mada kaḻiṭran ōḍāda tōḻ valiyan
nandagōbālan marumagaḻē ! nappinnāy !
kandam kamazum kuzali ! kaḍai tiṟavāy
vandeṅgum kōzi azaittana kāṇ
mādavi ppandal mēl pal kāl kuyil inaṅgaḻ kūvina kāṇ
pandār virali ! un maittunan pēr pāḍa
śendāmarai kkaiyāl śīrār vaḻai olippa
vandu tiṟavāy magizndēlōr em pāvāy

## meaning

Nanda has the strength of a tusker with dripping fat; he takes delight in fighting the elephants, of which he has a herd; and his might of arms knows no rebuff from the enemy! O you, daughter-in-law of Nandagōpa, arise! Nīladēvī, radiant with tresses wafting fragrance! Open the latch of the doors. The cocks have been crowing everywhere. The kokils, perching in groups on the lattice supported by mādhavī-creeper, have begun singing. Note, it is dawn. You who hold a ball in your hand! We tell you that we have come to glorify the qualities of our brother-in-law. Please wake up and walk to the threshold in joy and open the door, with the beautiful bracelets on your red-lotus hand making a loud jingling sound.

## purport

Kṛṣṇa still asleep, the gopikās realize that they have to approach someone who is inseparable to him, under whose influence he lives, without whom he can't stay away even for a moment. She is Mahālakṣmī called as Śrīdēvī. She manifests herself as Bhūdēvī and Nīladēvī. Here, Nīladēvī incarnates as Nappinnai, the daughter of a Yādava chieftain Kumbha who was a close friend of Nandagōpa. The chieftain is worried by few bulls causing issues to his subjects and speaks about it to Nandagōpa. Kṛṣṇa hearing this, defeats these bulls and immersed by this act, Nappinnai agrees to marry him. It is Nappinnai whom Āṇḍāḷ prays to wake up and thereby waking Kṛṣṇa too. The Pāñcarātra texts from which the Lakṣmī Tantra originates speaks about the divine relationship between the Supreme controller Nārāyaṇa and Śrī, his divine consort. Śrī represents the Kriyā Śakti of Bhagavān. Though she herself and Bhagavān are part and parcel i.e., different but can never be separated, as per the saṅkalpa of Bhagavān, she makes the prakṛti run. If he is sun, she is sun rays; if he is happiness, she is satisfaction. If we all are the jīva tattvas and the prakṛti is acetana tattva, Śrī and Nārāyaṇa who are inseparable together form the Īśvara tattva. She also represents the Dayā śakti or compassion of Bhagavān. She is the one who induces the will to grant liberation in him. Hence, Āṇḍāḷ and gopikās pray to her. The word Śrī represents 6 divine qualities of her. They are: One who solicits support, One whose support is sought, One who listens, One who communicates what one hears, One who helps ripening and the One who causes infliction of pain. It is she who acts as mediator called as Puruṣakāra when we have to be liberated. It is practically impossible to please Him without her support. It is her words which mesmerized Him to accept our surrender. Gopikās pray to her to wake up Kṛṣṇa and grant the opportunity to not just serve Kṛṣṇa but both of them together as a couple.

Nandagōpa here is said to hold tusks of defeated elephants. Though this might sound questionable, it is here Āṇḍāḷ referring to Nandagōpa as ācārya. Ācāryas should have the power to destroy all kinds of obstacles which come between disciples and the supreme such as ignorance, arrogance and others. Ācāryas are powerful like the mahouts who control strong elephants. Purandaradāsa calls Kṛṣṇa as māri aṇṇe i.e., baby elephant. He is powerful like an elephant but for sure yields to the mahout i.e., ācārya. The elephant listens to the small poking spear which is devotion. The elephant bends its leg for the mahout to climb similarly the Supreme does when he has to grant mokṣa to ācāryas. Once permission given by mahout, the elephant grants permission for others to climb upon just like the Supreme does for the disciples. Throughout the past eleven pāsurams, Āṇḍāḷ prays to different gopīs who are compared to ācāryas and calls upon them to shower their divine knowledge and experience. Now the ācārya too doesn't approach Bhagavān directly, he too approaches via Śrī i.e., here Nīladēvī. It is by her mediation we attain the compassion of Kṛṣṇa. In the Praṇava, A-kāra represents Bhagavān and m-kāra represents us. The u-kāra is the mediation which is Śrī who helps us connect to Bhagavān. So gopikās approach the divine mother now calling her daughter-in-law of Nandagōpa.

While Bhagavān is angered by our ill-actions which are against the śāstras and Vedas, he is disappointed we are not approaching him and if we do, we are doing it after eternity. He is unable to bear this but is mellowed down by the divine mother. She touches him and hugs him thereby letting the soft flowers press his chest softening him. Her compassion, beauty, her qualities make Bhagavān go insane for her. He listens to her without a question, and by her referral to forgive us, he burns our sins. Hence gopikās call her to be wearing the flowers. The divine mother holds prakṛti as a ball in one hand of hers and Bhagavān's hand in another. He listens to her words. Sustaining, protecting this universe like a ball for a long time, her hand has turned red like lotus. Like the lotus she has beauty, like its fragrance she has knowledge which she grants to jīvātmās like us. It is by her grace we climb the stairs of bhakti from viveka, vairāgya, bhakti, parā bhakti, parā jñāna and finally a state where we have lost ourselves and only in service to Bhagavān called parama bhakti. Āṇḍāḷ calls upon a creeper on which kokils are singing. Here the creeper is the divine mother and the kokils are the ācāryas and āḷvārs. She supports our devotion and great ācāryas enable it to reach to larger mass. Gopikās want Nīladēvī to come and open the door by waking and walking towards the door. They too want to see her mesmerizing beauty which has mesmerized the Supreme being itself, see her bangles which jingle when trying to open the door. This pāsuram is nectarful and speaks about the essence of dvaya mantra. Rāmānujācārya used to recite Tiruppāvai everyday but used to love this pāsuram and the upcoming 29th one.
