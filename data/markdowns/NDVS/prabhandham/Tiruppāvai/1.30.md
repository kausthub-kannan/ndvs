## english_scripts

vaṅga kkaḍal kaḍainda mādavanai kkēśavanai
tiṅgaḻ tirumugattu ccēyizaiyār śenṟiṟaiñji
aṅga ppaṟai koṇḍavāṭrai
aṇi puduvai ppaiṅgamala ttaṇ teriyal paṭṭar pirān kōdai śonna
śaṅga ttamiz mālai muppadum tappāmē
iṅgippariśuraippār īr iraṇḍu māl varai ttōḻ
śeṅgaṇ tirumugattu ccelva ttirumālāl
eṅgum tiruvaruḻ peṭrinbuṟuvar em pāvāy

## meaning

The gopikās, adorned with otherworldly ornaments, approach Mādhava, the Lord who secured Lakṣmī by churning the ocean of milk. He, the benefactor of Brahmā and Rudra, receives their benediction. In return, they obtain the paṟai—symbol of divine reward—and the eternal privilege of Kainkarya, selfless service to Him. Āṇḍāḷ, daughter of Bhattarpirāṉ of Śrīvilliputtūr, offers this garland of thirty pāsurams, capturing the soul’s journey to the Lord. Those who recite them regularly and with devotion will receive the same blessings as the gopikās—the Lord’s everlasting grace. Śrīmannārāyaṇa, with lotus eyes and four mighty arms, will bless such devotees with joy, both now and forever.

## purport

The gopikās recall the Lord as “Mādhava” and “Keśava”—names that evoke His churning of the ocean of milk, a divine act undertaken ostensibly for the nectar but culminating in the possession of Śrī Lakṣmī. This metaphor is mirrored in the way Kṛṣṇa, under the pretext of community rituals, orchestrates events to unite with the gopikās, fulfilling their longing and His own joy. The Lord's love is such that He once labored for others’ sake—granting the nectar to the devas—but now exerts Himself for those who seek Him alone. The "ships" on the ocean suggest not only His skill in preserving all beings amid turbulence, but also signify the sādhaka’s body sailing through saṁsāra, with the Lord as both the churner and the vessel of deliverance.

The gopikās, whose faces glow like the moon, approach Kṛṣṇa adorned with ornaments bestowed by Him, reflecting their inner joy and sanctification. Unlike the sun-like brilliance of Kṛṣṇa that scorches foes, the gopikās’ moon-like beauty is soothing even to enemies. Their bliss mirrors the serenity found in sages upon seeing the Supreme. This vision highlights a transformation—external beauty now becomes a symbol of internal spiritual radiance, as divine contact purifies and glorifies the devotee. Their ornaments, gifts from Kṛṣṇa, point to the soul’s adornment through grace, and their urge to rush towards Him speaks of uncontainable bhakti.

The gopikās obtain the parai—not merely the ceremonial drum, but the symbolic fruit of divine union and eternal service. In surrender, they make no claims of spiritual merit, but emphasize the Lord’s fullness and their intimate bond with Him. Their path exemplifies śaraṇāgati—complete reliance, transparency in confessing lapses, and trust that the Lord accepts and blesses them as His own. This process, illustrated by Āṇḍāḷ, becomes the blueprint for aspirants: approach God with humility, not through effort or entitlement, but as one whose longing aligns with His grace.

Srivilliputtur, the divine land of Bhattar Pirān (Periyāḻvār), is celebrated as the world’s ornament, sanctified by its link to Āṇḍāḷ. Just as Vṛndāvana is exalted through Nandagopa, Srivilliputtur gains distinction through Āṇḍāḷ’s birth and devotion. Her father, adorned with a lotus-seed rosary, symbolizes constant remembrance of Nārāyaṇa, unlike the perishable tulasi garland. Periyāḻvār brought the Vedic essence to the world, and his daughter offers herself and her composition—the Tiruppāvai—to the Lord. This lineage and locale are not mere background but integral to the sacredness of the hymn and its message.

Āṇḍāḷ, adopting the gopikās' voice, composes a “garland of Tamil verses”—each a distilled gem of Vedantic and devotional truth. Like rain clouds giving life to the parched earth, she renders lofty Upaniṣadic secrets in sweet, simple Tamil, accessible to all. Her identification with the gopikās allows her to experience and express Kṛṣṇa-bhakti in its fullest depth. Tiruppāvai is meant to be sung collectively, evoking the joy of satsangha and divine presence. The truths once buried in Sanskrit Vedas now bloom in poetic Tamil, as Āṇḍāḷ the garland (Koḍai) binds the Lord through her devotional offering.

These thirty pāsurams, perfectly ordered, lay out a complete sādhana for God-realization—from recognizing divine grace to embracing complete surrender. They outline the aspirant’s journey: identifying auspicious moments, valuing others’ prosperity, revering saints, desiring the Lord’s qualities, and surrendering all agency. The devotee who recites with intent (ippadi sureippar) receives not only worldly joy but eternal bliss from Śrīman Nārāyaṇa—the lotus-eyed Lord with four mountain-like arms and a face glowing with love. His gracious glance bestows fulfilment now and forever. Regardless of caste, gender, or stage of life, all who approach Tiruppāvai with devotion will attain divine union and eternal service—just as the gopikās attained Kṛṣṇa and Āṇḍāḷ attained Śrī Raṅganātha.
