## english_scripts

ambaramē taṇṇīrē śōṟē aṟam śeyyum
em perumān nandagōbālā ! ezundirāy
kombanārkkellām kozundē ! kula viḻakkē
em perumāṭṭi yaśōdāy ! aṟivuṟāy
ambaram ūḍaṟuttōṅgi ulagaḻanda
umbar kōmānē ! uṟaṅgādezundirāy
śem poṟ kazal aḍi ccelvā paladēvā !
umbiyum nīyum uṟaṅgēlōr em pāvāy

## meaning

O Nandagōpa! How great is your munificence! You clothe the naked; you quench the thirsty; you feed the hungry—and all this you do with rectitude and without expecting return. O our revered master! Arise! O Yaśodā! You who are like a tender shoot among women with delicate bodies resembling the prabhālī trees! You, the auspicious light of our community! Our dear mistress! Awake! O Trivikrama! You who have grown in stature splitting the mid-sky, and measuring all the worlds with your feet! Wake up! You, Lord of the Nityasūris, the Ever-wise! You should not sleep. Get up. O Balarāma! You who wear an anklet of pure shining gold on your foot! You and your brother, pray, wake up!

## purport

Requesting the guard to open the doors, the guard argues let Kṛṣṇa himself come and open the door. Here Āṇḍāḷ along with all gopikās try to awaken Nandagōpa, Yaśodā, Kṛṣṇa and Balarāma in respective order. Nanda stays awake and alert. He remains always in alert state, trying to protect Kṛṣṇa from all harms. Not just that, he is a man of kindness. He always helps the needy by donating what they seek for; clothing, food, cows, land and so much more. Nandagōpa is none other than the ācārya, or more of communities of all ācāryas. The gopikās request him to wake up and open the door, to serve Kṛṣṇa they are here. Nanda being the ācārya is always prepared to preach the mantra to the disciple letting them reach Bhagavān. Similar to Nandagōpa's character, ācāryas too have several auspicious qualities which the Taittirīya Upaniṣad speaks about in Prathama praśna that righteousness, control of senses, truthfulness, forgiveness, tapas, agnihotra all are characters of an ācārya. An ācārya who has such divine nature is eligible to teach the mantra. Like Rāma was pleased to be with Sītā because his father had approved it, Bhagavān too will be pleased with the jīvātma because of the permit given by ācārya. Hence, Āṇḍāḷ wakes up ācārya first.

Next she wakes up Yaśodā, who represents the Thirumantra. Yaśodā with complete love towards him lays peacefully thinking about her child. It is only after the permission of the mother, we can access Bhagavān. By waking up the parents, she asks for their permission to awaken Kṛṣṇa. Yaśodā represents the divine aṣṭākṣara mantra. In Vaiṣṇava theology there are many bījākṣara mantras among them three are important; ṣaḍakṣarī (Viṣṇu mantra), dvādaśākṣarī (Vāsudeva mantra) and aṣṭākṣarī (Nārāyaṇa mantra). Due to these mantras speaking about the all-pervasiveness of Bhagavān, they stand apart. Among these three, the aṣṭākṣarī is highly glorified by the Taittirīya Upaniṣad and Pāñcarātra Āgamas. It explains the all-pervasiveness of Bhagavān in complete sense along with the svarūpa of jīvātma. Yaśodā represents this mantra. By waking her up and then Kṛṣṇa, it shows the right process of upāsana. First it is to obtain the mantra from ācārya and then to meditate upon it thereby attaining Bhagavān.

Āṇḍāḷ now wakes up Kṛṣṇa, the Lord of Nitya Muktas. As the Viṣṇu Sūktam states ""tadviṣṇōḥ paramam-padagṃ sadā paśyanti sūrayaḥ"" i.e., there in Paramapadham, the Supreme abode, Viṣṇu is being glorified by the Nitya Sūris. The Nityas like Ananta, Garuḍa, Viśvaksena eternally serve Him, never touched by karma and this saṃsāra. When he incarnates, they too incarnate. The Lord of these Nityas is our Kṛṣṇa. Nammāḷvār starts Tiruvāymoḻi with this point of view calling him as the Lord of immortals i.e., the Nityas. That Bhagavān is now fast asleep laying on the bed with his divine consort, hence Āṇḍāḷ asks how right is it for you to sleep being the Lord of these Nitya sūris. Similar to the third pāsuram, here too Āṇḍāḷ describes about Trivikrama avatāra. When Aditi and Indra were in distress, He decided to help Indra and with begging as nimitta kāraṇa, he sprung to a massive form as Trivikrama and measured the whole world. That person who measured the whole world is sleeping now. Hence request the Supreme Lord to wake up and open the doors for them so that they can come and serve him.

Now seeing Kṛṣṇa still laying on the bed, Āṇḍāḷ tries to awaken his elder brother Balarāma. She realizes that only after waking up Balarāma will the mischievous Kṛṣṇa wake up! Balarāma is none other than Lakṣmaṇa. In Dvāpara he was conceived in the womb of Devakī later to be transported to Rohiṇī with the yogamāyā of Bhagavān who is also called as Durgā. In Rāmāyaṇa, fight between Vāli and Sugrīva took place with Rāma's task to slay Vāli. Though Rāma could easily distinguish both, the first try he enacted as if he couldn't. This was because Sugrīva paid respect to Rāma alone and not to Lakṣmaṇa. Only after Lakṣmaṇa put the garland on Sugrīva thereby blessing him that Rāma slayed Vāli. Hence here too Āṇḍāḷ asks Balarāma to wake up and wake up Kṛṣṇa too. Kṛṣṇa always listens to his elder brother. Hence Balarāma is capable of waking up Kṛṣṇa on command. It was Balarāma who consoled the gopikās when they left to Mathurā. Hence here they knew Balarāma will help them for sure. Rāma could live away from Sītā for 10 months in sorrow but for sure he cannot live without Lakṣmaṇa for a minute, that's how attached he was to him. Similarly Kṛṣṇa is closely attached to Balarāma. Balarāma being Ādiśeṣa eternally serves Bhagavān. If Kṛṣṇa sits, he becomes his chair; he stands, becomes umbrella; walks, he becomes pāduka; sleeps, he becomes the couch. Such is the service of Ādiśeṣa. Now laying down in the bliss due to the touch of Kṛṣṇa, Balarāma should wake up and help them listen to them. Isn't Balarāma the ācārya Rāmānuja who helped prapannas to reach Vaikuṇṭha? How so can he now stay asleep? Thereby she requests him to get up
