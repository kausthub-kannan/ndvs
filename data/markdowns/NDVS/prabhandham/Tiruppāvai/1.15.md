## meaning

The Gopikās standing outside called out sweetly to their friend inside, addressing her with the tenderness of a parrot's tone and asking why she was still asleep. From within, the Gopikā responded with mild irritation, calling them perfect gopikās but requesting them not to summon her with their shrill, annoying voices, assuring them she would come presently. The Gopikās outside then teased her, calling her clever and acknowledging they were already familiar with her harsh and adroit way of speaking. The Gopikā inside retorted sarcastically, saying they alone were clever and harsh, questioning their argument before conceding with irony that she alone was harsh. The Gopikās outside then questioned what made her so special and why she insisted on staying alone, urging her to come out quickly. The Gopikā inside inquired if all the gopikās were present, to which they confirmed and invited her to count them herself. She then asked what they wanted her to do, and the Gopikās outside requested her to come out and sing the glory of Kṛṣṇa, specifically praising Him who had killed the mighty elephant Kuvalayāpīḍam, routed the arrogance of enemies, and wielded mysterious power.

## purport

After waking up nine gopīs, Āṇḍāḷ is waking up the last gopī who is partially awake. Āṇḍāḷ calls the gopī a parrot. While everyone sings, she too joins in chorus and melody as they sing together. Her voice is so mellow and soft like the tune of the parrot, and her youth looks like that of the parrot too. Immersed in Kṛṣṇa anubhavam, the face of the gopikā has turned red just like the beak of the parrot. Āṇḍāḷ requests the gopī to wake up and share the experience of Kṛṣṇa she has had. While the gopikā listens to this, she gets annoyed. She was immersed in experiencing Kṛṣṇa, and these voices are not letting her continue, thereby making her irritated. The call 'tender parrot' irritates her. She who has vanquished all ego and mamakāra feels she doesn't deserve such a title. She states, "You are, indeed, the perfect ones. How can I have the fullness which you have? Those only are perfect who, on hearing the voices of devotees, enjoy bliss and bestow praise on them. I am not fortunate to have such opportunity. Do not trouble me further. You are all gathered together; and you are enjoying together one another's experiences; and that makes for fullness. Since you have that ripeness, you could call me 'the tender parrot.' Those who are really suffering from the misery of separation from Kṛṣṇa will have fainted on hearing his name; and felt annoyed with, and not praised, those who uttered it. You should have, like me, forgotten yourselves if you are yearning for him. But you hear my singing of his praises, feel happy and compliment me on my song. This is proof of your fullness. So please, I shall be with you in a moment."

The to and fro conversation which goes between Āṇḍāḷ and the gopikā represents humility. When the gopikā calls the gathered group perfect ones, they argue they are not. They call her harsh for using such great words to them, though they feel incompatible with it. These words could bring mamakāra and ahaṅkāra which would lead them away from Kṛṣṇa. Humility is one of the important qualities of a Vaiṣṇava. With humility, a true Śrīvaiṣṇava sacrifices mamakāra, ahaṅkāra; sorrow and joy look the same to him as Kṛṣṇa states in Bhagavad Gītā 12.13 as "adveṣṭā sarva-bhūtānāṃ maitraḥ karuṇa eva ca, nirmamo nirahaṅkāraḥ sama-duḥkha-sukhaḥ kṣamī." A true devotee looks at everyone as a friend, sacrifices all sense of difference in happiness and sorrow. In such a state, he remains as sthitaprajña and thereby lives in humility. Āṇḍāḷ and the gathered groups of gopikās continue asking her to wake up fast and join them. Spiritual experience should always be shared and enjoyed together. She asks if all have come, Āṇḍāḷ replying yes, all have! If you want to make sure, you can count them too. Calling upon the gopikā, Āṇḍāḷ asks her to join them to sing about Kṛṣṇa who killed Kuvalayāpīḍa, the elephant which represents ahaṅkāra.
