## meaning

O daughter of aunt, who sleeps soundly on the comfortable couch, surrounded by flaming wicks and pervaded with the aroma of scented sticks, in your mansion made of pure gems of all nine varieties! Lift the latch of your gem-studded door. O aunt, at least you may awaken her. Is your daughter mute or deaf or ill? Or are guards preventing her movement? Or is she under a spell of deep slumber? Sing and glorify His names: 'Mahāmāyāvi' (the great mystic), 'Mādhava' (consort of Mahālakṣmī), 'Vaikuṇṭhavāsa' (resident of Vaikuṇṭha), and other such names, and see that she wakes up.

## purport

Waking up the gopī, Āṇḍāḷ describes her sleeping in a room decorated with different types of gems, of nine varieties. Along with gems, the room has several lamps lit everywhere with their aroma spreading around. Āṇḍāḷ calls the gems pure. Bhagavān is pure and stainless. He is beyond the prakṛti guṇas. Unlike us, He creates with His sheer will alone. The Nārāyaṇopaniṣad calls Him as "niṣkalo nirañjano nirvilkalpo nirākhyātaḥ śuddho deva eko nārāyaṇaḥ" - that is, He is pure, stainless, not subject to change; such characteristics are His alone and second to none. The gopikā who is immersed in Bhagavad-dhyānam also attains a similar character. The mukta, when attaining liberation, obtains similar characteristics as that of Bhagavān. Here too, the mumukṣu who is the gopī is immersed in Kṛṣṇa-prema and hence remains pure like gems. The nine gems decorating the mansion represent nine types of relationship (navavidha-bhāva) with Bhagavān. They are: Pitṛputra Bhāva (Father-son relationship), Rakṣyarakṣaka Bhāva (Protected-protector relationship), Śeṣa-Śeṣī Bhāva (Master-servant relationship), Bhartṛbhāryā bhāva (Husband-wife relationship), Jñātṛ-Jñeya Bhāva (Knower-known relationship), Sva-svāmī Bhāva (Owner-object owned relationship), Ādhāra-ādheya Bhāva (Sustainer-sustained relationship), Śarīrātma Bhāva (Body-soul relationship), and finally Bhoktṛ-Bhogya bhāva (Enjoyer-object enjoyed relationship). Bhagavān is the father, husband, savior, protector, master, owner, the ātmā, object to be known, the enjoyer, and sustainer. The gopī immersed in this navavidha bhāva is not waking up.

The mansion is lit with several lamps. The light emitting from the lamp represents knowledge. Like how light dispels darkness, so does knowledge dispel ignorance. The gopikā who is immersed in Kṛṣṇa-bhakti has such immense knowledge, and her ignorance is destroyed. Āṇḍāḷ also mentions that the wicks emit scented aroma. If light represents knowledge, then the scented aroma represents practice or anuṣṭhānam. A true devotee will be like the lamp with light and aroma - knowledge and anuṣṭhānam.

Āṇḍāḷ requests the gopikā to open the door studded with gems. This gem-studded door represents ignorance. The gopikā who plays the role of the ācārya needs to wake up and help the gathered group by removing ignorance, thereby bringing them closer to Kṛṣṇa. Irritated by the gopikā not waking up, Āṇḍāḷ asks her mother who is seated nearby to wake her up. She questions, "Is your daughter deaf? Dumb? Tired and in fatigue lying on the bed?" The gopikā, well knowing Kṛṣṇa alone is the means to attain Him, is immersed in love towards Him. Hence Āṇḍāḷ asks her, "Are you dumb? Have you stopped speaking because you are in a state immersed in Kṛṣṇa-anubhava? But this doesn't suit your character! Though you have sacrificed all means to attain Him but accept He alone is the means to attain Him, you need to engage yourself in service to Him along with us. Hence respond to our calls and wake up!" She calls her dumb, stating, "Are you dumb because you are listening only to the music of Kṛṣṇa's flute? Have you isolated yourself only to listen to Kṛṣṇa's words? Won't you wake up and grant us the knowledge of spiritual experience?" She further continues, "Spending time with Kṛṣṇa all night, are you in fatigue and unable to get up? O mother of hers, please wake her up. Let her at least respond to us!"

The gathered group of gopikās question the gopikā inside the room, asking her, "Are you in deep sleep because of Kṛṣṇa? Has He put any kind of spell on you? Sure He would have! Isn't He māyān, the Lord of Māyā!" Losing the sense of ahaṅkāra and mamakāra, the gopikā is immersed in Kṛṣṇa-anubhava and not ready to wake up. Surely it has to be Kṛṣṇa who is not letting her wake up. Āṇḍāḷ beautifully calls that Kṛṣṇa as māyān, Mādhava, Vaikuṇṭha and puts it in a wonderful order. He is called māyān because He controls māyā. He plays with this creation which is His potency called māyā. He incarnates with His saṅkalpa with His yoga-māyā. He has mesmerized the gopikā indeed with His beautiful form like māyā. Māyā is the wonderful power of Bhagavān as stated by Rāmānujācārya as 'adbhutam'. Then she calls Him as Mādhava, the dear of Mahālakṣmī. Then she calls Him as Vaikuṇṭha. The beauty of this order is, when the jīvātmā loses ignorance and gains the knowledge that it is the servant of Bhagavān, it surrenders. Mahālakṣmī, His divine consort and our divine mother, shows compassion on us, acting as means to attain Him. By her compassion, we attain Him who is the supreme goal (Vaikuṇṭha) and attain paramapadam (Vaikuṇṭha). This starts with the realization of His power, i.e., māyā. We realize He is beyond birth and death. He is the creator, sustainer, and destroyer. He has pervaded everywhere and inside as antaryāmī. But how do we know this controller of māyā is Kṛṣṇa, the Supreme? It is because Śrī has housed herself on His chest forming the mark of Śrīvatsa. The Puruṣa Sūktam glorifies Him as the Supreme Puruṣa and ends by saying He who is the divine consort of Lakṣmī and Bhūdevī, indeed He is the Supreme Puruṣa described who shines like the stars in night. Now that we know we are jīvātmās and there is a Supreme Lord who is the controller of this creation using māyā, and Kṛṣṇa is that Supreme Lord, we follow the śāstras to attain Him, His abode Vaikuṇṭha. Hence finally she calls Kṛṣṇa as Vaikuṇṭha-vāsa where He shines as Para-Vāsudeva served by nitya-muktas. By calling these names, she tries to wake up the gopikā.
