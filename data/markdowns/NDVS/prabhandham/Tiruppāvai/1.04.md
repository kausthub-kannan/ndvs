## english_scripts

āzi mazaikkaṇṇā ! onṟu nī kai karavēl
āzi uḻ pukku mugandu koḍārttēṟi
ūzi mudalvan uruvam pōl mey kaṟuttu
pāziyan tōḻuḍai ppaṟpanāban kaiyil
āzi pōl minni valamburi pōl ninṟadirndu
tāzādē śārṅgam udaitta śaramazai pōl
vāza ulaginil peydiḍāy
nāṅgaḻum mārgazi nīrāḍa magizndēlōr em pāvāy

## meaning

Oh Parjanya, the majestic celestial being presiding over rain! Do not diminish your customary liberality in giving. Plunge deep into the midst of the majestic ocean, drink all its waters to the full, rise with a roar and spread yourself across the sky, with your dark form resembling the divine image of Śrīman Nārāyaṇa, who is the Cause of the whole universe. Flash forth in lightning, like the Sudarśana-cakra held in the handsome right hand of the lotus-naveled (Padmanābha), broad-shouldered Lord; startle the world with thunder, resembling that which emanates from the Pāñcajanya śaṅkha in His left hand; shoot forth showers, like the arrows that rain from His bow (Śārṅga), in such abundance and continuity that the whole world feels refreshed; and that we feel happy to have our bath in Mārgaśīrṣa.

## purport

Āṇḍāḷ gives the comparison of Varuṇa, who bestows rain, to the Ācāryas who bestow knowledge of the Supreme like the rain. By the blessing of Kṛṣṇa (referred here as Kaṇṇa), the clouds shower rain. By stating "onṟu nī kai karavēl," Āṇḍāḷ requests the clouds to shower rain without holding back. When such rain is experienced, it brings flourishment to the land and hence brings prosperity to us. This is similar to the preceptor or the Ācārya. When the knowledge about the divine attributes of the Lord is being showered upon us by the preceptor, she requests to do so without holding back, hence the disciples can experience Bhagavān with full bliss as per their capacity. Bhagavān has assigned different tasks to different devatās who oversee specific elements or tasks: Yama for death, Agni for heat and burning, and several others. But Varuṇa, on the other hand, unless provoked, is large-hearted. He bestows rain everywhere with large-heartedness. Similarly, Ācārya is large-hearted; irrespective of good or bad, he bestows the knowledge of Nārāyaṇa.

Āṇḍāḷ calls the clouds and says "āzi uḻ" which refers to her saying the clouds to go to the mid-sea and drain the salt water, later to be rained as sweet rain. The clouds follow the cycle of nature to drain the water from the salty sea to be showered upon as beautiful rain. The Ācāryas are similar too. The Vedas are like the ocean, magnanimous in nature and difficult to be understood even by the intelligent ones, hence similar to the salty water. The Ācāryas are clouds which extract the required Vedānta from the Vedas which speaks about the Brahma-tattvam and shower the knowledge upon the disciple, hence converting the hard and difficult to understand Vedas to sweet teachings. The specific need to go to the sea, not to reservoirs like ponds or lakes, indicates from where the knowledge has to be extracted by the Ācārya. Vedas are the most authoritative as they are called apauruṣeya. But these are huge and contain all knowledge similar to an encyclopedia. But for a surrendered ātmā, it only requires the knowledge about Brahman which is hidden in corners of Vedānta. Bhagavad Gītā 2.46 states "yāvān artha udapāne sarvataḥ samplutodake | tāvān sarveṣu vedeṣu brāhmaṇasya vijānataḥ" i.e., a thirsty man will only take what is required from the reservoir; he won't drink the entire water body. Similarly, the one who desires to know Brahman (Brāhmaṇa), only fetches the knowledge about the Brahman from the Vedas which provide all kinds of rituals to attain spiritual and materialistic desires. The Ācārya has to fetch this knowledge alone and shower upon us.

Next, Āṇḍāḷ requests the clouds to turn like the color of the all-controller Nārāyaṇa and shower rain upon us with lightning like the Sudarśana-cakra! The simile which she brings here itself is beautiful as it describes the divine divya-maṅgala-rūpa of Nārāyaṇa. The Vedas like Nārāyaṇa Sūktam state that the inner soul of all, Śrīman Nārāyaṇa, has the divine color of kṛṣṇa-piṅgala which is similar to dark grey of clouds with a mix of sapphire dark blue and emerald green (ṛtaṃ satyam paraṃ brahma puruṣaṃ kṛṣṇapiṅgalam). Āṇḍāḷ now calls upon the clouds to turn into the color of that Brahma-tattvam and shower rain upon us. The Taittirīya Upaniṣad 5.8.1 states "brahmopāpnavānīti" i.e., attains and becomes like the Brahman (Sarūpya Mokṣa) to the one who performs the upāsana of the Praṇava mantra. When an Ācārya is immersed in such knowledge, he too experiences this and overflows with knowledge and bhakti. Like the Supreme's dark color which represents coolness, he too provides knowledge about Bhagavān with whole-hearted coolness. Sages who have such identity are fit to preach about Bhagavān.

She further expresses that the rain should show lightning like the Sudarśana-cakra of Bhagavān. The auspicious disc which dispels all ignorance and darkness, that which represents time, that which represents the Saṅkalpa Śakti (will-power) of the Supreme Controller is called as the Sudarśana. Viṣṇu, the all-preserver with His broad beautiful shoulders, protects one and all! His disc glows with such great effulgence even though He is still. Similarly, Ācāryas might be immersed in bhakti towards Nārāyaṇa and remain still, but their effulgence through their knowledge (Jñāna Śakti) and activities performed as per śāstras which please Kṛṣṇa (Kriyā Śakti) glow like the disc. It too provides protection to all the disciples who are stuck in the ignorance of this saṃsāra.

Āṇḍāḷ further elaborates that the cloud also needs to thunder loud and clear, saying "valamburi pōl ninṟadirndu." The comparison she gives for the sound is that of the divine Pāñcajanya conch of Bhagavān. The gopīs keep the divine conch dear to them. Unlike the disc which leaves the divine hands of the Lord for performing duties of killing demons and asuras, the conch, on the other hand, always stays with Nārāyaṇa, and in fact, the sound it vibrates is when it associates itself with the divine mouth of the Supreme. The sound itself is enough to destroy enemies with fear. Just like the sound of the conch which symbolizes the sacred Praṇava, the Ācārya too opens his divine mouth to chant Praṇava and then showers the knowledge of the divine attributes of the Lord. She calls upon the clouds to shower rain in such an unstoppable manner similar to the Śārṅga bow of Viṣṇu. Until the enemy falls defeated, the bow keeps striking arrows, such is its power. The Ācārya who is immersed and is overflowing with divine knowledge about the attributes of Bhagavān, like a cow which showers milk when its udders are filled to unbearable extent, he too showers grace and knowledge upon us like the arrows of Śārṅga. May the rains shower to their full extent and make all the elders and gopīs happy. Seeing their happiness without envy, we rejoice as our Ācāryas have showered grace upon us to gain this divine knowledge.
