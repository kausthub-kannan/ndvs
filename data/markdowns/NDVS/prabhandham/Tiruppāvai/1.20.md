## meaning

O Puissant Warrior! How graciously you advance into the battlefield long before danger threatens, and stand in the forefront if only to rescue thirty-three crores of celestials from fear of their enemies! Pray, wake up! Of immense strength and great uprightness and absolute purity, you are by nature inclined always to give protection; and regarding the foes of your suppliants as your own enemies, you strike them with terror. We request you to wake up. O Nīladēvī, how perfect you are in beauty with breasts like gold tumblers and ruby nether lip (resembling the fruit of coccinia indica—doṇḍa, kovai), and slender waist! Pray, wake up! Full of perfection, you are equal to Lakṣmī. Do give us the fan and the mirror; and help us to bathe together with your lord, Śrī Kṛṣṇa.

## purport

Trying to wake up Kṛṣṇa now, the gopikās question him for lying down even after their call. They ask him, "Will you only wake up and answer calls to the 33 devatās who come and seek your protection from asuras, daityas, and rākṣasas?" Bhagavān exists in different states. The Pañcarātra texts split these states into 4 primary forms: Para, Vyūha, Vibhava, and Arcā. It is in the Vyūha rūpa that he stays as Kṣīrābdinātha, laying on the cosmic ocean. Here is where devatās come and seek his help. When Indra lost all wealth by the curse of Durvāsa, he and the 33 devatās—8 Vasus, 12 Ādityas, 11 Rudras, and the twins Aśvinī Kumāras—come and seek his help. From his advice, they churn the cosmic ocean yet face difficulty in taking Mount Meru from its position. It is Bhagavān with his Sudarśana cakra who removes the Meru and then carries it upon Garuḍa. Later, when the churning Meru is sinking without a base, again Bhagavān incarnates as Kūrma, letting the Meru churn. The devatās and asuras lose strength while churning, and it is Bhagavān who enters them and provides strength. Again, at the end, he is the one who brings the elixir, Amṛta, as Dhanvantari. When this Amṛta was stolen by asuras, he is the one who deludes them as Mohinī. In all instances, he protects the devatās. Hence, Āṇḍāl questions, "Will you only listen to their calls? Are you going to wait for them to join us and call you? Won't you listen to us who have come here to serve you? The devatās sought your help for their own purposes, but we seek you to serve you, for you alone, yet why don't you listen to us? Have you vowed not to help us? Please awaken, O protector of all!"

Kṛṣṇa is known for his uprightness and purity. As he himself states in Bhagavad Gītā 9.20: "samo'haṃ sarvabhūteṣu na me dveṣyo'sti na priyaḥ ye bhajanti tu māṃ bhaktyā mayi te teṣu cāpyaham," i.e., he doesn't hate anyone or hold anyone especially dear, yet those who have realized he is the source of all and surrendered to him, they reside in him and he resides in them. He is like the sun whose grace, in the form of sun rays, indiscriminately reaches everywhere. Due to our clouds of karma, we might sometimes be deprived of these rays, but when we remove the ignorance, his rays fall upon us. Hence, though he never discriminates and provides everyone what they deserve, he loves his devotees, especially the prapannas. Hence, he is pure and always protects his devotees. Āṇḍāl reminds Kṛṣṇa of his affection towards his devotees. When he entered the court of Hastināpura as a messenger from the Pāṇḍavas, the courtiers saw who he truly was! Later, when asked to stay in the palace, he replies by saying, "It is improper to eat in the house of enemies or to feed enemies. Though I am a friend to all, you have enmity towards the Pāṇḍavas, who are my life and breath; hence you have gained my enmity too." When Arjuna decides to kill Jayadratha for not letting Yudhiṣṭhira protect Abhimanyu, he vows that if he doesn't do so by sunset, he would enter the fires to perish. Kṛṣṇa, angered by Arjuna's impetuous action done in anger, advises him to be well prepared. The previous night, worried about Arjuna, he asks Sātyaki to prepare his chariot. He is ready to break his vow of not fighting to protect Arjuna. When Bhīṣma threatens Arjuna, an angered Kṛṣṇa picks up the chariot wheel, transforming it into Sudarśana, ready to slay Bhīṣma—he broke the vow for the sake of protecting Arjuna. Without a doubt, he loves his devotees incomparably. Now Āṇḍāl asks, "Such is your nature, and hence you need to protect us and listen to us; please wake up!"

Seeing Kṛṣṇa still sleeping, the gopikās continue to pray to Nīlādevī. They beautifully describe her beauty. Her waist is thin as she desires nothing and is immersed in Bhagavān. But Kṛṣṇa too is in deep love with her. He rests on her bosom. Her two breasts represent the two states of bhakti: parā bhakti and parama bhakti. He has been captivated by her and would not move an inch without her permission. Her bosom is soft and has captivated him. Only when she lets him move from her captivating bosom is when he can awaken and listen to their plea. Hence, please wake up and awaken Kṛṣṇa too!

The gopikās seek Nīlādevī to grant them fan and mirror and arrange a bath for them with Kṛṣṇa. The bath represents total immersion with Kṛṣṇa by serving him. The fan dries sweat and provides relief. The 'Namaḥ' mantra provides the jīvātmā relief from sorrows of ahaṅkāra and mamakāra. On the other hand, the mirror shows the reflection. Similarly, the Praṇava mantra shows the svarūpa of jīvātmā—that it is the subservient of the Lord. Only when these three are granted is the jīvātmā eligible for mokṣa; hence they seek Nīlādevī to grant them.
