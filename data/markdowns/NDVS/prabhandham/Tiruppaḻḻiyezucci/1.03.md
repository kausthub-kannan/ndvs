## meaning

Oh Lord of Śrīraṅgam! The Sun's lustrous rays have now pervaded all sides. The lightning-like lustre of the stars has diminished, and the bright moon has grown pale. The darkness that enveloped everything has now dispersed.
A gentle morning breeze now blows over the gardens, cleaving through the branches of areca nut trees, carrying their sweet fragrance. Oh Lord Rañganātha, holding the mighty and bright discus-weapon (cakrāyudha) on Your glorious and long hand! Please arise and bless us!

## purport

The Āḷvār declares that the night has waned, and the stars have gradually faded from the celestial canvas. The moon is losing its luminescence as the Sun ascends, and thus he implores: "Awaken, Raṅganātha!" The Sun radiates with such brilliant intensity that its rays disperse everywhere, causing the lustrous moon and stars to vanish entirely. This magnificent solar nature emerges from the presence of Nārāyaṇa within, who infuses such extraordinary radiance into the Sun. Consequently, He is revered as Āditya-Hṛdayam (the one who resides at the heart of Āditya). Raṅganātha bears a profound connection to the illustrious Ikṣvāku dynasty, the Solar clan. When Brahmā presented Vivasvān with the archa mūrti of Raṅga, the twelve Ādityas worshipped Him, as proclaimed in the Ṛg Veda: "dhyeyaḥ sadā savṛtr-maṇḍala-madhya-varti nārāyaṇaḥ sarasijāsana-sanniveśitaḥ" - signifying that Nārāyaṇa eternally resides at the very center of the Solar mandala. Therefore, as the Sun ascends, Oh Raṅganātha - worshipped by the solar deity himself - please awaken! Rise to reveal a radiance unparalleled, as the Viṣṇu Purāṇa (1.9.67) declares: "tejasām rāśim ūrjitam" - His effulgence glows incomparably. Just as the Sun dispels darkness, similar to how Asuras flee from Your Sudarśana Chakra in its ready-to-launch position, illuminate our world with Your divine presence. 

The lotuses have blossomed, and the fragrance of the areca nut tree has intermingled with the gentle morning breeze. This exquisite aroma heralds the arrival of dawn. The Āḷvār beckons Raṅganātha to awaken to these fragrances and deploy His Chakra to defend all deities and devotees assembled before Him. The Āḷvār's emphasis on Raṅganātha's prayoga chakra subtly entreats the Lord for comprehensive protection and grace upon all bhāgavatas. Moreover, it represents a profound request to dispel our ignorance and bestow divine wisdom.
