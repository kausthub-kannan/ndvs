## meaning

Fragrant lotus blossoms have unfurled! The Sun has risen magnificently above the roaring waters of the eastern sea! Slender-waisted ladies, having bathed in the river Kāvēri, have rinsed and delicately shaken water from their lustrous tresses, tying them up and adorning themselves with fresh garments as they gracefully ascend the riverbanks.\
Oh Śrī Raṅganātha, nestled on the banks of the sacred Kāvēri, who has graciously accepted me - Toṇḍaradippoḍi, the blissful devotee who rejoices in the dust of Your sacred feet! Bearing in my hands a garland of sacred tulasī, an offering most beloved by You, I implore You to awaken and bless Your devoted followers!

## purport

Alvār concludes waking up Rañganātha by asking him to provide bhāgavata kaiṅkaryam. The sun has grown away from the ocean to the sky and magnificently shining. Ladies following their daily routine and rituals, having bathed in Kāveri and are returning home to dry their clothes. Adorning fresh garments, they are preparing themselves to serve you.

Alvār says to Bhagavān that, with great compassion he has accepted him as his devotee and now requests him to grant him to serve his devotees. This pattern is well observed in Divya Prabandham and every grantha-stotra-prabandham written by any Śrī Vaiṣṇava ācārya. The concepts of bhāgavata śeṣattvam, i.e., the acceptance to serve the devotees of the Lord with great respect, is the character of a prapanna. Hence Alvār, who was originally called Vipranarayana, has the name Toṇḍaraṭṭupuṭi, which literally translates to "the one who holds the dust emerged out of the feet of the devotees on his forehead." Rāmānujācārya in his Śrīraṅga Gadhyam repeats the same as "prītikāritāśēṣāvasthōcitāśēṣaśēṣataikaratirūpa nityakaiṅkarya prāptyupāyabhūtabhakti" where the Ācārya asks Namperumāḷ seated along with Rañganāyaki Nācchiyār to bless him the opportunity to serve their devotees. Hence Alvār calls himself Toṇḍaraṭṭupuṭi, who is holding the garland of Tulasī ready to be worn by Rañganātha, who is raising his head up from Ananta!
