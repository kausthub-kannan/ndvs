## meaning

Oh Lord of Śrīraṅgam! The following dēvās have assembled near the gates of Your holy shrine, which resembles a great mountain, to receive Your first and foremost merciful and matchless glances: Here are the twelve Ādityas with their lofty chariots bedecked with jewels; the eleven Rudras riding their bulls, who rule over the worlds; the six-faced Subrahmaṇya riding the peacock; the Maruts and the eight Vasus. They have all arrived with their retinues, singing and dancing, accompanied by their horses and chariots. Oh Lord! Please awaken from Your divine slumber and bless us all!

## purport

This pāsuram bears striking similarities to the first pāsuram and Veṅkateśvara Suprabhātam. All the devatās seeking Bhagavān's protection have assembled before Raṅganātha. The Āḷvārs reference the Ekādaśa Rudras as controllers of mortal beings. The Yajur Saṁhitā (6.2.8) elucidates how these Rudras obtained their power to control mortals: "sa abravīt varam vṛṇa aham eva | paśūnām adhipatiḥ asāṇīti tasmāt rudraḥ paśūnām adhipatiḥ ||" - essentially praying to become the leader of saṁsāris (worldly beings) who are likened to paśus (animals). 

Even these Rudras have arrived, riding their bulls, to serve the Lord. The twelve Ādityas have also assembled to offer their reverence. These Ādityas symbolically represent the twelve months of the cosmic cycle. The Āḷvār emphasizes that celestial deities seeking protection and bhāgavatas yearning for divine grace and kaiṅkarya (sacred service) have congregated to serve the Lord. "Awaken, Raṅganātha, and accept our service!" The Āḷvār invokes the concept of kumāra-dhaṇḍa, which carries two profound interpretations: First, Śanmukha (Kārttikeya), the son of Rudra, has assembled here. Second, these deities stand like dhaṇḍa (thick sticks), representing their unwavering devotion. The term kumāra often signifies youthfulness, suggesting these devatās - eternally young - stand densely packed, eagerly awaiting divine blessings. To these devatās and prapannas, the Āḷvār implores: "Oh Raṅganātha, awaken! Rise to accept our sacred service!"
