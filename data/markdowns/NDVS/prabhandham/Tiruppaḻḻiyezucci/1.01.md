## meaning

The sun has arrived at the peak of the eastern side, dispelling the heavy darkness of the night. As the beautiful morning time has arrived, honey is oozing out of the most exquisite flowers.\
The dēvās and kings are arriving quickly in groups, pushing each other to the south side of the sannidhi (temple) where your divine vision will reach. They stand there, filling the entire place. Along with them, large groups of male and female elephants (which are the vehicles of the dēvās, kings, etc.) and persons playing musical instruments have arrived.\
Their sounds and noises that reach all directions in eager anticipation of your waking up resemble the great sound of an ocean with fierce waves. Thus, you kindly wake up now and give your blessings to all who have assembled here, Oh Lord who is lying down in Śrīraṅgam

## purport

The Sun (kadiravan) has arrived at the peak of Udaya Giri in the east. His rays, blazing everywhere, remove the darkness of the night. All the dēvās and kings have assembled to seek your grace as morning has arrived. The Sun is able to remove the darkness of light, but not the ignorance within us. This ignorance of ours leads us to be stuck in saṃsāra, ever captive by the things of this world. But you, the Supreme Being, by your grace alone, can destroy this ignorance. The knowledge granted by your compassion can destroy all our false assumptions. As Nammālvār states in the first paśuram of Thiruvāymōḻi, "mayarvu aṟa mati nalam aruḷiṉaṉ evaṉ avaṉ" - by His grace itself, the ignorance can be destroyed. He himself would grant us the supreme knowledge. 

Kings and dēvatās have assembled in front of you, ready to serve you and seek your grace. To grant them grace, Alvār requests Rañganātha to wake up. Bhagavān is indeed the Master of all, just as Thiruppāṇ Alvār states in his Amalanādhipirān, which directly translates to Him being the blemish-free Lord who is the ruler of dēvatās. Just as humans and animals like Gajendra seek to serve bhagavān, even the dēvatās seek to serve the Supreme Being. Pratividhi Bhayaṅkara Svāmi states in Veṅkaṭēśvara Suprabhātam: "sevāparāḥ śiva sureśa kṛśānudharma rakṣo'mbunātha pavamāna dhanādināthāḥ | baddhāñjali pravilasan nijaśīrṣa deśāḥ śrīveṅkaṭācalapate tava suprabhātam" That is, Śiva (Īśāna), Sureśa (Indra, the Lord of the Dēvās), Kṛśānu (Agni, God of Fire), Dharma (Dharmārāja or Yama), Rakṣa (Nirṛti), Ambunātha (Varuṇa, the Lord of Water), Pavamāna (God of Air), and Kubera (the Lord of Wealth)—who are themselves shining with a Divine Glow—are standing with their palms joined in devotion, asking: "Wake, Oh Lord of Veṅkaṭādri to shower grace on us!" Similarly, Alvār states that the kings and dēvatās have assembled to serve you. Wake up to shower your grace upon them! 

Flowers have been arranged here to shower upon your lotus feet, to serve you, my Lord! These flowers have blossomed in the rays of the Sun and are oozing honey from their buds. As they reach your feet, they are indeed blessed! Thoṇḍaripperumal Alvār feels just like Kulaśēkhara, who in Periya Thiruvāymōḻi wanted to be a Champaka tree on Tirumala. Elephants and drummers have assembled, playing the sounds of drums. Oh Lord of Śrīraṅgam, these sounds are like the waves of the ocean. Alvārs call Rañganātha "Karuṇā Sāgara," the ocean of compassion. Hence, to shower your compassion upon us, please wake up!
