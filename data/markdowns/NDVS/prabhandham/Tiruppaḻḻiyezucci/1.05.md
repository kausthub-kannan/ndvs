## meaning

Oh Lord! The birds in the flower garden have awakened. The night has passed away, and the morning has arrived. The sounds of ocean waves are emerging from the east. The celestials have arrived with beautiful garlands made of manifold flowers, humming with honey bees, ready to offer them at Thy holy feet. Therefore, my Master, Oh Lord of the temple where the ruler of Laṅkā (Vibhīṣaṇa) worships Thee! Wake up from Thy divine slumber and bless us!

## purport

The melodious chirping of birds heralds the morning, signaling the sacred time to serve the Lord. Night induces the tāmasic guṇa, compelling us into slumber, whereas the morning sun bestows sāttvika guṇa, making brahma muhūrta the most auspicious moment for divine worship. Śrīraṅgam is renowned for its ethereal natural beauty. Gardens brim with blossoming flowers, while bees voraciously drink their nectar. These bees' melodious hum seems to chant "Raṅga! Raṅga!" and the peacocks dance in sublime rhythm. The Āḷvār poignantly asks: "Despite all these vibrant activities and sounds, why do You still slumber, Oh Raṅga?"

The ocean's waves crash thunderously, creating a mysterious backdrop. One might wonder why the Āḷvār speaks of the ocean when Raṅganātha resides near the Kāvēri River. Periyavāccān Piḷḷai provides a profound interpretation. From across the ocean, from Laṅkā, came Vibhīṣaṇa - the very being who transported Raṅganātha from Ayodhyā to Śrīraṅgam. Facing eastward, it appears as though Raṅganātha awaits Vibhīṣaṇa's arrival. Thus, the Āḷvār provocatively questions: "Until Vibhīṣaṇa comes to awaken You, will You not rise?" Nañjīyar provides a complementary commentary. Vibhīṣaṇa, born as Rāvaṇa's younger brother - the same Rāvaṇa whose lustful gaze fell upon Sītā - was nonetheless wholly accepted by Rāma. Despite his lineage, Vibhīṣaṇa possessed profound bhakti towards Nārāyaṇa, ultimately surrendering to Rāma for divine protection.
Rāma, our Raṅganātha, embodying the Prapanna Parijāta (wish-fulfilling divine tree for those who surrender), embraced Vibhīṣaṇa's śaraṇāgati (complete surrender) and offered him unequivocal protection. The Āḷvār's rhetorical question resonates: "With celestial deities standing before You, are You, out of compassion for prapannas, waiting specifically for Vibhīṣaṇa?"
