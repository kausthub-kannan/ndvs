## english_scripts

vambaviz vānavar vāyuṟai vazaṅga
mānidi kapilai oṇ kaṇṇāḍi mudalā
emberumān paḍimakkalam kāṇḍaṟku
ēṟpana vāyina koṇḍu nan munivar
tumburu nāradar pugundanar ivarō
tōnṟinan iraviyum tulaṅgoḻi parappi
ambara talattil ninṟagalginṟadiruḻ pōy
araṅgattammā ! paḻḻi ezundaruḻāyē

## meaning

Oh Lord of Śrīraṅgam! Many deities have arrived with magnificent treasures, offering them reverentially to receive Your benevolent glances. They have brought the vāyurai (arugam grass); the sacred cow Kapilā; mirrors and other sacred implements like padimakkalam for Your morning ārādhana. The illustrious sages, Tumburu and Nārada, have descended to glorify You with their divine vīṇās. Dawn has already broken, with the radiant Sun ascending and dispersing his luminous rays across the celestial expanse. Darkness has retreated, even from the ethereal realms. Oh Lord of Śrīraṅgam! Awaken and bestow Your divine blessings upon us!

## purport

As the Sun has ascended, its radiant rays have illuminated everywhere, dispelling the darkness bestowed by night. This luminescence extends not only to the human realm but to the celestial domains of the devatās as well. This profound imagery suggests that Bhagavān removes ignorance not just for humanity, but for the exalted celestial beings praised across countless traditions. To shower divine grace upon all the assembled beings, the Āḷvār implores: "Awaken, Raṅganātha!"

The Āḷvār beautifully explicates the sacred process of awakening Raṅganātha in Śrīraṅgam. The devatās and yogis assembled before Him have brought auspicious objects to present upon His awakening. According to Āgamas like the Pāñcarātra's Parameśvara Saṁhitā, specific objects must be ceremonially presented: mirrors, vessels containing milk, and collyrium (the sacred black substance used to adorn divine eyelids). Accompanying these ritual objects, an elephant and a cow with its calf are brought before the sanctum, for His beloved consort Mahālakṣmī is believed to reside within these sacred animals. These objects originated during the Kṣīra Manthana (churning of the cosmic ocean) and were subsequently gifted to the devatās. The profound theological principle dictates that before these sacred objects are enjoyed by devatās or humans, they must first be reverentially offered to the Supreme Being.

The archakas of Śrīraṅgam, meticulously adhering to ancient Āgamic traditions, simultaneously bring waters from the river Kāvēri. In the morning light, the river shimmers magnificently, reflecting the Sun's rays like liquid gold. These consecrated waters are gathered for Raṅganātha's daily ritual ablutions. Divine music becomes the final element of this sacred awakening. Tumburu and Nārada have assembled to play their vīṇās and tambūris, creating a melodious invocation. Sama Gāna, the most sublime of musical offerings, is especially beloved by Bhagavān. "Oh Raṅga," the Āḷvār entreats, "Awaken and bless us!"
