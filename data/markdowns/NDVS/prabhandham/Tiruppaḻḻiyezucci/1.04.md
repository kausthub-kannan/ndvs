## meaning

Oh Śrīraṅganātha! The sounds of huge and tender buffaloes moving, the flute songs of cowherds who have gathered them, and the jingling noises of bells tied to the cattle's necks have spread in all directions. The honey-imbibing bees have emerged from their hives. Oh Śrīraṅganātha! Master of the Celestials! Oh Protector of Viśvāmitra's yāga and destroyer of Laṅkā's rākṣasa hordes! Oh wielder of the kōdaṇḍa bow, who was anointed as the crowned ruler of Ayōdhyā and ruled from there for the purpose of destroying the enemies of light and truth! Please awaken from your divine slumber and bless us!

## purport

Cattle gather enchantingly at the melodious call of the cowherd's flute. The commentator Periyavāccān Piḷḷai delightfully highlights the Āḷvār's subtle sense of humor. In the Purāṇas, humans are considered superior in intelligence compared to beings like cattle, as stated in the Narasiṁha Purāṇa (16.13): "āhāra nidrā bhaya maithunāni tulyāni kalvatra samasta-jantoḥ, jñānāt viśiṣṭo hi naraḥ parebhyo jñānena hīnaḥ paśubhiḥ samānaḥ" - suggesting that while eating, sleeping, fear, and procreation are common across all creatures, humans are distinguished by their capacity for higher knowledge, and those devoid of such wisdom are equivalent to animals. The Āḷvār provocatively notes that even the cattle have awakened, yet You - the Supreme Being proclaimed as Brahman by the Vedas - continue to slumber. Awaken, Raṅganātha! 

Next, the Āḷvār experiences the mūla mūrti (primary form) of Raṅganātha as Kṛṣṇa, and the utsava mūrti, Namperumāḷ, as Rāma. He contemplates  Rāma's vīra-tattva (heroic essence) during the protection of Viśvāmitra's yajña. Viśvāmitra's sacred ritual was being disrupted by rākṣasas and asuras like Mārīci, Tāḍakā, and others. Thus, he requested Daśaratha to send Rāma to defend the yajña. Though Viśvāmitra was himself capable of protecting the ritual, and Daśaratha equally powerful, the sage's deeper desire was to facilitate the meeting of Rāma and Sītā, ultimately leading to their divine marriage while simultaneously revealing Rāma's heroic nature. Rāma, wielding his legendary kōdaṇḍa bow, decimated these asuras with such extraordinary might that when he directed an arrow at Mārīci-son of Tāḍakā - the rākṣasa was hurled multiple times across the ocean. Eventually, Mārīci was compelled to recognize Rāma's divine supremacy. Such was the magnificent power of our great Lord! He who destroys enemies called asuras (and ignorance), he who ruled Ayodhya, remains asleep while the cows have awakened. Rāma ruled for a millennium, while You - existing beyond time - have been, are, and will be eternally present to bless us. Therefore, Raṅganātha, awaken!
