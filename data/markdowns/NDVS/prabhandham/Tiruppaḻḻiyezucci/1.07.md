## english_scripts

andarattamarargaḻ kūṭṭaṅgaḻ ivaiyō
arundava munivarum marudarum ivarō
indiran ānaiyum tānum vandivanō
emberumān un kōyilin vāśal
śundarar nerukka viccādarar nūkka
iyakkarum mayaṅginar tiruvaḍi ttozuvān
andaram pāriḍam illai maṭriduvō
araṅgattammā ! paḻḻi ezundaruḻāyē

## meaning

"Oh Lord of Śrīraṅgam! Numerous significant personages have gathered at the entrance of Your sacred shrine, reverently paying homage to Your divine lotus feet. They include multitudes of celestial deities, profound sages of supreme austerities, the Maruts, and Lord Indra accompanied by his majestic elephant Airāvata.\
Hosts of Sundaras (Gandharvas) and Vidyādharas have arrived, standing so closely pressed together that they occupy every available space. The Yakṣas have also assembled with fervent devotion and profound spiritual earnestness. There is no room left at the entrance of Your hallowed temple (kōyil), both in the celestial heavens above and upon the earthly realm below.\
Oh Lord! Awaken and bestow Your most gracious blessings upon these devoted beings and upon adiyēn (Your humble servant)!"

## purport

Following the previous pāsuram, Alvār quotes that all the devatās have assembled here. Indra, who is considered the ruler among the devatās, appears in front of the Lord seeking His grace. Alvār speaks about the paratattva of Bhagavān with profound devotional insight.

The scene depicts a magnificent celestial gathering where various divine entities converge, showcasing the supreme lordship of Rañganātha. Indra, the king of devatās, arrives mounted on his legendary elephant Airāvata, symbolizing both his royal status and his complete submission to the divine will. This description emphasizes a crucial theological concept in Śrī Vaiṣṇava philosophy: even the most powerful celestial beings are ultimately subservient to the supreme divine principle.
