## meaning

With the sonorous and resonant vīṇā resting on her breasts, with a smile showing out the teeth just a little and with the soft fingers becoming red due to plucking (at the vīṇā strings), my daughter prattled variously like the parrot as, "My Elephant in rut at Pādakam in Kāñcī surrounded by tall and extensive stone fortifications!" "My Lord sweet like ripe fruit reposing in Milk-ocean!" and "My Lord standing with relish at lovely Aḷundūr bounded by wet fields and tanks where blossom lovely water lily flowers!"

## purport

With the parrot continuing to recite the names of the Lord in response to her prompts, as described in the previous pāsuram, the heroine gradually enters a state of joyful absorption. The divine names soothe her heart and transport her into a world entirely centred upon the Lord. Though outwardly separated from Him, her mind becomes so immersed in His remembrance that she begins to experience His presence everywhere around her.

In this state of ecstatic devotion, the heroine beholds the Lord in objects that would ordinarily appear mundane. Her love has reached such intensity that the distinction between remembrance and direct experience begins to fade. The longing born of separation transforms itself into an inward vision of union.

Thus, she takes up her Vīṇā and imagines it to be the Lord Himself. Embracing it closely to her bosom, she addresses it as though she were speaking directly to her beloved. What appears externally as an inanimate musical instrument becomes, in her devotional perception, a living manifestation of the Lord's presence. Through this act, she seeks solace from the anguish of separation and experiences a momentary sense of union with Him.

This episode beautifully illustrates the transformative power of divine love. For a devotee whose heart is wholly occupied by Bhagavān, every object becomes a reminder of Him, and every experience becomes an opportunity for communion with Him. The heroine's embrace of the Vīṇā is therefore not an act of imagination alone, but an expression of the depth of her absorption in the Lord.
