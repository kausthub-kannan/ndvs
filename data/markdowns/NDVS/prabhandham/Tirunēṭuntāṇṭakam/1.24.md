## meaning

The Lord with huge mouth which swallowed the worlds (in pralaya) came right here, made me distressed shedding tears from my eyes which are like two fishes fighting, and went away saying that His place is water-rich Śrīraṅgam. He was like dark cloud (with lightning and thunder) richly laden with water sucked up to the grains of sand beneath from the mighty sea. He had the Conch in one hand and the Discus in the other hand and was surrounded by Nityasūris, and servents. Alas! My sins! Now my bangles do not stay in place in my two hands!

## purport

Āḻvār continues to express the agony of separation in this pāsuram. As Bhagavān prepares to depart, Āḻvār is unable to withstand the pain of being separated from Him. Overwhelmed by grief, Parakāla Nāyaki begins to shed tears incessantly. The very sight of the Lord's divine form, capable of enchanting all beings, only deepens the intensity of her longing. The more she beholds His beauty, the more unbearable His departure becomes.

Āḻvār describes the Lord as resembling a dark rain-bearing cloud appearing in the night sky. Upon that dark form shines a brilliant radiance, just as lightning illuminates a cloud. This description closely echoes the imagery of the Nārāyaṇa Sūktam: *"nī̠latō̍yada̍madhya̠sthā̠dvi̠dyullē̍khēva̠ bhāsva̍rā"*. The Lord possesses the deep hue of a dark cloud, often described as *Kṛṣṇa-piṅgalam*, while the golden splendour that shines upon Him is identified with Mahālakṣmī, who eternally resides upon His chest and is never separated from Him. Thus, the divine form manifests both majesty and tenderness through the inseparable presence of the Divine Couple.

The Lord's hands are adorned with the great Pāñcajanya conch and the Sudarśana Cakra. Yet, in Āḻvār's vision, these are not merely weapons intended for the destruction of evil. They appear as beautiful ornaments enhancing the splendour of His divine form. Everything associated with Him becomes an object of beauty and delight to the devotee. Around Him stand the Ṛṣis, Nityasūris, and Muktātmas, all engaged in His service and glorification.

Āḻvār beholds an extraordinary scene. The dark cloud-hued Lord is seated upon Garuḍa, surrounded by the eternal servants of Paramapada. The vision recalls the declaration of the Viṣṇu Sūkta: *"tad viṣṇoḥ paramaṃ padaṃ sadā paśyanti sūrayaḥ"*—the enlightened ones eternally behold the supreme abode of Viṣṇu. The Lord is praised on all sides by the Nityasūris, whose eyes never tire of beholding Him and whose service never ceases. Witnessing such a vision, Āḻvār loses all awareness of himself. His heart becomes completely absorbed in Bhagavān, and his devotion rises to an even greater intensity.

Yet, with that intensification of devotion comes an equally intense experience of separation. The closer the devotee comes to Bhagavān, the more painful even a moment of separation becomes. Thus, the very vision that fills Āḻvār with ecstasy also becomes the source of profound anguish when the Lord prepares to depart.

The Lord who once swallowed the entire universe during Mahāpralaya, holding all existence within Himself, now turns to Āḻvār and declares that His abode is Śrīraṅgam. Having revealed His beauty, majesty, and accessibility, He then ascends upon Garuḍa and departs. Left behind, Āḻvār is consumed by longing. The Lord's departure transforms the joy of divine vision into the pain of separation, making this pāsuram a powerful expression of the paradox of supreme bhakti, where the experience of the Lord simultaneously grants the highest bliss and the deepest yearning.
