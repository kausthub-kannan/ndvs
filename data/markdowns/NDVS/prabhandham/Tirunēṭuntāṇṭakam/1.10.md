## meaning

You are in the south (at Tirumalirumcōlai) eulogized by the worlds. You are in the north (at Tirumalai). You are in the west (at Śrīraṅgam). You are in the east (at Kaṇṇapuram) like an elephant in rut. You are always in the forefront of the Nityasūris. You are at Tirumōlikkalam with effulgence for veneration by subsequent generations of devotees. You are the primordial Lord. My cherished Lord! Lord with the glory of protecting the seven worlds! What does this insignificant devotee, bereft of any means, know apart from saying "My Elephant! My Elephant!"

## purport

In the fourth pāsuram, Āḻvār had declared, *"Mantirattai mantirattai maṟavādu eṉṟum vāḻttiyēl vāḻalām"*—that one can truly thrive by constantly glorifying the Protector through the Tirumantram without interruption. Reflecting upon this, Āḻvār wonders whether the Lord had refrained from appearing before him because He might have mistaken this constant recitation for an act of personal sādhanā undertaken to attain Him. However, Āḻvār clarifies that this was never his intention. His purpose was merely to remind himself of the true nature of the soul, to remain absorbed in thoughts of the Lord, and to spend his time in a meaningful and beneficial manner while awaiting divine grace.

In this pāsuram, Āḻvār expresses both his own utter helplessness and the Lord's incomparable greatness. He emphasizes that attainment of the Lord is not the result of individual effort but solely of the Lord's compassion and initiative. To illustrate this truth, Āḻvār compares the Lord to a majestic elephant.

An elephant cannot be ignored because of its immense size and commanding presence. Yet, despite its strength, it can be controlled only when it willingly cooperates. A rider cannot mount an elephant through his own effort alone; the elephant itself must kneel down and make it possible. Likewise, if it so chooses, the elephant can lift a person with its trunk and place him upon its back. Through this analogy, Āḻvār conveys that the jīvātmā cannot attain Bhagavān merely through personal endeavour. The Lord Himself must graciously make Himself accessible and elevate the devotee through His own will.

Āḻvār further compares the Lord to the mighty Diggajas, the celestial elephants that are traditionally said to uphold and protect the directions of the universe. In a similar manner, the Lord stands protecting and sustaining the world through His presence in the great sacred abodes of Vēṅkaṭam, Kāṇṇapuram, Tirumalirumcōlai, and Śrīraṅgam. From these holy places, He extends His grace to all who seek Him.

Thus, although the Lord is immeasurably exalted, He remains within easy reach of His devotees. By contemplating His presence in these sacred kṣetras, Āḻvār finds solace in the assurance that the Lord who sustains the universe is also the compassionate Master who graciously makes Himself accessible to those who depend entirely upon Him.
