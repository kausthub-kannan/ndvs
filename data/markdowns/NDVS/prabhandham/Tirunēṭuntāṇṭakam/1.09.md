## meaning

Lord at Māmallaipuram where precious gems are brought by ships and dumped! Lord of the town of Kāñcī with fortifications! Lord at Tiruppērai! Lord who has Śiva with Pārvatī beside him on the left and decorated by garland of fragrant honey-soaked bilva flowers on his chest, at His side! Lord of the Milk-ocean! Lord who came on the earth (in avatāras)! Lord at the top of the cool mountain (Tirumalai)! Lord Pavaḷavanna (at Kāñcī)! Looking for you all over, this fickle-minded self is wallowing in this body.

## purport

In an earlier pāsuram, Āḻvār celebrated the boundless compassion of the Lord, who extends His grace even to those who are not inclined towards Him. If such is the Lord's nature, Āḻvār wonders why He has neither summoned him to Tirukkāñūr nor come personally to grant him His presence. The only explanation he can find is the burden of his own accumulated sins, which continue to obstruct the fulfilment of his longing.

Filled with this sense of helplessness, Āḻvār laments his condition. He finds himself unable either to endure the separation from the Lord or to cast aside the body that sustains that suffering. Thus, he remains caught in a state of anguish, yearning for union with the Lord while feeling constrained by the limitations imposed by karma.

The phrase *"Vaṅkaṭṭāl māmaṇi vandu uṇḍu"* is explained through a beautiful analogy. Precious gems are often brought by ships from the depths of the ocean and deposited upon the shore. In the same way, the Lord, who eternally resides in Paramapada, has graciously descended and established Himself on the seashore at Māmallapuram for the delight and benefit of His devotees. Just as a rare jewel becomes accessible when brought from distant waters to the shore, the Lord has made Himself easily accessible to those who seek Him.

Through this imagery, Āḻvār highlights the Lord's extraordinary simplicity and accessibility. Though He is the Supreme Being who transcends all worlds, He willingly leaves His exalted abode and manifests among devotees so that they may approach Him without difficulty. Yet, despite such accessibility, Āḻvār feels unable to attain Him and therefore attributes the delay solely to his own shortcomings. This pāsuram thus becomes a moving expression of humility, longing, and the pain of separation experienced by a devotee who sees the Lord's nearness everywhere but feels himself unworthy of receiving His grace.
