## meaning

He has shown him that the insentient things seen around are fleeting like lightning. He has revealed the four Vedas and is like the light of a lamp, dispelling the darkness called ignorance from him. He has given him knowledge, clear like the blemishless moon risen on the horizon, and as perceptible as the moon shining in the sky above. He has shown him that the ātmā is distinct and free from the disease, old age, and rebirth associated with the insentient things seen around. He does not deign to grant him Kaivalya. He has shown him His precious form, which cannot be cognized by anyone at any time. He possesses a human form composed of the divine elements (Śuddha Sattva). He is pleasant and refreshing to devotees like cool water, and to inimical beings, like fire. He is the effulgence within all and is supremely radiant. The sacred feet of such a One are enshrined in his heart.

## purport

Āḻvār expresses his gratitude to Bhagavān in this pāsuram. Tirunēṭuntāṇṭakam was composed by Āḻvār during the final stage of his life. Throughout his life, he had repeatedly pleaded with Bhagavān to grant him the vision of His Divya Maṅgala Rūpa. Indeed, the biography of Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār records that Bhagavān, accompanied by His divine consort, appeared before him in the form of a newly married couple and personally initiated him into the sacred Aṣṭākṣarī mantra. From that moment onward, Āḻvār became fully immersed in bhakti. Therefore, he reflects here on how Bhagavān blessed him and redirected his mind towards the Lord's lotus feet. Bhagavān revealed to Āḻvār the transient nature of this material world and the eternal nature of the ātmā, which is free from disease, suffering, and decay. This knowledge was bestowed upon him with the sudden brilliance and clarity of a lightning flash.

Similar to the opening pāsuram of Tiruvāymoḻi by Nammāḻvār, Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār also acknowledges that Bhagavān alone grants the knowledge that dispels the darkness of ignorance. What made Āḻvār even more grateful was that, in addition to imparting divine knowledge, the Lord also revealed His own divine form, which is not constituted of the elements of this material world. Unlike material substances, His Divya Maṅgala Rūpa is completely free from rajas and tamas and is therefore described as Śuddha Sattva. Just as the material world is constituted of five prākṛtika elements, the Lord's divine form is said to be constituted of five Śuddha Sattva principles, glorified in the Pañcopaniṣad mantras as Parameṣṭhi, Pumān, Nivṛtta, Viśva, and Sarva. Āḻvār is overwhelmed with gratitude that the Lord revealed such a transcendent form, a vision inaccessible even to Brahmā and Rudra. It is through the Lord's grace in bestowing both this divine knowledge and the experience of His divine form that Āḻvār was able to develop profound love and devotion towards Him.
