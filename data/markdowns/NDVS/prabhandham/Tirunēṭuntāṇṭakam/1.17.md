## meaning

Her breasts, soft and juvenile and fully developed are paling with tears gushing from her eyes like two fishes fighting. She goes out and stares. Her heart melts hearing the subdued tone by which the red-footed youthful pigeon talks to its mate and she then recalls to her mind (her previous conversation when she was in union with the Lord). Hearing her speaking endearingly of Tiruttaṅkal and cool Tirukkuḍandai and lovingly singing to Kōvalūr, when I asked her "My girl full of feminine charm! Is this (your demonstrative behavior) befitting our family?", she started singing of Naraiyūr too!

## purport

Witnessing the heroine's intense distress in the previous pāsurams, the mother had attempted various means to console her. She brought the Vīṇā and the pet parrot, hoping that these familiar companions would soothe her sorrow and provide some relief from the pain of separation. For a brief period, they appeared to offer comfort. The parrot recited the Lord's names, and the Vīṇā became an object through which the heroine expressed her devotion and longing.

However, the mother soon realized that these remedies were producing the opposite effect. Rather than alleviating the heroine's suffering, they only deepened her absorption in the Lord and intensified her yearning for Him. Every sound from the parrot and every association evoked by the Vīṇā served only to remind her of the beloved from whom she remained separated. Concluding that these objects were aggravating her condition, the mother removed them from her daughter's presence.

Believing that practical and matronly counsel might succeed where other efforts had failed, the mother attempted to reason with the heroine. She advised her that such public displays of emotion were unbecoming of her dignity and inconsistent with the honourable traditions of their family. As a concerned elder, she hoped that an appeal to propriety and social decorum would restore some measure of self-control.

The heroine, however, is no longer governed by ordinary considerations. Her heart is wholly possessed by love for the Lord, and worldly conventions hold little meaning for her. Rather than accepting her mother's advice, she responds with defiance. Unwilling to suppress her emotions for the sake of social expectations, she leaves the house and openly expresses her longing for the Lord.

Indeed, she cries out even more loudly than before, as though deliberately provoking her mother. Yet this apparent defiance is not born of disrespect. It arises from the overwhelming force of divine love, which has surpassed all concern for reputation, custom, or public opinion. For one whose mind is entirely fixed upon Bhagavān, the demands of the world become insignificant when compared to the pain of separation from Him.

Through this episode, Āḻvār portrays the irresistible nature of supreme devotion. The heroine's love has reached such intensity that neither external restraints nor well-meaning advice can contain it. Her only concern is the Lord, and her cries become the spontaneous expression of a soul that can no longer conceal its longing for union with its eternal beloved.
