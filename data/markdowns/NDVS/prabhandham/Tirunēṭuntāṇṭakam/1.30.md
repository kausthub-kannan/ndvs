## meaning

Those who study this garland in Tamil conforming to prosody, indited by the chieftain of Tirumaṅgai with hoary gem-like mansions, who, with the mighty spear, is like the Lord of death to the enemies and who is of great prowess, on the Lord who brought forth the precious Vedas as the Swan with the Celestials and Ṛṣis eulogizing, saying "The great Lord of the Nityasūris! The Lord benevolent like clouds gliding gracefully with lightning and copious rains! Please grace me," will of themselves be capable of rooting out the age-long "prārabdha karmas."

## purport

In the final pāsuram, Āḻvār once again glorifies the divine form of Bhagavān. His complexion is compared to a dark rain-bearing cloud ready to pour down showers of grace upon all beings. Just as a rain cloud nourishes the world without discrimination, the Lord bestows His compassion upon those who seek refuge in Him.

Āḻvār recalls the episode involving the four Kumāras. When the Kumāras approached Brahmā with profound questions concerning Yoga and the highest spiritual truths, the lotus-born creator found himself unable to provide the answers. At that time, Bhagavān Himself manifested in the form of a divine Swan (*Haṁsa*). Assuming this extraordinary form, He instructed Brahmā, the Kumāras, and the assembled Ṛṣis, revealing the truths of the Vedas and illuminating the path of spiritual wisdom. Thus, the Lord demonstrated that He alone is the ultimate source of all knowledge and the supreme teacher of both gods and sages.

This same Supreme Lord is eternally praised by the Nityasūris, who never cease to glorify His auspicious qualities and divine splendour. Āḻvār concludes with a prayer that this Lord may bestow His grace upon all devotees and bless them with the vision of His divine form.

The phalaśruti of the pāsuram extols the greatness of this sacred composition. Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār compares this Prabandham to a garland of fresh tulasī lovingly offered to Bhagavān. Just as a tulasī garland delights the Lord, these verses, woven together with devotion, knowledge, and divine experience, serve as a fragrant offering at His feet. Those who study, recite, and reflect upon this beautiful Prabandham will be blessed with true spiritual knowledge and deep devotion towards Bhagavān. Through His grace, they will gain clarity regarding the nature of the soul, the supremacy of the Lord, and the path of loving service to Him.

Thus, with praise of Bhagavān's divine form, remembrance of His role as the supreme teacher, and blessings upon future devotees, Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār brings the magnificent Prabandham known as *Tiruneduntāṇḍakam* to its conclusion.
