## meaning

She wears choice silk sari (to attract Him). She feels faint and sobs. She has no desire for the dolls. With her cool and drawn-out eyes filled with tears, she does not lie down. She does not stay on my lap even for a moment. She murmurs, "Where is my Lord of Tiruvāraṅgam?" When I asked the (female) soothsayer, "Tell me truly who did this to my young doe with locks of hair humming with lovely bees spitting the honey sucked by them?", she said, "The Lord of sea-hue did this. My life-companions! Who else is there to protect her (if He does not)!"

## purport

The ten pāsurams beginning with this one are spoken through the voice of the mother of a heroine who suffers the anguish of separation from her beloved after having experienced union with him. At this stage, Āḻvār has attained complete spiritual maturity. His mind is fixed exclusively upon the Lord and upon nothing else, embodying the principle of *Ananyā Śeṣatva*, the state of belonging solely to Bhagavān. This exclusive devotion is traditionally expressed through the mood of a chaste heroine whose thoughts are entirely absorbed in her beloved.

When femininity is attributed to Āḻvār in such passages, it does not refer in any way to physical characteristics. Rather, it signifies a particular spiritual disposition and emotional attitude towards the Lord. The soul, recognizing its complete dependence upon Bhagavān, assumes the role of the heroine, while the Lord is understood as the sole beloved and protector. Such imagery serves as a powerful means of expressing the intensity of divine love experienced by the devotee.

The love experienced by Āḻvār has now become so overwhelming that it leaves no room for self-control or ordinary worldly awareness. Because of the depth of this experience, the condition of Āḻvār is portrayed through the observations of a mature and concerned mother. This literary device allows the inner state of the devotee to be described from an external perspective while preserving the emotional richness of the experience.

In this pāsuram, the mother describes the condition of her daughter, the heroine, whose suffering has become evident to all around her. Seeking to discover the cause of this mysterious affliction, she approaches a godly inclined soothsayer and asks for an explanation. Upon learning that the heroine's condition arises not from any worldly cause but from her intense love for Bhagavān, the mother turns to the maidens gathered around her and reflects upon the situation.

She realizes that the Lord alone is the true protector and refuge of all souls. If the very One who exists to safeguard His devotees does not come to the aid of this love-stricken heroine, then who else could possibly offer her protection? Thus, the pāsuram becomes a poignant expression of absolute dependence upon Bhagavān, emphasizing that the soul has no refuge apart from Him and that relief from the anguish of separation can come only through His grace.
