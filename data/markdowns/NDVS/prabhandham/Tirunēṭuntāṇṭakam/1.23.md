## meaning

He took away, right here, my bright bangles and lovely complexion, having given me exclusively a deep-rooted mental agony. I will see in my dream, the Lord with the garland of fresh tulasī dripping honey who said that His residence was Śrīraṅgam- where fishes are intoxicated by the toddy from tender coconuts with clear water. At that time, I will tell Him, "You deceitful Lord riding Garuḍa! Do not go away from me." Even then, indeed! this (existence in this world) is a distressing feature to us.

## purport

Continuing from the previous pāsuram, the Lord, seated upon Vinateya (Garuḍa), prepares to depart, assuring Parakāla Nāyaki that they will meet again at Śrīraṅgam. Yet, alas, Parakāla Nāyaki is unable to bear even a moment of separation from Bhagavān. He had come before her, captivated her heart, claimed her mind, and now was departing. Having once experienced His presence, she finds it impossible to remain apart from Him. Such is the condition of a true Prapanna. Bhagavān is everything to those who have surrendered unto Him. He is their refuge, their protector, their wealth, their goal, and their very life.

Overwhelmed by the pain of separation, Parakāla Nāyaki longs to call out to Him and plead with Him not to depart upon Garuḍa. She wishes to beg Him to remain with her, unable to endure the thought of His leaving. Yet, at that very moment, a profound realization dawns upon her.

She recognizes the harsh reality of existence within saṁsāra. The bound souls (*baddhātmās*) remain trapped within the endless cycle of birth and death because of their accumulated karma. This karma acts like a dense cloud, obscuring the vision of the Supreme Lord and preventing the soul from constantly experiencing His presence. Even when Bhagavān reveals Himself through His grace, the limitations imposed by embodied existence continue to subject the soul to periods of separation and longing.

Parakāla Nāyaki therefore understands that, so long as one remains within saṁsāra, the pain of separation cannot be entirely avoided. Until the soul is liberated from the bondage of karma and attains Paramapada, it must endure the alternating experiences of union and separation. The devotee is left with no choice but to bear this suffering while continuing to depend upon the Lord's grace.

Reflecting upon this truth, she arrives at a deeper appreciation of the fortune enjoyed by the Nityasūris. Unlike the bound souls of this world, the Nityasūris dwell eternally in the presence of Nārāyaṇa. They never experience separation, forgetfulness, or obstruction caused by karma. Their service to the Lord is uninterrupted, their vision of Him is constant, and their bliss is everlasting.

Thus, the pāsuram concludes with a poignant contrast between the condition of the bound soul and that of the liberated and eternal servants of the Lord. Parakāla Nāyaki's sorrow arises from the realization that she must still endure the limitations of saṁsāra, while her aspiration remains fixed upon the state of the Nityasūris, who forever enjoy the unbroken company of Nārāyaṇa and render eternal service at His divine feet.
