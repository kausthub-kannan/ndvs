## meaning

I will be ever languishing by thinking that I would be enabled to bind Him between the mounds of my pale breasts making Him feel sorry and not capable of escaping, the Elephant, huge like a mountain, who shattered the ramparts of southern Laṅkā so as to destroy Rāvaṇa, who trod on the three worlds, and who steered the chariot to destruction in the Bhārata war fought by the famed chiefs, so as to make my limbs gratified.

## purport

In the two preceding pāsurams, the heroine sent messengers to the Lord, hoping to convey the depth of her longing. Various auspicious signs seemed to indicate that the Lord would respond favorably and come to her. Yet, as she reflected further, a troubling thought arose in her mind. Even if the Lord were to come and grant her the joy of union, would it not eventually be followed by another separation? Having already experienced the unbearable pain of parting from Him, she feared that any future union might only lead to renewed sorrow. Consequently, she resolved that when He came, she would not respond to His advances as before. Instead, she would end her life in His presence and thereby put an end to the endless cycle of union and separation.

Though these thoughts remained hidden within her heart, their effects became visible to those around her. The companion observed unmistakable signs of her distress. The heroine had become indifferent to food, dress, adornment, and all the ordinary concerns of life. Her entire being was consumed by thoughts of the Lord, and her outward neglect revealed the turmoil within.

Having understood her condition, the companion attempts to counsel her. She reminds the heroine that Bhagavān is not an ordinary lover towards whom one may behave like a disappointed maiden. He is the Supreme Lord, the ruler of all worlds and the controller of all beings. Moreover, His compassion towards those who seek Him is boundless. Did He not destroy Laṅkā for the sake of Sītā? Did He not orchestrate the events of the Mahābhārata to uphold the honour and vows of Draupadī? These examples demonstrate that the Lord never abandons those who place their trust in Him.

The companion further warns that resistance to the Lord is ultimately futile. Just as a wild elephant can effortlessly destroy barriers placed before it, the Lord can easily overcome any defenses the heroine might erect. The same Lord who shattered the mighty ramparts of Laṅkā can surely dissolve the resolve of a single devotee. The companion recalls the experience of Parāṅkuśa Nāyaki in Tiruvāymoḻi, where Nammāḻvār, assuming the role of a heroine, attempted to hide from the Lord but was ultimately won over by the irresistible force of His divine personality.

Yet the heroine remains steadfast in her resolve. She declares that however great and powerful the Lord may be, she intends to bind Him to herself, just as one would tether a wild elephant to a strong pillar. Having endured such prolonged suffering on His account, she desires that He should remain with her and witness the consequences of the pain He has caused. She imagines that, seeing her condition, He will feel remorse for the distress she has undergone.

The heroine's longing has become so intense that even her body bears the marks of separation. Āḻvār compares her weakened limbs to the hungry children of a householder during a famine, languishing from lack of nourishment. The sight of the Lord would satisfy and revive them, just as food relieves the suffering of the starving. Though her determination to bind the Lord may seem impossible, she continues to cherish this thought. The very impossibility of the desire only reveals the depth of her love, for in the world of devotion, longing often dares to imagine what reason declares unattainable.
