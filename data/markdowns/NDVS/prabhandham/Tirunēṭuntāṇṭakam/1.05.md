## english_scripts

## meaning

One leg stopped short of sweeping the Earth and the waters with a single charming step. The other leg, beyond the imagination of the fortunate Mahābali, who beheld the Lord face to face, rose up from the sacrificial ground and penetrated the cosmic egg, reaching up to Brahmaloka beyond the vast heavens, the cool Moon, the Sun, and even the world of stars. He prostrates before the sacred feet, fresh as newly blossomed flowers, of his Father, who stood thus and took possession of all the worlds.

## purport

Āḻvār visualizes the Vāmana and Trivikrama incarnations in this pāsuram. Here, the relationship between the jīvātmā and Īśvara, namely that of servant and Master, is beautifully brought out. When Mahābali agreed to grant the dwarf brāhmaṇa a piece of land measuring three footsteps, the Lord revealed His true majesty and resolved to measure the entire universe with just two steps, thereby once again establishing His sovereignty over all creation.

With His first step, He encompassed the Earth, the waters, and all the worlds below. His second step expanded beyond all measure, reaching the very limits of the universe. At the boundary of Satyaloka, Brahmā worshipped those divine lotus feet with great reverence. It is said that Dharma Devī herself flowed as sacred water upon those feet, and from that divine contact the holy river Gaṅgā came into existence. Thus, those lotus feet became the source of one of the most sacred rivers revered throughout the worlds.

Āḻvār therefore offers his prostrations to those very feet that rose beyond the Moon, the Sun, and even Satyaloka; to those feet that bestowed eternal blessings upon Mahābali; and to those feet from which the sacred Gaṅgā emerged. The Lord's cosmic stride was not merely an act of measuring the worlds, but a revelation of His supreme lordship and boundless grace.

Through this contemplation, Āḻvār recalls that the same Trivikrama is the Supreme Īśvara, the ruler and owner of all existence. In contrast, all jīvātmās are eternally dependent upon Him and exist solely as His servants. By meditating upon the Trivikrama incarnation, Āḻvār reaffirms this fundamental truth of existence: that Bhagavān is the eternal Master, and the essential nature (*svarūpa*) of the jīvātmā is loving and eternal service to Him.
