## meaning

With teardrops trickling down her twin breasts, she laments saying, "The young Bull who felt greatly elated grazing the calves!" "My ripe fruit in Kaṇṇapuram surrounded by fragrant orchards!" "Kṛṣṇa who was pleased to dance to the assembled people!" "The chief at northern Tiruvēṅkaṭam!" "The Hero who destroyed the chief (Rāvaṇa) of Khara, Dūṣaṇa etc. whom you defeated!" "Lord who is standing at Tirunaraiyūr surrounded by flower gardens!" and "My dark-bodied life companion with dense hair!"

## purport

The joy experienced by the heroine in the previous pāsuram proves to be fleeting. The union with the Lord that she seemed to enjoy was not a physical reality but an experience born of intense longing, akin to a dream. For a brief moment, she felt as though she had attained the presence of her beloved, but when that experience faded and she became aware of her continued separation, her sorrow returned with even greater force.

Realizing that the happiness she experienced was only momentary, the heroine cries out loudly to the Lord. In her anguish, she dwells especially upon His role as the protector and refuge of those who depend upon Him. Having no one else to turn to, she appeals directly to Him, expecting the compassion and protection that are intrinsic to His nature.

The commentators compare her condition to that of Sītā in Aśokavana. When Hanumān delivered Rāma's Cūḍāmaṇi to her, she experienced immense joy. The jewel was not merely an ornament; it was a tangible reminder of Rāma's love and concern. Seeing it, Sītā felt as though she had come into direct contact with Rāma Himself. Overwhelmed with emotion, she first placed the jewel upon her head in reverence and then held it close to her bosom, as though seeking relief from the burning fever of separation.

Yet that joy, too, was temporary. Once the initial elation subsided and she became conscious of her continued separation from Rāma, her grief resurfaced. Unable to contain her anguish, she cried aloud, "O Rāma! O Lakṣmaṇa! O Sumitrā!" Her lament expressed both the depth of her love and the unbearable pain of separation.

The heroine's condition in this pāsuram closely mirrors that experience. The brief sense of union that had comforted her only serves to intensify her sorrow when it vanishes. The memory of the Lord's presence makes His absence feel even more painful. Therefore, she calls out to Him with renewed urgency, emphasizing His protective nature and seeking refuge in the One who alone has the power to relieve her suffering. Through this portrayal, Āḻvār vividly expresses the alternating states of joy and anguish that characterize the highest forms of devotional love, where even a fleeting remembrance of the Lord can both console and torment the devotee.
