## meaning

Even after seeing the Dame (Lakṣmī), with lovely breasts yet not fully developed, on the lovely chest of the Wondrous Lord, she laments, loses her decorum and sighs deep. She says, "Friends, shall we go prancing to lovely Śrīraṅgam?" She, begotten of me, does not listen to words spoken by me in the least. Eulogizing Tiruppēr and the cool town of Tirukkuḍandai, she has gone to plunge herself in the lovely lotus pond (to be in union with the Lord). My daughter has severed my company. Is your daughter also like this?

## purport

In the previous pāsuram, the heroine became so overwhelmed by her longing for the Lord that she cast aside all concern for feminine dignity and openly asked for directions to His abode. Having reached a state where worldly conventions no longer restrained her, she was determined to seek Him regardless of social expectations or personal reputation.

Anticipating the questions of the women and companions around her, the mother explains her position. They may well ask whether she had not warned her daughter that the Lord, being inseparably united with Mahālakṣmī and absorbed in Her divine charms, would not pay attention to another maiden seeking His affection. The mother replies that she did indeed offer such advice and attempted to dissuade her daughter. However, despite all her efforts, the heroine remained resolute and proceeded towards the Lord without heeding those warnings.

The expression *"umpoṇṇum anṭē"* carries the sense, "Is your daughter also like this?" Through these words, the mother turns to the women around her and wonders whether their daughters too exhibit such overwhelming devotion and disregard for worldly considerations when it comes to the Lord. The statement is both an expression of astonishment and an acknowledgment of the extraordinary nature of the heroine's love.

A deeper theological significance is also present here. Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār exhibits an exceptionally intimate identification of the Arcāvatāra with the transcendent Lord Himself. For him, the deity residing in the temple is not merely a representation or accessible manifestation of the Supreme; it is the very same Lord of Paramapada, complete with all His divine qualities, majesty, and grace. Consequently, the heroine's longing for the Arcā form is no different from longing for the Supreme Lord Himself.

This intense identification distinguishes Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār's devotional experience. Therefore, the mother's question may also be understood in a broader sense. Looking at the companions around her, she wonders whether their daughters, representing the experiences of the other Āḻvārs, have likewise become so completely captivated by the Arcāvatāra that they perceive no distinction between the Lord enshrined in the temple and the transcendent Lord of Vaikuṇṭha.

Thus, the pāsuram highlights both the irresistible attraction of the Lord's Arcā form and the extraordinary depth of Āḻvār's devotion. The heroine's actions, though appearing unconventional from a worldly perspective, arise from a vision in which the Lord of the temple and the Lord of the highest abode are one and the same, worthy of complete surrender and unwavering pursuit.
