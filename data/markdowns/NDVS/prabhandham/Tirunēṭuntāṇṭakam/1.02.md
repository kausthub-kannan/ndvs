## meaning

He stands in a divine form as the Trinity. Yet, when the celestials conceive of their forms differently, one (Brahmā's) is of the nature of gold, one (Śiva's) is of the nature of red-hot fire, and one (the Lord's) is of the nature of the great ocean. Upon examining these three forms along with their associated natures, it is seen that our Master is the One who created the Pañcabhūtas, namely Earth, Water, Fire, Air, and Ether (primary creation), and who stands pervading the innumerable and distinct orderly creations (secondary creation), which shine forth as a unified effulgence of love, bearing the nature of the clouds.

## purport

Āḻvār beautifully expounds the supremacy of Bhagavān (Paratattvam) in this passage. He first declares, "He stands in a divine form as the Trinity" (*pār uruvil nīr eri kāl viśumbum āgi*). Through this, Āḻvār conveys that Bhagavān Himself created Brahmā and Rudra and entered into them as their Antaryāmī to carry out the functions of creation and destruction respectively. Thus, the three fundamental activities of the universe—creation, preservation, and destruction—are ultimately performed by Bhagavān alone.

At first glance, it may appear that Āḻvār is equating all three members of the Trinity. However, he clarifies this in the following line. While Bhagavān enters Brahmā and Rudra as their Antaryāmī and empowers them to perform their respective functions, He also distinctly manifests as Viṣṇu. In this way, Āḻvār establishes both the Lord's indwelling presence within all beings and His unique, independent identity. Although these three forms appear different, Āḻvār speaks of Brahmā and Rudra in terms of their indwelling Self, namely Bhagavān Himself. Therefore, the One who truly possesses and governs all these forms is none other than our beloved Master.

He is the One who first brought forth the pañcabhūtas and subsequently carried out the secondary creation of all animate and inanimate entities. Yet His role does not end with creation alone. Having brought all beings into existence, He enters each of them as Antaryāmī, sustaining them from within and enabling all their activities. As their inner ruler and support, He continually provides the very life force and existence upon which they depend.
