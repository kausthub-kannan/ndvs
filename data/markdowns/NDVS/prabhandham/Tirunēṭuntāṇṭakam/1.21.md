## meaning

He came to this very place accompanied by Lakṣmaṇa and he stood before me. My dear Friend! His curly hair, black like collyrium and exuding fragrance, was flowing down at the back in strands. The shark-crocodile shaped ear ornaments were dangling sparkingly on both his sides. He had with Him the fiery bow ready to shoot. Palms were of the hue of lotuses. The lips were like lotus buds. The two eyes were like lotuses. The feet were also like lotus flowers. Even after seeing the nature of such a charming person, we were frightened that He might be an inaccessible Ishwara!

## purport

Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār is utterly captivated by the svarūpa of Bhagavān, especially in the divine form of Śrī Rāmacandra. In this pāsuram, Āḻvār assumes the mood of Parakāla Nāyaki and narrates her experience to her companion. She describes how the divine brothers, Śrī Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, are approaching her. The curly locks of Rāma's hair sway gracefully as He walks, a sight capable of enchanting even the Gandharvas and Apsaras. His Makara-kuṇḍalas swing gently on either side of His face, as though proclaiming, "Behold! He is the Supreme Being. He is the Puruṣārtha, the ultimate goal of our lives." In His hands rests a radiant bow, blazing with power and ever ready to destroy the Asuras and Daityas who oppose righteousness.

His eyes are especially captivating. Soft and expansive like the petals of a freshly blossomed lotus, they possess the power to attract and overwhelm even the most composed observer with divine ecstasy. Āḻvār praises those lotus eyes, *Aravinda-nayana*, in the same manner as the Purāṇas glorify them. His lips are tender and beautiful, resembling newly blossomed lotus buds. The two youthful brothers radiate extraordinary beauty and divine splendour. Though they appear outwardly as human princes, their attire, bearing, and effulgence unmistakably reveal that they transcend the realm of ordinary creation. Their very presence proclaims that they are beyond anything fashioned by Brahmā. The lotus-like feet of Rāma, which constitute the supreme goal of human existence, further reveal His true nature.

As Parakāla Nāyaki contemplates these features, she gradually realizes that these are no ordinary men. The recognition dawns upon her that the Supreme Lord Himself, the Īśvara who governs all existence, is walking towards her. At that moment, Āḻvār is overcome with awe and wonder. For an instant, he is struck by the realization that this divine person, who remains inaccessible even to Brahmā and Rudra, has nevertheless chosen to reveal Himself directly to him.

The beauty of this pāsuram lies in the harmonious blending of two profound divine qualities: *Sauśīlyam* and *Saulabhyam*. Sauśīlyam is the Lord's supreme majesty, by which the Sovereign of all worlds remains ever exalted, while Saulabhyam is His extraordinary accessibility, by which He makes Himself available to His devotees. Āḻvār experiences both simultaneously in the person of Śrī Rāma. Throughout His earthly life, Rāma identified Himself simply as Dāśarathi, the son of Daśaratha. Yet He was far more than an ideal human prince; He was Īśvara Himself, concealed beneath the appearance of humanity. He displayed unparalleled accessibility to devotees such as Hanumān and Vibhīṣaṇa, while revealing His sovereign majesty through the destruction of Rāvaṇa and the restoration of dharma.

Āḻvār's genius lies in presenting these two qualities together. Just as milk and honey, when blended, produce a sweetness greater than either possesses individually, the simultaneous experience of Saulabhyam and Sauśīlyam creates a devotional rasa of incomparable beauty. By portraying the Lord as both the approachable companion of His devotees and the majestic ruler of the universe, Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār serves a feast of divine experience. Through this exquisite combination, he enables devotees to relish the fullness of Bhagavān's nature, making this pāsuram a remarkable celebration of both His intimacy and His transcendence.
