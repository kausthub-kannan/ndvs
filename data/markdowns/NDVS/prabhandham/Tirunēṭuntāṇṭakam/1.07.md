## meaning

Mind! Let us go to worship lovely Kōvalūr, guarded by the chaste virgin (Durgā) residing in the Vindhyas, with fragrant tanks, broad streets and lotus ponds, where the chieftains of the hills do service, the town of the Lord who wielded the lovely axe (as Paraśurāma) annihilating the Chieftains (like Kārtavīryārjuna), and the Lord (as Kṛṣṇa) who defeated the army led by Subrahmaṇya who wielded the unique spear in the huge sea containing the mountains.

## purport

Because of his intense devotion, Āḻvār becomes concerned for the safety of the Lord, who stands before the world in all the beauty, tenderness, and charm of youthful splendour, as described in the previous pāsurams, amidst people of asuric disposition. Such is the nature of divine love that the devotee, forgetting the Lord's omnipotence, becomes anxious for His well-being and protection.

A similar sentiment is seen in the Rāmāyaṇa. When Vibhīṣaṇa approached Rāma on the seashore seeking refuge, Sugrīva strongly opposed accepting him. Since Vibhīṣaṇa had come from the hostile environment of Laṅkā, Sugrīva feared that he might intend harm to Rāma. In response, Rāma reassured him by explaining His unwavering vow to protect anyone who sought refuge in Him, His code of righteousness, and His incomparable prowess. In the same manner, Āḻvār is now made to realize the Lord's supreme ability to overcome all opposing forces, and this realization fills him with joy and reassurance.

The pāsuram alludes to two well-known episodes from the Purāṇas. According to the ancient legend, all mountains once possessed wings and moved freely through the skies. Their constant movement caused great disturbance to the worlds, prompting Indra to sever their wings. Only the mountain Maināka escaped this fate by concealing itself beneath the ocean. Later, Subrahmaṇya, also known as Skanda, the commander-in-chief of the celestial armies, discovered Maināka and cut off its wings with his spear.

Āḻvār then recalls an even greater demonstration of the Lord's supremacy. The same Skanda, together with his father Śiva, came to the aid of Bāṇāsura when Kṛṣṇa waged war against him. Despite the combined strength of these mighty allies, they were effortlessly subdued by Kṛṣṇa. Through this remembrance, Āḻvār understands that the Lord who conquered such formidable opponents has nothing to fear from any hostile force. The pāsuram therefore transforms the devotee's anxiety into delight, as Āḻvār reflects upon the Lord's boundless power, which remains ever ready to protect both Himself and His devotees.
