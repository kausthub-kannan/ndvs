## meaning

Obedient mind! We can live worthily in the company of the Nityasūris if we can forever, without interruption, eulogize through the Tirumantra that Protector (mantrāṁ trāyate iti mantraḥ, “the one who protects the chanter”), who is the originator of Indra and Brahmā (in secondary creation); who caused the five elements, namely Earth, Wind, Fire, Water, and Ether (in primary creation); who is accurately revealed in the Tamil Prabandham and the Sanskrit scriptures; who is the inner controller of everything in the four directions; who ordains the Moon and the Sun; who is the Immaculate One who, though dwelling in their hearts, cannot be understood even by the gods; and who is glorified in the Upaniṣads, the concluding portion of the sages' scriptures.

## purport

This pāsuram glorifies the Tirumantram, the sacred Aṣṭākṣarī mantra. Similar to the teachings found in Tiruvāymoḻi, Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār instructs the mind to contemplate the Supreme Lord through the recitation and meditation of the Tirumantram. The Nārāyaṇopaniṣad says the following about the Aṣṭākṣarī mantra:

&lt;verse&gt;om namo nārāyaṇāyēti ma̍ntrōpā̠sakaḥ |\
vaikuṇṭhabhuvanalōka̍-ṅgami̠ṣyati ।&lt;/verse&gt;

This states that one who performs upāsanā on this sacred mantra ultimately attains Vaikuṇṭha, the Supreme Abode from which the jīvātmā never returns to the cycle of worldly existence. Āḻvār echoes the same truth in this pāsuram when he urges, "O mind, recite the Tirumantram so that we may dwell in the company of the Nityasūris, the eternal servants of the Lord in Vaikuṇṭha."

Addressing his own mind, and thereby instructing all devotees, Āḻvār asks it to meditate upon Bhagavān, the source of even Brahmā and Rudra. This teaching is once again affirmed in the Nārāyaṇopaniṣad as follows:

&lt;verse&gt;nā̠rā̠ya̠ṇādbra̍hmā jā̠yatē ।\
nā̠rā̠ya̠ṇādru̍drō jā̠yatē।&lt;/verse&gt;

This declares that Brahmā and Rudra emerged from Nārāyaṇa. The pāsuram thus serves as a clear demonstration of how the Divya Prabandham embodies the very essence of Vedānta. This same Nārāyaṇa is the creator of the five great elements, the Pañcabhūtas, which constitute the material universe. Āḻvār further proclaims that this Supreme Lord is glorified both by the Vedas, the repository of Sanskrit revelation, and by the Prabandha Mālai, the treasure of Tamil revelation.

Āḻvār also affirms that the Moon, the Sun, and all the directions function entirely under the Lord's sovereignty. The Taittirīya Upaniṣad states the same truth:

&lt;verse&gt;bhī̠ṣā-'smā̠dvāta̍ḥ pavatē । bhī̠ṣōdē̍ti̠ sūrya̍ḥ । bhīṣā-'smādagni̍śchēndra̠ścha । mṛtyurdhāvati pañcha̍ma iti ।&lt;/verse&gt;

This means that the wind blows, the sun shines, Agni and Indra perform their respective functions, and even Death itself carries out its duty out of reverential awe for Brahman. That same Brahman remains beyond the comprehension of even the devas. He cannot be fully grasped by the mind or expressed through ordinary thought, a truth echoed in the Upaniṣadic statement *aprāpya manasā saha*. Thus, the theological vision presented in this pāsuram closely mirrors the teachings of the Taittirīya Upaniṣad. Through his divine insight, Āḻvār exhorts all seekers to receive the Tirumantram through proper initiation from an Ācārya, constantly remember the Lord through its recitation, attain His eternal abode, and abide forever in unending bliss.
