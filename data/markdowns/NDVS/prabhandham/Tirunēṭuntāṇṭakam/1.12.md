## meaning

With her heart melting and eyes shedding tears, she stands languished. She sighs deeply. She has not known food or sleep. She says, "The perfectly lovely Lord sleeping on the poison-emitting Ādiśeṣa!" She says, "The youthful Lord at Tiruvāli with fields full of lovely fragrant flowers!" Again, bringing to mind the flying banner with Garuḍa of lovely feathers, she says, "Friends, shall we go to lovely Tiruvāraṅgam?" O! My sins! Having got a girl who is not under my control, I got censured by the people of this sprawling world.

## purport

In the previous pāsuram, the heroine had become so overwhelmed by her love for the Lord that she lost awareness of herself and her surroundings. However, when the soothsayer uttered the words "the sea-hued Lord," she regained a measure of consciousness and immediately burst into lamentation, as described in this pāsuram. The mention of the Lord was enough to awaken her from her state of inward absorption and intensify the pain of separation that already consumed her heart.

When the matronly woman complains that she stands censured by the people, she is not criticizing the heroine's conduct. Rather, she laments her own inability to find a remedy for the heroine's condition. Observers naturally expect a mother to comfort and restore her daughter, but finding herself powerless before an affliction born of divine love, she becomes the object of criticism and concern. Her grief arises not from embarrassment, but from helplessness.

This pāsuram explores more deeply the experience of *viraha*, the anguish of separation in devotional love. The heroine's suffering symbolizes the longing of the individual soul for union with Bhagavān. Having once tasted the sweetness of His presence, the soul finds separation unbearable and becomes incapable of taking delight in anything else. The intensity of this longing is presented not as a weakness, but as the natural consequence of profound devotion.

The reference to the "sea-hued Lord" evokes the image of Lord Viṣṇu, whose divine complexion is traditionally compared to the deep blue of the ocean. The very mention of this description stirs the heroine's heart, illustrating how even the slightest reminder of the Lord can awaken powerful emotions in a true devotee. For one whose mind is wholly absorbed in Bhagavān, a single word, image, or memory is sufficient to rekindle both joy and sorrow.

The pāsuram also beautifully illustrates *mādhurya bhāva*, the devotional mood in which the soul relates to the Lord as a beloved. In this mode of devotion, the intensity, exclusiveness, and purity associated with human love are employed to express the soul's longing for the Divine. Through the voice of the heroine, Āḻvār conveys the depth of his own devotion, portraying a love so complete that separation from the Lord becomes the soul's greatest suffering and remembrance of Him its only solace.
