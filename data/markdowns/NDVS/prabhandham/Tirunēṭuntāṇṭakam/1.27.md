## english_scripts

śeṅgāla maḍa nārāy ! inṟē śenṟu
tiru kkaṇṇaburam pukken śeṅgaṇ mālukku
en kādal en tuṇaivarkkuraitti āgil
idu oppademakkinbam illai
nāḻum paiṅgānam īdellām unadē āga
pazana mīn kavarnduṇṇa ttaruvan
tandāl iṅgē vandinidirundun peḍaiyum nīyum
iru nilattil inidinbam eydalāmē

## meaning

Red-footed young crane! There is nothing that pleases me more than that you start right today, reach Tirukaṇṇapuram and put in a word about my love to my life-companion, the red-eyed Lord who is captivated by His devotees. I will allow you to peck the fish in the tank forever, considering all of this verdant sea-shore as yours. Then you and your female companion can come and stay with pleasure. You can indeed attain supreme bliss attainable in both the worlds.

## purport

The impatience born of intense longing does not allow the heroine to wait for the outcome of the bee's mission. Separation from the Lord has made every moment feel like an age, and she finds herself unable to remain idle until the messenger returns. Such is the nature of divine love that even the slightest delay becomes unbearable to the devotee.

Consequently, when she notices a crane nearby, she immediately chooses it as yet another messenger and sends it to the Lord. Her action reflects the urgency of her condition. She is unwilling to rely upon a single emissary when her entire being longs for reunion with Bhagavān. The commentators compare this to Śrī Rāma's efforts during His search for Sītā. Not content with a single attempt, Rāma dispatched numerous groups of monkeys in different directions to ensure that every possibility was explored. In a similar manner, the heroine sends messenger after messenger, hoping that one of them will quickly bring her beloved Lord back to her.

The phrase *"Tirukaṇṇapuram pukku"* receives a particularly beautiful interpretation. Earlier, the heroine had sent the bee as her emissary to seek out the Lord. The commentators imagine that the bee would have successfully met the Lord halfway at Aḻundūr and conveyed the heroine's message. Upon hearing of her condition, the compassionate Lord would naturally begin His journey towards her.

Thus, when the heroine now speaks of Tirukaṇṇapuram, it is understood that the Lord and the bee-messenger are already travelling together on their way to meet her, passing through Tirukaṇṇapuram. The mention of that sacred place therefore arises from her hopeful anticipation that the Lord has already begun His return journey.

This interpretation beautifully reveals the confidence underlying the heroine's sorrow. Although outwardly she appears overwhelmed by separation, inwardly she remains convinced of the Lord's compassion. She believes that once He learns of her suffering, He cannot remain indifferent. Just as she cannot bear separation from Him, He too cannot remain unmoved by the distress of a devotee who belongs exclusively to Him.

The pāsuram therefore combines longing with hope. The heroine's decision to send the crane demonstrates the impatience of love, while her expectation that the Lord is already on His way reveals her unwavering faith in His mercy. Even in the midst of separation, her heart remains sustained by the conviction that Bhagavān will not abandon His devotee and that every messenger sent in search of Him ultimately hastens the moment of reunion.
