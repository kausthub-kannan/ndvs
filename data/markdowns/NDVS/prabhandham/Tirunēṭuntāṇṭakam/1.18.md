## english_scripts

kār vaṇṇam tirumēni kaṇṇum vāyum
kaittalamum aḍi iṇaiyum kamala vaṇṇam
pār vaṇṇa maḍa maṅgai pattar pittar
pani malar mēl pāvaikku ppāvam śeydēn
ēr vaṇṇa en pēdai en śol kēḻāḻ
em perumān tiruvaraṅgam eṅgē ennum
nīr vaṇṇan nīr malaikkē pōvēn ennum
iduvanṟō niṟai azindār niṟkumāṟē

## meaning

The sacred body of the Lord is of the colour of clouds. His eyes, lips, palms and two feet are of the colour of lotuses. He is devoted to the ever-youthful Dame Earth, who shines in the worlds. He is infatuated by the Dame (Lakṣmī) on the cool lotus. I committed a sin (towards such a Lord). My daughter of lovely complexion does not heed my words. She says, "Which way is the Lord's Śrīraṅgam?" Again, she says, "I will go to Tirunīrmalai of Lord Nīrvaṇṇan (the name of the Lord there)." Is this not the fate of those who disregard their feminine dignity?

## purport

In the previous pāsuram, the mother attempted to counsel the heroine, telling her that such conduct was not in keeping with the traditions and dignity of their family. However, the heroine remains unmoved by these arguments. Far from moderating her behaviour, she becomes even more determined in her pursuit of the Lord. In this pāsuram, her longing intensifies to the point that she resolves to go personally to Śrīraṅgam and Tirunīrmalai in search of Him.

Observing this, the mother begins to regret her earlier advice. She realizes that she had committed a grave mistake by attempting to restrain a devotion that was directed towards the highest possible goal. What she had viewed as excessive behaviour now appears to her as the natural expression of an exalted spiritual state. Instead of correcting her daughter, she now understands that the fault lay in her own inability to appreciate the greatness of that devotion.

As explained in the introduction to *Periya Tirumoḻi*, the Lord revealed the essential nature of the soul, namely its eternal subservience to Him, simply through the manifestation of His incomparable beauty. Without elaborate instruction, the splendour of His divine form itself awakened in Āḻvār the realization of his true nature and purpose. In the present pāsuram, the Lord once again displays that same beauty, reminding Āḻvār of his ultimate goal (*puruṣārtha*), which is to attain His divine feet and render eternal service there.

The service envisioned by Āḻvār is not motivated by personal gain or self-interest. Rather, it is identical to the service rendered by the Lord's own divine consorts, Śrī and Bhū Devī. It is selfless, loving, and entirely centred upon the Lord's pleasure. Realizing that her daughter aspires to such a lofty ideal, the mother ceases to be embarrassed by her behaviour and instead becomes proud of her devotion.

The scriptures speak of various human goals (*puruṣārthas*), including prosperity, righteous living (*dharma*), social order, and the preservation of personal and familial honour. These are all valid pursuits within worldly life and have their own place in the scriptural scheme. The mother's earlier advice was rooted in concern for such lower-order goals, particularly family reputation and feminine dignity.

Yet the heroine has transcended these concerns. Her aspiration is directed solely towards the highest *puruṣārtha*, namely eternal service to Bhagavān in His presence, culminating in the bliss of Mokṣa. In comparison with this supreme goal, all other attainments appear secondary. Recognizing this truth, the mother comes to appreciate the greatness of her daughter's devotion and the exalted destination towards which it is directed.
