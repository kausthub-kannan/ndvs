## meaning

He sang in the unique rāga "Naivalam" and also made a few (suggestive) gestures pretending shyness to see me in my face. By then, my mind and eyes went out to rest at the feet of the Lord (I lost control over them to Him). I could no longer see (be aware of) my bangles and waist-belt. I could only see His prominent ear-ornaments and the four arms. When I asked Him "How far is my Lord's residence?", He said, "Is this not the lovely Tiruvaḻi?"

## purport

Parakāla Nāyaki realizes that the Lord has not come before her to display His supremacy or divine majesty, but rather to claim His beloved, namely herself. As she beholds Him, she is reminded of Kṛṣṇāvatāra, when He captivated the hearts of the Gopīs through the enchanting music of His flute. Now, before her very eyes, He stands singing in the Naivaḻam rāga. The unique quality of this rāga is that it profoundly stirs the hearts of both the singer and the listener. Such music should have completely overwhelmed her and driven her into ecstatic longing for the Lord. Yet, remembering the words of caution spoken by her mother, she strives to maintain control over her thoughts and senses.

The Lord, however, does not cease His efforts. Though He is *Svatantran*, absolutely independent and subject to none, He begins to offer subtle hints of His affection. In His divine playfulness, He even appears bashful, turning His gaze towards the sky as though concealing the advances He is making towards her. Through these gentle gestures, the Lord gradually melts the resistance that Parakāla Nāyaki had attempted to maintain.

At last, her composure completely dissolves. The notions of "I" and "mine" vanish from her mind. Her individuality is overwhelmed by the force of divine love, and she becomes incapable of bearing separation from Him any longer. Having lost all attachment to herself and everything connected with her, she longs only for union with the Lord.

Unable to contain her yearning, she asks Him where He resides. Her question is not motivated merely by curiosity. She knows that wherever the Lord chooses to dwell becomes sanctified by the presence of His devotees, and therefore His abode itself becomes an object of longing. The place where He resides is naturally filled with Bhāgavatas, divine service, and the constant celebration of His glories.

As the Lord begins to enumerate His various abodes, extending even to the supreme realm of Paramapada, Parakāla Nāyaki listens with intense eagerness. Yet her heart is fixed upon one sacred place in particular. Therefore, interrupting His description, she asks anxiously, "Is Tiruvāli among them?" Her question reveals the depth of her attachment to that Divya Deśam, for to her, any place associated with the Lord becomes infinitely precious. More importantly, it reflects her desire to remain wherever He is present, whether in this world or beyond it.

Thus, the pāsuram beautifully portrays the gradual dissolution of the soul's individuality in the presence of Bhagavān. The Lord's sweetness, accessibility, and loving initiative draw Parakāla Nāyaki beyond all self-awareness until her sole concern becomes the place where He resides and the opportunity to remain forever in His presence.
