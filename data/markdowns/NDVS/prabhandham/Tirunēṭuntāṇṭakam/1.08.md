## meaning

Lord Nīrakam (of the nature of water)! Lord at the top on the lofty mountain (Tirumalai)! Lord pleasing like (full) moon shining in a small place (Nilaṭiṭiṅgal Tuṇḍan)! Lord Uraga in Kāñcī! Lord reposing at Vēṅka near the lovely stream (Lord Yathōktakāri)! Lord in the hearts of those who meditate on you! Lord at Kāraga eulogized by the world! Lord like water-laden cloud (at Karvāṇam)! Deceitful Lord! Lord at Tiruppērai on the southern side of the highly cherished and lovely Kāvēri river! Lord established in my mind without parting ever! The lofty Lord! I have adorned your sacred feet.

## purport

Āḻvār was unable to physically experience the divine beauty and glorious qualities of the Lord that he had envisioned in the preceding pāsurams. As this composition belongs to the final period of his life, he would have been advanced in age and no longer possessed the strength required to undertake such a journey. Realizing his own limitations, he turns directly to the Lord, knowing that Bhagavān is not only the ultimate goal (*upeya*) but also the means (*upāya*) to attain that goal.

With this understanding, Āḻvār appeals to the Lord through the sentiment expressed in *kūvuḍal varuḍal ceyyāy*—"Either call me to You or come to me Yourself." This is an appeal founded entirely upon divine grace (*Tiruvaruḷ*). The devotee recognizes that his own efforts are insufficient and therefore seeks the Lord's intervention, whether by drawing him into His presence or by personally granting him the desired vision.

One may wonder why Āḻvār addresses the Lord in various sacred abodes when his longing is specifically to behold Him at Tirukkōvaḷūr. The answer lies in the Lord's all-pervading presence and His equal manifestation in every Divya Deśam. Just as the Sun illuminates the entire world uniformly while remaining one and the same, the Lord graciously resides in all the places that He has chosen as His abodes. Though the forms may appear in different locations, the Lord who dwells within them is one and indivisible.

Therefore, Āḻvār understands that his plea will reach the Lord regardless of the particular shrine to which it is addressed. Whether he prays at Tirukkōvaḷūr or at any other sacred abode, the same compassionate Lord hears his appeal. This conviction deepens his dependence upon divine grace, for he knows that the Lord who resides everywhere is fully capable of responding to the yearning of His devotee and granting the longed-for union.
