## meaning

Can it be said about this girl anything other than that she is a Nityasūri come on this earth who can eulogize the Lord of great glories, such as His destroying (through Hanumān) by virulent fire the bewildered Laṅkā in the south in the presence of Sītā thereby destroying the sword-wielding Rāvaṇa the famed charioteer, His excellence in breaking into the fortress in the turbulent sea and going to cut the thousand arms of the fight-worthy Bāṇa, and His mastery of the worlds by pulling out (as Varāha), swallowing (in pralaya), bringing forth (in creation), striding (as Trivikrama) and protecting (as Viṣṇu of the Trinity)

## purport

After repeatedly offering advice that proved futile, the mother now undergoes a change of heart. She no longer views her daughter's conduct as excessive or inappropriate. Instead, she begins to recognize how extraordinarily fortunate the heroine is. The devotion that once appeared to her as a cause for concern is now understood to be a rare and exalted blessing. The heroine's heart is so completely absorbed in Bhagavān that she aspires not merely for His presence, but for the privilege of serving Him in the manner of His own divine consorts.

The mother realizes that such an aspiration is not born of ordinary desire but of the highest form of devotion. To seek the Lord with the same exclusiveness, intimacy, and selfless dedication displayed by Śrī and Bhū Devī is the mark of a soul that has fully awakened to its true nature. Far from being ashamed of her daughter, the mother now regards her with admiration and reverence.

At this stage, the heroine has effectively passed beyond the protective care of her earthly guardians. The mother understands that she can no longer provide what her daughter truly seeks. Neither family affection, social customs, nor worldly comforts can satisfy a heart that longs exclusively for Bhagavān. Having outgrown all lesser forms of dependence, the heroine now seeks refuge in the Lord alone.
