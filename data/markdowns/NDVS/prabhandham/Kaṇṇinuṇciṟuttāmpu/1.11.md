## meaning

Emperumān lovingly embraces all beings, especially His devoted servants. Nammāḻvār, in turn, cherishes those very devotees of the Lord. I, Madhurakavi Āḻvār, remain steadfast in my affection for Nammāḻvār. Those who recite these verses with devotion will attain the radiant abode of Śrī Vaikuṇṭham and dwell there eternally.

## purport

In the final pāsuram of *Kaṇṇinuṇ Ciṟuttāmbu*, Madhurakavi Āḻvār brings his Prabandham to a glorious conclusion by summarizing the entire philosophy of Ācārya-bhakti. Throughout these eleven pāsurams, he has demonstrated that Nammāḻvār is his sole refuge, master, and means to attain Bhagavān. In this concluding verse, he extends that blessing to all who follow the same path.

The pāsuram reveals a beautiful hierarchy of devotion. At the highest level stands Bhagavān, the loving Lord (*Anban*), who is dear to all devotees and who showers His grace upon the universe. Nammāḻvār stands as the foremost devotee of Bhagavān and the embodiment of *Bhāgavata-bhakti*. Having attained the Lord and become immersed in His service, he serves as the perfect guide for all jīvātmās. Madhurakavi Āḻvār then stands as the ideal disciple, demonstrating unwavering devotion towards his Ācārya. Finally come those devotees who place their trust in the teachings of Madhurakavi Āḻvār and walk the path shown by him. Thus, the flow of grace descends through the lineage of devotion, from Bhagavān to Nammāḻvār, from Nammāḻvār to Madhurakavi Āḻvār, and from Madhurakavi Āḻvār to all future devotees.

The central message of the pāsuram is that those who faithfully accept the teachings of Madhurakavi Āḻvār and cultivate devotion towards Nammāḻvār will surely attain the highest spiritual goal. The path of the Ācārya is presented not merely as one among many means, but as the most secure and compassionate route to Bhagavān.

Madhurakavi Āḻvār concludes by inviting devotees to behold *Vaikuṇṭha* through the famous expression *"Vaikuṇṭham Kaṇmiṉē."* This statement carries several layers of meaning. At one level, it refers to the eternal abode of Bhagavān, Paramapada, which is the destination of liberated jīvātmās. At another level, it teaches that wherever a true Ācārya resides, that place itself becomes comparable to Vaikuṇṭha, for it is there that divine knowledge, devotion, and grace are freely distributed. The presence of an Ācārya transforms the ordinary into the sacred.

The commentators further explain that *Vaikuṇṭham Kaṇmiṉē* points towards the ultimate attainment of the jīvātmā. After completing its journey through saṁsāra, the liberated jīvātmā reaches Paramapada and continues rendering eternal *kainkaryam*. For Madhurakavi Āḻvār, this eternal service is inseparable from service to the Ācārya, for the relationship established with the Ācārya does not cease upon liberation but continues forever.

Periyavāccān Piḷḷai offers a particularly beautiful interpretation. He explains that while Āḻvār Tirunagari in this world is shared by Bhagavān and Nammāḻvār, Paramapada may be viewed as the realm where Nammāḻvār's greatness shines forth most brilliantly. Therefore, for those who have taken refuge in Nammāḻvār through Madhurakavi Āḻvār, Vaikuṇṭha becomes especially desirable because it is there that they may eternally remain in the company of their Ācārya and continue serving him without interruption.

The relationship between Bhagavān, Ācārya, and disciple may be understood through a simple analogy. Bhagavān is the source of all grace, like the source of electrical power. Nammāḻvār is connected directly to that source, while Madhurakavi Āḻvār remains firmly connected to Nammāḻvār. Those who hold on to Madhurakavi Āḻvār likewise become connected to that uninterrupted current of divine grace. One need not attempt to reach the source independently; by remaining attached to the lineage, the grace naturally flows.

This teaching beautifully echoes the words of Swāmi Vedānta Deśika, who praises the *tol vazhi*, the ancient and time-honoured path of the Ācāryas. The surest means of attaining Bhagavān is not self-reliance, scholarship, austerity, or personal effort alone, but humble dependence upon the grace of the Ācārya. Through the Ācārya, the meanings of the scriptures become clear. Through the Ācārya, devotion becomes firm. Through the Ācārya, surrender becomes possible. Through the Ācārya, Bhagavān becomes attainable.

Thus, Madhurakavi Āḻvār concludes *Kaṇṇinuṇ Ciṟuttāmbu* by glorifying the path of Ācārya-bhakti. The one who accepts Nammāḻvār as his refuge, follows the teachings transmitted through Madhurakavi Āḻvār, and remains steadfast in service to the Ācārya will surely attain the highest goal. Such a devotee will behold Vaikuṇṭha, enjoy the company of Bhagavān, the Nityasūris, and the Ācāryas, and engage forever in uninterrupted *kainkaryam*. This is the supreme blessing that Madhurakavi Āḻvār bestows upon all who study and cherish this divine Prabandham.
