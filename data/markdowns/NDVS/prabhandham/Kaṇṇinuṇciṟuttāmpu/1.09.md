## meaning

Nammāḻvār revealed to me the essence of the Vedas, treasured by great scholars, and firmly established that wisdom within my heart. Through his grace, I attained the exalted state of becoming his devoted servitor.

## purport

The Vedas possess immeasurable glory. They reveal truths that cannot be discovered through logic, speculation, or mere intellectual analysis. Bhagavān transcends the grasp of the mind and senses, and therefore knowledge of Him cannot be attained by independent effort alone. Only the Vedas, which originate from Bhagavān Himself, are capable of revealing His true nature, His auspicious qualities, the nature of the jīvātmā, and the means to attain Him. Those who have properly studied, understood, and realized the teachings of the Vedas are indeed worthy of the highest reverence.

Yet even the Vedas remain difficult to comprehend without the grace of an Ācārya. Their teachings are profound and often expressed in subtle and concealed forms. Without proper guidance, the essential meanings remain inaccessible even to the learned. It is only through the compassion of the Ācārya that the hidden truths of the Vedas become clear and capable of being put into practice.

Recognizing the difficulty faced by ordinary people, Nammāḻvār out of boundless compassion composed the Tiruvāymoḻi. The profound teachings that are scattered throughout the Vedas were gathered together and presented in sweet Tamil verses so that they could remain firmly established in the minds of devotees. What is difficult to grasp in the Vedas becomes natural and accessible through the pāsurams of Tiruvāymoḻi. Nammāḻvār not only explained the truths of the Vedas but also enabled devotees to internalize them and live according to them.

Madhurakavi Āḻvār, representing the condition of ordinary jīvātmās, marvels at this grace. He describes his own mind as being hard like stone, incapable of understanding lofty spiritual truths. Yet the pāsurams of Nammāḻvār penetrated even such a hardened mind and revealed the treasures hidden within the Vedas. Through Tiruvāymoḻi, he came to understand the true nature of the jīvātmā and its eternal relationship with Bhagavān. More importantly, these pāsurams immersed his mind in *Bhagavat-śeṣatvam*, the realization that the jīvātmā exists solely for the service and pleasure of Bhagavān.

Madhurakavi Āḻvār therefore declares that Śaṭhakōpan alone is truly qualified to sing the glories of Bhagavān. Who else possesses such purity of heart, such realization of divine truths, and such intimate experience of Bhagavān's auspicious qualities? Nammāḻvār alone was uniquely fitted for this sacred task. Through Tiruvāymoḻi, he revealed Bhagavān in a manner that no one else could. Yet what amazes Madhurakavi Āḻvār even more is Nammāḻvār's compassion. The very Āḻvār who was qualified to glorify Bhagavān in the highest manner did not remain distant and inaccessible. Instead, he accepted even an insignificant person such as Madhurakavi Āḻvār. Therefore, Nammāḻvār is not merely Śaṭhakōpan, the revealer of divine truths, but also Nambi, the complete and compassionate master who graciously accepted an unworthy disciple.

The greatest wonder, according to Madhurakavi Āḻvār, is the immediate efficacy of the Ācārya's grace. The moment a genuine desire arises to serve the Ācārya, the Ācārya responds with compassion and acceptance. Through his grace, the disciple is led towards surrender (*śaraṇāgati*) and spiritual transformation. The path that would otherwise require immense effort and countless qualifications becomes attainable through the mercy of the Ācārya.

Thus, Madhurakavi Āḻvār concludes that Nammāḻvār's compassion is unparalleled. The one who revealed the essence of the Vedas, established countless jīvātmās in Bhagavat-śeṣatvam, and accepted even the most unworthy among them as his own disciple remains his sole refuge. Therefore, he clings firmly to the feet of Nammāḻvār, convinced that the grace of the Ācārya is the surest means to attain Bhagavān and eternal service at His divine feet.
