## meaning

My Lord, Kaṇṇan Emperumān, the Supreme Being Himself, allowed Himself to be bound by thin little ropes in the hands of Yaśōdā. Yet sweeter than that divine sight, and more nectarean to my tongue, is the very act of chanting the name of Nammāḻvār, the glorious leader of Southern Thirukkuṟugūr. His name lingers on my tongue with bliss greater than any divine pastime of the Lord.

## purport

With exclusive devotion towards Nammāḻvār, his Ācārya, Madhurakavi Āḻvār composed the divine Prabandham *Kaṇṇinuṇ Ciṟuttāmbu*. Throughout this Prabandham, he establishes the principle of *Ācārya Śeṣatvam* to its highest extent. For Madhurakavi Āḻvār, Nammāḻvār is everything. His attachment to his Ācārya is so absolute that Nammāḻvār becomes dearer, closer, and more significant to him than even Bhagavān, the Sarvaśeṣi Himself. This is not a diminution of Bhagavān's greatness, but rather an expression of the supreme reverence accorded to the Ācārya, through whom Bhagavān is truly known and attained.

Madhurakavi Āḻvār begins the Prabandham by recalling Śrī Kṛṣṇa's celebrated Dāmodara līlā. Nammāḻvār, upon experiencing this divine event through his spiritual vision, became overwhelmed by the Lord's *Saulabhyam*. The Supreme Being, the ruler and controller of countless universes, allowed Himself to be bound by Yaśodā with a simple rope. The sweetness of this act so captivated Nammāḻvār that he remained immersed in divine ecstasy and unconscious to the external world for months. Therefore, Madhurakavi Āḻvār deliberately chooses this incident as the opening theme of his work.

The rope used by Yaśodā was a short cord tied together with many knots, yet it was insufficient to encircle the Lord. The Supreme Lord, who pervades all existence and transcends all limitations, cannot truly be measured or contained by anything within creation. Yet, out of affection for Yaśodā's boundless motherly love (*vātsalya*), Bhagavān Himself contracted His divine form and allowed Himself to be bound. The rope left marks upon His abdomen, earning Him the name Dāmodara. The Lord who binds the entire universe within His Māyā willingly submitted Himself to being bound by His devotee. The One who liberates all souls from the bondage of saṁsāra accepted bondage for the sake of love. What an astonishing mystery! What immeasurable compassion, accessibility, and affection shine forth in this divine pastime!

Yet Madhurakavi Āḻvār declares that his master Nammāḻvār, who experienced and revealed these divine truths, is even sweeter and more accessible to him than Bhagavān Himself. There was once a time when his heart was drawn directly towards Bhagavān. Now, however, he has surrendered completely to the sacred feet of Nammāḻvār, the exalted saint of Tirukkurugūr, lovingly known as *Teṉkurugūr Nambi*. Nammāḻvār is the very embodiment of *Kṛṣṇa-tṛṣṇā-bhakti*, an insatiable thirst for Kṛṣṇa and His divine qualities.

Such is Madhurakavi Āḻvār's devotion that merely uttering the name of his Ācārya fills him with indescribable joy. Every time he recites the name of Nammāḻvār, his tongue experiences a sweetness comparable to divine nectar. The name of the Ācārya becomes his sustenance, his delight, and his refuge.
