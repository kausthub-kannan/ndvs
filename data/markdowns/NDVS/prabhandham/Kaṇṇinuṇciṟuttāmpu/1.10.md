## meaning

O Nammāḻvār, who dwells in fertile Thirukkuṟugūr where cuckoos sing amidst flourishing groves! Though you seek no personal gain, you tirelessly uplift the people of the world, guiding them toward divine servitude through your words and example. It is toward such a compassionate master that I continually deepen my affection.

## purport

In this pāsuram, Madhurakavi Āḻvār provides a clear and concise instruction regarding the indispensable role of the Ācārya. It is the Ācārya alone who makes a jīvātmā realize its true nature. Without the grace of the Ācārya, how can one understand that one's real identity is not that of an independent being, but that of a servant of the servants of Bhagavān? *Bhāgavata-śeṣatvam* is the true svarūpa of the jīvātmā, while *Bhagavat-śeṣatvam* is its eternal and intrinsic nature. These truths, though repeatedly taught in the scriptures, become firmly established in the heart only through the compassionate guidance of the Ācārya.

The greatness of the Ācārya lies in the fact that he seeks nothing in return for this service. He gains no personal benefit by correcting, instructing, and uplifting others. His actions are motivated solely by compassion. Throughout the scriptures we find numerous examples of exalted personalities who instructed others without any selfish motive. Sītā Devī advised Rāvaṇa to abandon his sinful desire and return to the path of righteousness. Vibhīṣaṇa repeatedly counselled Rāvaṇa to release Sītā and avoid enmity with Śrī Rāma. Prahlāda instructed his father Hiraṇyakaśipu that Nārāyaṇa alone is the true protector, father, and master of all beings. None of them stood to gain personally from offering such advice. They spoke only out of compassion and a desire for the welfare of others.

The Āḻvārs and Ācāryas stand in the same tradition. They derive no benefit by correcting us, teaching us the truths of the scriptures, or directing us towards Bhagavān. Their sole purpose is the upliftment of jīvātmās. Even when faced with individuals who seem unwilling to change, like Rāvaṇa or Hiraṇyakaśipu, compassionate teachers continue to show the proper path and impart right knowledge. Whether the disciple is receptive or resistant, qualified or unqualified, the Ācārya never abandons his duty. He continues to bestow grace and guidance without discrimination.

Such is Nammāḻvār, the embodiment of all auspicious qualities. Born in sacred Tirukkurugūr, he appeared for the welfare of all jīvātmās. The holy land of Tirukkurugūr itself is celebrated for its beauty, where nature joins in devotion to Bhagavān. The birds, trees, rivers, and all living beings seem to participate in the divine atmosphere created by Nammāḻvār's presence and his songs glorifying Bhagavān. Through the pāsurams of Tiruvāymoḻi, he repeatedly called out to Bhagavān with intense longing, inviting Him to manifest for the benefit of devotees.

It is this Nammāḻvār who became the Ācārya of Madhurakavi Āḻvār. Reflecting upon the immeasurable compassion and greatness of his master, Madhurakavi Āḻvār desires only one thing: that his devotion towards the sacred lotus feet of Nammāḻvār should continue to grow without limit. He seeks neither worldly prosperity nor even the joys of heaven. His sole aspiration is to deepen his attachment to the Ācārya who revealed his true nature, corrected his misconceptions, and established him firmly in the path of eternal service to Bhagavān.
