inṟu toṭṭum\

## meaning

As the lord of Thirukkuṟugūr, my svāmi Nammāḻvār showered his boundless mercy upon me and inspired me to sing his glories. His compassion is unwavering, and he will never abandon me.

## purport

Immersed in the bliss of his Ācārya's grace, Madhurakavi Āḻvār continues to describe the unique greatness of Nammāḻvār. He reflects upon the distinction between the grace of Bhagavān and the grace of the Ācārya. Once a jīvātmā surrenders at the sacred feet of Nammāḻvār, that relationship continues forever. The disciple remains eternally engaged in the service of the Ācārya, both in this world and in the eternal realm.

Bhagavān, out of His absolute independence (*Svātantrya*), determines when a surrendered jīvātmā will attain Mokṣa and enter Paramapada. The timing rests entirely upon His divine will. The Ācārya's grace, however, manifests immediately and tangibly. From the very moment one takes refuge under the Ācārya, opportunities for service arise. In this material world, one serves the Ācārya through devotion, obedience, and remembrance. Upon attaining Paramapada, that service does not cease, for the relationship with the Ācārya is eternal.

Madhurakavi Āḻvār further reflects upon the accessibility of the Ācārya. Bhagavān is attained through His grace by those who possess the required spiritual maturity, knowledge, devotion, or surrender. The Ācārya, however, extends his compassion even to the most fallen. Nammāḻvār does not wait for qualification. Rather, he himself creates qualification within the disciple. He showers grace even upon those who consider themselves the lowest among the lowly. Such is the boundless mercy of the Ācārya.

Indeed, the devotion shown towards the Ācārya is itself a gift granted by the Ācārya. Left to themselves, jīvātmās wander endlessly in saṁsāra. It is the Ācārya who redirects their lives, implants devotion within their hearts, and teaches them the path leading to Bhagavān. Therefore, the true devotees of the Ācārya cherish service to him above all else. Whether in this world or in Paramapada, their greatest joy lies in serving the one who revealed Bhagavān to them.

Nammāḻvār, the glorious Tirukkurugūr Nambi, is thus celebrated as the embodiment of grace. His compassion does not stand apart from Bhagavān's compassion; rather, it carries and conveys it. Just as a pond contains water drawn from the vast ocean and makes it accessible to those nearby, the Ācārya makes the immeasurable grace of Bhagavān accessible to ordinary jīvātmās.

The greatness of the Ācārya is further demonstrated by Bhagavān Himself. Was it not Bhagavān who appeared as Nara-Nārāyaṇa Ṛṣi and instructed the sacred truths of the Aṣṭākṣarī? Was it not Bhagavān who became the Gītācārya and taught the Bhagavad Gītā to Arjuna? The Lord Himself repeatedly assumes the role of an Ācārya for the upliftment of the world. Such is the exalted nature of the Ācārya's position.

Madhurakavi Āḻvār's praise of Nammāḻvār should not be misunderstood as diminishing the ontological supremacy of Bhagavān. Bhagavān alone remains the Supreme Reality, the ultimate goal, and the master of all. Rather, Madhurakavi Āḻvār is emphasizing the indispensable role of the Ācārya. Without the guidance and grace of the Ācārya, attainment of Bhagavān is impossible. Therefore, he celebrates the greatness of Nammāḻvār with unsurpassed enthusiasm and gratitude.

The grace of the Ācārya is visible and transformative. One can clearly observe the difference between a person before receiving Ācārya-anugraha and after receiving it. The mind becomes purified, conduct becomes refined, devotion becomes steady, and life itself acquires a divine purpose. This transformation is the living testimony to the power of the Ācārya's compassion. Thus, Madhurakavi Āḻvār remains forever immersed in gratitude towards Nammāḻvār, whose grace transformed his life and directed him towards eternal service to Bhagavān.
