## meaning

Those learned in the four Vedas and renowned for their noble deeds rejected me, considering me lowly and insignificant. But Nammāḻvār accepted me as his own, becoming both my mother and my father. He alone is my refuge, protector, and lord.

## purport

There are great and compassionate personalities who overlook countless faults and focus instead upon a single auspicious quality. Even if a person is burdened with many shortcomings, one noble trait is sufficient for them to shower their grace. Such is their greatness. Just as Gajendra, standing amidst muddy waters, noticed the beauty of a single lotus and desired to offer it to Bhagavān, these exalted beings seek out whatever is good and worthy in others rather than dwelling upon their defects.

There are also those whose compassion extends even towards those who have wronged them. Prahlāda desired forgiveness and welfare for his father Hiraṇyakaśipu, despite the immense suffering inflicted upon him. Sītā Devī, while enduring hardship in Aśokavana, showed kindness towards Trijaṭā and her companions. Similarly, Kūrattāzhvān prayed for the liberation of Nāḷūrān, the very minister whose actions resulted in the loss of his eyesight. Such incidents reveal the extraordinary mercy possessed by the great devotees of Bhagavān.

Madhurakavi Āḻvār, however, speaks from the depths of humility. Although he is adorned with many auspicious qualities, he represents the condition of the ordinary jīvātmā and regards himself as one filled with faults and deficiencies. He feels that even these great and compassionate personalities, who readily overlook the transgressions of others, might reject him because of his innumerable shortcomings.

Yet there is one refuge who never abandons anyone: Nammāḻvār. Madhurakavi Āḻvār proclaims that his Ācārya accepts him without examining whether he possesses virtues or faults. Nammāḻvār's compassion is not dependent upon qualification. He embraces those who seek refuge in him simply because it is his very nature to uplift and protect.

To Madhurakavi Āḻvār, Nammāḻvār fulfills every role necessary for the spiritual welfare of the jīvātmā. Like a mother, he offers unconditional affection and acceptance. Like a father, he imparts instruction and teaches the path of righteousness. Like a leader, he guides the disciple towards Bhagavān and protects him from spiritual downfall. Thus, Nammāḻvār becomes the complete benefactor of the jīvātmā.

The very nature of Śaṭhakōpan is to remove ignorance and destroy everything that obstructs the jīvātmā's progress towards Bhagavān. Therefore, Madhurakavi Āḻvār lovingly addresses him as Śaṭhakōpan. He is also *Nambi*, the perfect and complete one, adorned with every auspicious quality and lacking nothing. For Madhurakavi Āḻvār, Nammāḻvār has become everything. He is mother, (father, Ācārya, guide, protector, and master, hence called as nambi.

Having experienced such immeasurable grace, Madhurakavi Āḻvār finds no refuge apart from Nammāḻvār. The one who accepted him without considering his faults, instructed him in the truths of Bhagavān, and led him onto the path of eternal *kainkaryam* has become his all in all. Therefore, he remains firmly established at the sacred feet of Nammāḻvār, whom he regards as his greatest treasure, eternal refuge, and *sarvasvam*.
