## meaning

In the past, I was attached to worldly pleasures and driven by desire and delusion. Through the grace of Nammāḻvār, I was corrected, uplifted, and transformed into his devoted servitor. That very Nammāḻvār is the illustrious leader of Thirukkuṟugūr, the town adorned with golden mansions.

## purport

Madhurakavi Āḻvār continues to express profound humility while glorifying the transformative grace of Nammāḻvār. He reflects upon his previous condition and how completely his life was redirected by the compassion of his Ācārya. He describes himself as one who had stolen another's property. What property had he stolen, and from whom? The answer lies in the fundamental truth of the relationship between the jīvātmā and Bhagavān.

Every jīvātmā belongs exclusively to Bhagavān. He alone is the rightful owner, while all jīvātmās exist as His eternal servants. Yet, when a person identifies with the notions of "I" and "mine" instead of recognizing their dependence upon Him, they falsely appropriate what belongs to Bhagavān. In that sense, Madhurakavi Āḻvār regards himself as a thief. By forgetting his true nature as a servant and imagining independence, he had unlawfully claimed ownership over what belonged solely to the Lord.

Nor was this his only fault. He had once been attracted to worldly pleasures, material possessions, and sensory enjoyments. Like ordinary people immersed in saṁsāra, his mind wandered towards transient objects and fleeting delights. But that belonged to his past. Everything changed through the grace of Nammāḻvār.

Madhurakavi Āḻvār beautifully compares this transformation to discovering an unexpected treasure. From a distance, he saw a radiant golden lustre shining beyond a great wall. Naturally, he expected to find something valuable, perhaps a heap of precious gems. Yet, when he approached and entered, he discovered something far more valuable than anything he had imagined. He found Nammāḻvār. What are ordinary gems when compared to an Ācārya who reveals Bhagavān and grants the wealth of eternal service? The treasure he discovered surpassed all worldly riches and even exceeded his highest expectations.

Such is the greatness of Nammāḻvār that he was prepared to grant even Vaikuṇṭha itself to Madhurakavi Āḻvār. This raises an astonishing question. How can one who considers himself a thief, burdened with faults and shortcomings, become worthy of such a reward? The answer lies entirely in the boundless compassion of the Ācārya. Nammāḻvār did not scrutinize his defects, nor did he demand qualifications. Instead, he accepted him exactly as he was and resolved to uplift him.

Through his grace, Nammāḻvār transformed Madhurakavi Āḻvār's entire outlook on life. The pride born of ego and possessiveness was removed. In place of self-centredness arose the realization that he was not an independent being but a servant. More specifically, he came to understand himself as a servant of his Ācārya, whose feet became his sole refuge and wealth.

Yet Madhurakavi Āḻvār marvels that Nammāḻvār's grace did not stop there. Accepting him despite all his shortcomings was itself extraordinary. Redirecting his life towards Bhagavān was an even greater blessing. But Nammāḻvār bestowed a favour surpassing all others: he accepted Madhurakavi Āḻvār as his own disciple. He became his Ācārya and took personal responsibility for his spiritual welfare.

For Madhurakavi Āḻvār, this is the pinnacle of grace and compassion. The one who was unworthy was accepted. The one who had forgotten his true nature was instructed. The one who wandered in worldly pursuits was guided towards eternal truth. Therefore, he stands overwhelmed with gratitude, seeing in Nammāḻvār not merely a teacher but the very embodiment of divine mercy, whose compassion transformed a fallen jīvātmā into a devoted servant.
