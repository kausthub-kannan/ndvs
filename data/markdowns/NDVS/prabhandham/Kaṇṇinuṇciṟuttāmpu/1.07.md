## meaning

Nammāḻvār, the illustrious son of Poṟkāri and known as Kārimāran, graciously accepted me into his service and erased the sins accumulated from beginningless time. I shall proclaim in all directions the greatness of his mercy and the glory of the divine Tamil pāsurams he composed.

## purport

Nammāḻvār, born to Kari and Uḍaiya Naṅgai, came to be known as Māraṉ and Māri Pirān. Through the divine outpouring of Tiruvāymoḻi, he transformed the direction of devotion for countless jīvātmās and established the path leading directly to Bhagavān. Therefore, Madhurakavi Āḻvār glorifies him as the one who bestows immeasurable benefit upon all, guiding them towards right knowledge and true devotion. In the overwhelming gratitude of Ācārya-bhakti, he regards Nammāḻvār's beneficence as even more immediate and meaningful to him than that of Bhagavān Himself, for it was through Nammāḻvār that Bhagavān became known and attainable.

Madhurakavi Āḻvār especially celebrates the grace-filled glance of his master. Nammāḻvār cast his compassionate eyes upon him and transformed his life forever. The greatness of the Ācārya's glance is beautifully described by Vedānta Deśika in *Subhāṣita Nīvī*: &lt;verse&gt; ekaiva guroḥ dṛṣṭir dvābhyām vāpi labhyate |\
Yan na labhyaṁ aṣṭabhiḥ, sahasreṇāpi kā ratiḥ || &lt;/verse&gt;

The meaning conveyed is that a single glance from the Ācārya possesses a salvific power that surpasses even the combined gaze of many divine eyes. Even if one were to combine the three eyes of Śiva, the eight eyes of Brahmā, and the thousand eyes of Indra, they would still not equal the transformative grace contained in a single compassionate glance from an Ācārya directed towards his disciple. The *kaṭākṣa* of the Ācārya is regarded as the supreme means for removing ignorance and bestowing divine grace.

Madhurakavi Āḻvār experiences this truth directly. By the grace of Nammāḻvār's compassionate glance alone, the ignorance accumulated from beginningless time is destroyed. The burden of karma, which had bound him to saṁsāra for countless births, begins to dissolve. Not only are past sins removed, but even those obstacles that hinder service to the Ācārya are gradually eliminated. Such is the power of Nammāḻvār's mercy.

Overwhelmed by gratitude, Madhurakavi Āḻvār resolves to dedicate his life to spreading the glory of his master. Just as messengers travel throughout a village announcing joyful news, he desires to proclaim the greatness of Nammāḻvār in all eight directions of the world. The divine pāsurams of Tiruvāymoḻi are like intoxicating honey, overflowing with sweetness, wisdom, and devotion. Having tasted that nectar himself, he wishes to share it with everyone.

Therefore, he vows to spread the teachings and hymns of Nammāḻvār to the fullest extent of his ability. The one to whom he has surrendered is Śaṭhakōpan, the great saint who overcame the influence of the *śaṭa-vāyu* and remained untouched by the delusions of Māyā. By conquering the forces that bind ordinary jīvātmās to saṁsāra, Nammāḻvār became the beacon who illuminates the path to Bhagavān for all generations.

Thus, Madhurakavi Āḻvār concludes with a heart overflowing with gratitude. The grace-filled glance of Nammāḻvār removed his ignorance, purified his life, and bestowed upon him the wealth of devotion. In return, he seeks only one service: to proclaim the sweetness of Tiruvāymoḻi and the greatness of Śaṭhakōpan throughout the world, so that countless other jīvātmās may likewise come under the shelter of his divine feet.
