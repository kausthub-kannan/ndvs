## meaning

Out of infinite compassion, Nammāḻvār composed the thousand pāsurams of Thiruvāymoḻi, presenting the very essence of the Vedas for the delight of the Lord’s devotees. This act of grace, through which profound divine truths were made accessible to all, surpasses every other blessing in this world.

## purport

The divine pāsurams of Nammāḻvār are especially relished by those who delight in the glories of Bhagavān. Periyavāccān Piḷḷai, in his commentary, explains that such devotees include the other Āḻvārs themselves, particularly Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār and the great devotees who recognized the immeasurable greatness of Nammāḻvār's compositions. Those whose hearts are immersed in Bhagavān naturally find themselves drawn to Tiruvāymoḻi, for it is nothing less than an ocean of His auspicious qualities, divine sports, and supreme truths.

Through the pāsurams of Tiruvāymoḻi, Nammāḻvār revealed the meanings of the Vedas in a manner accessible to all. The Vedas contain the highest truths concerning Bhagavān, the jīvātmā, and the means to attain Him. Yet their teachings are often presented in concise, symbolic, and deeply encoded language, making them difficult for ordinary people to comprehend. Their true meanings require extensive study, contemplation, and guidance from learned teachers.

Nammāḻvār, however, out of boundless compassion, took those profound truths and expressed them in the sweet language of Tamil. The essential teachings of the Vedas, which remain hidden beneath layers of mantra, upaniṣadic expression, flow openly and beautifully through the verses of Tiruvāymoḻi. What is difficult to grasp in the Vedas becomes clear and accessible through the grace-filled words of Nammāḻvār.

The Vedas themselves declare that their greatness lies in revealing Brahman, Bhagavān. As the scriptures affirm:  brahma̍ṇā̠ vāva sarvē̍ vē̠dā mahī̍yantē

The glory of the Vedas does not arise merely from their antiquity or sacredness, but because they reveal Bhagavān and impart knowledge of His true nature. That knowledge of Bhagavān is the hidden treasure contained within the Vedas. It is their essence, their purpose, and their highest teaching. Nammāḻvār brought forth this hidden treasure and distributed it freely to the world through Tiruvāymoḻi. By the grace bestowed upon him by Bhagavān Himself, he distilled the essence of the Vedas and poured it into the sacred language of Tamil. Therefore, Tiruvāymoḻi is celebrated as the *Drāviḍa Veda*, for it communicates the same eternal truths that are found in the Sanskrit Vedas while making them accessible to all seekers.

For this reason, Madhurakavi Āḻvār regards Nammāḻvār's grace as immeasurable. Bhagavān bestowed His grace upon Nammāḻvār, and Nammāḻvār, in turn, shared that grace with the entire world through his divine compositions. The treasure that remained hidden within the Vedas became available to all through the compassion of the Āḻvār. Periyavāccān Piḷḷai further makes a remarkable observation regarding this pāsuram. He explains that the greatness of Tiruvāymoḻi is not limited to this world alone. Even in Paramapada, the supreme abode of Bhagavān, the divine sweetness of Tiruvāymoḻi is celebrated. Its glory extends beyond the material realm and continues eternally. Such is its excellence that even Bhagavān Himself delights in hearing and enjoying the pāsurams of Nammāḻvār.

Thus, Madhurakavi Āḻvār glorifies Nammāḻvār as the one who unveiled the hidden treasure of the Vedas and offered it to all jīvātmās through Tiruvāymoḻi. The grace that made the inaccessible accessible, the hidden manifest, and the profound understandable is the grace of Nammāḻvār.
