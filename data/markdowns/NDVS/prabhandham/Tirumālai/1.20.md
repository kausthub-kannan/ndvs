## meaning

How can a devotee refrain from reveling in Your divine form? The magnificent form of Yours in Śrīraṅgam, reclining on the bed called Ādiśeṣa. That broad chest of Yours where Śrī Herself resides. Your Thirumēṉi, beautiful to behold like majestic mountains, with its emerald hue. How can a devotee resist, even after glimpsing those rosy lips, coral mouth, and the ever-captivating lotus-like eyes!

## purport

The Āḻvār, in response to saṃsārins questioning why he suffers in bhagavad viṣayam, causing his body to melt and eyes to shed tears, instead of joining them in enjoying the pleasures of saṃsāra, describes the beauty of the Lord. He states that this beauty is for bhaktas to enjoy, asking rhetorically, "How can one go away from that?" He notes that holier things take shelter at Śrīraṅgam. He observes that the environment of Śrīraṅgam is conducive for them not to be polluted by saṃsāra. He describes how the river Kāveri happily flows around Śrī Raṅganāthan. Āḻvār points out that over the water, the Lord has spread His Ādiśeṣan bed. He states that the Lord is engaged in yōganidrā. While seen in standing and sitting postures in many divyadeśams, here He is seen as an elephant resting on the ground. The Āḻvār suggests that seeing Him in such a beautiful posture transcends one somewhere, and that perhaps this beauty alone brings us towards Him, which is magical.

Āḻvār notes that with the presence of Mahālakṣmī, the Lord's broad chest has become a special part of His divine body. He compares the Lord's appearance to a flood of water, a cool sight for the eyes. He likens the Lord's shoulders to a float for bhaktas to grab and come up when they sink in the enjoyment of seeing other divine limbs. He describes the cool kaṭākṣam (divine glance) of the Lord's eyes, which does not change whether He sees Mahālakṣmī or Hiraṇyakaśipu. Āḻvār states that such a cool glance should not be compared even with the finest earthly things like cool gentle breeze and lotus flower, as these are good for some and terrible for others. Hence, he prefixes the adjective 'tūya' (pristine pure) to His glance. He notes that the divine mouth with reddish lips and rows of teeth attracts all sorts of people, whether they are jñānis or pāpis. He indicates the Lord's aiśvaryam by mentioning the crown. Up to this point, the Āḻvār has described the avayava śobhai (beauty of individual divine parts). By 'tejas' (divine bodily effulgence or brilliance), the Āḻvār describes the samudāya śobhai (overall splendor of Śrī Raṅganātha). Āḻvār rhetorically asks how bhaktas can leave away all such beauties, which are here only for their enjoyment. He states that the beauty of the Lord is for devotees to enjoy, and that they are unable to leave such wealth, even if they are carried away by it.
