## meaning

Oh! primeval supreme Being! Lord of the great city, Śrirangam! Oh sweet flow of bountiful compassion! Oh Lord of fresh youth (Śrī Kṛṣṇa) in which you lifted aloft on your little finger the mount Govardhana, for the Make of protecting the cowherds and Gokula (Āyarpāḍi) from the fury of Indra's torrential stony rain! Why are you going away without looking at me who am bound in the net of the doe eyed damsels? I do call You, Oh! supreme Lord!

## purport

Among the Āḻvārs, whenever they employ the term "maḻai" [rain], it invariably alludes to the hailstones that Indra [lord of celestial entities] showered when Kṛṣṇa lifted the Gōvardhan mountain, just as the word "āṉai" [elephant] always refers to Gajēndrāḻvāṉ [the elephant caught by the crocodile when it entered the pond to pluck a flower for emperumāṉ]. The Āḻvār now invokes the prevention of hailstones by lifting Gōvardhana mountain to illustrate his yearning to attain Him and overcome the sorrows of saṃsāra (materialistic realm). He recalls how the cowherds overcame the dangers posed by Indra by seeking refuge in Him. Just as the hungry call out to the wealthy, "Oh Lord! Please remove our hunger," the Āḻvār beseeches emperumāṉ, "Oh One who rids people of dangers! Please remove my peril."

The Śrīviṣṇu Purāṇam 5.11.13 states, "thathasthath gōkulam sarvam gōgōpījanasaṅkulam | athīvārththam harir dhṛṣṭvā maithrēyāchinthayath thadhā ||" (Oh Maitreya! Upon seeing the entire Gōkulam, full of cows and gōpīkās, in sorrow, [Kṛṣṇa] immediately began to ponder). Aṉṟu refers to the day when the calves, cows, and cowherds trembled at the sight of Indra's hailstones. The Āḻvār equates his present danger to that faced by the inhabitants of Gōkulam, beseeching emperumāṉ for protection from his shower of sorrow. When Indra commanded all clouds to open, they poured with such intensity that it resembled the great deluge. Seeing Indra's wrath for being denied food by Āyppādi, Kṛṣṇa, without time to summon His Sudarśana Chakra, lifted the nearest mountain. The Āḻvār infers that emperumāṉ cannot claim lack of suitable means to offer protection.

Having already used "aṉṟu" to denote a specific time, "muṉ" here signifies "before harm befell the cows." The Āḻvār emphasizes that emperumāṉ did not wait for harm to come to the cows and cowherds but protected them preemptively, as they had no other refuge. Āḷavandhār, in his Stōtra Ratnam 49, similarly beseeches, "avivēkaghanāndhathingmukhē bahudhā santhathadhu:kha varṣiṇi | bhagavan! bhavadhurthdhinē padha: skalitham māmavalōkayāchutha! ||"
The Āḻvār marvels at how Kṛṣṇa effortlessly lifted the mountain at merely seven years of age, as if it were a garland of flowers. He questions whether emperumāṉ's reluctance to protect him stems from a fear of fatigue.

Oh One of immense strength to lift a mountain! Alternatively, maindhaṉ indicates youthfulness, suggesting One who becomes young in spirit upon protecting His devotees. The Āḻvār laments that while protecting him benefits emperumāṉ, he must still call out for protection. Oh One who is like a river of sweetness! Even if You were not the protector but one who might chastise the devotee, Your beauty is such that none would forsake You. The Āḻvār likens emperumāṉ to a river rather than a sea, for river water proves useful for bathing on a hot day, washing one's face, or quenching thirst. Like a flowing river during floods, this loving river [emperumāṉ] gushes in, breaking the dam of unworthiness the Āḻvār had built around himself.

The Āḻvār, trapped in the net of women's fawn-like eyes, laments his suffering. These women, though devoid of genuine affection, feign devotion, ensnaring him with their deceptive gazes. He pleads, "ennai nōkkādhu oḻivadhē" – why do You not look upon me (protect me)? The Āḻvār's closeness to emperumāṉ and the latter's affability allow him to express such sadness. The Āḻvār reminds emperumāṉ of His responsibility as the creator, citing the Chāndōgya Upaniṣad 6.2.9, "thadhaikṣatha bahu syām prajāyēyēthi thath thējō'sṛjatha" (That Brahman took a vow "I will multiply myself" and created radiance).

The Āḻvār questions the need to seek protection at the origin of creation when emperumāṉ has already descended to Śrīraṅgam. He implores, "After You came to Śrīraṅgam to cultivate a taste for righteousness, and after I acquired that taste, should I lose it?" Even as the Āḻvār calls out, emperumāṉ remains silent, savoring the long-awaited sincere devotion. The efforts He had taken since time immemorial are now bearing fruit, and He wishes to hear the Āḻvār's earnest plea a while longer before responding.
