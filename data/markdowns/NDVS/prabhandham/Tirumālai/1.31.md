## english_scripts

tavattuḻār tammil allēn
tanam paḍaittāril allēn
uvartta nīr pōla
en tan uṭravarkkonṟum allēn
tuvartta śevvāyinārkkē
tuvakkaṟa tturiśan ānēn
avattamē piṟavi tandāy
araṅga mā nagaruḻānē !

## meaning

Oh Lord of the great temple town, Srirangam! I don't belong to any one group of the aspirants; nor do I belong to the wealthy class; I am, indeed, nothing worth the name even to my relatives, i.e., I am like brackish water (of slums); enticed by the rosy lips of damsels, I have become a knave. O Lord Ranganatha! You have merely given me a lease of life that I spend in vain.

## purport

The Āḻvār laments his spiritual inadequacy, confessing that he did not engage in tapas (penance) for attaining mokṣa. He cites the Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 6.4.22, which states that brahma jñānis seek to know Paramātmā through Vedic recitation, rituals, donations, and desireless penance. The Āḻvār admits he neither joined those who performed penance for mokṣa nor those who sought svarga (heaven). He even distanced himself from those who, upon realizing shortcomings in their penance, made atonements.

Regarding wealth, the Āḻvār confesses he was not among those who earned money for noble purposes, as prescribed in the Bhagavad Gītā 18.5 and 17.20. He acknowledges the Taittirīya Upaniṣad's directive to earn wealth even through questionable means if it's for hosting guests, but admits he failed to follow this path.

The Āḻvār expresses his inability to be of any use to his relatives, comparing himself to salty water that causes discomfort. He confesses to causing harm to his kin, bringing shame upon himself. Even towards women, whom he should have treated with affection, he behaved poorly, becoming a thief (thurisan) of their trust.

Addressing Raṅganātha, the Lord of Śrīraṅgam, the Āḻvār laments the apparent waste of his birth (avattamē piṟavi tandāy). He acknowledges that his karma led to his lowly state but doesn't blame the Lord, understanding that both his actions and inactions are under divine control. The Āḻvār alludes to the Bhagavad Gītā 10.10, recognizing that if the Lord chose to grant him good intelligence, nothing could prevent it.

Finally, the Āḻvār points out that despite the Lord's presence in Śrīraṅgam (Araṅga mā nagar) to guide those lost in worldly pursuits, this divine grace did not seem to fructify in his case. This poignant confession reflects the Āḻvār's deep introspection and his yearning for spiritual redemption.
