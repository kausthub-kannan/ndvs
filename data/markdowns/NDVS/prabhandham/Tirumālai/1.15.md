## meaning

The Lord Viṣṇu who has Garuḍa on His banner, is eternally real for those who acknowledge Him as real. But He is unreal to those who themselves are unreal, because of their atheistic attitude like me who am untruthful. The Lord Viṣṇu, viz. the Lord of the beautiful city Śrīraṅgam, reveals Himself to those who aspire for Ujjīvanam, cuts asunder all their doubts and grants the supreme knowledge that He is the only one supreme Person. Isn't it?

## purport

Āḻvār expresses the graceful nature of the Lord who grants divine knowledge to people who show unadulterated devotion towards him. When Gajendra offered him a lotus by calling him "Ādimūla," without a second's delay he appeared in front of the elephant to rescue him. Hence, he always listens to the calls of his pure devotees. Even to those who lack complete knowledge, he grants it to them slowly. He is compassionate to all his devotees, irrespective of the level of knowledge they have. Ultimately, he takes the responsibility that they attain him eventually. 

On the other hand are the foolish atheists who deny his existence. They will never be able to see his divine form. Because of their Tāmasic Guṇa, they see him as non-existent, which has occurred due to ignorance. Hence to those untruthful fools, he remains hidden. Āḻvār, practicing nikṛṣṭa bhakti, expresses himself as the lowest among personalities and tries to express his gratitude to the Lord for showing divine grace on him.
