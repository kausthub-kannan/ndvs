## english_scripts

tiruvaraṅgattuḻ ōṅgum
oḻi uḻār tāmē anṟē
tandaiyum tāyum āvār

ammavō ! koḍiyavāṟē !

## meaning

The supreme Lord of great splendour dwelling in the lofty shrine of Srirangam which is surrounded by the agitated muddy waters (of the holy river Kāvēri), indeed, is our father and mother. Would He not ward off my anguish, by showering His ineffable grace on me, after saying "This pitiable fellow is, indeed, my boy worthy of my blessings"? But, He has not yet blessed me. Alas! what a cruelty of fate!

## purport

As the flow of water in Kāvēri eases, it gains clarity. Yet, with increasing floods, it remains perpetually muddied. Similarly, this Āḻvār, like Kāvēri, is in constant agitation. Before attaining emperumāṉ, he frets, "I have not attained Him." Upon attainment, he becomes agitated due to his love for Him. This river too, flowing westward towards the temple, grows agitated in anticipation of seeing periya perumāḷ, and eastward, in sorrow at departing from Him. Thus, without distinction between chētana (Āḻvār) and achētana (river) entities, even those who walk near the temple experience this agitation.

Just as Nammāḻvār says in Thiruvāimoḻi 10.10.10 "adhaṉil periya eṉ avāvarach chūḻndhāy", periya perumāḷ envelops the Āḻvārs with His love. Likewise, Kāvēri envelops periya perumāḷ out of her love for Him. Parāśara Bhaṭṭar's Śrīraṅgarāja Stavam 1.21 eloquently describes Kāvēri's devotion: "dhugdhābdhir janakō jananīyahamiyam śrīrēva puthri vā śrīraṅgēśvara ēthadharhamiha kim kuryāmithvākulā | sañjaccāmara chandra chandana mahāmāṇikya mukthōthkarān kāvērī laharēekarairvidhadhathī paryēthi sā sēvyathām ||"

Just as it is challenging to navigate Kāvēri's floods, it is equally difficult to penetrate periya perumāḷ's resplendent radiance and admire His divine form. After entering Thiruvaraṅgam (Śrīraṅgam), even the brightness in Paramapadham diminished, like daylight. The radiance at Śrīraṅgam aids lowly saṃsārīs like night-time illumination. Emperumāṉ, amidst nityasūris, is like a sun among suns. But standing amidst saṃsārīs, who suffer from worldly sorrows, His radiance acquires dominance.

The Subālōpaniṣad 16 states: "māthā pithā bhrāthā nivāsaś śaraṇam suhruth gathir nārāyaṇa:" (Nārāyaṇa is mother, father, brother, dwelling place, means for attaining mōkṣa, friend, and the one attained in mōkṣa). The Āḻvār declares that for him, it is not Paramapadhanāthan, common to all; not Vyūha or Vibhava forms of perumāḷ, common to a few; not other Archāvatāra vigrahas common to other Āḻvārs; but only periya perumāḷ who is his father and mother. This exclusivity is emphasized by the term thāmē (He Himself). The Mahābhāratam Vana Parvam 192.56 ślōka reinforces this: "sarvēṣāmēva lōkānām pithā māthā cha mādhava: | gacchadhvamēnam śaraṇam śaraṇyam puruṣarṣabhā: ||"  Unlike the cowherds, there is no need for difficult tasks like mountain-lifting to protect me. The Āḻvār pleads, "Is it not enough if You glance at me with Your comforting eyes?" Bestowing favors upon me. Even when emperumāṉ is not glancing at him, the Āḻvār acknowledges past favors, beseeching Him for this one. The Āḻvār yearns for emperumāṉ to say, "He has something good in him to be shown my mercy. He is my little fellow." The Āḻvār feels that only if emperumāṉ, the master, addresses him thus, confirming their master-servitor relationship, will his servitorship endure. This echoes incidents in the lives of pūrvāchāryas, such as Maṇakkāl Nambi addressing Thirumagizhmārban as "my son" and Piḷḷaippiḷḷai Āḻvāṉ referring to Appiḷḷai Āḻvāṉ as "little brother" and "my little fellow." The Āḻvār exclaims in deep anguish, "When I cry out to Him in sorrow, should He not rise? How can He sleep even after hearing my voice? Has His divine heart become so hardened?" The word amma indicates profound distress.
