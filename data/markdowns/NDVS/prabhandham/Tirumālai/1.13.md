## english_scripts

eṟiyu nīr veṟi koḻ vēlai
mā nilattuyirgaḻ ellām
veṟi koḻ pūn tuḻava mālai
viṇṇavar kōnai ētta
aṟivilā maniśar ellām
araṅgam enṟazaippar āgil
poṟiyil vāz naragam ellām
pul ezundoziyum anṟē

## meaning

Men of high calibre — viz., those who reside in many dvīpas and many varṣas, surrounded by the tidal oceans and scented sea-shores — extol the supreme (Lord of Nityasūris) adorned with the garland of Tulasī with great fragrance. But those who lack the knowledge to praise the supreme Lord, even if they could just utter the name of the holy city Śrīraṅgam as 'Araṅgam', it is enough (for them to destroy their sins). Thus when all people get freed from sins, there won't be anyone to go to hell; and so, the hell will be bereft of any souls (to suffer for their sins), and thus there would be only grass growing; and later on, the hell itself would become extinct. Isn't it?

## purport

Āḷvār states that the seers and ṛṣis are able to praise the Lord who always wears the tulasī garland. Indeed, these are the Nityasūris who are serving the Lord eternally, singing his glories. The Viṣṇu Sūktam states the same as "tadviṣṇoḥ paramaṃ padaṃ sadā paśyanti sūrayaḥ." The supreme Lord blesses knowledge to the Ṛṣis and hence they are able to sing his glories. To people like us who lack such knowledge, chanting the divine residence of Raṅganātha, i.e., "Araṅgam" itself would destroy all the Karma which acts as the blocker for us to reach the divine. Āḷvār states, if every jīvātmā stuck here in this Saṃsāra (he calls this Bhūloka as Naraka as we are bounded by Indriyas and suffering due to it) uttered the great names of Bhagavān, then this saṃsāra would be devoid of any jīvātmā as all of them would have attained liberation, making this place a wasteland. Though this is certainly hypothetical as there are countless jīvātmās, yet Āḷvār tries to exclaim that such is the glory of the names of the Supreme that it can liberate any jīvātmā.
