## english_scripts

aḍimaiyil kuḍimai illā
ayal śaduppēdi māril
kuḍimaiyil kaḍaimai paṭṭa
kukkaril piṟappar ēlum
muḍiyinil tuḻabam vaittāy !
moy kazaṟkanbu śeyyum
aḍiyarai ugatti pōlum
araṅga mā nagaruḻānē !

## meaning

Oh Lord of the great city, Śrirangam! O supreme God with Tulasi leaves adorning your crown! it seems, You are more pleased with these who, though born in the lowest castes, seek the divine service under your pair of feet, and are highly devoted to You alone, than those who recite all the four Vedas, but do not seek the divine service under You, as the supreme goal.

## purport

Even those who do not recognize that performing kaiṅkaryam is inherent to their nature. It is fitting to think, "Kaiṅkaryam is natural to us. Just as we cannot live without food and water, we cannot exist without performing kaiṅkaryam." The Āḻvār refers to those who lack this understanding.

Those who differ from the chaturvēdhis who are not immersed in kaiṅkaryam. Sadhuppēdhimāril refers to those who study the four Vēdas. The benefit of Vēdic study is to realize that emperumāṉ is the master of all worlds, that we are His possessions, and that performing kaiṅkaryam to emperumāṉ and His devotees is our appropriate goal. Ayal signifies not knowing that the purpose of learning Vēdas is to obtain this knowledge. The Pādhmōttaram states: "viṣṇubhaktivihīnō yas sarvaśāstrārtha vēdhyapi | brāhmaṇyam tasya na bhavēt tasyōtpattir nirūpyatām ||" (Even if one knows the meanings of all śāstras, without devotion to Viṣṇu, one lacks the qualities of a brāhmaṇa, and their birth is questionable).

Kuḍimaiyil kaḍaimaippaṭṭa kukkaril piṟapparēlum - Those who, after many births of not following Vēdic injunctions and engaging in forbidden acts, are born into very low castes due to accumulated sins. Kukkar (kuṣka) refers to a class lower than chaṇḍāḷas. The Āḻvār's use of piṟapparēlum (even if they are born) indicates that birth status is not a determinant of understanding the meanings mentioned in the previous pāsuram. In Śrī Rāmāyaṇa Āraṇya Kāṇḍam 68.24, Śrī Rāma tells Lakṣmaṇa: "sarvatra khalu dṛśyante sādhavō dharmacāriṇa: | śūrā: śaraṇyās saumitrē tiryak yōni gatēṣvapi ||" (O Lakṣmaṇa! Those who are like sages, following the path of righteousness, fit to be surrendered to, are seen even among those born as animals and other species!). Just as emperumāṉ adorns tuḷasi on His head, these devotees are also fit to be kept on His head like tuḷasi. This adornment indicates His quality of being sarvēśvaran (lord of all).

These devotees cast aside their own happiness to serve only for His happiness, with affection, similar to sēvaḍi sevvi thirukkāppu (Thirupallāṇḍu 1). To them, this is the only purposeful way to spend time, and they consider this their goal. They consider His compassionate divine feet as the means. As mentioned in pāsuram 38 as sōmbar, they know that their ātma svarūpam (nature of the ātmā) is being eternally subservient to emperumāṉ and will not engage in self-protection. You delight in these followers who possess this knowledge, regardless of their lowly birth, rather than the chaturvēdhis who lack this understanding. The purpose of coming to Śrīraṅgam and reclining here is to hear these people who have affection towards You, even if they are of lowly birth. If You desired to hear only from the exalted, You would have remained in Śrīvaikuṇṭam.
