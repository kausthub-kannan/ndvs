## meaning

I was once a gambler and a thief, ensnared in the love-nets of fish-eyed women. But the beautiful Lord commanded, "Come forth!" He entered my mind and kindled my love for Him. Indeed, Raṅganātha permeated my being and ignited my devotion. Is it not so that He protected a helpless soul like me from the clutches of saṃsāra?

## purport

Āḻvār narrates how the Lord transformed his life, redirecting his love from materialistic pleasures to Raṅganātha. Toṇṭaraṭippoṭi Āḻvār was once mesmerized by the beauty of a dancer named Dēvadēvi. Her eyes, resembling fish, were captivating. Consequently, his interest, initially focused on Raṅganātha, gradually shifted towards Dēvi. However, the Lord always safeguards those who surrender to Him. When their minds stray from the spiritual path, He assumes the responsibility of guiding them back. Āḻvār's poverty was humiliated by Dēvadēvi's mother. Raṅganātha Himself brought a golden vessel to Dēvadēvi's house, introducing Himself as Āḻvār's disciple. 

Alas! The golden vessel was no ordinary object, but one used for nitya kaiṅkaryam in Śrīraṅgam to serve the Lord. When the temple discovered the vessel's loss and its presence in Dēvi's house, the king intervened and arrested Āḻvār. Āḻvār soon realized how misguided he had been from his true path of life and lamented his sinful ways. Raṅganātha promptly appeared to the king and ordered Āḻvār's release, declaring him very dear to Him. The Lord of Thiruvaraṅgam enacted such beautiful līlās with the Āḻvārs, demonstrating His personal care for the prapannas. For the śaraṇāgatas, He Himself ensures they remain on the right path, assuring liberation from saṃsāra. Indeed, He enters into these prapannas and inspires them to show Him the utmost possible love.
