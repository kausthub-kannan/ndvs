## meaning

O Lord Ranganatha! when the hosts of monkeys lifted up and carried hillocks for constructing a bund across the sea (in their service to Lord Rama), the small squirrel, free from deceit, transported particles of sand from the earth to the sea, by bathing in the sea first and rolling on the sands, and again bathing in the water of the sea. But, I am not like the squirrel free from malice. My mind is hard like the intractable trees. I am full of deceit in my heart. I do not carry out any service to You at all. Still I think that I am worthy of your grace. However, I do get lost myself.

## purport

The Āḻvār describes the scene of monkeys building the bridge to Laṅkā, emphasizing their eagerness to serve Rāma. He notes that many monkeys moved each mountain, showing their desire to participate in Rāma's service. He then speaks of the squirrels' contribution, who, seeing the monkeys' work as insufficient, decided to help. The squirrels would wet themselves in the ocean, roll in sand, and shake it off between the mountains. The Āḻvār admires their selfless service (salam ilā), contrasting it with his own lack of such devotion. The Āḻvār laments that his heart is as hard as trees, which unlike iron, cannot be shaped even when heated. He confesses to being deceitful, putting on an act of devotion while lacking true involvement in the Lord's matters. He admits to not serving the Lord with his heart as the squirrels did, despite the Lord valuing even small acts done with good intentions. The Āḻvār expresses sorrow at his failure to perform continuous service as expected of the ātmā. Finally, the Āḻvār states that he has forgotten the Lord and become useless, not due to distance from the Lord or lack of qualification, but due to his own forgetfulness. He references Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār's words to emphasize this point.
