meymmaiyai miga uṇarndu
āmbariśaṟindu koṇḍu

## meaning

Oh supreme Lord dwelling in the holy city of Srirangam made fertile by the surround- ing, flowing waters of Kāvēri! it seems You are pleased with those who refrain from leading prākṛtic (materialistic) way of life, and then, properly understand the nature of souls, and further, decide that the divine service done to You alone is the supreme end of one's life, and hence, who after restraining all their senses, withdraw themselves from all other pursuits and paths, and thus, who once for all, reject all unrighteous paths and stay at your threshold in order to do your divine service alone.

## purport

Alvār speaks about the nature of a true prapanna, a true bhāgavata. mēmpōrul refers to this body which can decay and is bound to perish once born. When a jīvātman realizes the essence of this life and understands that this body is perishable, while service to bhagavān is eternal, that jīvātman feels this body to be superficial. Only those who have surrendered to the lotus feet of Rañganātha can receive this beautiful knowledge; else, by māyā, the intertwined body makes the jīvātman feel "I am this dēha" which gives rise to ignorance. Refraining from such ignorance, a prapanna serves bhagavān. Until the jīvātman leaves material desire and the ignorance of "I am the body," he is destined to grow the tree called saṃsāra, as stated in Viṣṇu Purāṇa 7.11 as follows: "anāthmanyāthmā buddhir yā asve svam iti yā mati | saṃsāra-taru-sambhūti bījam ētat dvidhā sthitham ||" Hence, Alvār states to give up such traces completely. To give up such traces, the body must realize the divine nature of the ātman and īśvara. Once it realizes it is not the body, the tree called saṃsāra slowly withers away. When that ātman realizes its true nature, it realizes its svarūpam.

Even after realizing "I am not the body," the jīvātman must fully realize its true svarūpa—that it is the servant of bhagavān—and only then is liberation possible. When this knowledge is gifted to that jīvātman by bhagavān himself, it desires to serve the Lord eternally and strives to liberate itself from saṃsāra. In the attempt to liberate itself, it gives up the wealth, that is, the ignorance called "I am the body" and attaches itself to service to bhagavān. Realizing that removal of bondage through its own attempts is not possible, that jīvātman surrenders to bhagavān, keeping the Lord himself as the means to attain him. This is the essence of prapatti or śaraṇāgati, which was narrated to Arjuna by Kṛṣṇa in Bhagavad Gītā 18.66. 

Alvār compares the means to attain bhagavān to hair. Just as hair brings a sense of prestige to a person, these means can bring the essence of pride—"I am my own protector" or "It is by my efforts that I am attaining bhagavān or achieving liberation". One must forget this thought and realize that it is not the means (Karma Yoga, Bhakthi Yoga and others) but the compassion of bhagavān which brings us closer to Him. He alone can get us out of saṃsāra; indeed, He is the means itself. This itself becomes a prosperity to the jīvātman.

Alvār goes to the extreme of saying "aimpulan agathtadakki"—that is, while a jīvātman performs kaiṅkarya (divine service) to bhagavān, it experiences happiness due to the divine nature of the service. But a true prapanna should renounce this happiness too. He must perform the service for the happiness of bhagavān and not for himself.

Looking at Thiruvāraṅga's face, Alvār states that He is smiling happily while observing such great devotees who have left everything and are performing their rituals and duties assigned by the śāstras for the happiness of bhagavān alone, because bhagavān has ordered them to do so. Such souls are the foremost among His devotees. Due to the disappearance of grey clouds called ignorance, the love and compassion of the Lord, known as dayā, eternally falls upon them like sunlight.
