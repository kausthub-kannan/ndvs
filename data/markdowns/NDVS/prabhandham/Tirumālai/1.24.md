## english_scripts

veḻḻa nīr parandu pāyum
viri pozil araṅgan tannuḻ
kaḻvanār kiḍanda vāṟum
kamala nan mugamum kaṇḍum
uḻḻamē ! valiyai pōlum
oruvan enṟuṇara māṭṭāy
kaḻḻamē kādal śeydu
un kaḻḻattē kazikkinṟāyē !

## meaning

O wretched mind! do you think you are strong? You are not ready to accept Him as the only supreme Master. It is quite strange that even though you have already perceived His charming lotus-like face, and you have also seen his enthralling reclining posture. He, the enticing Lord who rests at the Śrīraṅgam Temple girt with vast groves flooded by the sweet Kāverī waters, nevertheless you are shrewd and your devotion is, indeed, delusive. Alas, you are wasting your time cunningly.

## purport

The sacred Kāveri, born from the hallowed Sahyādri mountains, cascades with abundant waters and love through the lush gardens of Śrīraṅgam. Her devotion to the Lord is palpable as she approaches this divine abode, her waters spreading wide to embrace Śrī Raṅganātha in a tender aquatic embrace. But why does our Lord recline here? He is Kaḷvanār, the Divine Thief, who has descended to 'steal' away the misguided notion of independence from us saṃsārins, who have ourselves stolen our own ātmavastu.

Ah, but how cleverly He steals! Not through grand declarations that might incite resistance as in His Rāma and Kṛṣṇa avatāras, but through the simple, irresistible allure of His divine repose (kiṭanta āṟu). It is not His kalyāṇa guṇas or the beauty of His divine limbs that captivates us, but this exquisite reclining posture alone.

Behold His lotus-like countenance (kamala nal mukam)! Though we compare it to a lotus in bloom - its redness, fragrance, and coolness - His face surpasses even this noble flower. For while the lotus has its imperfections, the Lord's visage is eternally auspicious, pure sattva unmarred by material modes. It is jyotirmayam, radiant beyond compare, never diminishing, the sole eternal coolness in this fleeting saṃsāra.

Yet, oh our mind! (uḷḷamē)! How hard (valiyai) it remain! Even after witnessing such divine form (tirukkōlam) and countenance (tirumukha maṇḍalam), our mind fail to recognize Him as the One without second (oruvan eṉṟu uṇara māṭṭāy). Had you truly realized His advitīya nature, wouldn't you be consumed by an unquenchable thirst for bhagavad viṣayam?

Alas, the love we profess (kātal seytu) is but artificial (kaḷḷamē), a mere phalabhakti. Even in the presence of such paramabhāgyam, our devotion falls short of its exalted subject. While the Lord showers boundless love, while Kāveri flows with extraordinary devotion, my manas still fails to exhibit fitting bhakti. Oh, how we squander (kaḻikkiṉṟāyē) this precious opportunity in mere pretense (kaḷḷattē)!
