## meaning

Oh! my supreme Lord who have encompassed all the worlds, without leaving any space, when You begged the great emperor, Mahābali, and got the three foot-steps of space from him! I am sure I won't bow to any other god except You. Oh! graceful Lord with reddish lotus-like eyes! Oh my supreme Self! Oh nectarine immortal Lord! Oh beneficent Lord similar to my rare life-birth! I will never think of any important goal other than You. Though I deem myself sinful, I do, again, sinful deeds alone.

## purport

The Āḻvār expresses his deep regret at emperumāṉ's unanticipated act of placing His divine foot upon the heads of saṃsārīs. He reflects that had emperumāṉ forewarned of His intention, the saṃsārīs, burdened with immense ego, would have refused this divine grace, commanding Him, "Do not place Your foot upon us." Just as those of sattva guṇa would have beseeched, "thiruvāṇai niṉṉāṇai" (Thiruvāimoḻi 10.10.2) to ensure emperumāṉ's divine foot rested upon their heads, these saṃsārīs would have vehemently opposed His approach. It was for this reason that emperumāṉ, in His infinite wisdom, placed His foot upon their heads without their knowledge, growing rapidly in form without causing fatigue to either Himself or the saṃsārīs.

On that day when the hierarchy between master and servitor lay in ruins, and Mahābali had seized the worlds. Not content with merely measuring the three worlds seized by Mahābali, His divine foot extended to Brahmalōka, after gracing the heads of all beings without discrimination, expanding in size. Āḻvār illustrates that the protector's [emperumāṉ's] excitement surpassed the magnitude of what He protected. The Puruṣa Sūktam 1 proclaims, "sahasra śīrṣā puruṣa: sahasrākṣa: sahasrapādh | sa bhūmim viśvathō vruthvā athyathiṣṭadhdhaśāṅgulam ||" (Nārāyaṇa, known as Puruṣa, possesses countless heads, eyes, and legs; He encompassed the earth on all sides and pervaded countless yōjanas beyond).

Āḻvār addresses emperumāṉ thus, recognizing that His divine feet, described by Thirumangai Āḻvār in Thirukkuṟundhāṇḍagam 6 as "amarar seṉṉippūviṉai" (the divine feet borne by nityasūris on their heads), had also graced his own head on that day. The Āḻvār reflects that it is due to the merit of that encounter that he has attained enlightenment today. He declares, "Having realized Your simplicity, I shall not depart from You hereafter, nor cling to anything else." He affirms that he will neither seek independence like a king nor become a servitor to another, echoing the essence of the Thirumanthram [aṣṭākṣaram]. Āḻvār assures emperumāṉ that his determination will last eternally, until his ātmā ceases to exist. Like Nammāḻvār in Thiruvāimoḻi 2.6.1, he offers solace to emperumāṉ, who grieves at the thought of the Āḻvār's departure. This epithet reflects not just the natural redness of emperumāṉ's eyes but the unique radiance acquired upon hearing the Āḻvār's declaration of eternal devotion. The Āḻvār, like Hanumān in the Rāmāyaṇa, offers refuge to periya perumāḷ in His archāvatāram, assuring Him of his unwavering devotion
