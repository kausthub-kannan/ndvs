## meaning

The Lord of glittering effulgence, unknown to Brahmā, Śiva and other gods, came out swiftly in anger, against the crocodile which lives on bloody flesh, to protect the elephant (ready to offer the lotus flower by his trunk). Are we able to save ourselves from any burden? No. We are merely dogs. We don't realise our meekness. Without doing service to our supreme Lord Ranganātha, for what purpose have I been born at all?

## purport

The Āḻvār humbly acknowledges his inability to observe the rigorous rites prescribed for the three varṇika people. He confesses his incapacity to adopt jñāna or bhakti yōgams as means to reach the Lord. The jñānam and bhakti that the Āḻvār has exhibited thus far have come to him as bhagavat prasādam, enabling him to relish the Lord's divine beauty. It is important to note that this jñānam and bhakti do not fall under the categories of jñāna yōgam and bhakti yōgam.

The Āḻvār eloquently speaks of the various types of snānam (ritual bathing) such as nitya snānam, naimittika snānam, and prāyaścitta snānam. He observes that each type of snānam serves a distinct purpose and differs for various occasions. He reverently mentions the three sacred fires - gārhapatyam, āhavanīyam, and dakṣiṇāgni - that a brāhmaṇa must diligently maintain.

Drawing a poignant comparison, the Āḻvār likens a karmayōgi to a devoted mother who constantly carries water and food for her weak child, always needing to gather firewood to maintain the sacred fire. These, he explains, exemplify the numerous rituals that must be meticulously followed even before embarking on karmayōgam.
With great humility, the Āḻvār points out that even a single lapse in observing these rigorous rituals - be it mantralōpam, kriyālōpam, dravyalōpam, or kālabhēdam - renders the ritual fruitless. As Periya Vachan Pillai wisely states, "Karaṇam tappiṉāl maraṇam" (a mistake in performance leads to spiritual death).

The Āḻvār candidly confesses that not only does he lack true brāhmaṇyam, but he has also feigned Brahminism to such an extent that his entire clan has been belittled in society's eyes. This, he laments, is the pitiful state of his karmayōgam. He humbly declares that as karmayōgam is irrelevant to him, so too is jñāna yōgam. Ātmāvalōkanam (direct perception of the self) is also beyond his reach.

Recalling Śrī Yāmunācārya's profound words from the stotra ratna, "na dharma niṣṭhōsmi na ca ātma vēdi na bhaktimān" (I am neither steadfast in dharma, nor a knower of the self, nor filled with devotion), the Āḻvār aligns himself with this sentiment. He refutes the notion that karmayōga anuṣṭhānam from a previous birth might have led to jñāna yōgam in this life, stating that even this is absent in him.

The Āḻvār humbly admits that as he cannot even perceive his own ātma, how can he hope to meditate on the Lord? Thus, he acknowledges that he is not a bhaktimān either. He declares with profound honesty that all three yōgās are beyond his reach, echoing his earlier sentiments expressed in the pāsurams "nōṟṟa nōṉbilēṉ" and "kaṟavaigaḷ piṉseṉṟu".

Acknowledging his lack of spiritual credits, the Āḻvār humbly admits to being 'akiñcanan' (one who has nothing). Yet, he fervently appeals to the Lord, who is guṇapūrṇan (the repository of all virtues), to save him. His heartfelt plea resonates with Śrī Āṇḍāḷ's words in Tiruppāvai: "aṟivoṉṟum illātha āykulam" followed by "kuṟai oṉṟum illātha gōvindā".

As in previous pāsurams where the Āḻvār reverently described the Lord as "aiyappāḍu aṟuttu tōṉṟum aḻagaṉ" and "āḍaram peruga vaitta aḻagaṉ", here too he extols the Lord's beauty, likening Him to the majestic hue of the sea. He humbly states that he is only capable of offering maṅgaḷāśāsanam (auspicious praises) to the Lord's divine beauty. Though unworthy in other respects, he wholeheartedly declares the Lord as his śaraṇyan (ultimate refuge).

With deep devotion, the Āḻvār implores the Lord to make him worthy of His grace and to bestow His blessings upon him. He gently reminds the Lord that His divine purpose is not to save the already blemishless, but to redeem those without spiritual credits. The Āḻvār humbly suggests that there can be no one more suitable for the Lord's grace than himself, as he looks solely to the Lord for salvation.

Though bereft of spiritual merits, the Āḻvār's unwavering dependence on the Lord and his constant contemplation of the Lord's divine beauty serve as his only solace. He respectfully reminds the Lord that as the repository of all virtues, it is incumbent upon Him to make the Āḻvār eligible for His grace and to ultimately save him from the cycle of saṃsāra.
