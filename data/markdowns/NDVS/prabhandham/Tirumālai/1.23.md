## english_scripts

kaṅgaiyin punidam āya
kāviri naḍuvu pāṭṭu
poṅgu nīr parandu pāyum
pūm pozil araṅgan tannuḻ
eṅgaḻ māl iṟaivan īśan
kiḍandadōr kiḍakkai kaṇḍum
eṅṅanam maṟandu vāzgēn
ēzaiyēn ēzaiyēnē !

## meaning

Could I forget Him and live? No. He is our supreme Lord, Tirumāl. I do look at His reclining posture as Śrī Raṅganātha. He dwells in the great Temple surrounded by the flowery groves watered by the holy river, Kāverī (Poṇṇi) which gushes forth and is more sacred than even the pure Ganges (Gaṅgā). When such is His great grace to be so nearer to our vision, am I to forget Him? If I could forget Him, then I should be poor, poor only.

## purport

The sacred Kāviri, surpassing even the holy Gaṅgā in purity, flows with divine grace. How does she excel? Not only in her enchanting beauty (darśanīyatā) and delightful presence (bhōgyatā), but most significantly, she cradles the reclining form of Śrī Raṅganātha Himself. While Gaṅgā once touched the Lord's feet, she later lost that honor, becoming 'raudram' as she fell from Śiva's matted locks. But Kāviri? She holds the very Lord who created Gaṅgā from His divine feet, much like Yaśōdā cradling Śrī Kṛṣṇa in her lap.

Behold how Kāviri, overjoyed at reaching Śrīraṅgam, spreads her waters with enthusiasm, embracing this holy place. Śrīraṅgam, a veritable paradise of blooming gardens (pūmpoḻil araṅgam), speaks of sanctity and tranquility. The abundance of water nourishes eternal spring, mirroring the Lord's eternal youthfulness (saukumāryam).

But why has our beloved Lord (eṅgaḷ māl) chosen this idyllic setting? Not for His comfort, but out of boundless love for His devotees. He is Iṟaivan, Īśan – the Supreme Lord to whom everything belongs, using all as He wills. And how He reclines! While others might appear ungainly in repose, Perumāḷ's reclining posture (kiṭanthatōr kiṭakkai) radiates unparalleled beauty.

Oh, how can one witness such splendor and continue to live in forgetfulness? Before beholding His sayanakōlam, life in saṃsāra might have been bearable. But now, having seen the Lord's magnificence, how can this lowly soul (ēḻaiyēṉ) ever forget? Alvar states he (we) lack the subtle mind to fully appreciate His presence, yet he (we) cannot bear His absence. Truly, He is (we) the most wretched (ēḻaiyēṉē), for how can he now live separated from such divine beauty?
