## english_scripts

pulai aṟam āgi ninṟa
puttoḍu śamaṇam ellām
kalai aṟakkaṭra māndar
kāṇbarō kēṭparō tām
talai aṟuppuṇḍum śāvēn
śattiyam kāṇmin aiyā
śilaiyināl ilaṅgai śeṭra
tēvanē tēvan āvān

## meaning

This is the noble truth! Those who are well-versed in Vedānta and Śāstras are not misled by the teachings of Jaina and Buddhist doctrines. Without a doubt, they realize that Rāma alone is the Brahman! I claim hereby that I will shave my head from my body and am ready to die if what I have stated is wrong, i.e., the vanquisher of ten-headed Rāvaṇa and the holder of Śārṅga is the Supreme Lord.

## purport

Āḷvār boldly claims without a doubt that Śrī Rāmacandra is Brahman! He is the Supreme Lord. With the rise of different philosophies and theologies, it is possible for our monkey-like brains to be deluded by various confusing statements. The Śākyas (i.e., Buddhists) and the Jainas deny the existence of a Supreme personality who is the Īśvara, the controller of all. On the other hand, Tāntric texts mislead by stating other devatās are the Supreme Brahman. But unlike these philosophies or texts, Vedas are apauruṣeya, i.e., there is no date which can be stated when the Vedas were created. They were not created by any human. For ages, they have been passed to the next generation through communication between Ācārya and Śiṣya. Even when the universe undergoes dissolution, they are always protected by the Brahman. 

That Veda, which shines due to its speaking about the Brahman, states that Nārāyaṇa is that Supreme Lord who has none second to him. The Nārāyaṇopaniṣad of Taittirīya states this explicitly as nirākhyātaḥ śuddho deva eko nārāyaṇaḥ । na dvitīyo'sti kaścit । When the eternal Vedas themselves state without a doubt that Nārāyaṇa (Śrī Rāma) is the Paratattvam, Āḷvār states there is no doubt that the one who slayed the ruler of Laṅkā and holds the bow called Śārṅga is indeed the one who will liberate us from this cycle of Saṃsāra. It has to be noted that Āḷvār condemns the actions of those followers of other faiths who don't show respect towards Bhagavān. As per Āḷvārs, they are least concerned about condemning people unless it comes to the fact that those people speak ill of Bhagavān.
