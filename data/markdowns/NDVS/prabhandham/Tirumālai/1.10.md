## english_scripts

nāṭṭinān teyvam eṅgum

kāṭṭinān tiruvaraṅgam
uybavarkkuyyum vaṇṇam
kēṭṭirē nambimīrgāḻ !
keruḍa vāgananum niṟka

## meaning

Just as the primordial Deity (Śrīman Nārāyaṇa) established many gods throughout the world, even so He has revealed Himself as Śrī Raṅganātha at Śrīraṅgam for the sake of the aspirants, out of His unbounded compassion. I beseech you, O believers of the Vedas and Śāstras! Listen to me. While there is our Lord with the supreme divine Garuḍa as His vehicle, why do you expect prosperity at the lap of Jyeṣṭhā?

## purport

The Īśvara, Śrīman Nārāyaṇa has revealed himself to humans as Raṅganātha due to his Dayā. To nityas and muktas he appears as Para Vāsudeva and to ṛṣis as Antaryāmī. To devatās he blessed them in Vyūha, but to mere humans like us these forms are inaccessible. To pave a path for us towards Arcāvatāra mārga, to quench our thirst to serve him, he has appeared in Thiruvaraṅgam as Śrī Raṅganātha, ready to accept our service. For the Supreme being himself to appear in the form of Arcā and accept all the service we provide to him, Āḷvār exclaims, how great is his compassion! Indeed it has no bounds just like the ocean! 

Next, Āḷvār explains how Garuḍa is the personification of Veda itself. Garuḍa is known to be the vehicle of Bhagavān. Garuḍa is Vedas personified. The Vedas glorify Śrīman Nārāyaṇa. Just as it holds and carries the glories of the Lord, so does Garuḍa. The two wings are said to represent the two divisions of the Sāma Veda, known as bṛhat and rathantara. Garuḍa's eyes are said to be the Gāyatrī mantra, which protects those who chant it. Just as the mantras travel from one world to another at the speed of light, so does Garuḍa. Āḷvār states to us, think about the Lord who rides the personification of Vedas itself. When he is ready to welcome us like a father to his children, why are we still unable to understand his compassion and end up stuck in this Saṃsāra, which is the greatest misfortune!
