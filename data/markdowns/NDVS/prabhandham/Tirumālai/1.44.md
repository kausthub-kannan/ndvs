## meaning

When Lord Siva with his matted locks of hair in which the girl makes a stroll (i.e. flows the holy river Gangā), and the four-faced Lord Brahmā are not able to perceive your true form - even though both of them (Siva and Brahmā) took to severe austerities (penance) for innumerable aeons and aeons and so both of them become dejected, Oh Lord Ranganatha! You have run swiftly towards the great devotee, Gajendra (the lordly elephant), and protected him by showering your grace on him. Oh Lord! are you without sympathy towards me? Why is it that I do not consider You as the only succour for me?

## purport

Bhagavān is the Creator of Jagat, the creator of Brahmā and Śiva. The Mahānārāyaṇopaniṣad states: "eko ha vai nārāyaṇa āseet, na brahma, nēśāna" - meaning at the beginning, only Nārāyaṇa existed, neither Brahmā nor Īśa (Śiva) were present. Hence, without a second doubt, Nārāyaṇa is considered the Paramātma. The Vedas state this with multiple pramāṇas. At the same time, he is the final Puruṣārtha to attain Mokṣa, for all jīvātmas from Brahmā to animals. Poygai Álvár states similarly in Mudalai Thiruvandadi: "kadikkamalam thannuḷ irundhum kāṇkilān kaṇṇan adikkamalam thannai ayan" - even the lotus-born Brahmā, though born from Bhagavān's navel, is unable to see the lotus feet of Para Vasudeva (Kṛṣṇa).

The fact that Rudra provides a seat to Gaṅgā, which is the śrīpāda tīrtha of none other than Trivikrama avatāra of Bhagavān, speaks to the fact that even he is devoted to Nārāyaṇa. As the Taittirīya Upaniṣad states, "yatho vācho nivarthanthe" - words cannot describe Brahman. Brahmā performed the thiruvārdhana of Śrī Raṅganātha in Satya loka. Rudra performed penance near Chandrapuṣkaraṇi to remove the doṣa attained by removing Brahmā's 5th head. The same Śiva glorifies Śrīraṅgam and Raṅganātha while describing it to Nārada in Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa. To access you and your divine form, even such great Sarva Jñānis have to perform severe austerities. How great is your compassion that you listened to the calls of Gajendra! The elephant finally understood its helplessness and called you with all love and devotion, surrendering to your lotus feet, praying to you as Ādimūla.

Indeed, as Bhagavān states in Bhagavad Gītā 11.48: "na veda-yajñādhyayanair na dānair na cha kriyābhir na tapobhir ugraiḥ evaṁ-rūpaḥ śhakya ahaṁ nṛi-loke draṣhṭuṁ tvad anyena kuru-pravīra" - neither by performing austerities nor by reading the Vedas can anyone see his divine form.
But when questioned about what will give us his protection, especially from the tiger called Saṁsāra, Bhagavān assures in Bhagavad Gītā 18.66: "sarva-dharmān parityajya mām ekaṁ śharaṇaṁ vraja ahaṁ tvāṁ sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣhayiṣhyāmi mā śhuchaḥ" - the famous Charma śloka stating us to surrender to the divine feet of the Lord and perform all activities of Dharma for the sake of the Lord, abandoning its result. Bhagavān himself assures protection to these Prapannas. These are indeed the highest among all! Gajendra performing total surrender to Bhagavān at that moment, the Lord jumps from his seat to save his devotee without a moment's delay.

Álvár quotes two important points here: first, the surrendered ones need not fear anything when Sathya Saṅkalpa Mahā Viṣṇu has assured their protection. Secondly, when Bhagavān himself accepts the devotion of devatas as well as an elephant equally, and even aided the elephant much faster, it states that there is no sense in discriminating people. Any jīvātma which desires Bhagavān alone is worthy to be celebrated.
