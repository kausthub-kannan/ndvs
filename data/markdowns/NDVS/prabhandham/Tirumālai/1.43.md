## english_scripts

amara ōr aṅgam āṟum
vēdam ōr nāngum ōdi
tamargaḻil talaivarāya
śādi andaṇargaḻēlum
numargaḻai ppazippar āgil
noḍippadōr aḻavil
āṅgē avargaḻ tām pulaiyar pōlum
araṅga mā nagaruḻānē !

## meaning

Oh Lord of the sacred temple city, Srirangam! among your devotees, if a section of devotees though born in higher classes of society, though taking a leading part in all activities, though well- versed in all holy scriptures, viz. the four Vedas and the six Vedāngas speak ill of other devotees belonging to lower classes of society, then, at that very moment, they are deemed to be.

## purport

Re-iterating the essence of the previous pasuram, Álvár speaks again to the learned ones that, though they might be well-read and versed in Vedas, if one speaks ill about the devotees of Bhagavān's devotees, they do not stand as learned ones; indeed, these are the lowest of all! The Vedas are considered to have six aṅgas: śikṣā (articulation or pronunciation of words), vyākaraṇa (grammar), chandhas (poetic metrics), niruktha (etymology), jyotiṣa (astrology), and kalpa. Parāśara Bhaṭṭar states in his Śrī Raṅgarāja Stavam that the Vedas with their aṅgas are speaking about none other than Śrī Raṅganātha! The ones who have studied these aṅgas and mastered them are worthy of celebration, as stated in the Purāṇas.  This celebration arises from the nature of Vedas as apauruṣeya - not created by humans. 

But Álvár provides an exception in celebrating such learned ones. If these learned ones end up speaking ill about a devotee, such people haven't learned the Vedas in their true sense. If a sense of hatred, especially towards the Bhāgavatas, arises even in those who have read the Vedas, how can they be considered learned? The Upaniṣads and Bhagavad Gītā, which are said to be the essence of the Vedas, state that devotees of īśvara are great and should be respected. Bhagavān goes to a greater extent, stating in Bhagavad Gītā 7.18: ātmaiva me matam āsthitaḥ sa hi yuktātmā mām evānuttamāṁ gatim - meaning the devotees who are ever devoted to Bhagavān and have considered him as the supreme goal, whose service to him is the supreme state, are very self of Bhagavān himself! Such jīvātmas are indeed rare. Hence, if Bhagavān has gone to such great lengths to praise his devotees and show the unbounded prema he has towards his dear devotees, how can the so-called learned ones speak ill of such great souls? Indeed, they haven't learned anything from the Vedas, which at their core speak about Bhagavān! Being a mumukṣhu is far from their sight when they do not have the maturity to look at the great devotees of Bhagavān.
