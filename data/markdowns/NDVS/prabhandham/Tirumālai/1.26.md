## meaning

Oh Lord of the great city, Śrirangam! I do not offer the fragrant flowers at the pair of your benign golden feet at any time. Nor do I praise you, delineating your auspicious qualities through the faultless words. Nor even am I devoted to you, heart and soul brimming with love. Therefore, you cannot count me as your own servant/devotee. Why have I been born? No purpose at all.

## purport

The Āḻvār laments his inability to worship the Lord properly. He states that while he had both eagerness and strength for worldly matters, he lacked these for divine service. He cites Viṣṇu Dharmam, which says only hands that worship Hari are true hands. The Āḻvār confesses to worshipping inappropriate things harmful to him in both saṃsāra and Śrī Vaikuṇṭha. He expresses his failure to praise the Lord's auspicious qualities with his speech, contrary to Viṣṇu Dharmam's teaching that the tongue praising Hari is the true tongue. The Āḻvār admits his tongue rolls easily for lowly matters but not for divine praise.

Regarding his heart, the Āḻvār confesses it hasn't shown true affection towards the Lord. He references Bhagavad Gītā to explain how worldly desires could be diverted towards the divine, but admits failing in this too. He cites Āḷavandār's Stotra Ratnam and Viṣṇu Purāṇam to illustrate his past insincere affections. The Āḻvār declares himself without anything (ēdhilēn) towards the Lord, who possesses all auspicious qualities as described in Stotra Ratnam. He laments losing the Lord not due to the Lord's distance or inappropriateness, but due to his own sin. Finally, the Āḻvār questions the purpose of his birth, stating that instead of using his God-given limbs for worship, he wasted them. He concludes that his birth, rather than benefiting anyone, has become a cause for many misdeeds for himself and others.
