## meaning

I don't belong to any sacred place; nor do I possess any acre of land; nor even do I have any kinsmen; on this earth, I have not even any trace of devotion towards the sole of the pair of your feet. Oh supreme Lord! O lustrous Lord of rain-cloud- hue! O Lord Kṛṣṇa of enticing beauty! prithee, I do cry aloud. Who else is there to weed out my difficulties other than You? Oh Lord of the great city, Śrirangam!

## purport

The "ūr" (town) that the Āḻvār refers to here is not any ordinary town. He speaks of divya dēśams where emperumāṉ eternally resides, such as Śrī Sālagrāmam, Śrī Rāma's birthplace Ayodhyā, and Śrīraṅgam (Thiruvaraṅgam), the foremost of all arcāvatāra sthalams. How can the term "ūr" refer to divya dēśam? It does so due to the context of this particular section of Thirumālai, which deals with the Āḻvār's ākiñcanyam - his utter helplessness in attaining emperumāṉ. Since he states "ūrilēṉ", the term "ūr" must signify a path to mōkṣam. As there is no sentient being who is not born in a town, if "ūr" were to refer to any town, then all towns would become means to attain mōkṣam. There is no authoritative source to support such an interpretation. Thus, considering various authentic sources, one must conclude that "ūr" refers specifically to divya dēśam.
The Āḻvār humbly admits, "Had I been born in a divya dēśam, You would have endeavored to rescue me, as I would have been born in a place that gives You joy. Alas, I do not even have that to my credit." Is there evidence to suggest that residing in a divya dēśam can lead to one's salvation? Indeed, there are many. The Gāruḍa Purāṇam 11 states: "dēśo'yam sarvakāma duk" (this place confers everything). Śrī Rāmāyaṇa, Āraṇya Kāṇḍam 1.20 declares: "tē vayaṁ bhavatā rakṣyā bhavat viṣaya vāsinaḥ" (we, who dwell and suffer in Your domain, are to be protected by You) [thus speak the ṛṣis of Daṇḍakāraṇyam to Śrī Rāma]. Śrī Rāmāyaṇa, Uttara Kāṇḍam 109.22 proclaims: "nocchvasat tadayodhyāyāṁ susūkṣmamapi dṛśyatē | tiryak yoni gatāścānyē sarvē rāmānuvratāḥ ||" (Not even the tiniest creature remains alive in Ayodhyā. All beings, including animals, have followed Śrī Rāma [to Paramapadam]). Thus, numerous authoritative sources confirm this.

"Even if you were not born in a divya dēśam, perhaps you have some connection through landed property. Do you possess such a link?" asks emperumāṉ. The Āḻvār humbly responds that he lacks even that. In bygone days, land was often gifted or leased for performing kainkaryams to emperumāṉ, such as reciting Tiruppallāṇḍu in the temple or creating gardens for stringing flower garlands for Him. The Āḻvār confesses that he lacks this connection as well. Emperumāṉ inquires further, "Even if you lack a connection through birth or landed property with a divya dēśam, do you have any association through people there with whom you exchange gifts or through mantras (either learning or teaching)?" The Āḻvār replies that he has no relatives there, thus no connection with divya dēśam. By saying "no one," he emphasizes the absence of even distant relatives he might have occasionally encountered or friends with whom he might have had some association - there is absolutely no one, the Āḻvār laments.
In that case, where does the Āḻvār reside?

The Āḻvār states that he dwells on this earth, which creates boundless difficulties for those without a place to stay. He contrasts this with Śrīvaikuṇṭham, described in Mūṉṟām Tiruvandhādhi 51 as "kalaṅgāp perunagaram" (the trouble-free great abode). In Śrīvaikuṇṭham, one can live without worry, free from the afflictions of lust, anger, and other vices. On this earth, however, seven people vie for the food and clothing meant for one person. "I am living in such a wretched place," the Āḻvār laments. "I have no connection with Your divine feet, which offer refuge to those who have nowhere else to turn," confesses the Āḻvār. Niṉ pādhamūlam - Śrī Rāmāyaṇa, Sundara Kāṇḍam 38.33 states: "sa pitrā ca parityaktas suraiśca sa maharṣibhiḥ | trīn lokān samparikramya tamēva śaraṇaṁ gataḥ ||" (Kākāsuran, abandoned by his father, mother, celestial beings, and sages, traversed all three worlds before finally seeking refuge at Śrī Rāma's feet). The Āḻvār expresses his deep anguish at being unworthy to seek refuge under emperumāṉ's divine feet, which granted sanctuary even to Kākāsuran, who had committed an intolerable offense and became the target of His brahmāstra. Periya Perumāḷ then tells him, "If you have no benefit in any way, it seems there is no alternative but to forfeit the opportunity of attaining Me."

The Āḻvār responds, "I have nothing to protect myself. But don't You have the means to protect me? You possess boundless resources. Aren't You the Lord of all, far above everyone else? Only by protecting me too can You truly be the Lord of all. If You do not protect a śēṣabhūtan (a devoted follower), You cannot be a śēṣi (the Supreme Lord). For that to occur, shouldn't a śēṣabhūtan realize his servitude?" The sentiment here is that even when the sentient being, in a state of delusion, believes himself to be independent, sarvēśvaran, considering Himself the owner and the sentient being His possession, will correct the being and make him realize the true relationship between them.

Emperumāṉ asks the Āḻvār, "Since it is undeniable that you are My servitor, what is the means for you to attain that servitorship?" To which the Āḻvār responds that His divine beauty is the means.
Kāroḷi vaṇṇaṉē - In pāsuram 16, the Āḻvār had said "ādharam perugavaitta aḻagaṉ" (the beautiful form of emperumāṉ that engenders affection). Emperumāṉ initially kindles interest towards Himself in the mind of the sentient being. Once this interest is awakened, His form (beauty) becomes the means for the being to attain Him. When that beauty resides in emperumāṉ, there is no obstacle for the Āḻvār to obtain servitorship, he declares.

Even if the form is beautiful, instead of remaining in Śrīvaikuṇṭham where You could not be reached, You made Yourself accessible to us by taking various incarnations and exhibiting auspicious qualities such as saulabhyam (simplicity). Since You are parama mūrtti, You are our incomparable Lord; since You are kāroḷi vaṇṇaṉ, You create in us, as upāyam (means), everything beginning with desire; since You are Kaṇṇaṉ, You possess auspicious qualities such as simplicity and affability. With all these attributes, how can I fail to achieve my goal? You are the perfect Lord for us all. Even if You were not the Lord, Your beauty is such that we cannot ignore You. Even if You lacked these two great qualities, You possess auspicious qualities like simplicity and affability, and You associate closely with us. With such an entity as You present, I have no deficiency.
Kadhaṟugiṉṟēṉ - When I contemplate such greatness in You, I cry out to You, just as those who observe other means such as bhakti cry out when there is a delay in attaining You.

Are You delaying in protecting me when I cry out to You because You think someone else will protect me? Can I protect myself with nothing in my possession? Can someone else, who doesn't know as well as I do that I have nothing, protect me? If the possession is destroyed, is it not a loss to You, the Owner? Even if You are the owner, if You were not near me, I might have remained calm, using that as an excuse. The meaning here is that "when You are reclining in the nearby temple [at Śrīraṅgam], anticipating whether someone will come calling out to You to attain You, how can I remain silent and not cry out to You?"
