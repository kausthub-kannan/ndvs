## english_scripts

oru villāl ōṅgu munnīr aḍaittu
ulagaṅgaḻ uyya
śeruvilē arakkar kōnai
śeṭra nam śēvaganār
maruviya periya kōyil
madil tiruvaraṅgam ennā
karuvilē tiru ilādīr !
kālattai kkazikkinṟīrē

## meaning

Periya Kōyil (the great Temple) is the shrine (of Lord Śrī Raṅganātha) with seven massive ramparts (prākāras). One should ever think of Śrīraṅgam, the abode of Raṅganātha, where our supreme Lord has permanently reclined on the Serpent-couch (Ādiśeṣa). Śrī Raṅganātha is our mighty Lord who, in his advent as Śrī Rāma, has slain Rāvaṇa, the demon-king, in battle, after constructing the bridge (setu) across the roaring ocean, for the sake of retrieving all the worlds by his inimitable Bow (Śārṅga). Oh men without the grace of the Lord (Raṅganātha) even when you were in the mother's womb! Why do you waste your time without chanting the name of Śrīraṅgam? Alas!

## purport

Āḷvār continues to glorify the greatness of chanting the divine names of Bhagavān. Here, he emphasises us to remember Śrīraṅgam, the divine abode of Śrī Raṅganātha. The great temple is surrounded by seven prākāras which cover overall a tremendous area of 157 acres. Like a treasure protected by several outer coating chests, the Supreme Lord, who is the ultimate goal of life, the true treasure is lying on Ādiśeṣa at the center of these prākāras. Āḷvār speaks to us to remember that Śrīraṅgam which hosts the divine Lord. Raṅganātha, who is non-different from Śrī Rāmacandra, destroyed the king of Laṅkā with his inimitable bow, the Śārṅga. Āḷvār questions us, calling us foolish to not remember him. The whole universe remains stable due to his Saṅkalpa Śakti alone just like the pearls are withheld by a thread ("mayi sarvaṃ idaṃ protaṃ sūtre maṇi-gaṇā iva"). Without his Saṅkalpa Śakti, neither would the universe be created or sustained nor would any of us be born from the womb of our mothers. He is the controller of all and hence is the Īśvara, and our not remembering him is the most unfortunate thing and it is the greatest waste of time, Āḷvār states.
