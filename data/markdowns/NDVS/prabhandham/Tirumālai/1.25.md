## english_scripts

kuḻittu mūnṟanalai ōmbum
kuṟi koḻ andaṇamai tannai
oḻittiṭṭēn en kaṇ illai
nin kaṇum pattan allēn
kaḻippaden koṇḍu nambī !
kaḍal vaṇṇā ! kadaṟuginṟēn
aḻittenakkaruḻ śey kaṇḍāy
araṅga mā nagaruḻānē !

## meaning

I have thoroughly neglected Varṇāśrama dharma, the duties of caste and stage of life, viz. bathing thrice and adoring the three fires properly. I don't even look at them as my duties. Nor am I devoted to you. Oh Lord full of immense auspicious features! I have not done anything properly. How could I be pleased? O Lord of ocean-like blue colour! I am just crying aloud. O Lord of the great city, Srirangam! I beseech you, protect me and shower your ineffable grace on me.

## purport

The Āḻvār states that he does not have the capability to observe the rigorous rites meant for the three varṇika people. He declares himself incapable of adopting jñāna or bhakti yōgams to reach Him. The jñānam and bhakti, which the Āḻvār has shown until now, have come to him as bhagavat prasādam and helped him to enjoy the Lord. This jñānam and bhakti do not fall under the categories of jñāna yōgam and bhakti yōgam.

Āḻvār speaks of the many types of snānam (bathing) such as nitya snānam, naimittika snānam, prāyaścitta snānam, etc. He notes that one type of snānam does not represent the other and they differ for various occasions. He mentions the three sacred fires - gārhapatyam, āhavanīyam, and dakṣiṇāgni - that a brāhmaṇa must maintain. The Āḻvār compares a karmayōgi to a mother who always carries water and food for her weak child, always needing to carry firewood to maintain the fire. These, he says, are examples of the many rituals that must be strictly followed even before indulging in karmayōgam.

Āḻvār points out that even if one slip occurs while observing these rigorous rituals - be it mantralōpam, kriyālōpam, dravyalōpam, kālabhēdam - the ritual bears no fruit. "Karaṇam tappiṉāl maraṇam," says Periya Vachan Pillai. The Āḻvār confesses that apart from not having brāhmaṇyam, he also feigned Brahminism to the extent that the whole clan was belittled in society's eyes. This, he states, is the status of his karmayōgam. He declares that as karmayōgam is not relevant to him, jñāna yōgam is also irrelevant. Ātmāvalōkanam (sākṣātkāram of ātma) is also not possible for him. He recalls Śrī Yāmunācārya's stotra ratna verse: "na dharma niṣṭhōsmi na ca ātma vēdi na bhaktimān". He counters the argument that karmayōga anuṣṭhānam of a previous birth might have led to jñāna yōgam in this birth, stating that even that is not present in him. Āḻvār states that as he cannot even see his ātma, how can he meditate on the Lord. Thus, he is not a bhaktimān either. He declares that all three yōgās are not relevant to him, recalling the "nōṟṟa nōṉbilēṉ" and "kaṟavaigaḷ piṉseṉṟu" pāsurams.

Āḻvār admits he has no credits from any of these ways and is thus 'akiñcanan'. But he appeals to the Lord, who is guṇapūrṇan (repository of virtues), to save him. His appeal is similar to the Tiruppāvai pāsuram, where Śrī Āṇḍāḷ says "aṟivoṉṟum illātha āykulam" followed by "kuṟai oṉṟum illātha gōvindā". As in previous pāsurams where the Āḻvār described the Lord as "aiyappāḍu aṟuttu tōṉṟum aḻagaṉ" and "āḍaram peruga vaitta aḻagaṉ", here too he praises the Lord's beauty, comparing Him to the color of the sea. He states that he is only able to do maṅgaḷāśāsanam to the Lord's beauty. Although unworthy in other ways, he declares the Lord as his śaraṇyan (refuge).

Āḻvār pleads with the Lord to make him eligible for His grace and to grace him. The Āḻvār reminds the Lord that He has not come down to save blemishless people, but to save those without credits. He states that there can be no one more suitable than himself, as he looks only to the Lord. Although he has no credits, he depends solely on the Lord. He spends his time enjoying the Lord's divine beauty. He reminds the Lord that as the repository of all virtues, He must make the Āḻvār eligible for His grace and save him.
