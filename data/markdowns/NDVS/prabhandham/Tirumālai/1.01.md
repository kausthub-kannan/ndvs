## meaning

Oh Lord! You are the primal cause who have kept and protected the three worlds within your stomach during deluge and then, made them manifest at the beginning of creation as the principal Brahman. Oh Lord residing at the great city of Srirangam! we have controlled our senses; we have indeed, leapt over the sins (and sinful deeds); and we tread on the heads of Yama and his hosts, erecting victory flagstafl's (on those heads). We are proud to say thus because of our practice of incessant chanting of your names as Sri Ranganitha, hearken!

## purport

Bhagavān holds the entire universe in His womb and hence he is called Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu. As He holds the animate and inanimate within His garbha, He is also called Hiraṇyagarbha. From this Hiraṇyagarbha, everything is created. In the Pāñcarātra Āgama, the expansion of Vāsudeva, i.e., Saṅkarṣaṇa, further expands as Garbhodakaśāyī Viṣṇu, holding the entire universe within Him. Āḻvār praises the Lord for protecting us all the time, even after pralaya. Once He decides to create the universe again, with His saṅkalpa alone, He creates the lotus-born Brahmā. Due to Him being the primal cause of everything, He is the Brahman. This Supreme Brahman resides in Śrīraṅgam on the bed of Ādiśeṣa.

Our senses (Indriyas) play a primary role in performing actions. The mind among them can either distract us from the primary goal (Brahman) or attract us towards Him. Once one controls his senses, he is able to leap over all sinful activities. All the results of the pious actions the jīvatma performs are surrendered at the lotus feet of the Lord. When one becomes a Prapanna, surrendering to the divine feet of the Lord, he is able to conquer the cycle of birth and death (personified by Ālvār as treading on the head of Yama). One can only attain liberation by chanting the beautiful names of the Lord. By chanting the Thirumantram, that yogi is able to enter the Supreme above where the Nityas sing the glories of the Supreme.
