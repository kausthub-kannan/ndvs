## meaning

Men of vile nature alone seek the pleasure of enjoying women; they suffer from untold miseries; they serve others to eke out their livelihood; they don't find time to eat properly, (and so) eat only late at night; and then, they only lie down and brood over their pitiable body alone. If at all, men could distinguish their self from body, they can surrender to the Lord Raṅganātha whose chest is adorned with the garland of cool tulasī leaves and become His devotees. Then they can enjoy singing His names and dancing in ecstasy — thus by doing such a divine service, they can taste the nectarine bliss. But, alas! The vile persons are mad after (mundane) food alone, and not the divine nectar.

## purport

Āḻvār continues to condemn the nature of saṃsāric men, devoid of understanding of the divine nectar of nāma saṅkīrtana due to their temporary happiness obtained in their world of ignorance. These men are after saṃsāric pleasures which are obtained by pleasuring the indriyas. Indulged in trying to obtain such pleasures, they forget to maintain their body which is granted to perform kaiṅkaryam to Bhagavān. The ignorance which gives birth to such men is from the fact that they are unable to realize that they (the jīvātmā) are different from the body. 

Due to this, they try to please the body alone, giving it the utmost importance. When they lack the knowledge of difference between jīvātmā and śarīra, how can they even know Raṅganātha who is the Self of the jīvātmā itself as Antaryāmī? How can they enjoy the nectar which is obtained by serving that Raṅganātha who always wears a tulasī garland? Devoid of such beautiful spiritual love, the vile persons run behind food and women. They sow the seeds of rebirth, disabling the chances of salvation.
