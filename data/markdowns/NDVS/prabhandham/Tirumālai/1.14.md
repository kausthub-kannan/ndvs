## meaning

In Śrīraṅgam, there are the resounding gardens full of innumerable humming honey-bees; there are also many gardens beautified by many flocks of dancing peacocks; there are also many bowers which rise up to the height of clouds (Kōṇḍal). Especially, Śrīraṅgam is the holy temple-town where the God of gods eternally resides. And so, Śrīraṅgam is extremely beautiful and lovable. Those who do not recite the name of Thiru Araṅgam are, indeed, arrogant people of haughtiness/foolishness. Therefore, Oh people! Remove forcibly the food which they (the ignorant and arrogant men) eat greedily, from their mouth, and serve it to dogs which are better than those arrogant men.

## purport

Āḻvār explains how beautiful Śrīraṅgam is. Śrīraṅgam is situated near the river banks of Kāvērī and its tributary Kollidam. Due to the rich river basin present, the island-like city has beautiful nature and plants. Because of this, several honey bees roam around the place. Peacocks are another unique bird which are always present in Śrīraṅgam. It is not surprising that Śrīraṅgam has such beautiful nature. This is due to the fact that the Īśvara of the universe resides as Raṅganātha, lying on the bed of Ādiśeṣa. Indeed, there cannot be such a place which is so lovable. Those people who refuse to recite the divine name of Thirurangam are arrogant. They are living in ignorance. Āḻvār quotes a statement which can be seen in Brahmāṇḍa Purāṇa in the conversation between Śiva and Nārada. Śiva states to Nārada that those who refuse to chant the divine name of Raṅganātha, deny giving food to them. It's better to give that food to the dogs who reside in Śrīraṅgam rather than giving it to such a cheap personality.
