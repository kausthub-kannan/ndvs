virumbi ninṟētta māṭṭēn

iṟaiyiṟai urugum vaṇṇam

## meaning

I know neither how to praise You nor how to love You adequately, O Lord! Neither am I able to glorify You with my tongue nor follow the scriptures righteously. Yet, how compassionate You are! O Lord of Śrīraṅgam, whose land is always filled with the melodious buzzing of bees, You entered my heart like molten iron, slowly melting it, enabling me to witness Your divine Thirumēṉi. How fortunate are my eyes, which are delighted to behold Your ever-radiant form!

## purport

Āḻvār expresses how fortunate his eyes are to witness the divine form of Raṅganātha. He narrates that he knows not how to praise the Lord, as His guṇas are infinite in nature. When Prajāpati Brahmā himself cannot fully glorify the Lord's form, how can we mere mortals hope to do so? We are unable to show the unadulterated love towards the Lord like the gopīs. When it comes to following the scriptures, which are the divine words of Bhagavān, we are most inaccurate.
 
We fail to perform the prescribed activities, instead engaging in vikarmas that should be avoided. Yet, how compassionate is the Supreme Lord! Āḻvār expresses the saulabhya guṇa of Bhagavān. That Para Vāsudeva, who is the Īśvara of the universe, reclines here on Ādiśeṣa in the form of arcā, ready to accept any service offered to Him. He entered Āḻvār's heart like molten iron, slowly melting it. This ignited divine love in Āḻvār's heart towards Bhagavān, enabling him to witness the Lord's divine form. Āḻvār emphasizes that Bhagavān Himself is the reason for such divine love.
