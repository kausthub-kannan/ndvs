## meaning

You have placed Your crowned head towards the West, and towards the East, You have stretched Your legs, revealing those beautiful lotus feet. To the Northern direction, You have shown Your back, and You sleep on Ādiśeṣa facing South where Laṅkā stands. Oh my! After beholding the Supreme Lord as Śrīraṅganātha, my body melts! What can I do?

## purport

The divine Lord of Śrīraṅgam, Raṅganātha, reclines on the bed of Ādiśeṣa, but what is His posture? Āḻvār narrates this to us. His divine crown rests towards the West, and His lotus feet rest towards the East. To the North, He shows His back, while He faces Laṅkā by looking South. Fulfilling His promise to Vibhīṣaṇa to always watch over his kingdom, Raṅganātha faces towards Laṅkā. The Lord's crown echoes, "The Lord here is the Supreme Brahman." When a devotee beholds the crown, they might fear that Īśvara Himself is present, but the Lord's smile assures that although He is Īśvara, He is also Bhaktavatsala. He indeed yearns to meet us and shower grace upon us. While His right hand gestures towards the crown, indicating His status as Īśvara, His left hand points to His lotus feet. The Lord speaks to us, inviting us to surrender to Him and assuring us that He will take care of our material and spiritual progress. After witnessing such a divine form, how can one remain still? Āḻvār, whose devotion surpasses ours, is unable to maintain composure. His body trembles in ecstasy upon beholding the Thirumēṉi of Raṅganātha.
