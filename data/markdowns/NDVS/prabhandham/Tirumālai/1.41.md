## meaning

If the devotees call You, as "Oh the supreme Lord dwelling in the Paramapada whom even the celestial beings cannot realise! O Lord with your head adorned by honey-laden garlands of Tulasi!" even if it be that they are of vile deeds, and they cause others commit crimes - nevertheless, if they could offer to us, the remnant of their food, it is, indeed, sacred and holy for us.

## purport

Verily, hearken to the words of the Alvar, who speaketh of the Lord who dwelleth eternally in Paramapadham, that celestial abode beyond the ken of even Brahma and the heavenly host. As those perched upon lofty balconies cannot discern the stars above, so too are the denizens of Satyaloka and other celestial realms unable to perceive the Lord of Paramapadham.

Though these celestial beings possess great knowledge and power, 'tis not sufficient to breach the gates of Paramapadham. The word "AgA" herein signifieth both impossibility and prohibition - as a lowly Chandala may not enter sacred grounds, so too are these celestial entities barred from Paramapadham's hallowed realm.

Yet, lo! The Alvars, those blessed souls, have glimpsed the Lord of Paramapadham. This cometh not from their own merit, but through His divine grace. As the Bhagavad Gita doth proclaim, those who fancy themselves as gods cannot perceive Him. The Alvars, like seafarers who cross vast oceans by ship, traverse the tumultuous sea of Samsara with Vishnu as their vessel. The celestial beings, in their pride, are as those who would attempt to swim across unaided.

The Alvars, in their wisdom, have surrendered fully to the Lord, recognizing Him as both the means and the end. They chant, "O Thou adorned with a honey dripping Tulasi garland upon Thy crown!" - proclaiming His supremacy among the gods and His sweetness that surpasseth all earthly delights.

Even those who once committed grievous sins - binding and slaughtering creatures for base sustenance - if they should come to know the Lord and take refuge in Him, they too may be redeemed. For the knowledge of the Divine transformeth the heart, turning even the most cruel to compassion. Alvar speaketh of the sanctifying power of the food remnants of the truly wise. As Parasara Bhattar once gifted his disciple Nanjiyer with such blessed morsels, so too can these remnants purify the partaker instantly, like a philosopher's stone transmuting base metal to gold. Thus doth the Alvar extol the greatness of the Lord and His devotees, showing that the path to salvation lieth not in celestial power, but in humble devotion and the grace of the Divine.
