## meaning

Oh the Lord of Śrirangam girt by seven Prakāras (ramparts)! I surmise that You have instructed the followers of Vedic lore as "O the reciters of the Vedas! O you who follow the age-old customs of your lineage faultlessly! even though the devotees of my feet have been born in the lowliest class (of the society), you have to bow to them; you offer to them whatever you know and whatever you can; you learn from them; treat them on par with me."

## purport

Álvár calls upon the learned scholars, the Brāhmaṇas who have studied the four Vedas, to transcend the ego associated with being born into an upper caste and to eliminate the pride of possessing greater knowledge than others. He explains that discrimination based on appearance and birth is meaningless and should be abandoned, as such prejudices stem from the belief that materialistic goals are the ultimate pursuits. When the jīvātmā is eternal and born into this world through karma, assigned to a specific gender and clan, how could this matter to a mumukṣhu who has realized their true nature as śeṣa of Bhagavān? This is the essence of Álvár's teaching. Every jīvātmā has Bhagavān as the inner witness. Consequently, a prappanna will perceive Bhagavān everywhere. Why would such a person harm another simply due to their birth? A person with wealth, knowledge, or prestigious lineage may easily develop ego, as illustrated in the traditional saying: vidhyāmadho dhanamadhaḥ tṛtīyo'bhijano madhaḥ: ēthē madhāvaliptānām ētha ēva satām damaḥ. Therefore, Álvár advises against taking pride in one's language or acquired wisdom, recognizing that such knowledge is ultimately a gift from Bhagavān. 

He further elaborates that speaking ill of others is unbecoming of a true Bhāgavata, and criticizing devotees of Bhagavān—particularly regarding their birth or background—is a grave transgression. Bhagavān himself teaches us to engage with his devotees: to share spiritual wisdom, show respect, and treat them as equivalent to the divine. Every devotee encountered should be honored as a manifestation of Bhagavān, for the divine resides within these devoted souls. Nammálvár's words in Thiruvāymoḻi 7.10 beautifully capture this sentiment: tīrththaṅgaḷē eṉṟu pūsiththu nalgiyuraippar tham dēviyarkē, even Nitya sūris speak about such great devotees! The teaching continues, niṉṉōdum okka vaḻipada aruḷiṉāy, suggesting that the devotees of Raṅganātha should be revered equally. These spiritual seekers must be respected as Ācāryas, regardless of their background or learning. Their sacred presence should be welcomed with utmost reverence and joy. This pasurama glorifies sripada teertham of Bhagavatas which have to taken with devotion and respect.
