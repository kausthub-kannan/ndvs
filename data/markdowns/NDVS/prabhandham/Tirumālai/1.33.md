## english_scripts

mey ellām pōga viṭṭu
viri kuzalāril paṭṭu
poy ellām podindu koṇḍa
pōzkkanēn vandu ninṟēn
aiyanē ! araṅganē !
un aruḻ ennum āśai tannāl
poyyanēn vandu ninṟēn
poyyanēn poyyanēnē

## meaning

Oh Lord! O Śri Ranganatha! I have completely abandoned truthfulness on thought, word and deed. I have been ensnared by damsels of beautiful locks of hair, and I have experienced untold miseries. I am a shameless person abounding in falsehood, and falsehood alone. Though I am falsehood incarnate, I have come to You, with the desire to obtain your grace. Nevertheless, I spoke untruth; even now, I am only untruthful.

## purport

The Āḻvār discusses the concept of mey (truth), defining it as that which leads to good for all jīvātmās and describes things as they are. He cites the Kaiśika Māhātmyam, where Nampāduvān declares the primacy of truth in sustaining the world.

The Āḻvār confesses, "ellām pōgaviṭṭu" (I got rid of all truths), implying he lacks truthfulness even towards relatives. He laments speaking lies to the omniscient Lord Himself.
When questioned about the source of his deceit, the Āḻvār attributes it to his association with women: "viri kuḻalāril paṭṭu" (entangled with women of flowing hair). He elaborates on how impossible it is to speak truth in women's company, comparing it to the impossibility of lying in the Lord's presence. Āḻvār describes himself as a repository of falsehoods, contrasting with his earlier renunciation of all truths. Addressing himself as "pōtkanēn" (one without refuge), the Āḻvār stands before the Lord despite his unworthiness, drawn by their inseverable relationship. He refers to the Lord as "Aiyane" and "Araṅgane", acknowledging this bond and the Lord's presence in Śrīraṅgam.

The Āḻvār expresses his desire for the Lord's grace: "un aruḷ eṉṉum āsai taṉṉāl vantu niṉṟēṉ" (I came standing with the desire for your grace). He cites the Rāmāyaṇa to illustrate the Lord's compassion even towards the undeserving. Finally, the Āḻvār admits that even his confession might be false: "poyyaṉēṉ poyyaṉēṉē" (I am a liar, indeed a liar). He references the Ahirbudhnya Saṃhitā, suggesting that acknowledging one's faults before the Lord can yield benefits. However, he questions the sincerity of his own admission, wondering how many lies he can tell in the divine presence where only truth should be spoken. This passage beautifully captures the Āḻvār's deep introspection, his struggle with truthfulness, and his complex relationship with the divine, all expressed through rich scriptural allusions and poetic imagery.
