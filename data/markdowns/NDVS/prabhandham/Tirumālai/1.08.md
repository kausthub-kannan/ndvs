## english_scripts

veṟuppoḍu śamaṇar muṇḍar
vidi il śākkiyargaḻ
nin pāl poṟuppariyanagaḻ pēśil
pōvadē nōyadāgi
kuṟippenakkaḍaiyum āgil
kūḍumēl talaiyai āṅgē
aṟuppadē karumam kaṇḍāy
araṅga mā nagaruḻānē !

## meaning

Oh Lord of the great city, Śrīraṅgam! If at all the bitter Jains and Śaivites and the unlucky Buddhists (Śākyas) are to deliver unendurable tenets of adharma and to insult you, O Lord, they will certainly perish and their faiths will not last long, because their speech itself becomes the incurable illness. And this is my wish: I would surely chop off their heads, if I am able and eligible, for they do not censure me alone, but even insult the supreme Godhead, Lord Raṅganātha.

## purport

Continuing from the previous pāsuram, Āḷvār expresses his anger towards those who speak ill of the Lord. For Āḷvārs, Raṅganātha was their dear child, their husband, their everything. In this pāsuram, Āḷvār speaks as sharp as a sword to those who speak ill of the Lord. Just as it is not possible for a mother to hear her child being spoken ill of by someone, Āḷvār cannot tolerate when others deny the Parattva of Nārāyaṇa. Āḷvār calls them unlucky and unfortunate. They are indeed following adharma by insulting Bhagavān. When the Vedas themselves are focused towards that Raṅganātha, insulting him is adharmic without a doubt. These will certainly perish as their speech itself becomes a disease to those fools! Unable to tolerate such words, Āḷvār is ready to slay them if he had the chance to do so, i.e., if Āḷvār were eligible to do so. But doing that would be asāttvic and would not bring glory to our Lord. It's clear from this pāsuram that Toṇṭaraṭippoṭi Āḷvār (and other Āḷvārs as well) cannot tolerate anyone who insults Raṅganātha, the Nātha of the entire universe.
