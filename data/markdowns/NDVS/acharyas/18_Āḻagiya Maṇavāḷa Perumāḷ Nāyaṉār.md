## bio

Āḻagiya Maṇavāḷa Perumāḷ Nāyaṉār was the younger brother of Piḷḷai Lōkācārya, and both were sons of Vaḍakku Tiruvīḍhi Piḷḷai. Their family lived in Śrīraṅgam, where Nāyaṉār grew up surrounded by the teachings of Nampiḷḷai and other distinguished ācāryas. He studied under his father and was deeply grounded in the Divya Prabandham and the philosophical teachings transmitted through the Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition. Traditional accounts state that both brothers remained committed to a life of celibacy and dedicated themselves to the study and propagation of their sampradāya.

Nāyaṉār’s most celebrated work is Ācārya Hṛdayam, a profound treatise that explores the inner meaning of Nammāḻvār’s Tiruvāymoḻi. Its title, meaning “The Heart of the Ācārya,” refers to the inner intent and spiritual experience expressed through the Āḻvār’s hymns. Composed in Maṇipravāḷam, a blend of Tamil and Sanskrit, the work draws extensively on the language of the Divya Prabandham to explain its philosophical depth. It also develops themes found in Piḷḷai Lōkācārya’s Śrīvacana Bhūṣaṇam, showing how the devotional expressions of the Āḻvārs communicate profound theological truths.

Alongside this work, Nāyaṉār composed commentaries on several important hymns of the Āḻvārs. His commentary on Āṇḍāḷ’s Tiruppāvai, known as the Āṟāyirappaḍi or 6,000-grantham commentary, explains the verses in the light of the wider teachings of the tradition. His commentaries on Tiruppāṇāḻvār’s Amalanādipirān and Madhurakavi Āḻvār’s Kaṇṇinuṇ Ciṟuttāmpu likewise explore the relationship between devotional experience, the Lord’s divine form and the role of the ācārya. In Aruḷicceyal Rahasyam, he explains the meaning of the three sacred teachings, the Rahasya-traya, through expressions drawn from the Āḻvārs’ hymns.

Nāyaṉār’s scholarship was distinguished by his ability to explain complex ideas through the words and experiences of the Āḻvārs themselves. Rather than treating the Divya Prabandham merely as devotional poetry, he demonstrated how its verses convey a carefully integrated understanding of the jīvātmā, the Supreme Lord, surrender and service. His writings preserve the connection between the emotional richness of the Āḻvārs’ hymns and the philosophical teachings developed by the ācāryas.

Nāyaṉār passed away at Śrīraṅgam, traditionally said to have attained Paramapadam at a relatively young age. His elder brother Piḷḷai Lōkācārya is remembered as having deeply mourned his loss, a reflection of the close bond between the brothers and the esteem in which Nāyaṉār’s learning was held. His works, especially Ācārya Hṛdayam, remain important resources for understanding Nammāḻvār’s Tiruvāymoḻi and the theological insights preserved in the Divya Prabandham.
