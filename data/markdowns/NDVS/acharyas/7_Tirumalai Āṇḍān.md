## bio

Tirumālaiyāṇḍān was one of the fifteen disciples who took refuge at the feet of Āḻavandār, and he is remembered as the teacher who taught Rāmānuja the meaning of the _Tiruvāymoḻi_. In Āḻavandār’s last days, he was among the senior disciples, along with Tiruvaraṅga Perumāḷ Araiyar, Periya Nambi, and Tirukkōṣṭiyūr Nambi, who asked their ācārya for the deepest teaching on surrender (_prapatti_). Āḻavandār told them to seek the grace of a true ācārya, surrender to the Lord in faith, live in remembrance of the lineage of teachers, and perform the obligatory and permitted services without fail.

His best-known role came after Rāmānuja had learnt the meaning of the _tirumantra_ and the _carama śloka_ from Tirukkōṣṭiyūr Nambi. Rāmānuja asked Periya Nambi to bless him with the teaching of the _Tiruvāymoḻi_. Periya Nambi was pleased and sent him first to Tiruvaraṅga Perumāḷ Araiyar for the “good words” (_nal vārttaikaḷ_) and a first instruction in the _Tiruvāymoḻi_. After that, he directed Rāmānuja to learn its full meaning from Tirumālaiyāṇḍān. Rāmānuja approached him with humility, and Āṇḍān taught him the meaning of the _Tiruvāymoḻi_ verse by verse in daily sessions.

The tradition remembers one session in particular. While explaining the verse _aṟiyāk kālattuḷḷē_ (2.3.3), Āṇḍān gave the interpretation that Nammāḻvār grieves because the Lord, after awakening in him the knowledge of his relationship to Him, placed him again in a body that hides that knowledge. Rāmānuja respectfully offered a different reading. He pointed out that the verses before and after are full of joy, so a sorrowful reading seemed out of place, and suggested that the verse speaks of joy: in the ignorant days of his youth, the Lord had created in the Āḻvār a love of service, and he is grateful for it. Āṇḍān was delighted, and shared this new interpretation with Tirukkōṭṭiyūr Nambi and Periya Nambi. They said that they had heard this very meaning from Āḻavandār himself, and concluded that Rāmānuja had received Āḻavandār’s full blessing. They compared his learning of the verse to Lord Kṛṣṇa learning from the sage Sāndīpani. Āṇḍān agreed, went with them to Rāmānuja’s maṭha, and the three blessed and praised him. Āṇḍān then continued teaching the rest of the _Tiruvāymoḻi_.

He is mentioned again at the end of his life. Along with Tirukkōṣṭiyūr Nambi and Tiruvaraṅga Perumāḷ Araiyar, he attained Paramapadam, and Periya Tirumalai Nambi, on hearing the news, performed the final rites for them. His contribution lay in teaching Rāmānuja the _Tiruvāymoḻi_, and in recognising, through the session on _aṟiyāk kālattuḷḷē_, that Rāmānuja’s insight carried the mind of Āḻavandār.
