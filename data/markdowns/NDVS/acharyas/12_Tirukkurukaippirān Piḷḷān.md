## bio

Tirukkurukaippirān Piḷḷān, known in the source as Kurukeśar, was the second son of Periya Tirumalai Nambi and one of the foremost disciples of Rāmānuja. According to the traditional account, he is revered as an incarnation of Pracaṇḍa, one of the gatekeepers (_dvārapālakas_) of Śrī Vaikuṇṭham. His father named him simply “Piḷḷān.” Unlike other infants, he neither wept nor smiled, but watched with deep attention whenever his father taught the meanings of the Divya Prabandham. Nambi was surprised and pleased, and felt sure that the child would one day be as devout and famous as his nephew Rāmānuja. Piḷḷān learnt his letters at the proper age and absorbed his lessons quickly, and he was especially bright in learning the Prabandham. When Śrīvaiṣṇavas visiting Tirumala from Śrīraṅgam described the glory of Rāmānuja, the child listened and talked about it to himself with joy.

After his upanayanam and marriage, his father sent him to Śrīraṅgam with Kiḍāmbi Āccāṉ, who longed to take refuge at Rāmānuja’s feet. They stopped at Kāñcī to worship Lord Varadarāja and then reached Śrīraṅgam. When Rāmānuja saw the boy, he asked, “Is this Piḷḷān?” and blessed him that the grace of Āḻavandār and of his father would be with him unasked. Piḷḷān later became a disciple at Rāmānuja’s feet.

The tradition remembers one day in particular. Rāmānuja was walking alone in his maṭha, deep in thought, silently enacting the scene of a pāsuram. Piḷḷān, watching through a gap in the door, asked quietly whether his ācārya was reliving the _Tiruvāymoḻi_ verse _tirumālirumcōlai eṉṟēṉ eṉṉa_. Rāmānuja was astonished and asked, “Is this my Piḷḷān?” He opened the door and called him in. He then told Periya Nambi, Kūrattāḻvān, and the others that Piḷḷān had deep knowledge of the _Śrībhāṣya_ from childhood and great reverence for devotees, and that the way he had named the very verse in his mind showed that he was fully qualified for the grace of Periya Nambi. He declared him his _jñāna putra_ (“son in knowledge”) and gave him another name of Nammāḻvār, Tirukkurukaippirān. From then on he was known as Tirukkurukaippirān Piḷḷān.

Rāmānuja asked him to write a commentary on the _Tiruvāymoḻi_ of about six thousand _granthas_. Piḷḷān wrote it and, instead of composing a new opening verse, used the same invocatory verse as the _Śrībhāṣya_. Rāmānuja read it, was greatly pleased with its detail, and said that the name he had given him was fully justified. He directed that it be taught through _kālakṣepa_ like the _Śrībhāṣya_, and named it _Bhagavad Viṣayam_. The commentary is known as the _Ārāyirappaḍi_. Periya Nambi, hearing of it, said Āḻavandār’s wish had been fulfilled. Piḷḷān’s father, Periya Tirumalai Nambi, had himself pointed him out to Rāmānuja at Tirumala as fit for his special blessing and given him as a disciple.

When Rāmānuja established order among his disciples, he named four chief teachers: Piḷḷān, Nāḍādūr Āḻvān, Kiḍāmbi Āccāṉ, and Mudaliyāṇḍān. He gave them the _Śrībhāṣya_ and directed them to teach it. Piḷḷān received an additional charge, to teach _Bhagavad Viṣayam_, and so became the _ubhaya siṁhāsanādhipati_, the master of both lines of teaching. Rāmānuja also named him his successor, to teach after his own passing. When Rāmānuja was near the end of his life, he handed all his disciples into Piḷḷān’s care and told them to study with him. He lay with his head on Piḷḷān’s lap and his feet on Kiḍāmbi Āccāṉ’s, and, meditating on Periya Nambi’s feet, ascended to Paramapadam. Piḷḷān performed his final rites, in the manner followed for Āḻavandār, and later consecrated the image of Rāmānuja at Śrīraṅgam. As Rāmānuja had wished, the disciples gave Piḷḷān great respect and affection, and he led the teaching of the tradition with distinction.

He also offered Rāmānuja two verses of praise that are still recited. One is _sarvadeśa-daśākāleṣv avyāhata-parākramā | rāmānujāryadivyājñā vardhatām abhivardhatām_, a prayer that Rāmānuja’s sacred command may grow ever greater, unobstructed in every place and time. He also presented a verse naming all the Āḻvārs, _bhūtaṁ saraś ca_, which Rāmānuja asked to be recited at the start of the Prabandham.

He is credited with the one who wrote the _Ārāyirappaḍi_ commentary on the _Tiruvāymoḻi_. His contribution lay in this first great commentary on the Āḻvār’s work, in his role as Rāmānuja’s successor, and in the way he combined deep learning with humility toward devotees. He is traditionally revered as the ācārya who carried Rāmānuja’s teaching into the next generation.
