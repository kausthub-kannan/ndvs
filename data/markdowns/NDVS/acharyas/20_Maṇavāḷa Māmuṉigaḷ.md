## bio

Maṇavāḷa Māmunigaḷ, also known by the names Varavaramuni and Yatīndra Pravaṇa, was a distinguished Śrīvaiṣṇava ācārya renowned for his devotion to Śrī Rāmānuja, his scholarship in the Divya Prabandham, and his efforts to preserve the teachings of the earlier ācāryas. His life was devoted to studying, teaching and explaining the works of the Āḻvārs and the theological writings of the ācārya lineage. Much of the traditional account of his life is preserved in Yatīndra Pravaṇa Prabhāvam, a work attributed to Piḷḷai Lōkam Jīyar.

He was born as Aḻagiya Maṇavāḷa Perumāḷ Nāyaṉār in the region of Āḻvār Tirunagari, to Tirunāvīṟuṭaiya Pirān Tādar Aṇṇar and Śrīraṅga Nācciyār. His father was learned in the sacred texts and introduced him to the Vedas, Vedānta and the Nālāyira Divya Prabandham. From an early age, Māmunigaḷ received a thorough grounding in the scriptural and devotional traditions of the Śrīvaiṣṇavas. He later sought the guidance of Tiruvāymoḻippiḷḷai, under whom he studied the works of the Āḻvārs, their commentaries and the teachings of the earlier ācāryas. This association shaped the direction of his life and scholarship.

At the instruction of Tiruvāymoḻippiḷḷai, Māmunigaḷ composed Yatirāja Viṃśati, a Sanskrit hymn of twenty verses expressing his reverence for Śrī Rāmānuja. He was also entrusted with the worship and service of the shrine dedicated to Rāmānuja at Āḻvār Tirunagari. His teacher encouraged him to devote himself to explaining the Divya Prabandham and the philosophical teachings of the earlier ācāryas. After his teacher’s passing, Māmunigaḷ travelled to Śrīraṅgam, where he continued his studies, taught disciples and undertook service to Śrī Raṅganātha.

At Śrīraṅgam, Māmunigaḷ became known for his extensive discourses on the Divya Prabandham and the writings of the earlier ācāryas. He took sannyāsa and devoted himself more fully to teaching and religious service. Traditional accounts state that he was given the name Aḻagiya Maṇavāḷa Māmunigaḷ in connection with Śrī Raṅganātha. His residence became a centre of learning, where disciples gathered to study the sacred texts and their commentaries. Among his well-known disciples were Vāṉamāmalai Jīyar, Tiruvēṅkaṭa Rāmānuja Jīyar, Kōyil Aṇṇan, Prativādi Bhayaṅkaram Aṇṇan, Eṟumpi Appā, Appiḷḷai and Appiḷḷār.

Māmunigaḷ’s literary contributions include original compositions as well as commentaries on the works of earlier teachers. Upadēśa Ratna Mālai presents the lineage of the Āḻvārs and ācāryas and highlights their contributions to the preservation of the tradition. Tiruvāymoḻi Nūṟṟandādi celebrates Nammāḻvār’s Tiruvāymoḻi, while Ārtti Prabandham expresses his longing for the opportunity to serve the Lord without separation. He also composed Dēvarāja Maṅgalam in praise of Varadarāja of Kāñcīpuram and Tiruvārādhana Kramam, concerning the procedure of daily worship. His commentaries on works such as Mumukṣuppaḍi, Tattvatrayam and Śrīvacana Bhūṣaṇam helped explain difficult theological teachings for later generations.

One particularly significant episode in the traditional account of his life concerns his discourses on Nammāḻvār’s Tiruvāymoḻi at Śrīraṅgam. Māmunigaḷ is said to have delivered an extended series of lectures explaining the work through the traditional commentaries. The episode reflects the importance he placed on preserving not only the verses themselves but also the accumulated explanations transmitted by earlier teachers. His work in this area helped ensure that the literary and theological heritage of the Āḻvārs remained central to the education of subsequent generations of Śrīvaiṣṇava scholars.

Towards the end of his life, Māmunigaḷ composed Ārtti Prabandham, a deeply personal work expressing his longing to reach the divine abode and continue his service to the Lord. The text is remembered for its earnest devotional tone and its emphasis on the value of service to the Lord and the ācārya. Traditional accounts place his passing at Śrīraṅgam, where he had spent much of his life teaching, composing and serving. His legacy endures through his writings, his disciples and the continuing study of the Āḻvārs’ hymns and the earlier ācāryas’ commentaries.
