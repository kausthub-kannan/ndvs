Tirukkōṣṭiyūr Nambi, named for his home at Tirukkōṣṭiyūr, was one of the fifteen disciples who took refuge at the feet of Āḻavandār, and he is remembered as the teacher who gave Rāmānuja the meaning of the two most precious mantras of the tradition. In Āḻavandār’s last days, he was among the senior disciples, along with Tiruvaraṅga Perumāḷ Araiyar and Periya Nambi, who asked their ācārya for the deepest teaching on surrender (_prapatti_). Āḻavandār told them to seek the grace of a true ācārya, surrender to the Lord in faith, live in remembrance of the lineage of teachers, and perform the obligatory and permitted services without fail.

His best-known role came after Rāmānuja became a disciple of Periya Nambi. Periya Nambi directed Rāmānuja to learn the meaning of the _tirumantra_ (the eight-syllable mantra) and the _carama śloka_ from Tirukkōṣṭiyūr Nambi. Rāmānuja went to Tirukkōṣṭiyūr, reached Nambi’s house, and prostrated, asking for the teaching. Nambi, wishing to impress the supreme importance of these mantras and to test the sincerity of the student, said he would teach it later and sent him away. Rāmānuja returned to Śrīraṅgam, and the same thing happened seventeen times. On one visit to Śrīraṅgam, some elders there expressed their sorrow at how strictly Nambi was testing him. Nambi said nothing, returned home, and sent a Śrīvaiṣṇava to bring Rāmānuja to Tirukkōṣṭiyūr.

Rāmānuja came with Kūrattāzhvān, Naḍādūr Āzhvān, and Mudaliyāṇḍān, asked the three to wait elsewhere, and went to Nambi alone. Nambi made one condition, that the meanings should not be taught to anyone else, and then taught him the meaning of the _tirumantra_ and the _carama śloka_. The next morning, Rāmānuja taught the special meaning of the _tirumantra_ to a crowd from the top of the temple tower at Tirukkōṣṭiyūr. Nambi heard of it, called him, and said he was displeased: an ācārya’s meaning is meant to be given in privacy, and disobeying the ācārya carries serious consequences. Rāmānuja replied that for breaking his teacher’s order, hell would be his alone, but all who heard the meaning would be freed, since they had come near the feet of the ācārya who gave it. Nambi was moved by his broad compassion and said, “We know that Emperumān is an ocean of grace; now we see the same in Udaiyavar, so he is Emperumānār.” He added that the fault of disobeying a teacher would not apply to Rāmānuja, given his noble intent and his wish to spread the tradition. From then on, the Viśiṣṭādvaita tradition has been called _Emperumānār darśanam_, and Rāmānuja was known as Emperumānār.

He is shown again, alongside Periya Nambi, when the two heard of the attempt to poison Rāmānuja at Śrīraṅgam, and both set out at once, deeply worried. When Rāmānuja saw them crossing the Kāveri, he prostrated again and again on the burning midday sand. The two ācāryas did not stop him. Kiḍāmbi Āccāṉ, unable to bear it, lifted him up and asked whether a teacher’s testing must go so far. Nambi told Āccāṉ that they had been testing whether anyone in Rāmānuja’s company would care for him, and were glad to see that Āccāṉ would. He ordered Āccāṉ to take responsibility for Rāmānuja’s meals from then on. Later, Tirukkōṣṭiyūr Nambi attained Paramapadam, along with Tirumālaiyāṇḍān and Tiruvaraṅga Perumāḷ Araiyar. Periya Tirumalai Nambi, on hearing of it, performed the final rites for them.

 His contribution lay in testing and then teaching Rāmānuja the meaning of the two central mantras, and in drawing out of that encounter the title “Emperumānār” that gave the tradition its name.
