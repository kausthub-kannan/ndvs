## bio

Maṇakkāl Nambi, whose personal name was Śrī Rāmamiśra, was the principal disciple of Uyyakkoṇḍār and the teacher who carried the Śrīvaiṣṇava teaching on to Āḻavandār. According to the traditional account, he is revered as an incarnation of Kumuda, one of the gatekeepers (_dvārapālakas_) of Śrī Vaikuṇṭham. He was born in the agrahāram of Maṇakkāl, on the banks of the Kāveri, east of Śrīraṅgam in the Chola country, and was popularly called Maṇakkāl Nambi after his birthplace. He learnt the Vedas and Vedānta thoroughly by serving his ācārya, Uyyakkoṇḍār, and when Uyyakkoṇḍār's wife passed away, he also took charge of cooking for his teacher.

His devotion to his ācārya is shown in a well-known episode. The young girls of the village once went to the Kāveri to bathe, eat the food they had brought, and play. Uyyakkoṇḍār's two daughters wished to join them and sought his permission, and at his direction Maṇakkāl Nambi accompanied them to help as needed. On the way home, the girls lost their way and were afraid to cross a canal. Seeing this, Nambi lay down across the canal, covering both banks, and asked them to walk over his back. They crossed safely, and he left them near the house, saying that he would bathe and return. Before he came back, Uyyakkoṇḍār had learnt of the whole incident and was greatly pleased. When Nambi returned and paid his respects, the ācārya praised him and blessed him. Nambi replied with the words of the Āḻvār, _"uṟṟēṉ ukantu paṇi ceytu uṉpātam peṟṟēṉ, īdē yiṉṉam vēṇṭuvadentāy"_ ("by serving you gladly I have gained your feet, and I want nothing more"). Uyyakkoṇḍār was delighted and blessed him again.

Nambi's most important role came from a charge given to Uyyakkoṇḍār by Nāthamunigaḷ, to instruct his grandson Yamunaitturaivan (later Āḻavandār) in the Śrīvaiṣṇava teaching. Since Uyyakkoṇḍār feared his life might end before the child was of age, he delegated the task to Maṇakkāl Nambi. Nambi waited for the right time. Hearing that the young Yamunaitturaivan had won a debate against the court scholar Akkiyāḻvān and now ruled half the Chola kingdom, he went to his city, but found it impossible to meet the king amid his royal duties. He then befriended the Brahmin cooks of the royal kitchen and learnt that a special spinach (_tūtuḷai kīrai_) was among Āḻavandār's favourite foods. For six months he brought it to the cooks daily, but still could not gain an audience. Then he stopped bringing it for three days. The king asked why it was missing and, on hearing of the old Brahmin who had supplied it, asked that he be brought when he next came. On seeing Nambi's radiance, the king rose to greet him, thanked him, and offered him any gift he wished. Nambi replied that he had come not to receive a gift but to give one, a treasure left for the king by his ancestors, and asked that he be allowed to meet him without interruption for teaching. The king agreed.

From the next day Nambi began instructing him, first in the inner meanings of the Bhagavad Gītā, and Āḻavandār came to understand the secret teaching of Kṛṣṇa to Arjuna on the battlefield. He regretted having wasted his life without knowledge of the true principles (_tattvas_), and his interest in royal pomp began to fade. He accepted Nambi as his ācārya, and Nambi continued with the meaning of the _carama śloka_. At the end of these lessons, he took Āḻavandār to Śrīraṅgam. Seeing Lord Raṅganātha there, Āḻavandār wondered how anyone wise could have missed such a sight. Nambi then told him that the Lord of Śrīraṅgam was the imperishable treasure (_nidhi_) his ancestors had kept for him, and asked him to enjoy and protect it. Overcome with joy, Āḻavandār took sannyāsa, settled in Śrīraṅgam, and began teaching the Viśiṣṭādvaita tradition.

Later, when Nambi felt that his own last days were near, he asked Āḻavandār to go to Kurugaikkāvalappan, to whom Nāthamunigaḷ had passed the secret of yoga (_yogarahasya_), and receive it from him. Āḻavandār went to Vīranārāyaṇapuram but, as the text relates, was unable to finally receive the teaching, and the practice was lost to later generations. Maṇakkāl Nambi lived in Śrīraṅgam and taught the deeper meanings of the tradition to his disciples, among them Āḻavandār, Deivattukkarasu Nambi, Gomaṭattu Tiruviṇṇagarappan, and Ciṟupuḷḷūrāvuḍaiya Piḷḷai Ācchi. He is not known to have composed any written works. His contribution lay in his devoted service to his teacher, his patient and resourceful effort to carry the teaching to Āḻavandār, and his preservation of the unbroken line of transmission. He is traditionally revered as the vital link between Uyyakkoṇḍār and Āḻavandār.
