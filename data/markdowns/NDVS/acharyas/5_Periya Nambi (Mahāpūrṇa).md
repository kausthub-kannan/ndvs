## bio

Periya Nambi, whose formal name was Mahāpūrṇar, was the foremost disciple of Āḻavandār and the teacher through whom Rāmānuja received the Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition. According to the traditional account, he was born at Śrīraṅgam in the Bhāradvāja gotra and is revered as an incarnation of Kumudākṣa, one of the gatekeepers (_dvārapālakas_) of Śrī Vaikuṇṭham. As his ācārya instructed, he remained at Śrīraṅgam teaching the traditional texts of Viśiṣṭādvaita. His six disciples were Emperumānār (Rāmānuja), Malai Kuniya Niṉṟār, Ārya Śrī Śaṭhakopa Dāsar, Aṇiyaraṅgattamudaṉār, Tiruvāikkulamudaiyān Bhaṭṭar, and Tirukkacchi Nambi.

His part in Rāmānuja’s story begins in Āḻavandār’s last days. Āḻavandār, who had blessed Rāmānuja from a distance without ever speaking to him, grew ill and wished to see him. He asked Periya Nambi to bring him from Kāñcī to Śrīraṅgam. At Kāñcī, Periya Nambi waited on the path by which Rāmānuja carried the sacred water for the Lord’s worship, reciting the _Stotraratna_ verse _brahmā śivaḥ śatamakhaḥ_, which says that even Brahmā, Śiva, and Indra are but drops in the ocean of the Lord’s glory. Rāmānuja asked who had composed so wonderful a verse. On learning it was Āḻavandār, he longed to meet him and set out with Periya Nambi. But Āḻavandār had already passed away before they reached Śrīraṅgam, and Rāmānuja could only pay homage to his body. There, as the disciples explained the three wishes the master had left unfulfilled, Rāmānuja vowed to fulfil them. Afterward, Āḻavandār’s disciples asked Periya Nambi to make Rāmānuja the leader of the tradition, and he agreed.

He set out for Kāñcī with his wife. At Madurāntakam, as it happened, Rāmānuja was making his way to Śrīraṅgam to ask Nambi to accept him as his disciple. This was in keeping with the six teachings given through Tirukkacchi Nambi from Lord Varadarāja, the last of which was that Rāmānuja should seek Periya Nambi as his ācārya. They met at the shrine of Eri Kātta Rāmar. Rāmānuja fell at Nambi’s feet and said, “I am your disciple, I have taken refuge in you.” Nambi performed the five sacraments (_pañca saṃskāra_) for him on the spot, beneath the _magiḻa_ tree, and taught him the secret mantras and the meaning of the _dvaya_. The shrine is therefore remembered as the place where the _dvaya_ took root. Periya Nambi then stayed at Rāmānuja’s home in Kāñcī, where Rāmānuja served him and his wife with great devotion. Nambi taught him the Vyāsa Sūtras and their special meanings, and the Divya Prabandham except for the _Tiruvāymoḻi_.

During this stay, however, Rāmānuja’s wife wounded the household’s honour in three ways: she refused to feed a hungry devotee, treated Tirukkacchi Nambi’s seat with contempt, and quarrelled with Nambi’s wife at the well while Rāmānuja was away. Periya Nambi, thinking it improper to stay, left for Śrīraṅgam with his wife without taking leave. Rāmānuja, learning what had happened, later gave up household life and took sannyāsa. In time, Rāmānuja came to Śrīraṅgam and again asked Periya Nambi for the deeper teachings. Nambi taught him the special meanings and then directed him to Tirukkōṭṭiyūr Nambi for the meaning of the _tirumantra_ and the _carama śloka_, and to Tiruvaraṅga Perumāḷ Araiyar and Tirumālaiyāṇḍān for the _Tiruvāymoḻi_. He took joy in seeing Rāmānuja grow into the tradition’s future leader. His daughter Attuḻāy once came to him complaining of ill treatment by her mother-in-law, and he told her to take it to Rāmānuja, who sent his disciple Mudaliyāṇḍān to serve her.

His life ended during the persecution by the Chola king, who forced Śrīvaiṣṇava scholars to sign a statement that there is no god greater than Śiva. When the king’s soldiers came to Śrīraṅgam to bring Rāmānuja and his companions, Kūrattāzhvān went in Rāmānuja’s place, wearing his robes, and Periya Nambi went with him. Before the king, both held firm that Nārāyaṇa alone is the supreme Lord. Kūrattāzhvān is said to have written a clever reply on the palm leaf, and Periya Nambi answered with many proofs from scripture. Furious, the king ordered both blinded. Kūrattāzhvān pulled out his own eyes, and Periya Nambi lost his sight. His daughter Attuḻāy guided them from the court. Weak with age and pain, Periya Nambi lay with his head on Kūrattāzhvān’s lap and his feet on his daughter’s. Both lamented that he would die far from Śrīraṅgam, in the Chola capital. He replied, citing the verse that wherever a Vaiṣṇava dies, the Lord of the Garuḍa banner at once comes to that place to bless him. He asked whether any place could be nobler than the lap of a devotee like Kūrattāzhvān. He then placed the feet of his ācārya Āḻavandār on his head and eyes and, in deep sorrow, ascended to Paramapadam. Kūrattāzhvān and Attuḻāy performed his final rites with the help of local Śrīvaiṣṇavas. When Rāmānuja later heard of it in Melkote, he grieved and performed the rites of mourning.

His contribution lay in bringing Rāmānuja to the tradition, in teaching him the foundations of the doctrine, and in giving his life for the faith. He is traditionally revered as the ācārya of Rāmānuja and the one who carried Āḻavandār’s wish to fulfilment.
