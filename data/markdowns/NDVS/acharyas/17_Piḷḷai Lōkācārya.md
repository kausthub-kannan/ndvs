## bio

Piḷḷai Lōkācārya was a distinguished ācārya of the Śrīvaiṣṇava tradition, remembered for his concise and profound exposition of the teachings of Śrī Rāmānuja and the Āḻvārs. His name, Lōkācārya, means “teacher of the world,” reflecting the enduring importance of his works in communicating the essential principles of the sampradāya. He lived during the period when Śrīvaiṣṇava learning flourished under the guidance of Nampiḷḷai and his disciples.

Piḷḷai Lōkācārya was the elder son of Vaḍakku Tiruvīḍhi Piḷḷai, one of the foremost disciples of Nampiḷḷai. His younger brother was Aḻagiya Maṇavāḷa Perumāḷ Nāyaṉār, who also became a respected ācārya and composed the Ācārya Hṛdayam. According to traditional accounts, Nampiḷḷai had a significant role in the circumstances surrounding Lōkācārya’s birth, and his father named the child in honour of his own teacher. Raised in an environment of scriptural learning and devotion, Lōkācārya dedicated his life to the service of the Lord and the preservation of the teachings received through the ācārya lineage.

His principal contribution was a collection of theological treatises traditionally known as the Aṣṭādaśa Rahasyas, or the Eighteen Esoteric Teachings. Composed largely in Maṇipravāḷam, a literary style combining Tamil and Sanskrit, these works present complex philosophical and spiritual ideas in a concise and systematic manner. Among them, Tattvatrayam explains the three fundamental realities: cit, the sentient beings; acit, the insentient world; and Īśvara, the Supreme Lord. Mumukṣuppaḍi explains the Rahasya-traya, the three sacred teachings central to Śrīvaiṣṇava practice, while Arthapañcakam sets out five essential subjects that a spiritual aspirant must understand. His most widely studied work, Śrīvacana Bhūṣaṇam, gathers and explains the teachings of earlier ācāryas and the Āḻvārs, presenting them as a coherent guide to spiritual life.

A central concern throughout his writings is the relationship between the jīvātmā and the Supreme Lord, and the importance of divine grace in attaining liberation. Lōkācārya explains the significance of prapatti, or surrender to the Lord, and the devotee’s dependence upon divine compassion. His works also emphasise the importance of the ācārya, whose instruction guides the jīvātmā towards a proper understanding of the Lord and the means of attaining Him. Rather than restricting theological instruction to highly learned scholars, his treatises present the essential teachings in an accessible form for those sincerely seeking spiritual understanding.

The later part of Lōkācārya’s life is remembered in connection with the invasion of Śrīraṅgam. Traditional accounts relate that, when the temple faced danger, he helped protect the processional deity of Śrī Raṅganātha and left Śrīraṅgam with the Lord to prevent the deity from falling into the hands of the invaders. During this journey, he eventually reached Jyotiṣkuḍi near Madurai, where he is traditionally said to have attained Paramapadam. His actions are remembered as an expression of his unwavering commitment to the Lord’s service, even in circumstances of great personal hardship.

Piḷḷai Lōkācārya’s legacy rests above all on his ability to preserve and explain the essential teachings of the Śrīvaiṣṇava ācāryas through compact yet profound works. His writings continue to be studied for their explanations of the nature of the jīvātmā, the supremacy of the Lord, the role of divine grace and the path of surrender. Through these works, he ensured that the philosophical and devotional inheritance of the tradition remained available to successive generations.
