## bio

Kūrattāḻvān, also called Kūreśa, was the chief disciple of Rāmānuja and is remembered as the ideal śiṣya, a master of all the śāstras, endowed with every quality a disciple should have, and so humble and gentle that people said there was no one like him. He is described as compassionate to the point that he would not even walk across grass for fear of disfiguring it, and he shunned cruelty in any form. He lived by _uñcavṛtti_, receiving grains as alms from Brahmin households, cooking them, offering them to the Lord, and taking them as prasāda. He was one of the three who sought refuge at Rāmānuja’s feet when he took sannyāsa, along with Nāḍādūr Āḻvān and Mudaliyāṇḍān, and from then on he stayed beside his ācārya. When Rāmānuja went to Tirukkōṭṭiyūr Nambi, Kūrattāḻvān went with him, and waited elsewhere while his master received the teaching alone.

The tradition tells how his sons were born. One day, heavy rain kept him from going out for alms, so there was nothing to cook. He offered fruit to the Lord, finished his worship, read his texts, and went to bed without food. His wife Āṇḍāl could not sleep. Hearing the temple bell that marks the Lord’s food offering, she prayed in distress: “Your devotee is starving while Your worship goes on without any break. Can You not bless him?” Lord Raṅganātha appeared in the dream of Uttamanambi and ordered him to carry the food just offered to Kūrattāḻvān’s house with full temple honours. Kūrattāḻvān, though troubled that the Lord had been moved by his wife’s cry, accepted it as the Lord’s command, took one third himself, and gave the rest to Āṇḍāl. By the greatness of that prasāda she conceived and bore two sons, Parāśara Bhaṭṭar and Vedavyāsa Bhaṭṭar (also called Śrīrāma Bhaṭṭar), whom Rāmānuja himself named. The elder was taken by the Lord as His adopted son.

His most famous service came in the writing of the _Śrībhāṣya_. Rāmānuja asked him to write down what he dictated, and to stop wherever he disagreed. At one point, when Rāmānuja said that the soul is not subservient to God (_bhagavat śeṣatva_) and is full of attributive knowledge, Kūrattāḻvān stopped writing. Rāmānuja pressed him to continue, and when he did not, said angrily, “You complete the _Śrībhāṣya_ yourself,” and sent him away. People asked Kūrattāḻvān if it was right to defy his teacher and risk his anger. He answered that his ācārya would bless him the very next day. That night Rāmānuja reflected and saw that his disciple’s view was right. In the morning he called him back, regretted his anger, and accepted the correction, and Kūrattāḻvān completed the writing.

His greatest trial came with the persecution by the Chola king, who forced Śrīvaiṣṇava scholars to sign a palm leaf that there is none greater than Śiva. When the king’s soldiers came to Rāmānuja’s maṭha, Nāḍādūr Āḻvān engaged them in conversation and learned the real purpose. Kūrattāḻvān admired his quick wit, and then put on Rāmānuja’s tridaṇḍa and ochre robe and went with the soldiers, with Periya Nambi accompanying. Before the king, he declared that Nārāyaṇa alone is supreme, that He creates, protects, and destroys, that He alone gives liberation, and that Brahmā and Rudra are His creation. He cited the verse that one raised His foot, another washed it, and another bore the water on his head, and asked who is greater. When the king ordered him to write “Śivāt parataram nāsti,” he took the leaf and wrote _droṇam asti tataḥ param_, using the fact that _śiva_ can mean a small grain measure (_kuṟuṇi_) and _droṇa_ is a larger one. The king saw the mockery and was furious. He then ordered his soldiers to blind both of them, and Kūrattāḻvān said, “Cola, I do not want these eyes that have looked upon a staunch Śaiva like you,” and pulled out his own eyes and threw them down in the court. Periya Nambi was blinded too, and Attuḻāy, Nambi’s daughter, led the two from the court. Nambi, in great pain, lay with his head on Kūrattāḻvān’s lap, and when Kūrattāḻvān lamented that Nambi would die far from Śrīraṅgam, Nambi answered that wherever a Vaiṣṇava dies, the Lord comes to him, and that no place is more fitting than the lap of such a devotee, and then he died. Kūrattāḻvān performed the funeral rites with local Śrīvaiṣṇavas, and was carried to Śrīraṅgam by palanquin the next night, grieving that his own body had not gone to Paramapadam with Nambi.

His loyalty to Rāmānuja was tested again at Śrīraṅgam. When he crawled toward the sanctum to worship, the guards at the door said that disciples of Udaiyavar were not allowed in, and that they would let him pass only if he declared he had no connection with Rāmānuja. Kūrattāḻvān closed his ears with both hands and said, “I do not want the sight of Emperumān without my connection to Emperumānār,” and went home. When Rāmānuja’s messenger Ciriyāṇḍān came from Melkote to ask after him, he said he was glad the danger to his ācārya’s life had passed at the cost of his eyes, and when the news came that the Chola king had died of his illness, the Śrīvaiṣṇavas were relieved.

When Rāmānuja returned to Śrīraṅgam after twelve years, Kūrattāḻvān prostrated before him again and again. Rāmānuja embraced him and wept, saying, “You lost your sight for the defence of our darśana.” Kūrattāḻvān replied that perhaps it came from some fault of his own, such as having thought a Śrīvaiṣṇava’s tilaka slightly crooked. Rāmānuja said no, it must have come from his own sins, and so consoled him. Later, at Kāñcī, Rāmānuja asked him to compose a hymn to Lord Varadarāja and ask for his sight back, saying the Lord at Kāñcī is the one who gives eyes. He composed the _Varadarājastava_, but in one verse he asked only that the Lord always be visible to his inner eye, not for his bodily sight. Rāmānuja was upset at this. While praying in the sanctum, Kūrattāḻvān then described the Lord’s ornaments in great detail, as if he could see them, and Rāmānuja was delighted. He also composed the verse of praise to Rāmānuja that begins _yo nityam acyuta-padāmbuja-yugma-rukma_, which is recited as the ācārya’s taniyan.

Near the end of his life, he asked Lord Raṅganātha for leave to depart for Paramapadam and serve Him there. He told Rāmānuja and asked for his śrīpāda tīrtham, took it, prayed at his feet, and ascended. Rāmānuja was grieved and had the funeral rites performed by Kūrattāḻvān’s son Bhaṭṭar.
