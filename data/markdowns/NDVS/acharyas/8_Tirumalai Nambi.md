name: Tirumalai Nambi
time_period: "970 CE – 1060 CE"
birthplace: Tirumala
---
Tirumalai Nambi was one of the fifteen disciples who took refuge at the feet of Āḻavandār, and he is remembered as the maternal uncle of Rāmānuja and the teacher who gave him the _Rāmāyaṇa_. He lived at Tirumalai, where he did service to Lord Veṅkaṭeśvara (Tiruvēṅgaḍamuḍaiyān) every day. He had two sisters, Bhūmi Pirāṭṭi and Periyā Pirāṭṭi. The elder was married to Keśava Perumāḷ of Śrīperumbudūr, and the younger to Kamalanayana Bhaṭṭar of Madhuramaṅgalam. He blessed both to have worthy sons, and these became Rāmānuja and Govinda Bhaṭṭar (later Empār). When Rāmānuja was born, Nambi came from Tirumalai to see the infant, and, seeing a face full of the radiance of service, like that of Lakṣmaṇa, he named him Rāmānujan (“younger brother of Rāma”). He also named Govinda Bhaṭṭar when he was born at Madhuramaṅgalam.

He played an important part in bringing Govinda Bhaṭṭar back to the Vaiṣṇava fold. Govinda Bhaṭṭar had been led by Yādavaprakāśa to worship a Śiva liṅga and had become a Śaiva devotee at Kālahasti. Rāmānuja, now a sannyāsin, asked his uncle to reform him. Nambi went to Kālahasti with his disciples, wrote on a palm leaf a line from Āḻavandār’s _Stotraratna_ (that the Lord alone has natural, unbounded lordship over all) and left it where Bhaṭṭar passed on the way to the temple. The words began to trouble Bhaṭṭar’s mind. On another day, Nambi sat teaching the _Tiruvāymoḻi_ under the trees from which Bhaṭṭar gathered flowers. When he reached the verse that flowers and worship are due only to Emperumān, Bhaṭṭar threw down his basket, cried “_tagātu, tagātu_” (“not proper”), and fell at his feet. He gave up the temple keys and ring, went with Nambi to Tirumalai, and was initiated as a Vaiṣṇava. Nambi taught him the Divya Prabandham from the _Tiruppallāṇḍu_.

When Rāmānuja visited Tirumalai, Nambi and other Śrīvaiṣṇavas received him with the temple honours, and Rāmānuja, in turn, bowed to his uncle. Rāmānuja stayed for a year and learned the _Rāmāyaṇa_ with its meanings from him. During that time, Nambi saw that Govinda Bhaṭṭar would lie on his teacher’s bed before preparing it for sleep. When Rāmānuja complained, Nambi asked Bhaṭṭar how he could commit such an offence. Bhaṭṭar replied that he knew it was a sin and that he would go to hell, but he did it to make sure the bed was comfortable for his teacher. Nambi and Rāmānuja both praised his love.

When Rāmānuja was leaving, Nambi made his sons prostrate to him. He pointed out Piḷḷān as fit for Rāmānuja’s special blessing and handed him over as a disciple. At Rāmānuja’s request, he also gave Govinda Bhaṭṭar’s brother Govinda Perumāḷ, pouring water in the formal gift. Later, Govinda Perumāḷ returned to Tirumalai out of longing for Nambi, but Nambi, saying that no one feeds fodder to a cow already sold, refused to take him back. Rāmānuja praised this, and eventually made him a sannyāsin with the name Empār.

Nambi’s household also figures in the story. His son Piḷḷān was born in the year Plava, in the month of Aippasi under Pūrvāṣāḍhā, and became Tirukkurukaippirān Piḷḷān, who wrote the first great commentary on the _Tiruvāymoḻi_. Years later, when Nambi was unwell, his other son Piḷḷai Tirumalai Nambi went to Tirumalai. Nambi attained Paramapadam, and his son performed the final rites. When Rāmānuja heard, he grieved and performed the mourning rites. Nambi’s birth star is recorded as Anusham in the month of Purattāsi.

His contribution lay in his guidance of Rāmānuja’s early life, his teaching of the _Rāmāyaṇa_, and his gift of Piḷḷān, Govinda Perumāḷ, and the reformed Govinda Bhaṭṭar to the tradition.
