## bio

Īśvaramuni, who was well-versed in the _Yogabhāṣya_ and Vedic studies, was born in the divine place of Vīranārāyaṇapuram, a sacred abode believed to be protected by Nārāyaṇa Himself. Well-versed in yoga, he authored two renowned works, _Yogarahasya_ and _Nyāyatattva_, which are now lost. He travelled towards the northern regions, visiting several sacred places and temples, including Mathurā, Śālagrāma, Ayodhyā, and others. While staying near the hills of Govardhana, Nāthamunigaḷ received a divine inspiration to return to his birthplace along with his family. During his return journey, he visited numerous sacred temples, from Puri and Tirumala to places difficult to access even for the celestial beings, such as Ahobilam.

After returning to Vīranārāyaṇapuram, an auspicious incident changed the course of his life. Nāthamunigaḷ heard a captivating hymn sung by the Śrīvaiṣṇavas of Āḻvār Tirunagari, beginning with the words _“Ārāvamudē!”_ Enchanted by its divine beauty, he found the experience more deeply moving and spiritually fulfilling than his own yogic practices. He approached the devotees and enquired about the hymn and its origin. They explained that these ten _pāsurams_ were composed by the great mystic saint Nammāḻvār in praise of the Lord Ārāvamudan of Tirukkuḍantai, presently known as Kumbakonam. These verses belonged to the _Tiruvāymoḻi_ and formed a portion of the thousand pāsurams attributed to Nammāḻvār, who was born in Āḻvār Tirunagari.

Eager to recover the complete collection, Nāthamunigaḷ journeyed to Āḻvār Tirunagari. Unfortunately, he discovered that only the ten pāsurams beginning with _“Ārāvamudē!”_ were available. The remaining verses had been lost to memory and were no longer accessible. Upon reaching the sacred temple of Ādinātha and the birthplace of Nammāḻvār, Nāthamunigaḷ enquired about the lost hymns. A Śrīvaiṣṇava belonging to the lineage of Madhurakavi Āḻvār, the devoted disciple of Nammāḻvār, introduced him to another set of verses: the ten pāsurams of Madhurakavi Āḻvār, beginning with _“Kaṇṇinuṇ Ciṟuttāmpu,”_ which glorify his revered Ācārya, Nammāḻvār.

According to the traditional account, Nāthamunigaḷ recited _Kaṇṇinuṇ Ciṟuttāmpu_ 12,000 times with unwavering devotion. Pleased by his devotion, Nammāḻvār appeared before him and blessed him with the sacred teachings of Vedānta, the complete _Tiruvāymoḻi_, and the magnificent collection of 4,000 hymns composed by all the Āḻvārs, now celebrated as the _Nālāyira Divya Prabandham_. Through the grace of Nammāḻvār, the lost treasure of the Āḻvārs was thus restored to the world.

Following this divine experience, Nāthamunigaḷ travelled to Śrīraṅgam. The temple had a distinctive festival tradition associated with the recitation of the _Divya Prabandham_, particularly the _Tiruvāymoḻi_, during which Nammāḻvār was ceremonially brought from Āḻvār Tirunagari to Śrīraṅgam. This observance, associated with _Adhyayanotsavam_ and the tradition established by Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār, had fallen into discontinuation. Through the efforts of Nāthamunigaḷ, this sacred practice was revived, ensuring the continued recitation and celebration of the Āḻvārs' divine hymns.

Nāthamunigaḷ continued his life in Vīranārāyaṇapuram and eventually attained Paramapadam. His contributions established a firm foundation for the Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya and restored the incomparable pāsurams of the Āḻvārs to their rightful place in religious life. He is traditionally revered as the first Ācārya of the Śrīvaiṣṇava Ācārya lineage after Nammāḻvār, excluding the divine preceptors Lakṣmīnārāyaṇa and Viṣvaksena in the traditional succession.

Nāthamunigaḷ also established the tradition of rendering the _Divya Prabandham_ in a distinctive musical style, preserved by the _Ariyars_ through the tradition of _Deva-gānam_. The musical modes and recitational traditions associated with these sacred hymns continue to form an integral part of Śrīvaiṣṇava temple worship, particularly at Śrīraṅgam. Thus, through the divine grace of Nāthamunigaḷ, the sacred hymns of the Āḻvārs were recovered, preserved, and transmitted to future generations, enabling the Śrīvaiṣṇava sampradāya to flourish through the enduring wisdom and devotion of the _Nālāyira Divya Prabandham_.
