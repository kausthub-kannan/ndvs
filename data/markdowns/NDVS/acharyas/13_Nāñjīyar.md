## bio

Nañjīyar, also called Vedāntī and Nam Jīyar, was a disciple of Parāśara Bhaṭṭar and the teacher who carried the tradition forward after him. According to the source, he was originally a learned Advaita scholar of Melnādu, in the Karnataka region, a master of the six śāstras and very wealthy, who taught many students and kept six seats stacked in his hall. Word of Bhaṭṭar's scholarship reached him through a Śrīvaiṣṇava pilgrim, who told him that a greater scholar lived at Śrīraṅgam and described Bhaṭṭar as learned in _Tiruneḍuntāṇḍakam_, a work Vedāntī had never heard of.

Bhaṭṭar set out for Melnādu to win him over. Hearing that the scholar's students kept visitors in debate for months, he laid aside his palanquin and finery and entered the house disguised as a poor Brahmin with a leaf cup, seeking alms. When Vedāntī asked what alms he wanted, Bhaṭṭar answered, "alms of debate" (_tarka bhikṣā_). Vedāntī guessed who he was. They debated nine days without result. On the ninth night Lord Raṅganātha appeared to Bhaṭṭar in a dream and told him to rely on the fine points of _Tiruneḍuntāṇḍakam_ and on Āḻavandār's works. The next day Bhaṭṭar entered with such confidence that Vedāntī bowed and conceded. Bhaṭṭar insisted they continue, and won. Vedāntī then called him the walking Perumāl, whereas the Lord at Śrīraṅgam lies sleeping, and asked to become his disciple. Bhaṭṭar gave him the five sacraments and told him to give up Advaita and propagate Rāmānuja's teaching.

Soon after, two hungry Śrīvaiṣṇavas were turned away from his house by his two wives. Vedāntī rebuked them, divided his wealth in three, gave two parts to his wives, took sannyāsa, and carried the remaining part to Śrīraṅgam. At Siruputtūr, Ananta Āḻvān met him with affection. At Śrīraṅgam he laid the wealth at Bhaṭṭar's feet. Bhaṭṭar told him to dump it on the Kāveri bank, where it lay untouched. Later Bhaṭṭar allowed him to build a flower garden with it, and had the surplus spent on feeding devotees. Moved by his devotion, Bhaṭṭar called him _nammuḍaiya jīyar_ ("our jīyar"), and from that came the name Nañjīyar. Bhaṭṭar kept him close and taught him all the śāstras and their meanings.

He once asked Bhaṭṭar why the Lord rests at the Candra Puṣkariṇī, and received the answer that Perumāl rests there to save Bhaṭṭar before he even calls. On a pilgrimage to Tiruvaṇai, Bhaṭṭar slept on his lap, and Nañjīyar sat without moving his legs so as not to disturb him, a model of a disciple's devotion. He asked Bhaṭṭar many other questions, among them why Tirumaṅgai Āḻvār names so many shrines, whether bodily purity is needed to utter the Lord's name, and what the surrender to the Lord through the Goddess gains.

After Bhaṭṭar, Nañjīyar became the head of the tradition at Śrīraṅgam. He composed the _Onpatināyirappaḍi_, a commentary on the _Tiruvāymoḻi_, in the manner of the _Śrībhāṣya_. He needed a scribe, and a man named Nambūr Varadarājar, who wrote well but had not yet received the sacraments, offered himself. Nañjīyar said such a work could only be written by one with an ācārya. He initiated him and taught him the commentary. Crossing the Kāveri, Varadarājar lost the palm leaves in a wave. That night Lord Raṅganātha told him to meditate on his ācārya and promised to help him recall. He rewrote the work from memory, with added meanings. Nañjīyar was delighted, embraced him, and said, "This is our child" (_ivan nammuḍaiya piḷḷai_), naming him Tirukkalikanṛi Dāsar. He is remembered as Nampiḷḷai. When Nampiḷḷai asked how one knows one has the Śrīvaiṣṇava nature, Nañjīyar said it lies in believing the Lord's image to be supreme, in showing affection to devotees as to one's own family, and in not holding a grudge at harsh words.

Nañjīyar lived to a hundred. In his last days, a disciple urged him to recite the _dvaya_. He replied that it should be said by everyone, not only the ill, and the disciple bowed in shame. He asked to see the Lord in full splendour, and when the procession stopped before his maṭha, the priest removed Perumāl's ornaments and cloth so he could see Him in His natural beauty. He told Nampiḷḷai that he had nothing left to learn, to teach the tradition, and to take no pride in titles, but remember that Rāmānuja's feet are his shelter. Lying on the lap of Bhagavat Senāpati Jīyar, with his feet on Pinpaḻagiya Perumāḷ Jīyar's lap, he meditated on Bhaṭṭar and ascended to Paramapadam. Nampiḷḷai performed his final rites as for an ascetic.

His contribution lay in his commentary, in the humility with which he gave up learning and wealth for his ācārya, and in preparing Nampiḷḷai to lead the tradition.
