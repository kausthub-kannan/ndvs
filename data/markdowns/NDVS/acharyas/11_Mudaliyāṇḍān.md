## bio

Mudaliyāṇḍān was one of the earliest and closest disciples of Rāmānuja, and one of the three who sought refuge at his feet when he took sannyāsa, along with Nāḍādūr Āḻvān and Kūrattāḻvān. He is also counted among the four chief teachers whom Rāmānuja appointed as _Śrībhāṣya siṁhāsanādhipatis_, and among the seventy-four _siṁhāsanādhipatis_ generally. From the day he took refuge, he served his ācārya without leaving his side. When Rāmānuja left Kāñcī for Śrīraṅgam at the call of Tiruvaraṅga Perumāḷ Araiyar, Mudaliyāṇḍān and Kūrattāḻvān fetched the Lord Varadarāja image Rāmānuja worshipped and the other things needed for worship, and then set out with him directly. He also went with him to Tirukkōṭṭiyūr Nambi, and waited elsewhere, as Rāmānuja had asked, while the teaching was given in private.

His loyalty to Rāmānuja is shown in an episode from the master’s later life. When Periya Nambi’s daughter Attuḻāy was scorned by her mother-in-law, who sarcastically told her to bring a maid as part of her dowry if she wanted help, she went in tears to her father, who sent her to Rāmānuja. Rāmānuja called Mudaliyāṇḍān and sent him with her as a “gift from her father’s side.” He helped her faithfully in her husband’s home. The in-laws, unable to bear this, complained to Periya Nambi, who sent them to Rāmānuja. Rāmānuja said that he had sent Mudaliyāṇḍān to help his ācārya’s daughter, and that if the family found it inconvenient, he would instead help her from where he was, and called him back. The story is remembered as a sign of Rāmānuja’s devotion to his own teacher’s family, and of Mudaliyāṇḍān’s readiness to do any service asked of him.

At the end of Rāmānuja’s life, Mudaliyāṇḍān is among the disciples who recited the Upaniṣads and the _Tiruvāymoḻi_ around him. Rāmānuja then asked Piḷḷān, Kiḍāmbi Āccāṉ, and Mudaliyāṇḍān to consecrate images of himself in the temples, and he embraced three images and placed his power in them. As he directed, Mudaliyāṇḍān consecrated the image at Śrīperumbudūr.

He is remembered through his descendants as well. His grandson was Kandāḍaiyāṇḍān, and his great-grandson was Kandāḍai Toḻappar, who in a fit of jealousy once rebuked Nampillai in the temple, but later repented, fell at his feet, and gave him the title _Lokācārya_. The tradition remembers Mudaliyāṇḍān as the ancestor of that distinguished line.

He is also remembered for a Sanskrit verse in praise of Rāmānuja, _rāmānujāryadivyājñā prativāsaram ujjvalā | digantavyāpinī bhūyāt sā hi lokahitaiṣiṇī_, a prayer that the sacred command of Rāmānuja may shine brighter each day, spread to all directions, and work for the good of the world. Along with Piḷḷān’s verse, it is recited at the end of the Prabandham recitation.
