    Verse 1.21¶

    PrabhandhamTiruppāvaiĀḻvārĀṇḍāḻAvatāramŚrī Kṛṣṇa

    Script¶

    ஏற்ற கலங்கள் எதிர்வபாங்கி மீதளிப்ப
    மாற்றாதே பால் வசாரியும் வள்ளல் வபரும் பகக்கள்
    ஆற்றப் பைடத்தான் மகேன! அறிவுறாய்
    ஊற்றுமைடயாய்! வபரியாய் உலகினில்
    வேதாற்றமாய் நின்ற கடேர! துயிலைழாய்
    மாற்றார் உனக்கு வலிவதாலந்து உன் வாசற்கண்
    ஆற்றாது வந்து உன் அடி பணியுமாவபாவல
    வபாற்றியாம் வந்தோம் பகழ்ந்து ஏல் ஓர் எம்பாவாய்
    ēṭra kalaṅgaḻ edir poṅgi mīdaḻippa
    māṭrādē pāl śoriyum vaḻḻal perum paśukkaḻ
    āṭra ppaḍaittān maganē ! aṟivuṟāy
    ūṭram uḍaiyāy ! periyāy !
    ulaginil tōṭramāy ninṟa śuḍarē ! tuyil ezāy
    māṭrār unakku vali tolaindun vāśaṟ kaṇ
    āṭrādu vandun aḍi paṇiyumā pōlē
    pōṭriyām vandōm pugazndēlōr em pāvāy

    Meaning¶

    O son of Nandagopa, guardian of countless mighty cows—those generous creatures whose udders overflow with milk, filling the pots beneath them without measure—awake! O embodiment of the Supreme, whose strength is extolled by the sacred scriptures, arise! O glorious protector, valiant and unwavering in your promise to shelter all who seek refuge, awaken from your divine slumber! You, who shine as a radiant form of effulgence in this world, hear us now. Just as your foes, subdued by your boundless valour, fall humbly at your feet in surrender, so too have we come—drawn by love, unable to remain apart. At your threshold we stand, yearning to sing your praise and offer our heartfelt benedictions.

    Purport¶

    O son of Nandagopa, guardian of countless strong cows—those bountiful givers whose udders overflow with milk, filling every vessel to the brim—awake! O embodiment of the Supreme, whose might is upheld by the scriptures and whose radiance manifests even in the darkest corners of this world, arise! O valiant protector, ever ready to shelter the surrendered, wake from your divine rest. Just as your enemies, vanquished by your boundless valor, come humbly to your feet, so too have we—irresistibly drawn by love—gathered at your threshold to sing your praises and offer our benedictions.

    Andal, in calling you the son of Nandagopa, presents more than a familial identity. She reveals a profound truth: Nanda represents the Acharya—the spiritual preceptor—through whom one gains access to the Divine. Without the Acharya’s grace, one cannot approach Bhagavan, let alone attain liberation. In likening Nanda to the owner of countless cows, Andal alludes to the boundless virtues of an Acharya—rooted in the Vedas—foremost among them being the eagerness to share spiritual knowledge the moment a disciple yearns for it, just as cows pour forth milk when pots are placed beneath them. Acharyas spiritual genius and influence are immense. Like Nanda’s cows—too numerous to count—their qualities are infinite, just as one cannot number the virtues of Sri Rama, the incarnations of the Lord, or the births of sentient beings. Even Krishna takes pride in this connection, preferring to be known as Nanda’s son, for the wealth of spiritual richness it represents. Though He is the Supreme, He chooses to descend among the cowherds, delighting in their simple devotion. The gopikas remind Him that His birthright and heritage are theirs too, and the abundance He has inherited should be for their protection—not to lull Him into slumber. Once He awakens, all sorrow dissolves; and if sorrow persists, the blame would be His alone. Thus, they call out to Him, urging Him to rise, that their longings may be fulfilled.

    The five attributes of Nandagopa—his vigilant protection, noble leadership, generous charity, great strength, and possession of infinite cows—mirror the fivefold glories of an Acharya: his deep love for the Lord whom he blesses as if in need of protection; his inability to keep spiritual bliss to himself, sharing it freely; his transmission of mantras that lead to realization; his power to overcome spiritual obstacles; and his eloquence that lights the way for countless disciples. These disciples, in turn, become vessels of wisdom, just as the cows of Nanda nourish without end. The gopikas further affirm that this Divine Being, Krishna, is no ordinary child but the very Paratattva—the Supreme Reality—firmly established on the highest authority, the Vedas, which are not of human origin and hence immune to error. The strength of the Supreme lies in being supported by such flawless testimony. Inferential logic falters, but the Vedas remain unshakable, eternal, and pure—united in declaring Krishna to be none other than Srimannarayana. Though He transcends thought and speech, He reveals Himself by grace to those who seek Him, appearing in flesh and form for the benefit of the world.

    So the gopikas exclaim: Ottamudaiyay! Periyay!—“O One founded on the strongest truth! O One of unmeasurable greatness!” The Vedas, trying to grasp His vast bliss, confess defeat: words and mind alike turn back. Yet, by His own will, this Effulgence emerges in the world—Ulaginil toṭṟamāy ninṛa sudarē!—taking human form, not due to karma but out of compassion. Though amidst the fallen world, He remains unsullied, shining even brighter. His greatness is such that even His partiality to devotees causes His enemies, like Śiśupāla and Duryodhana, to scorn Him. But for those who understand, this same favoritism becomes the gateway to grace. Even as a charioteer or envoy, Krishna embraces servitude for the sake of His devotees—showing that His compassion is His crowning glory. To ignore such excellence would diminish His own virtues, and so the gopikas urge Him to awaken, lest those qualities be defiled by inaction.

    They declare: “Like your enemies, who surrender at your feet after defeat, we too come—overcome not by fear but by your beauty, mercy, and charm. Our sins are as grave as theirs; our distance from you as deep. But unlike them, we come not for gain, but to sing your greatness. We’ve tried all else, failed, and now stand surrendered, hearts broken by your attributes.” Just as Kāka Asura, unable to escape, returns to Rama for protection, so too do the gopikas confess: “We are no better than Rāvaṇa, filled with pride and blindness. Yet now, we relinquish all notions of ‘I’ and ‘mine,’ and submit at your feet.” Though true surrender requires complete dependence and no initiative, the gopikas, like Sita awaiting Rama, break from protocol—drawn irresistibly to Krishna’s grace. They know their coming is forbidden, yet they come nonetheless, for their hearts have been captured by His virtues. They confess: “We, who should not have come, have come—like the enemies who surrender out of necessity, but we, out of love. Even if we perish, let your glory shine.” Like the Āḻvārs, they seek no return to their old lives of ego and bondage, but wish only to remain near Him, forever praising the One who was, is, and always will be their eternal refuge.
